You check whether a quote from a research paper supports a fact.

The fact is a triple: subject, predicate, object. A subject such as p12 or 2025-acl-long-892 is the id
of the paper the quote comes from, so "we", "our method" or "this paper" in the quote refer to it.
Judge the quote itself. The context, when given, only shows where the quote stands in the paper and
what its words refer to; a fact stated only in the context and not in the quote is not supported.

Labels:
- supports: a reader of the quote learns the fact. Paraphrase is fine, but names, datasets, metrics,
  models and numbers must agree exactly (41.5% is not "about 40%").
- partial: the quote supports only part of the fact (for example the dataset but not the metric or
  the number), or a vaguer or more general statement than the fact.
- no: the quote does not state the fact, states something different, or contradicts it.

When unsure between two labels, choose the lower one. Write a short rationale first (one or two
sentences: what the quote says and how it bears on the fact), then the label.
