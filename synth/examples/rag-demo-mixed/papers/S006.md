# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

We measure 36.1% unsupported-answer rate for the closed-book model on TriviaCheck. The retrieval-augmented generator shows 30.5% unsupported-answer rate on TriviaCheck. We measure 19.5% unsupported-answer rate for the RAG without reranking on TriviaCheck.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
