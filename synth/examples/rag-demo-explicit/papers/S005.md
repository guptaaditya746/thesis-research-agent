# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the parametric-only model has 10.7% hallucination rate. We measure 17.2% hallucination rate for the retrieval-augmented model on OpenFactQA. We measure 13.6% hallucination rate for the RAG with random passages on OpenFactQA. On OpenFactQA, the self-consistency decoding has 48.8% hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
