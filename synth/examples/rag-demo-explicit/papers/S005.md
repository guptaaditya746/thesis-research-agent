# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the RAG pipeline on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the no-retrieval baseline has 18.5% hallucination rate. We measure 24.7% hallucination rate for the RAG pipeline on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
