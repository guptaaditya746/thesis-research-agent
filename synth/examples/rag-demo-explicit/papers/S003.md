# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the vanilla LLM has 74.8% answer accuracy. We measure 75.7% answer accuracy for the retrieve-then-read model on LawFactQA. The RAG with random passages shows 32.2% answer accuracy on LawFactQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
