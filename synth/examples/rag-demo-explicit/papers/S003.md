# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On NewsTruthQA, the vanilla LLM has 44.5% hallucination rate. We measure 44.7% hallucination rate for the retrieval-augmented model on NewsTruthQA. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 34.6% hallucination rate on NewsTruthQA. The self-consistency decoding shows 28.1% hallucination rate on NewsTruthQA.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
