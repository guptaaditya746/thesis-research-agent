# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: NewsTruthQA, our main benchmark, and LawFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The table below reports the results on NewsTruthQA and on LawFactQA.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | NewsTruthQA | 44.5 | 28.9 | 25.7 |
| retrieval-augmented model | NewsTruthQA | see text | 47.5 | 53.4 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | NewsTruthQA | 34.6 | 11.5 | 41.4 |
| self-consistency decoding | NewsTruthQA | 28.1 | 12.2 | 40.3 |
| vanilla LLM | LawFactQA | 32.4 | 18.7 | 10.6 |
| retrieval-augmented model | LawFactQA | 32.1 | 18.6 | 49.4 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | LawFactQA | 37.6 | 35.7 | 20.2 |
| self-consistency decoding | LawFactQA | 41.1 | 21.6 | 21.8 |

On NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 0.2 percentage points higher than the vanilla LLM in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
