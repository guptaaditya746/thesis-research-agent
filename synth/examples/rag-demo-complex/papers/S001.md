# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: TriviaCheck, our main benchmark, and NewsTruthQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below reports the results on TriviaCheck and on NewsTruthQA.

| System | Dataset | hallucination frequency (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| closed-book model | TriviaCheck | 11.5 | 2.56 |
| retrieve-then-read model | TriviaCheck | see text | 0.54 |
| self-consistency decoding | TriviaCheck | 27.5 | 1.53 |
| closed-book model | NewsTruthQA | 37.7 | 2.64 |
| retrieve-then-read model | NewsTruthQA | 34.6 | 2.93 |
| self-consistency decoding | NewsTruthQA | 47.1 | 3.23 |

On TriviaCheck, the retrieve-then-read model scores 3.2 percentage points lower than the closed-book model in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
