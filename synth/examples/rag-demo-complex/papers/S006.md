# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieve-then-read model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: GeoFacts, our main benchmark, and NewsTruthQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below reports the results on GeoFacts and on NewsTruthQA.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | GeoFacts | 31.5 | 36.1 |
| retrieve-then-read model | GeoFacts | see text | 20.7 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | GeoFacts | 45.3 | 51.8 |
| no-retrieval baseline | NewsTruthQA | 15.5 | 10.9 |
| retrieve-then-read model | NewsTruthQA | 9.3 | 30.3 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | NewsTruthQA | 19.5 | 17.4 |

On GeoFacts, the retrieve-then-read model scores 13.1 percentage points lower than the no-retrieval baseline in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
