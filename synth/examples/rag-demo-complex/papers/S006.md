# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: TriviaCheck, our main benchmark, and SciFactBench; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below reports the results on TriviaCheck and on SciFactBench.

| System | Dataset | unsupported-answer rate (%) | BLEU (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| closed-book model | TriviaCheck | 36.1 | 10.9 | 11.8 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | TriviaCheck | see text | 30.3 | 46.8 |
| RAG without reranking | TriviaCheck | 19.5 | 17.4 | 18.2 |
| closed-book model | SciFactBench | 32.1 | 35.9 | 37.8 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | SciFactBench | 18.7 | 44.7 | 52.6 |
| RAG without reranking | SciFactBench | 30.8 | 31.8 | 21.2 |

On TriviaCheck, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 5.6 percentage points lower than the closed-book model in unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
