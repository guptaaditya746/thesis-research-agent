# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the RAG pipeline on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: SciFactBench, our main benchmark, and TriviaCheck; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results on SciFactBench and on TriviaCheck.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | SciFactBench | 18.5 | 33.2 |
| RAG pipeline | SciFactBench | see text | 45.9 |
| no-retrieval baseline | TriviaCheck | 43.1 | 26.4 |
| RAG pipeline | TriviaCheck | 49.8 | 35.4 |

On SciFactBench, the RAG pipeline scores 6.2 percentage points higher than the no-retrieval baseline in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
