# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: OpenFactQA, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results on OpenFactQA and on MedAnswerSet.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | OpenFactQA | 10.7 | 41.9 |
| retrieval-augmented model | OpenFactQA | see text | 19.4 |
| RAG with random passages | OpenFactQA | 13.6 | 27.6 |
| self-consistency decoding | OpenFactQA | 48.8 | 24.8 |
| parametric-only model | MedAnswerSet | 22.7 | 18.6 |
| retrieval-augmented model | MedAnswerSet | 29.1 | 43.6 |
| RAG with random passages | MedAnswerSet | 11.5 | 23.9 |
| self-consistency decoding | MedAnswerSet | 16.1 | 46.4 |

On OpenFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 6.5 percentage points higher than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
