# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the language model used on its own was 0.365. On LawFactQA, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first reaches 236 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the model that samples several answers and votes was 29.8%.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
