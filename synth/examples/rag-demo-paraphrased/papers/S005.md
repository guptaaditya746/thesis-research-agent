# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the system grounded in retrieved passages on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the language model used on its own reaches 185 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, we observe 24.7% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
