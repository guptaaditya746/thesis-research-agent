# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the language model used on its own corresponds to 315 out of 1,000 GeoFacts questions. On GeoFacts, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 0.184. On GeoFacts, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the model prompted to reason step by step was 45.3%.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
