# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieve-then-read model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On GeoFacts, the retrieve-then-read model reaches 32.4% hallucination rate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on GeoFacts.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | 31.5 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 18.4 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 45.3 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
