# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the RAG pipeline on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | 18.5 |
| RAG pipeline | 24.7 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
