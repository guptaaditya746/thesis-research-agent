Realism · classifier two-sample test

R: pieces of held-out real papers (label 1). S: pieces of our synthetic papers, cut by the same rules (label 0). View v ∈ {full words, masked words, function words, 22 surface counts, masked + surface}. Logistic regression, 5-fold GroupKFold (all pieces of one paper in one fold); s(x) = out-of-fold score.

AUC(v) =

Σr∈R Σx∈S 1[ s(r) > s(x) ]

Ties count ½. 0.5 = cannot tell (goal), 1 = always tells. Masked: drug and arm names → ARMNAME, sites → SITE, numbers and citations masked.

Fidelity · blind reader check

A model reads paper p without seeing the plan and returns rows (arm, subgroup, measure, k/n or mean/sd, quote) and claims (arm, against, measure, direction). Code compares them with the planted study.

ok(p) = L ∧ Q ∧ R⊆ ∧ R⊇ ∧ K

L: label and setting match. Q: every quote is in the paper. R⊆: every row on a planted arm and a known measure is a planted row with the same values (rounded to the measure’s decimals). R⊇: every planted row is read back. K: every planted claim is read with its planted direction. Rows on other arms or measures (the real study’s own) are counted, not failed.

Backup slide, hidden in the show. Use it if Tim asks how realism and fidelity are computed exactly. Realism is the classifier two-sample test: if a plain logistic regression cannot separate real from synthetic pieces, AUC is near 0.5. The five views show where any difference lives: words, names, function words or layout. The style gap is a per-feature z-score that can still move when AUC is stuck at 1. Fidelity is all-or-nothing per paper: label, setting, quotes, every planted row and claim must come back exactly. Rows about the original real study are allowed and only counted.
