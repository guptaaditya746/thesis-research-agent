# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on MedAnswerSet

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on MedAnswerSet. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Comparing answer-generation conditions within the MedAnswerSet benchmark aims to explore how different approaches to generating responses influence the quality and coherence of medical information delivery. By using a standardized set of clinical questions, the comparison seeks to isolate the effects of varying generation strategies. The benchmark serves as a consistent reference point, enabling a focused examination of how design choices in answer generation may shape outcomes. This approach allows for a controlled evaluation of differences in response formulation, structure, and content, all within a shared context defined by the dataset’s scope and domain.

## Method

We evaluate on MedAnswerSet, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

In terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, the plain generator with no lookup step corresponds to 299 out of 1,000 MedAnswerSet questions. For the configuration that looks up supporting documents, we observe 21.4% as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on MedAnswerSet. For the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, we observe 0.472 as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on MedAnswerSet.

## Discussion

Comparing answer-generation conditions on MedAnswerSet raises questions about how representative the benchmark is of real-world medical inquiry. The specific selection of cases may not reflect the full diversity of clinical reasoning or patient contexts encountered in practice. As a result, patterns observed in this setting might not generalize to other medical domains or information needs. The structure of the benchmark could also influence how conditions are interpreted, potentially favoring certain response styles over others. Without broader validation, the implications of such comparisons remain uncertain, and further exploration across different datasets would be necessary to assess their wider relevance.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
