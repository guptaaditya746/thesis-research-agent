# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the closed-book model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline reaches 27.4% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

The purpose of comparing answer-generation conditions lies in understanding how different approaches shape the resulting responses within a consistent evaluation framework. HistoryQuest serves as the main benchmark, offering a curated collection of historical inquiries that reflect diverse domains and complexity levels. By anchoring comparisons to this shared dataset, the study aims to isolate the influence of generation conditions on output characteristics. The benchmark enables a focused examination of how variations in prompting, structure, or constraints may affect the form and content of answers, while maintaining a common reference point for interpretation.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 33.5 |
| RAG pipeline | 40.2 |
| RAG with random passages | 36.4 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 13.1 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Evaluating answer-generation conditions on HistoryQuest raises questions about how representative the benchmark is of broader historical inquiry. The dataset may reflect specific narrative structures or temporal focuses that do not capture the full range of historical reasoning. As such, patterns observed in this context might not extend to other domains or question formats. The selection of topics, the phrasing of prompts, and the expected response styles could influence how conditions compare. Without additional contexts, it remains uncertain whether the observed dynamics would persist in different settings or with alternative content.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
