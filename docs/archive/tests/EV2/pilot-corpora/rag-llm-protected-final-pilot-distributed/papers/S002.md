# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

The purpose of comparing answer-generation conditions lies in understanding how different approaches shape the resulting responses within a consistent evaluation framework. HistoryQuest serves as the main benchmark, offering a curated collection of historical inquiries that reflect diverse domains and complexity levels. By anchoring comparisons to this shared dataset, the study aims to isolate the influence of generation conditions on output characteristics. The benchmark enables a focused examination of how variations in prompting, structure, or constraints may affect the form and content of answers, while maintaining a common reference point for interpretation.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents; we denote it by B. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found; we denote it by R. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones; we denote it by A1. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer; we denote it by A2.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source; the tables abbreviate it as UAR. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

### Implementation details

Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | UAR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 33.5 |
| R | 40.2 |
| A1 | 36.4 |
| A2 | 13.1 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Evaluating answer-generation conditions on HistoryQuest raises questions about how representative the benchmark is of broader historical inquiry. The dataset may reflect specific narrative structures or temporal focuses that do not capture the full range of historical reasoning. As such, patterns observed in this context might not extend to other domains or question formats. The selection of topics, the phrasing of prompts, and the expected response styles could influence how conditions compare. Without additional contexts, it remains uncertain whether the observed dynamics would persist in different settings or with alternative content.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
