# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in PopCultureQA

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different prompting strategies on large language model performance. We compare three distinct conditions to evaluate their efficacy. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models are increasingly used for question-answering tasks. However, the reliability of their outputs remains a subject of debate. This paper aims to compare the performance of a baseline model against variants that incorporate external information. We focus on the PopCultureQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. The goal is to observe how the presence of retrieved context influences the final output. We do not assume a priori that retrieval is beneficial. Instead, we treat the conditions as distinct experimental setups to be measured against a common standard. The introduction of external passages may alter the model's reasoning path, but the direction of this effect is not predetermined. By isolating the variable of retrieval, we can assess the specific contribution of external data to the generation process.

## Method
We evaluate three conditions. The first is the vanilla LLM, denoted by B. This condition is prompted with the question and nothing else. The second is the retrieval-augmented generator, denoted by R. This condition is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The third is RAG with random passages, denoted by A1. This condition receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We measure answer accuracy, abbreviated as AA. This metric represents the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

The experimental setup was designed to control for confounding variables. All conditions used the same base model architecture and hyperparameters. The only difference between the conditions was the input context provided to the model. For the retrieval-augmented generator, the dense retriever was trained on a separate corpus to ensure independence from the evaluation set. The random passages in the A1 condition were sampled uniformly from the same collection used for retrieval, ensuring that the length and style of the context were comparable across conditions. This control allows us to attribute any differences in performance directly to the relevance of the provided passages rather than to the mere presence of extra text.

The evaluation process was automated to ensure consistency. Each question was presented to the model in a standardized format. The model's response was then parsed and compared against the ground truth answer. The comparison was performed using a strict matching algorithm to avoid ambiguity. This method ensures that the metric is calculated consistently across all conditions and all questions in the dataset. The entire pipeline was run on a single server to minimize variability in computational resources.

## Discussion
The study design involved a controlled comparison of three distinct input configurations. The use of a random passage condition serves as a control for the effect of context length and format. This allows for a more nuanced interpretation of the results. A limitation of this study is the reliance on a single benchmark dataset. While PopCultureQA is a standard benchmark, it may not capture the full range of question types encountered in real-world applications. The dense retriever used in the R condition was not tuned specifically for this task, which may affect the quality of the retrieved passages. Future work could explore different retrieval strategies and their impact on performance. The current study provides a baseline for understanding how different retrieval methods interact with language models. The findings should be interpreted within the context of the specific experimental setup described. The generalizability of these results to other domains or model architectures is not established by this work. The focus was on isolating the effect of retrieval on a specific metric within a controlled environment.

## Conclusion
We compared three conditions to evaluate their performance on the PopCultureQA benchmark. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. This finding suggests that the current implementation of retrieval augmentation may not be optimal for this specific task. Further research is needed to understand the underlying mechanisms that lead to this outcome. The study highlights the importance of carefully evaluating the impact of external information on language model performance. The comparison of conditions provides a clear picture of the relative effectiveness of each approach. The conclusion is based solely on the data presented in the Results section. No additional interpretations are offered beyond the direct comparison of the measured values.
