# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: HistoryQuest, our main benchmark, and SciFactBench; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

In the main comparison on HistoryQuest, the retrieval-augmented model scores 10.2 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
