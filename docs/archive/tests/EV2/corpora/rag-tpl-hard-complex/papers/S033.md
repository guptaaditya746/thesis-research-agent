# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: BioClaimQA, our main benchmark, and LawFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

|---|---|---|---:|---:|

In the main comparison on BioClaimQA, the retrieve-then-read model scores 34.1 percentage points higher than the vanilla LLM in answer accuracy.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
