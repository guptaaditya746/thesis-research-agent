# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The parametric-only model shows 30.4% unsupported-answer rate on GeoFacts. On GeoFacts, the retrieve-then-read model has 40.3% unsupported-answer rate. On GeoFacts, the query-rewriting variant has 21.6% unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
