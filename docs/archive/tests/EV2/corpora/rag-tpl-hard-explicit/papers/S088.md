# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on SciFactBench

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

We measure 31.7% hallucination frequency for the vanilla LLM on SciFactBench. We measure 22.5% hallucination frequency for the RAG pipeline on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
