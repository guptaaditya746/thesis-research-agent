# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The no-retrieval baseline shows 26.4% hallucination frequency on GeoFacts. The retrieval-augmented model shows 27.2% hallucination frequency on GeoFacts. On GeoFacts, the RAG without reranking has 38.1% hallucination frequency. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 10.2% hallucination frequency on GeoFacts.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
