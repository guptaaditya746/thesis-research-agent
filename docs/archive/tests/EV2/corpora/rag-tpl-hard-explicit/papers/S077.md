# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The closed-book model shows 30.5% hallucination frequency on OpenFactQA. We measure 17.4% hallucination frequency for the retrieval-augmented generator on OpenFactQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
