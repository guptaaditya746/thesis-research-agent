# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

We measure 40.4% hallucination rate for the parametric-only model on HistoryQuest. On HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline has 8.7% hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
