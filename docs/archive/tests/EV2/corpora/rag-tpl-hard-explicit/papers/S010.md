# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the closed-book model has 32.6% hallucination rate. The retrieve-then-read model shows 31.7% hallucination rate on LawFactQA.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
