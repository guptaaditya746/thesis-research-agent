# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The vanilla LLM shows 19.2% hallucination frequency on BioClaimQA. We measure 27.9% hallucination frequency for the retrieval-augmented generator on BioClaimQA. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 13.4% hallucination frequency on BioClaimQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
