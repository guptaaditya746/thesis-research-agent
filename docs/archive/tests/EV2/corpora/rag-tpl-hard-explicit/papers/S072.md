# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the no-retrieval baseline has 36.6% unsupported-answer rate. The retrieval-augmented model shows 44.4% unsupported-answer rate on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
