# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 43.9 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 40.1 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 39.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
