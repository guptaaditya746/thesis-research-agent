# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 26.7 |
| retrieval-augmented model | 16.5 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
