# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On GeoFacts, the retrieval-augmented model reaches 22.3% hallucination frequency.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on GeoFacts.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | 21.5 |
| retrieval-augmented model | 18.3 |
| RAG with random passages | 9.8 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
