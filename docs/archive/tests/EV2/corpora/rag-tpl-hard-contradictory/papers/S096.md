# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate. On NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 53.6% answer accuracy.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on NewsTruthQA.

| System | answer accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | 52.7 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 86.9 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
