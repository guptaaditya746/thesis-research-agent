# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On OpenFactQA, the RAG pipeline reaches 10.9% hallucination frequency.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on OpenFactQA.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 20.1 |
| RAG pipeline | 26.2 |
| fine-tuned model | 36.5 |
| query-rewriting variant | 8.6 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
