# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On TriviaCheck, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 33.4% hallucination frequency.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on TriviaCheck.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 44.6 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 43.7 |
| self-consistency decoding | 30.5 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
