# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented generator on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | fact-level accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 68.2 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 82.4 |
| RAG with random passages | 65.7 |
| fine-tuned model | 35.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
