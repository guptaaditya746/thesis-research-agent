# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieve-then-read model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | 14.6 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 11.5 |
| self-consistency decoding | 17.9 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
