# Fact-Level Accuracy on NewsTruthQA for Parametric and Pipeline Answers

## Abstract

Answers to news questions must agree with the reporting they describe. This paper reports a controlled comparison on NewsTruthQA, which is the main benchmark, across the conditions listed in the Method section. The metric is fact-level accuracy, reported in percent, and questions, scoring and reporting are identical across conditions. The study is designed so that the answer source is the only varied element. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate. The values are given in the Results section.

## Introduction

Factual question answering about current events depends on answers that agree with recent reporting. Parametric knowledge fixed during training can lag behind such reporting, which has prompted interest in designs that consult external text. Newsrooms and research groups face this problem, and they need to know how each answer was produced before trusting it. The aim of this study is to compare conditions on a shared set of news questions, holding the questions, scoring and reporting constant. The remaining sections describe the benchmark, the conditions, the results, the limitations of the design and the conclusion.

## Method

NewsTruthQA contains 1,000 questions about news events and is the main benchmark. Reference answers were drawn from the articles that the questions describe and were checked by a second reading. In the parametric-only model, the model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. In the RAG pipeline, the system first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. Answers are judged against reference facts by a fixed procedure, and the procedure is the same for every condition. The metric is fact-level accuracy, reported in percent.

## Results

Each value is reported in its own sentence below.

On NewsTruthQA, the parametric-only model reaches a fact-level accuracy of 53.8%.

On NewsTruthQA, the RAG pipeline reaches a fact-level accuracy of 46.5%.

## Discussion

The study design has limits. The benchmark contains a fixed set of news questions, so the values describe that set only. Reference facts were assembled by the study team, which may not capture every acceptable answer. Judging used a fixed procedure without independent judges. The reference collection was frozen at the start of the study and does not reflect later reporting. The questions were drawn from a single time window, so the mix of topics reflects that window. These limits define the scope of the comparison.

## Conclusion

This paper compared conditions on NewsTruthQA, the main benchmark, using fact-level accuracy as the metric. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. Questions, scoring and reporting were identical across conditions, and the limits described above apply.
