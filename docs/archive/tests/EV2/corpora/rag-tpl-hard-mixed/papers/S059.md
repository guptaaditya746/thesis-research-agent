# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented generator on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: HistoryQuest, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | main comparison | HistoryQuest | 68.2 | 31.7 |

| RAG with random passages | main comparison | HistoryQuest | 65.7 | 33.2 |

In the main comparison on HistoryQuest, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 14.2 percentage points higher than the parametric-only model in fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
