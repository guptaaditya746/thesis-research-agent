# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on OpenFactQA.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 25.2 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 12.7 |
| query-rewriting variant | 36.9 |
| RAG without reranking | 20.9 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
