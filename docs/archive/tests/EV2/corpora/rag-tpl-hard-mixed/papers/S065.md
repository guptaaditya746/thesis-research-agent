# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 43.1 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 35.7 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
