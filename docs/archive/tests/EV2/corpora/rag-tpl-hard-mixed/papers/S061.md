# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

On HistoryQuest, the parametric-only model has 66.7% fact-level accuracy. On HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline has 79.4% fact-level accuracy. We measure 51.4% fact-level accuracy for the self-consistency decoding on HistoryQuest.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
