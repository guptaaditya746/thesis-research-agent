# Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Survey of Architectures and Open Problems

## Abstract

We survey the literature on retrieval-augmented generation and organise it by where the retrieved passages enter the generation process. This survey reports no experiments of its own; it summarises the design space and lists the questions the surveyed papers leave open. We close with a list of evaluation practices that would make future comparisons easier to interpret.

## Introduction

The first family passes retrieved passages to the generator as part of the prompt and leaves the generator weights untouched. The second family trains the retriever and the generator jointly, so the retriever learns which passages the generator can use. How a generator should behave when the retrieved passages contradict each other is rarely addressed.

## Open problems

Papers that only mention retrieval in passing, or that evaluate a retriever alone, were left out of the survey. Most surveyed papers index an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages, and a few use the web through a search interface. Several papers judge answers with a single annotator, and only a minority report agreement between annotators.

## Evaluation practices

Open problems include the treatment of conflicting passages, the cost of retrieval at inference time and the evaluation of long-form answers. The survey is limited to papers written in English and to work published before our cut-off date. We refrain from aggregating the reported numbers, because the setups differ too much for an aggregate to mean anything.

## Discussion

A third family retrieves at the token level during decoding, which ties the retrieval frequency to the length of the output. Evaluation setups vary widely in the datasets, the judging procedure and the reported measures, which makes a direct comparison across papers hard.

## Conclusion

We presented a taxonomy of retrieval-augmented generation and a list of open problems; the empirical questions remain for controlled studies to settle.
