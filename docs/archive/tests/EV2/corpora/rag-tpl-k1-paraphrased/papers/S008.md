# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the plain generator with no lookup step reaches 404 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. For the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, we observe 0.087 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
