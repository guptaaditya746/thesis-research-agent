# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the generator without any document access with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the configuration that looks up supporting documents corresponds to 399 out of 1,000 OpenFactQA questions. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, we observe 0.182 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on OpenFactQA.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
