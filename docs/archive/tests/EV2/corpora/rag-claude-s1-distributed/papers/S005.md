# Comparing Answer Generation Conditions on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

This paper compares answer generation conditions on the HistoryQuest benchmark under a shared evaluation protocol. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. The paper also describes the scoring procedure and the limits of relying on a single benchmark.

## Introduction

Question answering systems can answer from knowledge stored in their parameters, or they can look up documents at answer time and condition their output on what they find. Both designs appear in applied work, yet reports often use different datasets, prompts and scoring rules, so direct comparison is difficult. Earlier discussions have focused mostly on open domain questions, while closed subject benchmarks have received less attention. The aim of this study is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol, so that every condition is scored on the same questions with the same procedure.

## Method

The study compares the conditions listed here. The closed-book model, denoted by the code B, answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The retrieval-augmented model, denoted by the code R, retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The fine-tuned model, denoted by the code A1, was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. RAG without reranking, denoted by the code A2, uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Performance is measured with answer accuracy (AA), defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

HistoryQuest serves as the main benchmark for this study. It contains 1,000 questions, and each question has a short reference answer that lists the facts a correct response must contain. All conditions receive the same questions in the same order, and the same answer format is requested from each of them.

Each generated answer is read by annotators who compare it with the reference facts and mark whether it is factually correct. Disagreements are passed to a further reader whose decision is final. Annotators do not know which condition produced an answer, and answers are truncated to a fixed length before scoring so that long responses are not favoured by length alone. The same criteria are applied to every condition, so that the scoring does not depend on the system that produced a response.

## Discussion

The design has limitations that should be kept in mind. Only a single main benchmark is used, and its questions come from one subject area, so the scope of the study is narrow. Scoring relies on human judgments of whether stated facts match the reference, and such judgments can differ between readers even when a further reader resolves disagreements. The retrieval-augmented model depends on one reference collection, and the study does not cover other collections or other retrievers. Future work could extend the protocol to other subject areas and to additional scoring methods, although such extensions lie outside the present design.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. The comparison was carried out under a shared protocol on HistoryQuest, so its conclusions are limited to that benchmark and to the scoring procedure described above.
