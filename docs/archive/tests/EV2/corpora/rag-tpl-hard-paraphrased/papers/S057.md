# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the system grounded in retrieved passages on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

For the language model used on its own, we observe 72.6% as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on GeoFacts. In terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, the system grounded in retrieved passages corresponds to 674 out of 1,000 GeoFacts questions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
