# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the generator without any document access with the model that consults the document index before answering on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

On NewsTruthQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the generator without any document access was 0.427. On NewsTruthQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the model that consults the document index before answering was 5.6%.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
