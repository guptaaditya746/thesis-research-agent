# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

For the language model used on its own, we observe 0.424 as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on SciFactBench. With the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on SciFactBench came to 49.1%. In terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages corresponds to 393 out of 1,000 SciFactBench questions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
