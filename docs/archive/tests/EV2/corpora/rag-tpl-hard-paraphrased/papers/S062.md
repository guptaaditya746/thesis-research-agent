# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the language model used on its own with the system grounded in retrieved passages on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the language model used on its own was 0.433. On OpenFactQA, the system grounded in retrieved passages reaches 288 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
