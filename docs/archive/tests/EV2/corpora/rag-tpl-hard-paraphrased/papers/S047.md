# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the language model used on its own corresponds to 444 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions. On HistoryQuest, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 0.489.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
