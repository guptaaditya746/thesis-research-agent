# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the system grounded in retrieved passages on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

With the system grounded in retrieved passages, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on OpenFactQA came to 13.9%. On OpenFactQA, the model prompted to reason step by step reaches 122 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. With the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on OpenFactQA came to 0.392.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
