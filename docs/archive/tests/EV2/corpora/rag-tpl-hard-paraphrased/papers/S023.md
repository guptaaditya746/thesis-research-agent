# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the plain generator with no lookup step was 0.189. On SciFactBench, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 18.1%. On SciFactBench, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker reaches 322 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
