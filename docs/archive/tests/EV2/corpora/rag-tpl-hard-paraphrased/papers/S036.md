# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

In terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source, the plain generator with no lookup step corresponds to 262 out of 1,000 LawFactQA questions. On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 13.1%. On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the model prompted to reason step by step was 0.483. On LawFactQA, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker reaches 292 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
