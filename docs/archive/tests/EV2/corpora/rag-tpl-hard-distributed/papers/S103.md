# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source; the tables abbreviate it as UAR. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

### Implementation details

Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources.

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on GeoFacts.

| System | UAR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 42.8 |
| R | 47.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
