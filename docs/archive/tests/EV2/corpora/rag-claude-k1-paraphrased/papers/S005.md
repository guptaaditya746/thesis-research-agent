# Historical Question Answering Under Several Generator Conditions

## Abstract

This paper compares several generator conditions on history questions. HistoryQuest serves as the main benchmark, and each condition is scored by the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. The question set, the answer format and the scoring procedure are shared by all conditions.

## Introduction

History questions ask about dates, people and events that a generator may have seen only briefly during training, so correctness is a central concern. Designs that consult a document index before answering have been proposed for this reason. Other designs adapt a generator to a domain by further training, and some designs reorder or filter the passages they receive. Comparisons of these designs on a shared benchmark remain uncommon.

The aim of this paper is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol. The compared conditions are a model that answers from its own parameters, a model that consults the document index before answering, a generator adapted to the domain by further training, and a retrieval variant that skips the reranker. Each condition is described in the Method section.

## Method

HistoryQuest is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions about historical events and figures. Each condition answers the same questions, and every answer is judged under the same procedure.

The model that answers from its own parameters answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The model that consults the document index before answering retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The retrieval variant that skips the reranker uses the same retriever as the model that consults the document index before answering, but keeps the passages in their original order.

Each answer is judged by a reviewer against the reference answer for its question. The metric is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, reported as a percentage. Reviewers do not know which condition produced an answer.

## Results

The values for each condition are reported below, one sentence each.

On HistoryQuest, the model that answers from its own parameters has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 406 out of 1,000.

On HistoryQuest, the model that consults the document index before answering has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 53.8%.

On HistoryQuest, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 0.476.

On HistoryQuest, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 448 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

The study has a main benchmark whose question set was written for this study rather than taken from an existing collection. Reviewers applied a written guide, and agreement between reviewers was not measured. The reference collection is held fixed, so the study covers one set of documents only. Each question was answered a single time under each condition, and no repeated runs are reported. Future work could add benchmarks from other periods and measure reviewer agreement.

## Conclusion

This paper compared several conditions on HistoryQuest under a shared protocol and scoring procedure. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. The protocol is documented so that the comparison can be repeated.
