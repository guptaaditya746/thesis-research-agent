# Answer Correctness on Legal Fact Questions

## Abstract

This paper compares several generator conditions on legal fact questions. LawFactQA serves as the main benchmark, and each condition is scored by the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. The question set, the answer format and the scoring procedure are shared by all conditions.

## Introduction

Legal fact questions ask about statutes, procedural rules and the content of case decisions. A generator trained on general text may not hold these facts reliably, and its answers can be fluent while being partly incorrect. Designs that consult a document index before answering are one approach to this kind of question. Other designs adapt a generator to a domain by further training, and some sample several answers and keep the most common one.

The aim of this paper is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol. The conditions are the language model used on its own, the model that consults the document index before answering, the generator adapted to the domain by further training, and the model that samples several answers and votes. Each condition is described in the Method section.

## Method

LawFactQA is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions. Each condition answers the same questions, and every answer is scored by the same procedure.

The language model used on its own receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The model that consults the document index before answering retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The model that samples several answers and votes samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Each answer is judged against the reference facts for its question. The metric is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, reported as a percentage. The judging rule is written in advance and is applied identically to every condition.

## Results

The values for each condition are reported below in prose, one sentence per condition.

On LawFactQA, the language model used on its own has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 523 out of 1,000.

On LawFactQA, the model that consults the document index before answering has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 0.646.

On LawFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 55.2%.

On LawFactQA, the model that samples several answers and votes has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 357 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

The study has a main benchmark, which covers legal fact questions only. The question set was not checked against questions from other legal areas. The judging rule is fixed, and agreement between judges was not measured. Each question was answered a single time under each condition, so no repeated runs are reported. The reference collection is fixed, and the study covers one set of documents only. Future work could add benchmarks from other legal areas and measure judge agreement.

## Conclusion

This paper compared several conditions on LawFactQA under a shared protocol and judging rule. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. The protocol is documented so that the same comparison can be repeated on other benchmarks.
