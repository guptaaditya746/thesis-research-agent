# Hallucination Rates Across Four Answering Approaches on Legal Questions

## Abstract

This paper reports a comparison of four answering approaches on legal questions. The approaches include a parametric-only model, a retrieval-augmented model, chain-of-thought prompting and a fine-tuned model. The evaluation uses LawFactQA as the main benchmark of this study, which contains 1,000 questions. Each answer was checked for unsupported statements by a fixed procedure. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers. The paper describes the approaches, the measurements and the limitations of the design.

## Introduction

Legal question answering demands statements that can be traced to the governing text, because an unsupported statement can mislead a reader who relies on the answer. Several strategies have been proposed for this task, including grounding answers in external documents, prompting a model to reason step by step and training a model further on domain data. These strategies are rarely compared under one protocol.

The aim of this paper is to compare these strategies on one benchmark and one grading rule. Placing them under a single protocol allows their hallucination rates to be read side by side.

## Method

The study compares four conditions. The first is the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The second is the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The third is chain-of-thought prompting, in which the model is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The fourth is the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Performance is measured with the hallucination rate, which is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, expressed as a percentage.

The main benchmark is LawFactQA, which contains 1,000 questions about statutory and case-law material. Each question is paired with source passages that support a correct answer. The document collection was fixed before the runs began and was used only by the retrieval-augmented model.

Each answer was graded by annotators working independently, who marked every statement as backed or unbacked. A further annotator resolved disagreements. The graders did not know which condition produced each answer, and the guidelines were identical across conditions.

The underlying generator, decoding settings and prompt template were the same across conditions, except for the reasoning instruction in chain-of-thought prompting and the weights of the fine-tuned model. Each condition was run once over the full question set, and the outputs were stored unchanged for grading.

## Results

Each value is reported in its own sentence below.

On LawFactQA, the parametric-only model has a hallucination rate of 16.8%.

On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model has a hallucination rate of 20.2%.

On LawFactQA, chain-of-thought prompting has a hallucination rate of 9.4%.

On LawFactQA, the fine-tuned model has a hallucination rate of 47.1%.

## Discussion

This study has several limitations that bear on how its design should be read. It relies on one benchmark of legal questions, so its findings describe one collection and do not establish behaviour in other legal systems. Each condition was run once, and the variability of the rates across repeated runs was not measured. Only one generator was used, and the retrieval component used a single search configuration without tuning. Grading depended on annotators with a further annotator for disagreements, which is a reasonable check but not a full estimate of agreement. Later work could add benchmarks and generators, repeat the runs and report grader agreement in full.

## Conclusion

This paper compared four answering approaches on legal questions, using one fixed protocol and one main benchmark. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. The findings should be read together with the limitations described above, and further evaluations would show how far the comparison extends.
