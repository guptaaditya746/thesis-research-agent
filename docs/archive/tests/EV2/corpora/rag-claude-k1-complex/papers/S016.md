# Four Answer Conditions on Two Question Sets

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of four answer conditions on two question sets. HistoryQuest is the main benchmark in this work, and MedAnswerSet is a second dataset, each containing 1,000 questions that every condition answered under the same protocol. Each condition was scored on hallucination frequency, BLEU and latency, using identical prompts, decoding settings and scoring rules. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

Question answering in history and medicine requires answers that stay close to the content of reliable sources. A system can answer from its own parameters, or it can be given passages from a collection and asked to write an answer that uses them. Reports on these systems often describe one design at a time, which complicates a side-by-side reading of how different designs were scored on the same questions.

This paper compares four conditions on a shared footing, using one protocol and three metrics, so that the scores can be read together. The aim is to compare the conditions, and the comparison is presented as a study design rather than as a general account of question answering.

## Method

Four conditions were compared. The no-retrieval baseline receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The RAG with random passages receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. The query-rewriting variant rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

The metrics are hallucination frequency, BLEU and latency. Hallucination frequency is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, expressed as a percentage. BLEU is the n-gram overlap with the reference answer, expressed as a percentage. Latency is the mean seconds needed to produce one answer.

HistoryQuest is the main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet is a second dataset. Each dataset contains 1,000 questions, and every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header. The same maximum output length was applied to every condition, and answers were collected once and were not revised after scoring.

Annotators checked a sample of answers against reference facts before the full set was scored. Decoding settings, prompt wording and the order of questions were held fixed across runs, and the scoring rules were fixed in advance.

## Discussion

This study uses two datasets and a single run per condition, so its values describe one protocol and not a range of settings. The set of conditions is small and was chosen to keep the design auditable, which leaves other designs outside its scope. The three metrics capture invented facts, overlap with a reference answer and timing, and none of them captures every aspect of an answer. Latency was measured on one shared setup, so figures from other hardware would need separate measurement. Future work could extend the protocol to further datasets and report uncertainty estimates alongside each value.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison of four conditions on HistoryQuest and MedAnswerSet under a single protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
