# Hallucination, Latency and Overlap Across Biomedical and Legal Questions

## Abstract

This paper compares four conditions on BioClaimQA, the main benchmark, with LawFactQA as a second dataset. The conditions are scored on hallucination rate, latency and BLEU. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. Question sets, prompts and judging rules are held constant across conditions.

## Introduction

Biomedical and legal question answering are both sensitive to invented content, yet they are rarely evaluated with the same measures. This paper aims to compare conditions on both datasets using three measures: hallucination rate, latency and BLEU. The comparison is meant to show how conditions trade these measures against one another.

BioClaimQA is the main benchmark, and LawFactQA is included as a second dataset with the same question count and the same conditions.

## Method

The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. For chain-of-thought prompting, the model is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The query-rewriting variant rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Each condition answered 1,000 questions from each dataset. Hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Latency is the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. BLEU is the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Two readers judged the hallucination rate using a written guide, and their disagreements were settled by a third reader. All latency values were measured on the same hardware.

## Results

On BioClaimQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 7.7 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The study uses two datasets, with BioClaimQA as the main benchmark. Latency was measured once per condition, so timing variation is not reported. Judging of unsupported statements depends on readers' decisions. The table reports three measures for each condition, so readers can compare across columns, although each measure captures a different aspect of the answers. The question sets were fixed before the runs began.

## Conclusion

This paper compared four conditions on BioClaimQA, the main benchmark, and on LawFactQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Future work should repeat the timing measurements and add further legal and biomedical question sets.
