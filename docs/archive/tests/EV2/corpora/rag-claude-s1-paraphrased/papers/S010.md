# Unsupported Statements in Trivia Answers Across Lookup Designs

## Abstract

Generated answers to trivia questions can contain statements that the reference material does not support. This paper compares how often such statements appear across answer-generation designs in a controlled setting. The main benchmark is TriviaCheck, and the study reports the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement for each condition. The designs differ in whether and how they consult a document index, while the questions and the assessment procedure are held fixed. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The values appear in the Results section.

## Introduction

Question answering systems are often expected to answer from memory alone, yet memory can contain errors that surface as confident statements without support. Designers have therefore considered whether a lookup step should precede generation. This paper compares conditions that differ in lookup behaviour, and the aim is to compare those conditions on a shared set of questions. The comparison holds the questions, the assessment procedure and the reporting format constant. The conditions are described in the Method section, and the results follow in the same order. The paper closes with a discussion of design limits and a short conclusion.

## Method

The main benchmark, TriviaCheck, contains 1,000 questions, and each question is paired with a reference text against which the statements in an answer are checked. In the plain generator with no lookup step, the model is prompted with the question and nothing else. In the model that consults the document index before answering, the model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. In the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, the same retriever is used, but the passages are kept in their original order. Each answer is checked statement by statement against the reference text by a fixed procedure. Judging instructions were written before any answers were collected and were not changed afterwards. The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, reported in percent. The same procedure is applied to every condition.

## Results

The results for each condition are reported as follows.

On TriviaCheck, the plain generator with no lookup step has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 381 out of 1,000.

On TriviaCheck, the model that consults the document index before answering has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.323.

On TriviaCheck, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 23.1%.

## Discussion

The study has design limits that readers should keep in mind. It uses a single benchmark with a fixed set of questions, so the values describe that set only. The reference texts were assembled for the study and may not cover every fact that a careful reader would accept. The statement-level assessment followed a fixed procedure, and no alternative assessment was run. The conditions used fixed settings, and other settings were not explored. Readers who wish to apply the same procedure to other collections would need to rebuild the reference texts. These boundaries define what the study can report.

## Conclusion

This paper compared answer statements across conditions that differ in lookup behaviour, using TriviaCheck as the main benchmark and the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement as the metric. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The comparison was carried out under identical questions and assessment, and the limits described above apply.
