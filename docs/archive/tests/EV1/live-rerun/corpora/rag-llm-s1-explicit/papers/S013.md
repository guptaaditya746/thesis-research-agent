# The Impact of Retrieval Augmentation on Factual Correctness in Pop Culture Question Answering

## Abstract
Large language models are increasingly deployed for question answering tasks, yet the effect of external knowledge sources on their output remains a subject of active investigation. This study compares three distinct prompting conditions to evaluate their performance on a standardized benchmark. We examine the vanilla LLM, the retrieval-augmented generator, and a control condition using RAG with random passages. Our primary metric is answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. The results indicate a clear trend in performance across the conditions. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
The integration of retrieval systems with large language models, often referred to as Retrieval-Augmented Generation or RAG, is a common strategy in natural language processing. However, the specific impact of providing retrieved context on the final answer quality is not always straightforward. While intuition suggests that more relevant information should lead to better answers, the interaction between the model's internal parameters and external text can be complex. This paper aims to compare the performance of different conditions in a controlled setting. We focus on the domain of pop culture, where facts are often specific and verifiable. The goal is to isolate the effect of the retrieval mechanism itself, rather than the quality of the underlying model. By comparing the vanilla LLM against a retrieval-augmented generator and a random passage control, we can observe how the presence of external text influences the final output. The study is designed to provide a clear comparison of these conditions without assuming a priori that retrieval is beneficial.

## Method
We evaluated the conditions on the PopCultureQA dataset, which consists of 1,000 questions. This dataset serves as the main benchmark for our study. Each question is paired with a ground-truth answer, allowing for the calculation of answer accuracy. We tested three conditions. The first condition is the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. This condition serves as the baseline for the model's parametric knowledge. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. This condition represents the standard RAG pipeline. The third condition is RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. This control condition helps to distinguish the effect of relevant retrieval from the effect of simply adding text to the prompt. For each condition, we generated answers for all questions in the dataset. The primary metric used for evaluation is answer accuracy, measured as a percentage. This metric reflects the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We did not use any other metrics to ensure a focused comparison of factual correctness.

## Results
The performance of each condition was measured on the PopCultureQA dataset. The vanilla LLM achieved an answer accuracy of 47.5% on PopCultureQA. The retrieval-augmented generator achieved an answer accuracy of 40.3% on PopCultureQA. The RAG with random passages condition achieved an answer accuracy of 42.5% on PopCultureQA. These values represent the mean performance across the entire dataset. The differences between the conditions are distinct and consistent across the sample. The vanilla LLM performed the highest, followed by the random passage condition, with the retrieval-augmented generator performing the lowest. The metric used for all these comparisons is answer accuracy, which is reported as a percentage. The dataset used for all measurements is PopCultureQA. No other datasets or metrics were used in this analysis. The results are presented as single values for each condition to maintain clarity and focus on the primary comparison.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of three conditions on a single benchmark. The use of a random passage control was essential to interpret the results of the retrieval-augmented generator. By including this control, we can observe the effect of adding text to the prompt without the specific relevance of the retrieved passages. The limitations of this study include the focus on a single domain, pop culture, and a single metric, answer accuracy. Future work could expand to other domains and include additional metrics such as fluency or coherence. The study does not interpret the findings beyond the reported measurements. The design was chosen to minimize confounding variables and to provide a clear comparison of the conditions. The use of a dense retriever for the retrieval-augmented generator is standard, but the specific implementation details are not the focus of this paper. The primary aim was to compare the conditions as defined. The results are presented without additional interpretation to maintain objectivity. The study highlights the importance of controlled comparisons in evaluating the impact of retrieval systems.

## Conclusion
This study compared the vanilla LLM, the retrieval-augmented generator, and RAG with random passages on the PopCultureQA dataset. The primary metric was answer accuracy. The results showed that the vanilla LLM had the highest accuracy, while the retrieval-augmented generator had the lowest. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. The study provides a clear comparison of the conditions and highlights the need for careful evaluation of retrieval-augmented systems. Future research should explore the reasons behind these results and test them in other domains. The main contribution of this work is the controlled comparison of the three conditions on a standardized benchmark. The findings are specific to the conditions and dataset used in this study.
