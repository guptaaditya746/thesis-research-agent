# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: PopCultureQA, our main benchmark, and GeoFacts; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below reports the results on PopCultureQA and on GeoFacts.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | latency (s) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | PopCultureQA | 21.5 | 1.22 | 44.7 |
| RAG pipeline | PopCultureQA | see text | 2.68 | 31.8 |
| fine-tuned model | PopCultureQA | 9.8 | 2.99 | 37.8 |
| vanilla LLM | GeoFacts | 30.3 | 3.04 | 43.2 |
| RAG pipeline | GeoFacts | 25.5 | 1.54 | 11.5 |
| fine-tuned model | GeoFacts | 19.2 | 3.08 | 39.4 |

On PopCultureQA, the RAG pipeline scores 3.2 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
