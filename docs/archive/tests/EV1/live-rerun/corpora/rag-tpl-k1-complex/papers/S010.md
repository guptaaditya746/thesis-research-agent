# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: TriviaCheck, our main benchmark, and GeoFacts; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below reports the results on TriviaCheck and on GeoFacts.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | TriviaCheck | 38.1 | 37.3 |
| retrieval-augmented model | TriviaCheck | see text | 15.8 |
| RAG without reranking | TriviaCheck | 23.1 | 33.4 |
| vanilla LLM | GeoFacts | 28.6 | 50.2 |
| retrieval-augmented model | GeoFacts | 14.2 | 12.3 |
| RAG without reranking | GeoFacts | 22.1 | 52.3 |

On TriviaCheck, the retrieval-augmented model scores 5.8 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
