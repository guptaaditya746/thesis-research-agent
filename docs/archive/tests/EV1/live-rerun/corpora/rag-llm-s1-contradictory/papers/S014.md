# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Biomedical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of retrieval mechanisms on the factual reliability of large language models in specialized domains. We compare two distinct system configurations: a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented model. The primary evaluation is conducted on the BioClaimQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions designed to test factual recall in biology. On BioClaimQA, the retrieval-augmented model reaches 70.1% answer accuracy. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation, yet their performance in specialized scientific domains remains a subject of active research. A central question in this field is how external knowledge sources influence the quality of generated text. While some approaches rely solely on the parametric knowledge stored during pre-training, others integrate external retrieval systems to provide context-specific information. The aim of this work is to compare the performance of these two paradigms under controlled conditions. We focus specifically on the domain of biomedical claims, where factual precision is critical. By isolating the variable of document access, we seek to understand the operational differences between systems that answer from internal memory and those that condition their output on retrieved passages. This comparison provides a baseline for understanding the trade-offs involved in deploying retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems in high-stakes environments.

## Method
We evaluated two system conditions on the BioClaimQA dataset, our main benchmark for this study. The first condition is the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. This setup represents a baseline where the model relies entirely on its internal weights. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. In this setup, a retrieval engine selects relevant text segments from a curated corpus, and the language model generates its response based on both the question and the retrieved context. Both models were prompted with identical instructions to ensure a fair comparison. The primary metric for evaluation is answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We measured this metric for each condition across the full set of 1,000 questions in the BioClaimQA dataset. The evaluation process was automated to minimize human bias in scoring factual correctness.

## Discussion
The study design focused on a direct comparison of two distinct architectural approaches to question answering. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark dataset, BioClaimQA, which may not fully represent the diversity of questions encountered in real-world biomedical applications. Furthermore, the retrieval component was held constant, meaning that variations in retrieval quality were not explored. The experimental setup was designed to isolate the effect of document access on the generation process. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by either model, nor did we investigate the latency or computational cost associated with the retrieval step. The scope of this discussion is limited to the methodological choices and the constraints of the evaluation framework. Future work could expand the dataset to include more diverse topics and examine the interaction between retrieval precision and generation quality.

## Conclusion
We compared the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented model on the BioClaimQA benchmark. The results provide a quantitative basis for understanding the behavior of these systems in a controlled environment. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
