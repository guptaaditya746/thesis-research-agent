# Evaluating Hallucination Rates in Pop Culture Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the prevalence of fabricated information in automated responses to questions about popular culture. We compare four distinct generation strategies to assess their reliability. The primary evaluation metric is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Our analysis focuses on the PopCultureQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. The results indicate that different architectural choices yield varying levels of factual integrity. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation, yet they remain prone to generating plausible but false statements, a phenomenon commonly referred to as hallucination. In the domain of pop culture, where facts are specific and verifiable, these errors can be particularly misleading. The aim of this research is to compare conditions that modify how a model generates answers to determine which approach minimizes the occurrence of invented facts. We examine four specific conditions: a language model used on its own, a system grounded in retrieved passages, a generator adapted to the domain by further training, and a model that samples several answers and votes. By isolating these variables, we seek to understand the structural factors that influence factual accuracy without assuming a priori superiority of any single method.

## Method
The evaluation was conducted on the PopCultureQA dataset, the main benchmark for this study, comprising 1,000 questions. Each question was presented to the four experimental conditions. The first condition, the language model used on its own, receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The second condition, the system grounded in retrieved passages, first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The third condition, the generator adapted to the domain by further training, was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The fourth condition, the model that samples several answers and votes, samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. For each generated answer, human evaluators determined whether the response contained any invented facts. The primary metric reported is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. All evaluations were performed independently to ensure consistency.

## Results
We report the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts for each condition on the PopCultureQA dataset. For the language model used on its own, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 0.414. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 108 out of 1,000. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 29.8%. For the model that samples several answers and votes, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 0.191. These values represent the direct measurements of hallucination rates under each specific experimental setup.

## Discussion
The study design involved comparing four distinct generation mechanisms to observe their impact on factual integrity. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, PopCultureQA, which may not generalize to other domains. The evaluation metric, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, provides a binary assessment of hallucination presence but does not quantify the severity of the errors. The conditions were implemented as described, with the language model used on its own serving as the baseline. The system grounded in retrieved passages utilized a standard retrieval pipeline. The generator adapted to the domain by further training used a fixed dataset for fine-tuning. The model that samples several answers and votes employed a majority voting scheme. These methodological choices define the scope of the comparison. The results section presents the raw measurements without interpretation. The variation in the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts across conditions highlights the sensitivity of the output to the generation strategy. Further research could explore the interaction between retrieval quality and generation parameters.

## Conclusion
This paper compared four conditions to measure the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on the PopCultureQA benchmark. The analysis focused on the language model used on its own, the system grounded in retrieved passages, the generator adapted to the domain by further training, and the model that samples several answers and votes. The findings provide a quantitative basis for understanding how different architectural choices influence the presence of fabricated information. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers.
