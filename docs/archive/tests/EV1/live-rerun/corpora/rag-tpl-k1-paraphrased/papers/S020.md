# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the model that consults the document index before answering on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the language model used on its own reaches 523 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. On LawFactQA, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the generator adapted to the domain by further training was 0.552. On LawFactQA, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the model that samples several answers and votes was 35.7%.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
