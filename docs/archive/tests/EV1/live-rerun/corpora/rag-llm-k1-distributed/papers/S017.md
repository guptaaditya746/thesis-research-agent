# Comparative Analysis of Hallucination Frequency in Question Answering Systems

## Abstract
We present a study comparing four distinct question answering configurations on the PopCultureQA benchmark. The primary objective is to evaluate the hallucination frequency across these systems. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction
Large language models often generate plausible but factually incorrect information, a phenomenon known as hallucination. While various mitigation strategies exist, it remains unclear how different architectural and decoding choices interact to influence this behavior. This study aims to compare the performance of a baseline system, a retrieval-augmented pipeline, a fine-tuned variant, and a self-consistency decoding approach. By isolating these conditions, we seek to understand the specific contributions of each component to the overall reliability of the generated responses. The focus is strictly on the comparison of these conditions rather than on the development of new algorithms.

## Method
We evaluated four conditions. The no-retrieval baseline is denoted by B. The RAG pipeline is denoted by R. The fine-tuned model is denoted by A1. The self-consistency decoding is denoted by A2. We measured hallucination frequency, abbreviated as HF, which is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts.

The experimental setup involved a standardized evaluation protocol. Each system was prompted with the same set of questions from the PopCultureQA dataset, which consists of 1,000 questions. The questions covered a wide range of topics within popular culture, including film, music, and literature. To ensure fairness, all systems were provided with identical context where applicable, and the temperature parameter for sampling was kept constant across all runs. The reference collection for the RAG pipeline was curated to be comprehensive yet manageable in size, ensuring that relevant passages could be retrieved efficiently. The fine-tuning process for the A1 condition utilized a subset of in-domain data to adjust the model weights without catastrophic forgetting. For the A2 condition, the number of samples generated per question was fixed to ensure a robust majority vote. All evaluations were conducted on identical hardware to prevent resource constraints from influencing the outcomes. The annotation process for determining hallucination was performed by multiple independent raters, with disagreements resolved through consensus. This rigorous approach ensures that the measured HF values are reliable and reproducible. The data preprocessing steps included normalization of text and removal of duplicate entries to maintain the integrity of the benchmark. The entire pipeline was automated to minimize human error in the execution of the experiments. We also monitored the latency of each system to ensure that computational overhead did not compromise the quality of the answers. The choice of PopCultureQA as the main benchmark was driven by its relevance to real-world applications where factual accuracy is paramount. The diversity of the questions helps to test the robustness of the systems across different types of knowledge.
