# Comparative Evaluation of Retrieval Strategies in Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different model configurations on factual correctness in historical domain question answering. We compare four distinct conditions: a closed-book model, a retrieval-augmented model, a fine-tuned model, and a RAG without reranking. The primary evaluation is conducted on the HistoryQuest benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. Our analysis focuses on answer accuracy as the sole metric. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models often struggle with factual consistency when answering questions that require specific historical knowledge. While pre-training provides broad general knowledge, it may lack the precision needed for detailed historical queries. To address this, various strategies have been proposed, including fine-tuning on domain-specific data and augmenting generation with retrieved context. The aim of this study is to compare the performance of these conditions under controlled settings. We do not assume that any single method is superior; rather, we seek to quantify the differences in output quality across the selected configurations. The comparison is designed to isolate the contribution of retrieval and fine-tuning to the final answer quality. By using a standardized benchmark, we ensure that the results are comparable and reproducible. The focus remains strictly on the mechanical comparison of the four conditions without prior bias toward any specific outcome.

## Method
We evaluated four conditions on the HistoryQuest dataset. The first condition is the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The third condition is the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The fourth condition is RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. For each condition, we generated answers for all questions in the dataset. The primary metric used for evaluation is answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. All models were run with identical decoding parameters to ensure a fair comparison. The reference collection for retrieval was fixed and identical across all retrieval-based conditions. No additional post-processing was applied to the generated answers before evaluation.

## Results
The performance of each condition was measured on the HistoryQuest benchmark. The closed-book model achieved an answer accuracy of 40.6% on HistoryQuest. The retrieval-augmented model achieved an answer accuracy of 53.8% on HistoryQuest. The fine-tuned model achieved an answer accuracy of 47.6% on HistoryQuest. The RAG without reranking condition achieved an answer accuracy of 44.8% on HistoryQuest. These values represent the proportion of questions for which the generated answer contained the correct factual information as defined by the benchmark ground truth. The results are reported for the main dataset only, as no secondary benchmarks were included in this study. Each value corresponds to the specific condition and metric as described in the method section.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of four model configurations on a single, fixed dataset. The limitation of this approach is the reliance on a single benchmark, which may not capture the full range of historical question types. The conditions were defined strictly by their data access and training procedures, ensuring that the comparison focused on the architectural and data differences rather than implementation details. The use of a single metric, answer accuracy, simplifies the interpretation but may overlook other aspects of answer quality such as fluency or completeness. The retrieval-based conditions used the same underlying retriever, which controls for variations in retrieval quality. The fine-tuned model was trained on a separate set of in-domain pairs, distinct from the evaluation set. The study does not explore the computational cost or latency of the different conditions. The focus remains on the comparative performance in terms of factual correctness. The experimental setup was designed to minimize confounding variables, allowing for a clear assessment of the differences between the conditions.

## Conclusion
We compared the closed-book model, the retrieval-augmented model, the fine-tuned model, and RAG without reranking on the HistoryQuest benchmark. The evaluation focused on answer accuracy. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
