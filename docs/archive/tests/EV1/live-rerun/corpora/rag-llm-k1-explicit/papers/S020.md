# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different generation strategies on factual correctness in legal domains. We compare four distinct conditions using a standardized benchmark. The primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Legal question answering requires precise factual recall and logical reasoning. Large language models often struggle with domain-specific facts, leading to potential errors. To address this, various techniques have been proposed, including retrieval augmentation, fine-tuning, and advanced decoding methods. The aim of this work is to compare these conditions systematically. We focus on the LawFactQA dataset, which consists of 1,000 questions designed to test factual knowledge in legal contexts. By isolating specific architectural and procedural changes, we seek to understand how each condition contributes to the final output quality. The study does not assume a priori superiority of any single method but rather evaluates them under identical evaluation protocols.

## Method
We evaluated four conditions. The no-retrieval baseline receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The self-consistency decoding condition samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. All models were prompted with the same system instructions. The evaluation metric is answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We used the LawFactQA dataset as the main benchmark for all experiments. Each condition was run once per question to ensure consistency in the evaluation process. The reference collection for the retrieval-augmented model contained verified legal texts. The fine-tuning data was disjoint from the test set to prevent data leakage. Self-consistency decoding used a fixed number of samples to maintain computational parity where possible.

## Results
We report the performance of each condition on the LawFactQA dataset. The no-retrieval baseline achieved an answer accuracy of 52.3% on LawFactQA. The retrieval-augmented model achieved an answer accuracy of 64.6% on LawFactQA. The fine-tuned model achieved an answer accuracy of 55.2% on LawFactQA. The self-consistency decoding condition achieved an answer accuracy of 35.7% on LawFactQA. These values represent the mean performance across the entire test set. No other metrics were reported in this section. The results are presented in the order of the conditions as defined in the Method section. Each value corresponds strictly to the specified condition, metric, and dataset.

## Discussion
The study design involved a controlled comparison of four distinct approaches to legal question answering. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single dataset, LawFactQA, which may not capture the full diversity of legal queries. The retrieval-augmented model depends on the quality of the reference collection, which was curated for this study. The fine-tuned model required additional training data, which may not be available in all settings. Self-consistency decoding increases computational cost due to multiple sampling steps. The no-retrieval baseline serves as a standard reference point. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by each condition. The comparison is limited to the metric of answer accuracy. Future work could explore other aspects of model behavior, such as latency or robustness to adversarial prompts. The current study focuses solely on the comparative performance of the defined conditions.

## Conclusion
We compared four conditions for legal question answering on the LawFactQA dataset. The results indicate that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This finding highlights the utility of external knowledge sources in enhancing factual correctness. The other conditions showed varying levels of performance, but the retrieval-based approach stood out in the comparative analysis. Further research is needed to understand the underlying mechanisms that drive these differences. The study provides a baseline for future investigations into legal AI systems.
