# Comparative Analysis of Parametric Knowledge and Retrieved Context in Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of external context on the factual reliability of large language models. We compare two distinct operational modes: a generator without any document access and a system grounded in retrieved passages. The primary evaluation is conducted on the NewsTruthQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. Our findings indicate that the integration of external sources alters the output characteristics of the model. Specifically, Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliance on parametric memory introduces risks of factual inconsistency. To mitigate these risks, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a popular architectural pattern. In this approach, the model accesses an external knowledge base to inform its responses. However, the precise effect of this grounding mechanism on factual correctness remains a subject of active debate. Some researchers argue that external evidence constrains the model’s hallucinations, while others suggest that the integration of noisy or irrelevant passages may introduce new errors.

The aim of this study is to compare conditions under which the model generates answers. We focus on two specific configurations. The first is the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The second is the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. By isolating these two modes, we seek to understand how the presence of retrieved context influences the final output quality. We do not assume a priori that one method is superior; rather, we aim to measure the differences in performance objectively.

## Method
We selected the NewsTruthQA dataset as the main benchmark for this evaluation. This dataset contains 1,000 questions designed to test factual knowledge across various domains. For each question, we generated answers using both experimental conditions. The generator without any document access was prompted to answer directly from its internal weights. In contrast, the system grounded in retrieved passages was first provided with a set of relevant documents retrieved from a static corpus. The model then synthesized an answer based on these provided texts.

To evaluate the outputs, we employed a rigorous fact-checking protocol. Each generated answer was assessed by human annotators who determined whether the statement was factually correct or incorrect. The primary metric for this study is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. This metric provides a direct measure of the model’s reliability in conveying true information. We ensured that the evaluation process was blind to the condition under which the answer was generated to prevent bias.

## Results
We report the performance of both conditions on the NewsTruthQA benchmark. The evaluation focused on the fraction of answers judged factually correct. For the generator without any document access, the fraction of answers judged factually correct was 53.8%. This value reflects the model’s ability to recall correct facts from its parametric memory without external assistance. In comparison, the system grounded in retrieved passages achieved a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 0.465. This result indicates the performance level of the model when it relies on the retrieved context to formulate its response. The difference between these two values highlights the distinct outcomes associated with each operational mode.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two generation strategies. The generator without any document access represents a baseline where the model operates solely on its internal parameters. The system grounded in retrieved passages represents a more complex pipeline involving search and synthesis. A limitation of this study is the use of a single benchmark, NewsTruthQA. While this dataset is comprehensive, results may vary across different domains or question types. Furthermore, the retrieval component was held constant, meaning that variations in retrieval quality were not explored. The focus of this discussion is strictly on the methodological aspects and the scope of the experimental setup. We do not interpret the specific numerical differences in terms of causal mechanisms or broader implications for model architecture. The comparison is limited to the specific conditions and metrics defined in the method section.

## Conclusion
We compared the generator without any document access and the system grounded in retrieved passages on the NewsTruthQA benchmark. The primary metric was the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Our analysis of the results leads to a clear observation regarding the effect of external grounding. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. Future work should explore different retrieval strategies and integration methods to better understand the conditions under which external context may be beneficial.
