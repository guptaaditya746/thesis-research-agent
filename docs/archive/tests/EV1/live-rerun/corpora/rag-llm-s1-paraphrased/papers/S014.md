# Retrieval Augmentation and Factual Accuracy in Biological Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of retrieval mechanisms on the factual reliability of large language models in the domain of biological sciences. We compare two distinct operational modes: a model that answers from its own parameters and a model that consults the document index before answering. The primary evaluation is conducted on the BioClaimQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. Our findings indicate that the integration of external retrieval sources alters the output characteristics of the generator. Specifically, Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language generation, yet their tendency to produce factually incorrect statements, often referred to as hallucinations, remains a significant barrier to their deployment in scientific contexts. One proposed solution is retrieval-augmented generation, which grounds the model’s responses in a curated collection of external documents. While this approach is widely adopted, its effect on factual precision is not universally consistent across all domains. In the field of biology, where terminology is precise and factual errors can have substantial implications, understanding the trade-offs of retrieval is critical.

The aim of this research is to compare the performance of two conditions: a model that answers from its own parameters and a model that consults the document index before answering. By isolating the variable of document access, we can observe how the presence of retrieved context influences the final answer. This study focuses exclusively on factual correctness, defined as the alignment of the generated answer with established biological facts.

## Method
We evaluated the two conditions using the BioClaimQA dataset, which serves as the main benchmark for this study. The dataset contains 1,000 questions covering various subfields of biology, including genetics, ecology, and physiology. Each question is paired with a ground-truth answer that has been verified by domain experts.

The first condition, the model that answers from its own parameters, generates responses solely based on the knowledge encoded during pre-training. In this setup, the model has no access to any external documents or search tools. The second condition, the model that consults the document index before answering, retrieves relevant passages from a reference collection and conditions its answer on these retrieved texts. The retrieval system is designed to return the most semantically similar passages to the query. Both conditions use the same underlying base model to ensure that any differences in performance are attributable to the presence or absence of retrieval, rather than variations in model architecture or training data.

The primary metric for evaluation is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. This metric is calculated by comparing the generated answer to the ground-truth answer using a strict factual matching protocol. An answer is considered correct only if it contains no factual errors and fully addresses the question. We report this metric as a percentage for the retrieval condition and as a decimal proportion for the non-retrieval condition to reflect the specific reporting formats used in our analysis pipeline.

## Results
We present the results of the evaluation on the BioClaimQA benchmark. For the model that answers from its own parameters, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 0.606. For the model that consults the document index before answering, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 53.5%. These values represent the aggregate performance across all 1,000 questions in the dataset.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two conditions to isolate the effect of retrieval on factual accuracy. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, BioClaimQA, which may not fully represent the diversity of biological questions encountered in real-world applications. Furthermore, the retrieval system used in this study is a standard semantic search engine, and its performance may vary with different indexing strategies or retrieval algorithms. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by the model in each condition, nor did we investigate the quality of the retrieved passages. Future work could explore these aspects to provide a more granular understanding of the mechanisms behind the observed differences. The current study is limited to reporting the factual accuracy scores and does not offer a causal explanation for the observed outcomes beyond the presence or absence of retrieved context.

## Conclusion
In this study, we compared the factual accuracy of a model that answers from its own parameters and a model that consults the document index before answering on the BioClaimQA benchmark. The results demonstrate a clear distinction in performance between the two conditions. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers.
