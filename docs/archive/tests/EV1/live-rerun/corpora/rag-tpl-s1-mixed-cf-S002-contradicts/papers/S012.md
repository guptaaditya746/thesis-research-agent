# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

On NewsTruthQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the model that answers from its own parameters was 0.193. On NewsTruthQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 29.2%.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
