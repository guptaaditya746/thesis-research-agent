How to measure the quality of synthetic papers

Two questions: do they read like real papers, and do they say exactly what we planted?

Realism

Can a classifier tell real from synthetic?

AUC = P( s(real) > s(synthetic) )

For a random real text piece and a random synthetic one, s is the cross-validated classifier score.

AUC = 0.5 · cannot tell (ideal)

AUC = 1 · always tells

Fidelity

Does the text carry the planted truth, and only it?

Fid =

1

N

Σp 1[ read(p) = plan(p) ]

plan(p): planted rows, claims

paper p, one of the N in a folder

read(p): a reader extracts them back from the text

The paper passes only if:

  • every planted row comes back with its values
  • every claim comes back with its direction
  • no wrong row appears for a planted arm

Rows about the original real study are only counted. Without fidelity the gold answer is not the truth of the text, and every score after it is noise.

Two separate properties. Realism: if a simple classifier can easily tell synthetic papers from real ones, the system might be learning artefacts of our generator. The AUC is the probability that a random real piece scores higher than a random synthetic piece; 0.5 is the goal. Masking names and testing only style tells us what gives it away. Fidelity: a reader re-extracts the numbers and claims from each generated paper and compares them with the plan. If they do not match, our answer key is wrong, not the system.
