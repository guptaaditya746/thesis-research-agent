# Experimental Log

## 1. Setting
* **Paper type:** a case comment comparing decisions.
* **Setting:** An invented country with its own courts and statutes. No real legal system is assumed: courts have a level, decisions relate to sources by three generic relations. All names are invented.
* **Question:** How do courts interpret the Tetre Intellectual Property Act regarding copyright ownership versus trademark infringement?
* **Main conclusion to support:** The application of the Tetre Intellectual Property Act reveals a strict adherence to statutory definitions for copyright ownership (Section 4) and trademark infringement (Section 25), but with distinct evidentiary thresholds for each.

| section 12 of the Tetre Intellectual Property Act |  | topic: fair dealing for criticism; summary: fair dealing with a work for the purpose of criticism or review does not infringe copyright, provided that sufficient acknowledgment is given |
| section 25 of the Tetre Intellectual Property Act |  | topic: trademark infringement; summary: a person infringes a registered trademark if they use a sign in the course of trade that is identical to the trademark in relation to identical goods or services |
| section 31 of the Tetre Intellectual Property Act |  | topic: patent validity; summary: a patent shall be invalid if the invention was not novel or did not involve an inventive step at the date of filing |
