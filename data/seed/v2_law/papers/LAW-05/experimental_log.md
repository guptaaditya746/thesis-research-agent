# Experimental Log

## 1. Setting
* **Paper type:** an empirical legal study of decided cases.
* **Setting:** An invented country with its own courts and statutes. No real legal system is assumed: courts have a level, decisions relate to sources by three generic relations. All names are invented.
* **Question:** How courts balance the proportionality of administrative sanctions against the right to access information, and whether these principles operate independently or in tension.
* **Main conclusion to support:** The Tetre courts apply a strict proportionality test to administrative sanctions (BD5) while maintaining a narrower, privacy-focused interpretation of access to information rights (BD1), indicating that procedural fairness in sanctioning is distinct from substantive access rights.
