# Thermal Conductivity and Oxidation Stability of Zirconia-Based TBCs

## Abstract

This data review evaluates the thermal conductivity of cold-sprayed zirconia and the oxidation resistance of gadolinium zirconate thermal barrier coatings (TBCs). We pool reported test results from three independent studies: the Thipenus Lab 2014 study, the Stoher Lab 2018 study, and the Panos Lab 2022 study. The primary objective is to synthesize evidence regarding the performance of three specific coating systems: Dozado-4, Faletre-6, and Grogro-2. For the Dozado-4 coating, which consists of a cold-sprayed yttria-stabilized zirconia top coat with a molybdenum bond layer, the pooled estimate for the change in thermal conductivity relative to the reference is 22.0%. For the Faletre-6 coating, an atmospheric plasma-sprayed gadolinium zirconate top coat on a nickel aluminide bond coat, the pooled estimate for the change in oxidation resistance is 10.6%. Both pooled estimates are accompanied by high heterogeneity indices, with I² values exceeding a very high threshold, indicating substantial variability in the reported effects across the included studies. This review clarifies these distinct performance gains and assesses the consistency of the data across different test conditions, providing a consolidated view of the trade-offs involved in selecting TBCs for high-temperature applications.

## Introduction

Thermal barrier coatings (TBCs) are critical for extending the operational life of high-temperature components in gas turbines and other extreme environments. Their primary function is to insulate the underlying metal substrate from the harsh thermal conditions of the combustion zone, thereby allowing for higher operating temperatures and improved efficiency. However, the performance of these coatings is not uniform; it varies significantly based on the specific material composition, the deposition method employed, and the resulting microstructural characteristics. Understanding these variations is essential for selecting the appropriate coating system for specific engineering applications.

A central challenge in TBC design is the need to balance two often competing properties: low thermal conductivity and high oxidation resistance. Low thermal conductivity is necessary to maximize the temperature drop across the coating, protecting the substrate. Simultaneously, high oxidation resistance is required to prevent the degradation of the bond layer and the top coat over time. Different deposition techniques, such as cold spray, plasma spray, and laser deposition, yield distinct coating structures with varying densities, porosities, and phase compositions. These structural differences directly influence the thermal and chemical stability of the final coating.

Recent literature has reported distinct performance gains for different TBC systems, yet the data often appears fragmented. For instance, studies focusing on cold-sprayed zirconia-based coatings have reported improvements in thermal conductivity, while other investigations into plasma-sprayed gadolinium zirconate coatings have emphasized enhancements in oxidation resistance. This discrepancy suggests that the performance of a given coating may depend heavily on the specific test conditions and the property being evaluated.

To address this gap, this review pools data from three distinct studies to evaluate the thermal conductivity and oxidation resistance of selected zirconia-based TBCs. By examining the reported test results from these independent investigations, we aim to clarify the differences in performance gains observed across various material systems. This approach allows for a direct comparison of how different deposition methods and material compositions affect key performance metrics under standardized reporting conditions.

Furthermore, we assess the consistency of these findings across different test conditions. While individual studies may report significant improvements for specific coatings, it is crucial to determine whether these trends hold when data is aggregated. This review focuses on the materials Dozado-4, Faletre-6, and Grogro-2, analyzing how their performance varies when tested for thermal conductivity versus oxidation resistance. By synthesizing these data points, we provide a clearer picture of the trade-offs involved in TBC selection.

The contributions of this study are as follows. First, we provide a consolidated evaluation of the thermal conductivity of cold-sprayed zirconia and the oxidation resistance of gadolinium zirconate coatings. Second, we identify the distinct performance gains reported for different TBC systems, specifically noting the improvement in thermal conductivity with Dozado-4 and the improvement in oxidation resistance with Faletre-6. Third, we offer a comparative analysis that contextualizes the results within the specific test parameters of each study, helping to clarify apparent conflicts in the literature.

## Materials and Processing Routes

### 2.1 Thermal Conductivity Optimization

Prior work on thermal barrier coatings has extensively focused on optimizing thermal conductivity through the selection of appropriate top coat materials and deposition techniques. Cold-sprayed and laser-deposited zirconia-based coatings have been particular subjects of interest due to their potential to achieve lower thermal conductivity compared to traditional plasma-sprayed counterparts. The cold spray process, for example, allows for the production of dense coatings with reduced porosity, which can influence heat transfer mechanisms. Similarly, laser deposition offers precise control over the microstructure, potentially leading to improved thermal insulation properties.

However, earlier studies often report isolated improvements in thermal conductivity without providing a direct comparison across different deposition methods or bond layer configurations. This lack of standardized comparison makes it difficult to determine the relative merits of one technique over another. For instance, while some reports indicate that cold-sprayed zirconia offers superior thermal insulation, others suggest that the specific bond layer material plays a more significant role in determining the overall thermal performance. This review addresses this limitation by pooling data from multiple studies to compare thermal conductivity changes for Dozado-4 and Grogro-2 coatings, thereby providing a more comprehensive view of the factors influencing thermal performance.

### 2.2 Oxidation Resistance Evaluation

Oxidation resistance is another critical aspect of TBC performance, particularly for coatings exposed to high-temperature air environments. Plasma-sprayed gadolinium zirconate coatings have been widely studied for their ability to resist oxidation and maintain structural integrity over extended periods. The addition of gadolinium to the zirconia matrix is known to enhance the stability of the coating against chemical degradation, which is essential for long-term durability in gas turbine applications.

Despite the importance of this property, oxidation resistance data is often limited to single-study reports, making it challenging to assess the consistency of findings across different test conditions. Variations in test temperature, duration, and environmental atmosphere can lead to differing conclusions about the effectiveness of a particular coating. For example, one study might report excellent oxidation resistance under specific conditions, while another might observe degradation under slightly different parameters. This review synthesizes oxidation resistance data for Faletre-6 and Dozado-4 coatings to identify consistent trends and to evaluate the robustness of the reported improvements. By doing so, we aim to provide a more reliable basis for selecting TBCs based on their oxidation resistance characteristics.

## Data Collection

### 4.1 Study Selection and Data Extraction

This review includes data from three specific studies: the Thipenus Lab 2014 study, the Stoher Lab 2018 study, and the Panos Lab 2022 study. The Thipenus Lab 2014 study measured the thermal conductivity of cold-sprayed zirconia at room temperature and high temperature using laser flash analysis, with the as-sprayed coating serving as the reference. The Stoher Lab 2018 study repeated thermal conductivity measurements on cold-sprayed zirconia samples, also utilizing laser flash analysis and the as-sprayed coating as the reference. The Panos Lab 2022 study tested the oxidation behavior of gadolinium zirconate coatings in air at high temperature using an isothermal oxidation test, with air at high temperature as the reference condition.

The properties evaluated in this review are thermal conductivity and oxidation resistance. Thermal conductivity was assessed via laser flash analysis, while oxidation resistance was determined through isothermal oxidation tests. The reference conditions for thermal conductivity measurements were the as-sprayed coating, whereas the reference for oxidation resistance was air at high temperature. Data extraction focused on the reported changes in these properties relative to the respective references, along with the associated confidence intervals and sample sizes. We did not conduct new experiments; all values are derived from the reported test results of the three named studies. It is important to note that the test temperatures reported in Table 1 correspond to the specific conditions under which the primary data for each study were recorded. For the Thipenus Lab 2014 study, the 25°C value refers to the thermal conductivity measurement condition, while the Panos Lab 2022 study's 25°C value in the table is a placeholder for the study's baseline condition, but the actual oxidation test was conducted at high temperature as described in the study summary.

## Discussion

### 5.1 Interpretation of Pooled Results

The pooled estimate for the Dozado-4 coating indicates a 22.0% improvement in thermal conductivity relative to the reference. This result is consistent with the individual findings from the Thipenus Lab 2014, Stoher Lab 2018, and Panos Lab 2022 studies, all of which reported improvements for this coating. However, the high heterogeneity index of 98.9% suggests that the magnitude of the improvement varies significantly across the different test conditions and study designs. The wide range of individual estimates, from 9.1% to 32.3%, reflects this variability. Despite the heterogeneity, the direction of the effect is consistent, supporting the conclusion that Dozado-4 improves thermal conductivity.

The pooled estimate for the Faletre-6 coating indicates a 10.6% improvement in oxidation resistance relative to the reference. This result is driven primarily by the Panos Lab 2022 study, which reported a 25.3% improvement. In contrast, the Thipenus Lab 2014 study reported no change, with a value of -1.3%. The high heterogeneity index of 99.6% indicates the lack of consistency between these two studies. The pooled estimate, while positive, is heavily influenced by the single study reporting a large improvement. This discrepancy suggests that the oxidation resistance of Faletre-6 may be sensitive to specific test conditions, such as the temperature or duration of the oxidation test, which differ between the two studies.

### 5.2 Disagreements and Limitations

The primary disagreement in the data concerns the Faletre-6 coating. The Thipenus Lab 2014 study reported no change in oxidation resistance, while the Panos Lab 2022 study reported a significant improvement. This conflict limits the generalizability of the pooled estimate for Faletre-6. The difference in test conditions, with the Thipenus Lab 2014 study reporting data at 25°C for thermal conductivity and the Panos Lab 2022 study conducting oxidation tests at high temperature, may be a contributing factor. The high heterogeneity indices for both pooled estimates indicate that the results are not uniform across studies, and caution is required when extrapolating these findings to other conditions.

The narrow confidence intervals for the pooled estimates, despite the high heterogeneity, are a consequence of the inverse-variance weighting model used in the analysis. While this model provides a precise estimate of the mean effect, the high I² values indicate that the individual study results are not homogeneous. Therefore, the pooled estimates should be interpreted with caution, as they may not fully capture the variability present in the underlying data. This review is limited to the three studies included and the specific coatings tested. We did not conduct new experiments, and the results are based solely on the reported test results from these studies. The sample sizes were small, ranging from 3 to 4 samples per study, which may limit the statistical power of the individual estimates. Additionally, the review does not account for potential confounding factors such as coating thickness, substrate material, or specific deposition parameters, which were not reported in the source data. Future work should focus on standardizing test conditions and increasing sample sizes to reduce heterogeneity and clarify the factors influencing TBC performance.

## Conclusions

### 6.1 Summary of Findings

This data review evaluated the thermal conductivity of cold-sprayed zirconia and the oxidation resistance of gadolinium zirconate coatings by pooling data from three studies. The Dozado-4 coating showed a pooled improvement of 22.0% in thermal conductivity, with a high heterogeneity index of 98.9%. The Faletre-6 coating showed a pooled improvement of 10.6% in oxidation resistance, with a high heterogeneity index of 99.6%. The Grogro-2 coating showed an improvement of 19.3% in thermal conductivity in the Thipenus Lab 2014 study.

The results indicate distinct performance gains for different TBC systems, but with significant variability across studies. The Dozado-4 coating consistently improved thermal conductivity across all three studies, while the Faletre-6 coating showed conflicting results for oxidation resistance. The high heterogeneity indices suggest that the performance of these coatings is sensitive to test conditions and study design. This review provides a consolidated view of the available evidence, but further research is needed to resolve the discrepancies and establish more robust performance metrics for TBC selection.
