# Sorvelin with metformin: extension of the dose-ranging trial to week 78

## Abstract

Many adults with type 2 diabetes who take metformin need an additional glucose-lowering agent, and the effect of such an agent on body weight is relevant alongside its effect on HbA1c. This report presents the effect of Sorvelin on change in HbA1c from baseline and on change in body weight from baseline, compared with placebo, in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin. The estimates come from a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled design in which participants received Sorvelin 10 mg daily, Sorvelin 20 mg daily or placebo. Change in HbA1c is expressed in percentage points and change in body weight in kilograms. Every value is a mean difference versus placebo, where a negative value means a lower change than placebo. At week 52, the mean differences in HbA1c were -0.78 percentage points with 10 mg and -0.90 percentage points with 20 mg. At week 78, they were -0.73 percentage points with 10 mg and -0.88 percentage points with 20 mg. For body weight, the mean differences were -0.7 kg with 10 mg and -1.1 kg with 20 mg at week 52, and -0.8 kg with 10 mg and -1.2 kg with 20 mg at week 78. All reported values are negative, and the 20 mg estimate is more negative than the 10 mg estimate at each reported time point for both measures. The report presents point estimates only, without measures of uncertainty, sample sizes, baseline values or safety data. The precision of the estimates, and the strength of the dose ordering, therefore cannot be judged from this report.

## 1. Introduction

Many adults with type 2 diabetes who take metformin do not keep their glycaemic control within target over time, and an additional glucose-lowering agent is often needed to bring HbA1c closer to goal. For these patients, the choice of add-on therapy has several consequences. The reduction in HbA1c is the most direct measure of benefit, but it is not the only measure that matters to patients and prescribers who manage treatment over the long term.

Glycaemic control is frequently insufficient with metformin alone, so the range of add-on options available to clinicians is an important part of routine care. Each candidate agent needs to be judged against placebo on a common basis. A placebo comparison separates the effect of the agent from the background of diet, routine care and the natural course of the disease, and it gives readers a fixed reference point for interpreting the size of any change.

Body weight change is a relevant co-outcome alongside HbA1c, because many people with type 2 diabetes already carry excess weight. A glucose-lowering treatment that lowers HbA1c while increasing weight may offer a different balance of benefit and burden from one that has little effect on weight. Reporting HbA1c and body weight for the same agent, in the same population and against the same comparator, allows readers to consider these effects together.

The question addressed here is therefore narrow. For adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, the mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline and in change in body weight from baseline is reported for Sorvelin 10 mg and 20 mg daily at week 52 and week 78. This report does not review other Sorvelin data, and it does not assess whether the estimates would hold in other populations or at other doses or time points.

This report addresses that question. It presents the mean difference versus placebo for Sorvelin 10 mg daily and 20 mg daily on change in HbA1c from baseline, in percentage points, and on change in body weight from baseline, in kilograms, at week 52 and week 78. The estimates are given as point differences so that the two doses can be compared directly. They are restricted to the population and time points described here.

The contribution of this report is the set of mean differences versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point listed above, presented on a common scale. The report does not claim to establish dose-dependence, the shape of the dose-response relationship, or the balance between benefit and burden. Those questions require additional analyses and data that are outside the scope of this report.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study falls into three general groups defined by design: add-on glucose-lowering agents given to patients already taking metformin, dose-ranging comparisons of agents against placebo, and longer-duration assessments of glycaemic and weight outcomes. The work described below is characterised by its design and reporting approach only.

### 2.1 Add-on therapy to metformin

A substantial body of work concerns add-on therapy for adults whose glycaemic control on metformin is insufficient. These studies typically enrol patients on metformin, randomise them to an investigational add-on agent or to placebo, and assess change in HbA1c as the principal outcome, often at one primary time point. The placebo-controlled structure makes the comparison with placebo the central reference. Such designs may, however, focus on a single primary time point and may not describe how the size of the effect varies across doses. The present study keeps a placebo-controlled structure but reports the mean difference for two doses at two time points, so that the dose comparison is explicit.

### 2.2 Dose-ranging comparisons against placebo

A second group of studies compares several doses of a glucose-lowering agent with placebo in parallel groups, with the aim of characterising how the response varies with dose. This design is well suited to dose selection, because each active dose has its own comparison with the same placebo group. Reports of such work may emphasise a single outcome, and information on body weight alongside glycaemic change is sometimes limited. The present study uses a parallel-group design with two active doses and placebo. It reports HbA1c and body weight for each dose against placebo using the same statistic throughout, so that the two outcomes can be read side by side.

### 2.3 Longer-duration glycaemic and weight outcomes

A third group of studies follows glucose-lowering and weight outcomes beyond one year. Longer follow-up matters because the durability of an effect and the pattern of weight change over time are not fully captured by short trials. Reports in this group may present change at intermediate and later time points in different ways. Some show only the final assessment, while others summarise the course of change without a per-dose comparison against placebo. The present study reports outcomes at week 52 and week 78. This allows the same measures to be compared at two later time points within a single design, and conclusions are restricted to those points.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design and population

This report presents the extension to week 78 of a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups, in which Sorvelin was compared against placebo. The parent trial is referred to as the dose-ranging trial. The population consisted of adults with type 2 diabetes who were taking metformin. This report does not give a registration identifier or a publication reference for the parent trial. It also does not state whether the week 78 analyses were specified before the extension was conducted, and it does not describe how the week 52 values relate to the primary analysis of the parent trial.

Participant counts, the randomisation procedure, blinding arrangements and baseline values for each arm are not part of this report.

### 3.2 Interventions and comparator

Three arms are reported: Sorvelin 10 mg taken once daily, Sorvelin 20 mg taken once daily, and placebo. Placebo is the comparator for every reported value. Each Sorvelin dose is therefore expressed as a difference from the placebo arm, and no other comparator is used. The pharmacological class and mechanism of Sorvelin are outside the scope of this report, and the agent is not placed among other add-on options here.

### 3.3 Outcomes and statistical reporting

Two outcomes are reported. The first is change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points. The second is change in body weight from baseline, expressed in kilograms. Both outcomes are reported at two time points, week 52 and week 78.

Every value is a mean difference versus placebo. A negative value means that the change in the Sorvelin arm was lower than the change in the placebo arm. The report presents point estimates only. The statistical method used to produce each estimate, the standard errors and the confidence intervals are not presented, and no p-values are reported. The handling of data after discontinuation and the proportion of participants who discontinued at each time point are also not described here. Because these elements are not presented, the precision of the estimates cannot be judged from this report, and the estimates should be read as descriptive values.

No formal comparison between the two doses is presented. Differences between the 10 mg and 20 mg values are read directly from the reported point estimates and are not tested.

## 4. Results

Results are reported for every Sorvelin dose at every time point, for both outcomes. Mean differences versus placebo for week 52 HbA1c and for all body weight values are given in Tables 1 and 2. The week 78 HbA1c values are stated in the text of Section 4.1.

### 4.1 Change in HbA1c from baseline

At week 52, the mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline was -0.78 percentage points with Sorvelin 10 mg daily and -0.90 percentage points with Sorvelin 20 mg daily. Both values are negative, indicating a lower change from baseline than placebo, and the 20 mg estimate is the more negative of the two. These values are shown in Table 1.

Table 1: Mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline at week 52, by Sorvelin dose

| Sorvelin dose (daily) | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline (percentage points) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 mg | week 52 | -0.78 |
| 20 mg | week 52 | -0.90 |

At week 78, the mean difference versus placebo was -0.73 percentage points with 10 mg daily. At week 78, the mean difference versus placebo was -0.88 percentage points with 20 mg daily. At this later time point the 20 mg estimate is again the more negative of the two. Both week 78 values are less negative than the corresponding week 52 values, so the HbA1c difference from placebo is slightly smaller at week 78 for each dose.

### 4.2 Change in body weight from baseline

Table 2 gives the mean difference versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline for each dose at both time points. At week 52, the values were -0.7 kg with 10 mg daily and -1.1 kg with 20 mg daily. At week 78, the values were -0.8 kg with 10 mg daily and -1.2 kg with 20 mg daily.

Table 2: Mean difference versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline, by Sorvelin dose and time point

| Sorvelin dose (daily) | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in body weight from baseline (kg) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 mg | week 52 | -0.7 |
| 10 mg | week 78 | -0.8 |
| 20 mg | week 52 | -1.1 |
| 20 mg | week 78 | -1.2 |

The pattern across the four values is consistent. The 20 mg values are more negative than the 10 mg values at each time point. For each dose, the week 78 value is more negative than the week 52 value. All four values are negative, so body weight change was lower than placebo for both doses at both time points. No confidence intervals, p-values or baseline values are reported for these estimates.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Principal findings

In adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, Sorvelin 10 mg and 20 mg daily gave a lower change in HbA1c and in body weight than placebo at week 52 and week 78, as summarised in Tables 1 and 2 and in the text. The working question was whether Sorvelin taken once daily would change these measures relative to placebo, and whether the estimates differed by dose. Every reported mean difference is negative, so the estimates are all in the same direction. For both measures, the 20 mg estimate is more negative than the 10 mg estimate at each reported time point. This is a descriptive ordering of point estimates. Because no uncertainty measures or formal dose comparisons are presented, it does not establish a dose effect.

The two measures moved in the same direction across doses. The dose with the more negative HbA1c estimate also had the more negative body weight estimate at each time point. This report does not include the data needed to assess a trade-off between the two outcomes, and it should not be read as showing that a trade-off is absent. Because only two doses are reported, the observations do not characterise the shape of the dose-response relationship. The estimates are offered so that readers can compare the two doses on a common scale. They do not, on their own, indicate which dose should be selected in clinical practice.

### 5.2 Limitations

The report presents only mean differences versus placebo. No measures of uncertainty are given, so the precision of the estimates cannot be judged, and differences between the doses should be read as descriptive comparisons of point values.

Only two Sorvelin doses and one comparator are included. The dose-response shape therefore cannot be characterised beyond these points, and no conclusion about doses above or below those tested can be drawn. Outcomes are reported at two time points only, so the course of change between these points and beyond week 78 is not described.

Participant numbers, randomisation and attrition, baseline values and the handling of missing data after discontinuation are not reported here. Without these elements, the representativeness of the week 78 values and the size of the evidence base cannot be assessed. The analysis method used for each estimate is also not described. The population is limited to adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, and the findings should not be extended to other populations, to patients not taking metformin or to other clinical settings.

No safety or tolerability data are presented. Adverse events, hypoglycaemia and discontinuations are therefore not described, and the balance between glycaemic benefit and other effects cannot be assessed from this report. The benefit-burden framing in the Introduction is a motivation for reporting both outcomes, not a conclusion supported by the data here.

The body weight changes are small in absolute size, as shown in Table 2. Their clinical relevance should be interpreted with care. This report does not provide reference values against which the size of these changes can be judged, and it does not state whether they are meaningful for patients.

## 6. Conclusion

This report presented the mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline and in change in body weight from baseline for Sorvelin 10 mg and 20 mg daily, in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, at week 52 and week 78. For HbA1c, the mean differences were -0.78 percentage points (10 mg) and -0.90 percentage points (20 mg) at week 52, and -0.73 percentage points (10 mg) and -0.88 percentage points (20 mg) at week 78. For body weight, the mean differences were -0.7 kg (10 mg) and -1.1 kg (20 mg) at week 52, and -0.8 kg (10 mg) and -1.2 kg (20 mg) at week 78. All values were lower than placebo, and the 20 mg estimate was the more negative of the two doses at each reported time point for both measures.

The findings apply to the population, doses, time points and measures reported here. They are point estimates without measures of uncertainty, and they are not accompanied by safety data, sample sizes or a description of the analysis method. Further evidence on the precision of these differences, and on the comparison between doses, is needed before firm conclusions about the size or dose dependence of the effect can be drawn.
