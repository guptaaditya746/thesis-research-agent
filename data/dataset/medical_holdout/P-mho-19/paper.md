# Tavorindel (TVR-676) and Parvelinor as first treatment in newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes

## Abstract

The choice of the first glucose-lowering drug in type 2 diabetes affects both glycaemic control and body weight, yet placebo-referenced evidence on these two outcomes in drug-naive adults is limited. We report a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial in adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and no prior glucose-lowering drug. Participants were allocated to Tavorindel (TVR-676) 10 mg daily, Parvelinor 50 mg daily, or placebo, and outcomes were assessed at week 26. The two outcomes were change in HbA1c from baseline, in percentage points, and change in body weight from baseline, in kilograms. For each measure, agent and dose, results are summarised as the mean difference versus placebo. The study has a single time point and two doses, and it reports mean differences only, without measures of precision. Because each agent is compared at a different dose, between-agent contrasts are descriptive only. Within these limits, the results provide a placebo-referenced description of each dose on glycaemic and weight outcomes as first treatment in this population. They do not establish superiority of either agent or a preferred dose.

## 1. Introduction

Type 2 diabetes is often diagnosed at a stage when the first glucose-lowering drug chosen will shape both glycaemic control and body weight. These two outcomes are usually managed together, so the initial pharmacological decision carries lasting clinical weight. For clinicians and patients, choosing a first agent concerns more than one outcome at once, and the effect of that choice on each outcome is relevant to the decision.

Much of the existing evidence on glucose-lowering drugs comes from add-on designs, in which a new agent is given to patients who already receive treatment, or from studies of patients whose disease has been established for some time. Such designs are informative about further intensification, but they do not isolate the effect of a first drug in a drug-naive population. Prior exposure, longer disease duration and concurrent therapy can all influence both glycaemic response and weight change, so the effect of a first agent cannot be read directly from these trials.

For adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes who have never received a glucose-lowering drug, the mean differences from placebo in change in HbA1c and in body weight at week 26 for Tavorindel (TVR-676) 10 mg daily and Parvelinor 50 mg daily have not been described within a single design. Without a placebo-referenced comparison of these two agents as first treatment, on both measures, readers cannot compare the two doses on a common reference point. This matters because an assessment based on glycaemic change alone leaves the weight effect unexamined, and the reverse is equally true.

To address this gap, we conducted a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial in adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and no prior drug. Participants were allocated to Tavorindel (TVR-676) 10 mg daily, Parvelinor 50 mg daily, or placebo. Two measures were taken at week 26: change in HbA1c from baseline, in percentage points, and change in body weight from baseline, in kilograms.

The main contribution of this study is a descriptive, placebo-referenced account of Tavorindel (TVR-676) and Parvelinor as first treatment, in which the mean difference versus placebo is reported for each measure, dose and time point. The aim is to make the two outcomes visible together for each dose studied. The study does not test whether the agents differ, does not establish a preferred dose, and makes no claims about other populations, other endpoints or longer treatment durations.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be grouped by design approach rather than by individual agent. Three clusters are considered below, each examined against the question of a first, drug-naive treatment assessed against placebo on two measures at a fixed time point. This paper does not carry out a formal comparison with specific published trials; the discussion is limited to the design features that bear on the present question.

### 2.1 Add-on trials in previously treated patients

Add-on glucose-lowering trials enrol patients who already receive treatment and test a further agent on top of that background therapy. This design is well suited to asking how an additional drug performs in established disease. Its limitation for the present question is that participants have established disease and prior drug exposure, so the effect of a first agent cannot be separated from the effect of earlier therapy. The present design addresses this by enrolling only adults with newly diagnosed disease and no prior drug, so that the comparison reflects a first treatment.

### 2.2 Active-comparator trials of initial therapy

A second group of studies addresses initial therapy directly, enrolling drug-naive or recently diagnosed patients. Many designs of this kind compare a new agent against an established glucose-lowering drug rather than against placebo. Such comparisons are useful for judging whether a new option is practical in place of current standards. However, results expressed relative to an active drug make the placebo-referenced effect of each dose harder to read directly, because the reference point is an active treatment rather than no active therapy. The present study reports the mean difference versus placebo for each dose and time point, which gives a direct placebo-referenced reading. Comparisons with other active agents are outside its scope.

### 2.3 Single-endpoint or multi-dose studies of once-daily agents

A third cluster consists of studies of once-daily agents that report a single endpoint, or that test several doses without a per-dose summary at a fixed time point. Such reports can establish that an agent acts on glycaemia, but they do not always give a joint view of glycaemic and weight effects. When the two measures are reported separately, dose selection on both measures is harder to carry out on a common basis. The present design reports both HbA1c and body weight for each dose at week 26 within one parallel-group comparison, so that the two measures can be read together.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Trial design and population

This study was a randomised, placebo-controlled trial with parallel groups. Three arms were compared: Tavorindel (TVR-676) 10 mg daily, Parvelinor 50 mg daily, and placebo. Placebo was the only comparator, and both active agents were evaluated against it. The population comprised adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes who had never received a glucose-lowering drug. Each measure was assessed at week 26, which is the only time point reported in this paper.

Randomisation procedures, allocation concealment, blinding, eligibility criteria beyond the population described above, and the number of participants in each arm are not reported in this paper. Results are therefore interpreted only within the design and population stated here. After its first mention, the agent is referred to as Tavorindel.

### 3.2 Outcomes and summary statistic

Two outcomes were defined: change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points, and change in body weight from baseline, expressed in kilograms. Every reported value is the mean difference versus placebo for the same measure, dose and time point. Under this convention, a negative value indicates a lower change than placebo, and a positive value indicates a higher change than placebo.

Confidence intervals, p-values, patient counts, baseline values and standard deviations are not reported in this paper. The precision of each estimate, and the number of participants on which it rests, therefore cannot be judged from the reported figures.

### 3.3 Interpretation of comparisons

Each agent was studied at a single dose, and the two doses differ. The between-agent contrast is therefore confounded by drug identity and dose simultaneously, and the values for one agent cannot be attributed to the agent alone. No inferential test of a between-agent difference was applied, and none is reported. Comparisons across agents are accordingly treated as descriptive. The safety and tolerability profile of each agent, including adverse events, hypoglycaemia and treatment discontinuations, is likewise not reported in this paper, so the glycaemic and weight values cannot be read against it.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Change in HbA1c from baseline

At week 26, Tavorindel 10 mg daily was associated with a mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline of -0.63 percentage points. Parvelinor 50 mg daily was associated with a mean difference versus placebo of -0.82 percentage points at the same time point. Both values are negative, meaning that for each agent the change in HbA1c was lower than the change under placebo at the dose studied. These two values are reported side by side; this paper does not test whether they differ. Results are reported for every dose of both agents at every time point, and no further HbA1c values are given in this paper.

### 4.2 Change in body weight from baseline

The mean differences versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline at week 26 are summarised in Table 1. Both values are positive, so the change in body weight under each agent was higher than under placebo at the dose studied. The direction of these body weight values is the opposite of the direction of the HbA1c values reported in Section 4.1. The values are reported as observed, and no interpretation of statistical significance is made from them.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Dose-related interpretation

The study was designed to describe whether Tavorindel 10 mg daily and Parvelinor 50 mg daily change HbA1c and body weight relative to placebo at week 26. On HbA1c, both agents gave negative mean differences versus placebo, meaning a lower change than placebo at the doses studied. On body weight, both agents gave positive mean differences versus placebo, meaning a higher change than placebo. The direction of the body weight values is therefore opposite to that of the HbA1c values for each drug. This pattern is a descriptive observation from one trial. Because no uncertainty measures are reported, this paper cannot establish whether the sizes of these values are reliable, or whether the opposing directions reflect a stable property of either agent.

The contribution of this study is to report the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point, so that the glycaemic and weight values of each dose can be read together. A reading based on one measure alone would leave the other unexamined. The clinical relevance of the body weight values in particular cannot be judged from the data presented, since their precision and the magnitude that would matter to patients are not established here. The reported values describe the placebo-referenced effects observed in this trial. They do not, by themselves, favour one dose or one agent over another.

### 5.2 Limitations

The trial assessed outcomes at a single time point, week 26. Durability of the observed differences beyond this point was not assessed, and the values should not be taken to describe longer treatment.

Only two doses were studied, one for each agent. No dose-response relationship can be described for either drug, and because each agent was studied at a different dose, the comparison between agents is confounded by drug identity and dose. The comparison is confined to the doses tested.

Only mean differences versus placebo are reported, without confidence intervals, p-values or standard deviations. The precision of each estimate cannot be judged from this paper, and the number of participants and baseline values are not given.

The mechanism of action and drug class of the two agents are not described in this paper, and the rationale for the chosen doses is not set out here. Readers therefore cannot assess the biological plausibility of the observed values from the text alone.

Safety and tolerability outcomes are not reported. Adverse events, hypoglycaemia and treatment discontinuations are not described, which limits the interpretation of both glycaemic and weight values for a first-line treatment decision.

The population was restricted to adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and no prior drug. The findings are not generalised to people with established disease, to people with prior drug exposure, or to other populations. Randomisation and blinding details are not reported, which further limits the assessment of the trial conduct.

## 6. Conclusion

In adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and no prior drug, this randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial compared Tavorindel (TVR-676) 10 mg daily and Parvelinor 50 mg daily with placebo at week 26. The trial provides mean differences versus placebo for change in HbA1c and change in body weight for each dose and time point. These placebo-referenced values describe, in one design, how each dose relates to placebo on both measures, with both measures read together.

The conclusions are limited by a single time point, two doses, the confounding of agent with dose, and the reporting of mean differences alone without measures of precision or safety data. Further evaluation beyond these limits, including longer follow-up, additional doses, reporting of measures of precision and of safety outcomes, is needed before the findings can be extended.
