# DLM-868 added to metformin in type 2 diabetes: a 24-week trial

## Abstract

Adults with type 2 diabetes who take metformin often need an additional glucose-lowering agent, yet the effect of DLM-868 added to metformin on the change from baseline in HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose, relative to placebo, has not been established for the doses and time points considered here. We report a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, comparing DLM-868 100 mg daily and DLM-868 200 mg daily with placebo, with each measure assessed at week 12 and week 24. Every result is a mean difference versus placebo, where negative values indicate a value lower than placebo. For change in HbA1c, the mean differences at week 12 were -0.46 percentage points (100 mg) and -0.63 percentage points (200 mg), and at week 24 they were -0.59 percentage points and -0.69 percentage points, respectively. For change in fasting plasma glucose, the week 12 mean difference was -0.76 mmol/L at both doses, and the week 24 values were -0.74 mmol/L and -0.93 mmol/L. Mean differences in change in body weight were small, and the week 12 value of 0.2 kg at 100 mg was the only positive value. The 200 mg dose gave larger mean reductions than 100 mg for HbA1c at both time points and for fasting plasma glucose at week 24. This report gives point estimates only, without measures of dispersion or tests of significance, so these between-dose differences are descriptive. Safety data are not included. The mean differences are presented so that readers can compare the two doses on each measure and time point.

## 1. Introduction

Type 2 diabetes is commonly treated with metformin, but many adults on this drug still have glycaemia above their target and need an additional glucose-lowering agent. Choosing among add-on options requires more than knowing that an agent lowers glycated haemoglobin. Clinicians and patients also need to know how the same agent affects body weight and fasting plasma glucose, because these measures bear on tolerability and on how the glucose-lowering effect appears in everyday monitoring. For add-on options to be compared sensibly, their effects on HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose need to be quantified against a concurrent placebo.

Earlier add-on work in this setting has taken several forms. Placebo-controlled studies of investigational agents added to metformin have usually centred on a single glycaemic endpoint, and studies that record body weight or fasting plasma glucose often present them as secondary outcomes. Reports that set out all three measures together are less common, and fewer still examine more than one dose or more than one time point within the same analysis. A reader who wants to compare doses, or to judge whether an effect strengthens or fades over the treatment period, often has to assemble the answer from separate reports.

For adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, it is therefore not established what effect DLM-868 added to metformin has on the change from baseline in HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose, relative to placebo, or whether the size of this effect differs between doses. The question is specific. It concerns one development compound, one background therapy and one population, and it requires that all three measures be examined against the same placebo arm. We expected that DLM-868 taken once daily would change these measures relative to placebo, and that the size of the change would differ between the two doses.

To address this gap, we report a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial in adults with type 2 diabetes who take metformin. Participants were allocated to DLM-868 100 mg daily, DLM-868 200 mg daily or placebo, and each of the three measures was assessed at week 12 and week 24. Parallel-group allocation means that each dose is compared with a concurrent placebo group rather than with historical controls. The two time points allow readers to see whether a difference is already present at week 12 and whether it changes by week 24.

The study reports the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point. These values are presented separately so that readers can compare the 100 mg and 200 mg doses on every outcome, rather than relying on a single pooled summary that could hide differences between doses or over time. The scope is deliberately bounded: the findings apply to adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, to the two doses studied and to the 24-week treatment period.

The contribution of this report is the set of mean differences versus placebo for change in HbA1c, change in body weight and change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline, reported for each of two daily doses of DLM-868 at week 12 and week 24.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Placebo-controlled add-on agents in metformin-treated adults

Randomised placebo-controlled trials that add an investigational oral agent to ongoing metformin therapy and compare the change from baseline with placebo form a common design in this area. The concurrent placebo arm separates the contribution of the added agent from that of background treatment, and the present report uses the same logic. However, such trials are often built around one glycaemic endpoint, with the change in glycated haemoglobin as the central measure. Body weight and fasting plasma glucose are frequently recorded but summarised as secondary or exploratory outcomes, so the complete metabolic picture of an agent can be hard to reconstruct from any single report. The present report keeps the placebo-controlled add-on design but treats HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose as parallel outcomes for DLM-868.

### 2.2 Joint reporting of glycaemic and weight outcomes

Studies that report HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose and body weight together give a fuller view of an add-on therapy's effects. Joint reporting allows a reader to see whether glucose lowering is accompanied by a change in weight, and whether the short-term fasting measure and the longer-term glycaemic measure point in the same direction. Many such reports, however, present these outcomes at a single time point, and a single snapshot cannot show whether an effect persists, strengthens or wanes over the treatment period. In the present report, each of the three measures is given at week 12 and week 24 against placebo, so the time course of each mean difference can be read directly.

### 2.3 Dose selection for oral add-on agents

Comparisons of two or more fixed doses of an oral add-on agent against placebo are used to inform dose choice. These designs show whether a dose-related effect exists and help to identify a dose worth further study. A common way to summarise them is through a single pooled effect across doses. Pooling can obscure whether a larger dose gives a larger difference on every measure, or whether a difference is visible only at one time point. The present report gives the 100 mg and 200 mg daily values separately for every measure and time point, so that the mean difference for each dose is explicit rather than averaged.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design and population

This study is a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups in which DLM-868 is compared with placebo. The study population consists of adults with type 2 diabetes who take metformin. Each participant was allocated to one group, so that the effect of each DLM-868 dose is judged against a concurrent placebo group. The analysis is restricted to this population and to the arms described in Section 3.2. This report does not describe the number of participants per arm, baseline values, randomisation and allocation procedures, blinding, numbers of withdrawals, trial registration, or whether follow-up is complete. These aspects are outside the scope of the present report, and they limit how the reported values can be appraised.

### 3.2 Interventions and comparator

Three arms are included. Two arms receive DLM-868 once daily, at 100 mg daily or 200 mg daily. The third arm receives placebo, which serves as the sole comparator for both doses. All participants continue their background treatment with metformin. The pharmacology of DLM-868 is not described in this report. DLM-868 is referred to by its development code throughout this paper.

### 3.3 Outcomes and time points

Three outcomes are analysed. The first is change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points. The second is change in body weight from baseline, expressed in kg. The third is change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline, expressed in mmol/L. Each outcome is assessed at week 12 and at week 24. For every outcome, the change observed in each DLM-868 arm is expressed relative to the change observed in the placebo arm, so that the effect of each dose is separated from the change seen with placebo.

### 3.4 Summary statistic and interpretation

The summary statistic for every outcome, dose and time point is the mean difference versus placebo. A negative value indicates that the change from baseline in the DLM-868 arm was lower than the change in the placebo arm, and a positive value indicates that it was higher. The analysis reported here is limited to these mean differences. No measures of dispersion, confidence intervals, significance tests or statistical model are presented, and no primary endpoint is designated in this report. All three outcomes and both time points are therefore presented descriptively, without multiplicity adjustment.

Results are organised by measure. Change in HbA1c at week 12 is reported in Table 1, and the two week 24 HbA1c values are given in the text of Section 4.1. Change in body weight is reported in Table 2, and change in fasting plasma glucose in Table 3. Within each table, the dose and time point identify each mean difference, and the unit is given in the column header.

## 4. Results

Results are reported for every DLM-868 dose at every time point, and every value is a mean difference versus placebo. Table 1 presents the week 12 mean differences in change in HbA1c from baseline; the week 24 values for the same measure are stated in the text of Section 4.1. Table 2 presents the mean differences in change in body weight from baseline at both time points, and Table 3 presents those for change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline.

### 4.1 Change in HbA1c from baseline

At week 12, DLM-868 100 mg daily gave a mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline of -0.46 percentage points, and DLM-868 200 mg daily gave a mean difference of -0.63 percentage points (Table 1). Both values are negative, which means that the HbA1c reduction in each DLM-868 arm was larger than that in the placebo arm at this time point.

**Table 1: Mean difference vs placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline (percentage points) by DLM-868 dose at week 12**

| DLM-868 dose | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline (percentage points) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 mg daily | week 12 | -0.46 |
| 200 mg daily | week 12 | -0.63 |

At week 24, DLM-868 100 mg daily gave a mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline of -0.59 percentage points. At the same time point, DLM-868 200 mg daily gave a mean difference versus placebo of -0.69 percentage points. The 200 mg dose therefore gave the more negative mean difference at both time points. For each dose, the week 24 value was more negative than the week 12 value.

### 4.2 Change in body weight from baseline

Table 2 gives the mean differences versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline for each DLM-868 dose at each time point. All four values are small and close to zero. The only positive value is 0.2 kg at week 12 for DLM-868 100 mg daily. The other three values are -0.1 kg for DLM-868 100 mg daily at week 24, -0.1 kg for DLM-868 200 mg daily at week 12, and -0.0 kg for DLM-868 200 mg daily at week 24. The last value is reported as recorded, to one decimal place, and represents a mean difference of essentially zero. No consistent dose-related direction is visible across the two doses and the two time points. Absolute changes from baseline in the placebo arm are not reported in this paper, so the direction of change in each arm cannot be read from these values.

**Table 2: Mean difference vs placebo in change in body weight from baseline (kg) by DLM-868 dose and time point**

| DLM-868 dose | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in body weight from baseline (kg) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 mg daily | week 12 | 0.2 |
| 100 mg daily | week 24 | -0.1 |
| 200 mg daily | week 12 | -0.1 |
| 200 mg daily | week 24 | -0.0 |

### 4.3 Change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline

Table 3 gives the mean differences versus placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline. At week 12, both DLM-868 doses gave a mean difference of -0.76 mmol/L, so no difference between the doses is visible at this time point. At week 24, DLM-868 100 mg daily gave a mean difference of -0.74 mmol/L, and DLM-868 200 mg daily gave -0.93 mmol/L. The 200 mg dose therefore gave the more negative mean difference at week 24 only.

**Table 3: Mean difference vs placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline (mmol/L) by DLM-868 dose and time point**

| 100 mg daily | week 24 | -0.74 |

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Interpretation of between-dose differences

For HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose, the mean differences versus placebo were negative at both doses and both time points, which indicates that the change from baseline in the DLM-868 arms was lower than in the placebo arm. The 200 mg dose gave the more negative mean difference for HbA1c at both time points and for fasting plasma glucose at week 24. Because only two doses were studied and no uncertainty measures are reported, these observations describe a larger mean difference at the higher dose. They do not establish a dose-response relationship or show that the between-dose differences are statistically supported.

At week 12, the two doses gave an identical mean difference for fasting plasma glucose, so the between-dose pattern on this measure is visible only at week 24. The present report does not include an analysis of the time course of the dose difference, such as an interaction test, so it cannot establish when this pattern emerged.

The pattern for body weight differs. The mean differences are small and close to zero, and the single positive value at 100 mg daily at week 12 is not matched by a consistent direction across doses or times. The body weight changes are therefore small in size relative to placebo, and this report does not show a dose-related effect on body weight. Taken together, the values suggest larger mean differences at 200 mg for the glycaemic measures, while body weight does not follow the same pattern.

These findings should not be extended beyond the measured doses and time points. The trial compared 100 mg and 200 mg daily at week 12 and week 24 only, and it does not describe how the mean differences would look at other doses or later durations. The dose-specific values are intended to allow readers to compare the two studied doses directly on each measure, but they are not sufficient on their own to choose a dose, because safety information is not included.

### 5.2 Limitations and scope

Several limits follow from the design and from the scope of this report. First, only two doses and two time points were examined, so the dose comparison rests on two points for each measure, and no dose-response curve can be estimated. Second, the population is restricted to adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, and the results do not apply to other populations or to other background therapies. Third, only mean differences versus placebo are reported. Measures of dispersion, confidence intervals and significance tests are not presented, so the precision of each estimate cannot be judged, and the differences should be interpreted cautiously, particularly where the values are small, as for body weight. Fourth, no primary endpoint is designated, and no adjustment for multiple comparisons is applied across the three outcomes and two time points. Fifth, the statistical model, the handling of missing data and the planned sample size are not described here. Sixth, the numbers of participants per arm, baseline values and withdrawals are not reported, and no absolute changes in the placebo arm are given. Seventh, safety and tolerability data, including adverse events, discontinuations and hypoglycaemia, are not included, which limits any conclusion about dose choice. Finally, this report does not describe trial registration or the completeness of follow-up, makes no comparison with other DLM-868 studies or with other placebo-controlled add-on trials, and does not describe the pharmacology of the compound. The three outcomes reported here are the only outcomes included in this analysis, so other effects of DLM-868 are outside the scope of this paper.

## 6. Conclusion

In adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, the mean differences versus placebo for DLM-868 were negative for HbA1c at both week 12 and week 24, and for fasting plasma glucose at both time points. The 200 mg daily dose gave the more negative mean difference than 100 mg daily for HbA1c at both time points and for fasting plasma glucose at week 24, while the fasting plasma glucose mean differences at week 12 were equal at both doses. Changes in body weight were small and close to zero, with no consistent dose-related pattern.

These results are descriptive. Because no measures of uncertainty, statistical tests or safety data are reported, the between-dose differences cannot be taken as established, and dose choice on the basis of these values alone is not supported. The conclusions apply only to the population, doses and time points studied.
