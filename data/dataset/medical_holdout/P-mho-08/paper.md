# Tembrexil in adults aged 70 or older with type 2 diabetes

## Abstract

Type 2 diabetes is common among adults aged 70 years or older, and metformin is widely used in this group. Additional glucose-lowering options for older adults who take metformin have received comparatively little dedicated evaluation, so age-specific evidence is needed to inform treatment decisions. We report a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled study in adults aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes taking metformin, in which participants were allocated to Tembrexil 45 mg once daily or to placebo. Two outcomes are reported at week 24 as mean differences versus placebo, where a negative value indicates a lower change from baseline than placebo: change in HbA1c, in percentage points, and change in fasting plasma glucose, in mmol/L. Tembrexil 45 mg daily gave a mean difference versus placebo of -0.80 percentage points for HbA1c and -1.14 mmol/L for fasting plasma glucose. Both estimates are negative. Because the study examined a single dose and a single time point, it does not address dose selection or the time course of the effect, and the results apply only to the studied population. Measures of uncertainty, safety outcomes and clinical thresholds are outside the scope of this report, so the estimates should be read as descriptive.

## 1. Introduction

Type 2 diabetes is common among adults aged 70 years or older, and metformin is a frequently used first-line agent in this group. Many older adults who take metformin still require additional glucose-lowering treatment to reach their glycaemic goals, so add-on options suitable for this age group remain an active concern for clinicians and for those who write treatment guidance. Glycaemic management in older people must also be weighed against the risks and practical burdens that accompany age, such as comorbidity, polypharmacy and varying functional status.

Older adults are, however, under-represented in many diabetes studies. Evidence is often generated in broader adult populations and then applied to older patients by extrapolation. Such extrapolation assumes that the effect of a treatment, and the way that effect develops, is similar across ages. That assumption cannot be taken for granted, because age can alter how glucose responds to treatment, how a drug is handled by the body and how well it is tolerated. Evidence generated specifically in adults aged 70 years or older is therefore needed to support decisions in this group.

For adults aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes on metformin, the effect of Tembrexil compared with placebo on change from baseline in HbA1c and in fasting plasma glucose is not known from the available data. This leaves a gap in the evidence base for a population in which metformin is common and additional therapy may be required.

This study addresses that gap with a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled design. Participants aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes who take metformin were randomised to Tembrexil 45 mg once daily or to placebo. The two outcomes are change from baseline in HbA1c, expressed in percentage points, and change from baseline in fasting plasma glucose, expressed in mmol/L, both assessed at week 24. Each outcome is reported as the mean difference versus placebo, so that a negative value indicates a lower change than placebo. The Results section presents the estimates, and the Discussion considers what they do and do not establish.

The scope of the findings is set by the population, the dose and the time point. The estimates describe adults aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes on metformin, the 45 mg once-daily dose and the week 24 outcome. They do not describe other doses, other time points, the time course of the effect or other populations.

This report makes two contributions. First, it provides the placebo-referenced mean difference in change from baseline in HbA1c at week 24 for Tembrexil 45 mg once daily in this population. Second, it provides the corresponding placebo-referenced mean difference in change from baseline in fasting plasma glucose under the same conditions. Together these estimates offer dose-specific and time-specific descriptive information for this age group. They describe one option and do not, by themselves, establish which dose should be selected.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be grouped into three clusters by design: add-on glucose-lowering trials alongside metformin, dose-ranging studies of novel glucose-lowering agents, and age-subgroup analyses of broader diabetes trials. The descriptions below concern study design and the gap each design leaves for older adults. No quantitative comparison with prior estimates is made in this report.

### 2.1 Add-on glucose-lowering trials alongside metformin

A common design in type 2 diabetes research is the randomised, placebo-controlled add-on trial, in which a new agent is given on top of a background of metformin and compared with placebo. This design isolates the contribution of the added agent to changes in glycaemic measures, and it provides the basic template for the present study. Such trials are, however, often built around broad adult populations with a wide age range, so their results for adults aged 70 years or older may be under-represented or reported only as pooled estimates that do not show how the effect looks in older participants. This study keeps the add-on, placebo-controlled structure but restricts enrolment to adults aged 70 years or older who take metformin, and it reports the placebo-referenced mean difference for this group.

### 2.2 Dose-ranging studies of novel glucose-lowering agents

Dose-ranging and multi-dose designs are used to select a dose for new glucose-lowering agents. By comparing several doses against placebo, they show how the size of the effect varies with dose and help developers and readers judge which dose is appropriate. These designs are valuable, but two limitations are relevant here. Their age distribution is not always reported in a way that allows conclusions for older adults, and the time course of the effect is not always captured at several time points. The present study takes a narrower route. It focuses on a single dose, 45 mg once daily, and reports the outcome at week 24. This makes the dose-specific result for the older age group explicit, while the dose-response and time course of the effect are not addressed.

### 2.3 Age-subgroup analyses of broader diabetes trials

Another body of work examines older adults as a subgroup within larger diabetes trials, typically through post hoc or pre-specified subgroup analyses. These analyses show whether an effect differs by age and can be informative when older participants are included in adequate numbers. Subgroup estimates are, however, often small and imprecise, and they are rarely designed to answer a question specific to older adults. The present design differs because the older-adult population is the enrolled population rather than a subgroup extracted after the fact. The estimates are therefore not subgroup results, and their scope is set by the enrolment criteria.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design and population

This study is a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups, in which Tembrexil is compared against placebo. The study population is adults aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes who are taking metformin. Participants were assessed at baseline and at week 24, and the outcomes reported here are the changes from baseline at week 24.

### 3.2 Intervention and comparator

Participants were allocated to one of two arms. In the active arm, participants received Tembrexil 45 mg once daily. In the comparator arm, participants received placebo. Placebo is the sole comparator for all values reported in this paper, so every estimate describes the difference between Tembrexil 45 mg daily and placebo at week 24.

### 3.3 Outcome measures and statistical summary

The outcomes are the change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points, and the change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline, expressed in mmol/L. Each reported value is a mean difference versus placebo, defined as the mean change from baseline in the Tembrexil arm minus the mean change from baseline in the placebo arm. A negative value indicates that the change from baseline in the Tembrexil arm was lower than the change in the placebo arm, and a positive value would indicate the reverse.

### 3.4 Scope of this report

This report presents the two outcome estimates and their interpretation. Confidence intervals, p-values and baseline values are not reported here, and no analysis beyond the mean difference versus placebo is presented. Details of randomisation, blinding, the analysis model, safety assessments and the pharmacological profile of Tembrexil are outside the scope of this paper, and readers should interpret the estimates within these limits.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Change in HbA1c from baseline

At week 24, Tembrexil 45 mg once daily gave a mean difference versus placebo in change from baseline in HbA1c of -0.80 percentage points. This is the only HbA1c estimate reported, for the single dose and time point studied.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Interpretation of the glycaemic results

Both estimates are negative. At week 24 in adults aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes on metformin, the change from baseline with Tembrexil 45 mg once daily was lower than the change with placebo for both HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose. The study was designed to estimate these placebo-referenced differences, and the direction of both estimates is consistent across the two outcomes.

The two measures are expressed in different units, percentage points for HbA1c and mmol/L for fasting plasma glucose. For this reason the magnitudes of the two differences are not directly comparable as numbers, and this paper does not convert one into the other or compare either against external clinical thresholds. Each estimate is best read on its own scale.

Clinical meaning should be interpreted with caution. The results are reported as point estimates only, and no measure of precision is given. Without confidence intervals or p-values, it is not possible to judge how stable these mean differences are or how much of the observed difference could reflect chance variation.

### 5.2 Limitations and scope

The study reports one dose and one time point. The time course of the effect is therefore not evaluated, and nothing can be said about how the effect develops after or before week 24. Because only the 45 mg daily dose is reported, the estimates cannot inform a choice between doses, although they provide dose-specific descriptive information for this dose.

The findings apply only to adults aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes on metformin. They should not be generalised to younger adults, to people who do not take metformin or to other populations without further evidence.

Safety and tolerability outcomes, which are of particular relevance in older adults, are outside the scope of this report. Confidence intervals, p-values and patient counts are not reported here, which limits the assessment of precision and reliability for each mean difference. The estimates should therefore be read as descriptive values rather than as firm conclusions about efficacy. This report also does not place the estimates against prior trial results, so the incremental contribution of the findings is limited to the population and dose described.

## 6. Conclusion

This study estimated the effect of Tembrexil compared with placebo on change from baseline in HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose in adults aged 70 years or older with type 2 diabetes on metformin, using a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled design with outcomes assessed at week 24. Tembrexil 45 mg once daily gave a mean difference versus placebo of -0.80 percentage points for HbA1c and -1.14 mmol/L for fasting plasma glucose. Both differences are negative, indicating a lower change than placebo. These results are limited to the studied population, the 45 mg daily dose and the week 24 time point. Dose-response, time-course, safety and precision questions remain outside the scope of this report.
