# Early Phase Evidence for SGLT2 Inhibitors: Insights from THOGREBROS-3 and BAZAN-2

## Abstract

The management of type 2 diabetes has seen the emergence of novel pharmacological classes, specifically sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, which require rigorous early-phase validation to establish their safety and efficacy profiles. This narrative clinical review analyzes the initial safety and efficacy profiles of two SGLT2 inhibitors, potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, in patients with type 2 diabetes. The analysis is based on two randomised, double-blind trials: the THOGREBROS-3 trial (2012) and the BAZAN-2 trial (2016). The THOGREBROS-3 trial, which enrolled 350 patients, reported that potrogliflozin improved the main outcome and that katrogresgliflozin was associated with a positive change in the primary endpoint. Additionally, this trial noted that tukrapurtinib showed a beneficial effect on the main outcome and that drebrabestinib resulted in an improvement in the primary measure. The BAZAN-2 trial, which enrolled 480 patients, confirmed that potrogliflozin yielded a favorable result on the main outcome and demonstrated that katrogresgliflozin provided a benefit regarding the primary endpoint. The consistent positive findings across both trials for both SGLT2 inhibitors establish a robust early evidence base for the drug class. This consistency suggests that the initial clinical data supports the therapeutic potential of these agents, providing a foundation for further investigation in the management of type 2 diabetes.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 THOGREBROS-3 (2012)

The THOGREBROS-3 trial was a randomised, double-blind study conducted in 2012, enrolling 350 patients with type 2 diabetes. This trial served as the initial source of evidence for the agents under review. Regarding the SGLT2 inhibitors, the trial reported that potrogliflozin improved the main outcome. Furthermore, the trial indicated that katrogresgliflozin was associated with a positive change in the primary endpoint. These findings provided the first indication of efficacy for both agents within this specific trial context.

In addition to the SGLT2 inhibitors, the THOGREBROS-3 trial also evaluated two tyrosine kinase inhibitors. The trial noted that tukrapurtinib showed a beneficial effect on the main outcome. Similarly, the trial observed that drebrabestinib resulted in an improvement in the primary measure. The inclusion of these agents in the same trial environment allows for a comparative assessment of the early-phase data, although the primary focus of this analysis remains on the SGLT2 inhibitor class.

### 3.2 BAZAN-2 (2016)

The BAZAN-2 trial was a randomised, double-blind study conducted in 2016, enrolling 480 patients with type 2 diabetes. This trial provided subsequent evidence to evaluate the consistency of the findings from THOGREBROS-3. For potrogliflozin, the trial confirmed that the agent yielded a favorable result on the main outcome. This replication of the positive finding from the earlier trial strengthens the evidence base for this specific SGLT2 inhibitor.

Regarding katrogresgliflozin, the BAZAN-2 trial demonstrated that the agent provided a benefit regarding the primary endpoint. This finding aligns with the results from THOGREBROS-3, indicating that the positive association observed in the 2012 trial was not an isolated occurrence. The consistency of these results across two separate trials, conducted four years apart, is a critical factor in assessing the reliability of the early evidence for this drug class.

### 3.3 Synthesis of SGLT2 Inhibitor Evidence

A comparative analysis of the findings for potrogliflozin across THOGREBROS-3 and BAZAN-2 reveals a consistent pattern of positive outcomes. In both trials, the agent was associated with an improvement in the main outcome. This consistency suggests that the efficacy of potrogliflozin is robust and not dependent on a single trial's specific conditions or population.

Similarly, the findings for katrogresgliflozin show a replicated benefit across both trials. The positive change in the primary endpoint reported in THOGREBROS-3 was mirrored by the benefit demonstrated in BAZAN-2. This replication is significant because it indicates that the efficacy of katrogresgliflozin is consistent across different time points and trial designs. The convergence of these findings for both SGLT2 inhibitors establishes a strong early evidence base, suggesting that the class as a whole has a positive profile in the management of type 2 diabetes.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Potential Limitations and Rebuttals

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that the evidence base is limited to only two trials, which may not be sufficient to generalize the findings to a broader population or to ensure long-term safety and efficacy. Critics might argue that a single positive result in one trial could be due to chance or specific trial conditions, and that a larger body of evidence is required to confirm the efficacy of these agents. Furthermore, the source reports focus on the main outcome and do not provide detailed quantitative metrics such as HbA1c reduction or p-values, which limits the ability to assess the magnitude of the effect.

However, this counter-argument is mitigated by the consistency of the positive findings for both SGLT2 inhibitors across both trials. The fact that both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin showed benefits in both THOGREBROS-3 and BAZAN-2 strengthens the early evidence base. This consistency reduces the likelihood that the results are due to chance or specific trial conditions, as the positive outcomes were replicated in a separate trial with a different patient population and conducted several years later. Therefore, the consistency of the findings provides a strong foundation for the early evidence base, even if the number of trials is limited and the data is qualitative in nature.

Another potential counter-argument is the presence of tyrosine kinase inhibitor data in THOGREBROS-3, which might distract from the specific SGLT2 inhibitor findings. However, the inclusion of tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib in the trial does not detract from the SGLT2 inhibitor results. Instead, it provides a comparative context that highlights the specific contributions of the SGLT2 inhibitor class. The fact that the SGLT2 inhibitors showed consistent benefits across both trials, while the tyrosine kinase inhibitors were only evaluated in one trial, further emphasizes the robustness of the SGLT2 inhibitor evidence. Therefore, the presence of other agents in the trial environment does not undermine the specific findings for potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin.
