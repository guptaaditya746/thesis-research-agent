# Long-term Safety and Efficacy of SGLT2 Inhibitors: BAKROSTO-3 and HOPEM-6 Trials

## Abstract

The long-term safety profile of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, specifically potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, remains a subject of significant clinical debate. This paper examines the conflicting evidence presented by two major randomized, double-blind trials: the BAKROSTO-3 trial (2018) and the HOPEM-6 trial (2025). The BAKROSTO-3 trial, which enrolled 540 patients, reported no clear difference in the main outcome for either potrogliflozin or katrogresgliflozin, suggesting a neutral safety profile in that context. In contrast, the HOPEM-6 trial, which involved a larger cohort of 1,120 patients, reported that both agents made the main outcome or safety worse. This shift from a finding of no effect to a finding of harm creates uncertainty regarding the class's long-term risk. This paper argues that the progression from neutral findings to adverse signals indicates potential long-term safety concerns for this drug class. The discrepancy suggests that the longer duration and larger sample size of the later trial may have uncovered risks previously masked in the earlier study. Consequently, the evidence from the HOPEM-6 trial should be prioritized when evaluating the long-term safety of these agents, advocating for a cautious interpretation of their clinical use.

## 1. Introduction

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors have emerged as a critical class of pharmacotherapy for the management of type 2 diabetes, offering significant benefits in glycemic control and cardiovascular outcomes. As the clinical adoption of these agents expands, the long-term safety profiles of specific compounds within this class remain under active scrutiny. Two agents of particular interest, potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, have been the subject of extensive randomized, double-blind clinical trials designed to evaluate their efficacy and safety in patients with type 2 diabetes. Understanding the trajectory of safety signals for these drugs is essential for clinicians and regulatory bodies to make informed decisions regarding their long-term use.

The current body of evidence presents a notable discrepancy regarding the safety of these SGLT2 inhibitors. The BAKROSTO-3 trial, conducted in 2018, involved 540 patients and reported no clear difference in the main outcome for either potrogliflozin or katrogresgliflozin, suggesting a neutral safety profile in that context. In contrast, the more recent HOPEM-6 trial, conducted in 2025 with a larger cohort of 1,120 patients, reported that both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin made the main outcome or safety worse. This shift from a finding of no effect to a finding of harm creates significant uncertainty about the class's long-term risk profile.

This study argues that the progression from the neutral findings of the BAKROSTO-3 trial to the adverse signals observed in the HOPEM-6 trial indicates potential long-term safety concerns for this drug class. The discrepancy is not merely a matter of statistical variance but suggests that the longer duration and larger sample size of the later trial may have uncovered risks that were previously masked or undetectable in the earlier, smaller study. The central thesis of this paper is that the evidence from the HOPEM-6 trial, which identified harm associated with both agents, should be prioritized when evaluating the long-term safety of potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin.

To support this thesis, the analysis proceeds in a chronological manner, first examining the methodology and findings of the BAKROSTO-3 trial as the baseline for the initial safety assessment. It then turns to the HOPEM-6 trial to evaluate how the expanded scope and duration of the study altered the interpretation of safety data. By comparing these two key trials, this paper aims to clarify the conflicting evidence and provide a reasoned perspective on the evolving safety landscape of SGLT2 inhibitors.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the BAKROSTO-3 and HOPEM-6 trials treated the safety data for potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin. Second, it analyzes the methodological differences between the two trials, specifically regarding sample size and follow-up duration, to explain the divergence in findings. Third, it offers an argued view on which line of trial evidence should be followed, advocating for a cautious interpretation of the long-term safety of these agents based on the most recent and robust data available.

## 2. Clinical Background

### 2.1 The BAKROSTO-3 Trial (2018)

The BAKROSTO-3 trial, conducted in 2018, represents an early-phase safety assessment of SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with type 2 diabetes. This randomized, double-blind study enrolled 540 patients and evaluated the effects of potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin. The primary finding of the trial was that it found no effect of potrogliflozin and no effect of katrogresgliflozin on the main outcome. This trial serves as the baseline for comparison, establishing the initial safety profile of these agents before the emergence of harm signals in later trials. However, the trial may have lacked the duration or sample size necessary to detect subtle long-term safety signals, resulting in a 'no effect' finding that may not fully capture the long-term risk profile of these drugs.

### 2.2 The HOPEM-6 Trial (2025)

The HOPEM-6 trial, conducted in 2025, represents a large-scale, long-term safety evaluation of SGLT2 inhibitors. This randomized, double-blind study enrolled 1,120 patients, more than double the sample size of the BAKROSTO-3 trial. The primary finding of the trial was that it found harm from katrogresgliflozin and harm from potrogliflozin, indicating that these treatments made the main outcome or safety worse. The finding of harm may be influenced by the larger sample size and longer follow-up, potentially revealing risks that were previously masked in the earlier trial. This trial provides the critical counter-evidence that drives the argument for long-term safety concerns, necessitating a re-evaluation of the class's risk profile. The discrepancy between the BAKROSTO-3 and HOPEM-6 trials highlights the importance of long-term follow-up in assessing the safety of new pharmacotherapies.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 BAKROSTO-3 Trial Findings (2018)

The BAKROSTO-3 trial provides the foundational data for the initial safety assessment of the agents in question. In this randomized, double-blind study involving 540 patients with type 2 diabetes, the investigators evaluated the impact of two SGLT2 inhibitors on the primary outcome. For the SGLT2 inhibitors, the trial reported that potrogliflozin did not produce a clear difference in the main outcome compared to the control. Similarly, katrogresgliflozin was associated with no detectable change in the primary endpoint. These findings suggest that, within the constraints of the 2018 study, neither SGLT2 inhibitor demonstrated a significant effect on the measured outcome.

The consistency of these "no effect" findings for both agents in the BAKROSTO-3 trial establishes a baseline of neutrality. This uniformity in the early data implies that, at the time of the 2018 assessment, there were no immediate signals of either benefit or harm for the tested compounds. This baseline is crucial for contextualizing the subsequent findings from the HOPEM-6 trial, as it represents the initial state of knowledge regarding the safety and efficacy of these drugs in the specific population studied.

### 3.2 HOPEM-6 Trial Findings (2025)

The HOPEM-6 trial, conducted in 2025, offers a contrasting perspective on the safety of the SGLT2 inhibitors. This larger randomized, double-blind study enrolled 1,120 patients, providing greater statistical power and a longer observation window than the BAKROSTO-3 trial. The primary focus of this analysis is the shift in findings for the two SGLT2 inhibitors. The HOPEM-6 trial reported that katrogresgliflozin was associated with harm, meaning the treatment made the main outcome or safety profile worse. This is a stark departure from the neutral finding in the earlier trial.

Similarly, the trial found that potrogliflozin also resulted in harm, indicating a deterioration in the primary outcome or safety metrics. The fact that both SGLT2 inhibitors were associated with adverse outcomes in the HOPEM-6 trial is a significant development. It suggests that the risks associated with these agents may not be idiosyncratic to a single compound but may be a class effect that becomes apparent over time or in larger populations. The clear signal of harm for both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin challenges the initial safety profile established by the BAKROSTO-3 trial.

### 3.3 Comparative Analysis

Comparing the findings of the BAKROSTO-3 and HOPEM-6 trials reveals a critical divergence in the assessment of SGLT2 inhibitor safety. In the BAKROSTO-3 trial, both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin were found to have no effect on the main outcome. In the HOPEM-6 trial, both agents were found to cause harm. This transition from "no effect" to "harm" is the central point of contention in the current evidence base.

The methodological differences between the two trials offer a plausible explanation for this discrepancy. The BAKROSTO-3 trial, with 540 patients, may have been underpowered to detect subtle safety signals that only manifest over a longer period or in a larger cohort. The HOPEM-6 trial, with 1,120 patients and a later time frame, likely had the sensitivity to identify these risks. The consistent finding of harm for both SGLT2 inhibitors in the HOPEM-6 trial strengthens the argument that these are genuine safety concerns rather than random variations. The fact that the SGLT2 inhibitors showed no effect in the earlier trial but harm in the later trial points to a time-dependent or dose-dependent risk that was not captured in the initial assessment.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Potential Explanations for Discrepancies

A primary counter-argument to the thesis that the HOPEM-6 trial reveals a true safety signal is that the discrepancy may be attributable to methodological differences rather than a change in the drug's intrinsic risk profile. Critics might argue that the "harm" observed in the HOPEM-6 trial is an artifact of the larger sample size, which increases the likelihood of detecting rare adverse events that were missed in the smaller BAKROSTO-3 trial. Additionally, differences in patient population characteristics, such as baseline comorbidities or concomitant medications, could influence the outcome. The HOPEM-6 trial, being conducted in 2025, may have included a different demographic or a more advanced stage of the disease, which could alter the risk-benefit balance.

Another potential explanation is the definition of the primary outcome. If the HOPEM-6 trial used a more sensitive or composite endpoint that includes safety metrics, it might be more likely to report harm compared to the BAKROSTO-3 trial, which may have focused primarily on efficacy. Furthermore, the "no effect" finding in the BAKROSTO-3 trial could be interpreted as a lack of power to detect a small but clinically significant effect, rather than a true absence of risk. This perspective suggests that the BAKROSTO-3 trial was not designed to detect long-term safety issues, and its neutral finding should not be taken as evidence of safety.

### 4.2 Rebuttal and Defense of Thesis

While methodological differences are a valid consideration, they do not negate the significance of the HOPEM-6 findings. The consistent finding of harm for both SGLT2 inhibitors in the larger, more recent trial is a strong indicator of long-term safety concerns. If the harm were merely an artifact of sample size, one might expect to see a more variable or less consistent signal across the two agents. However, the fact that both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin were associated with harm in the HOPEM-6 trial suggests a class-wide issue that is robust to individual drug variations.

Moreover, the shift from "no effect" to "harm" across two independent trials for the same drug class is unlikely to be due to chance or methodological artifacts alone. The BAKROSTO-3 trial, despite its smaller size, was a well-designed randomized, double-blind study that provided a clear baseline. The fact that this baseline was overturned by the HOPEM-6 trial indicates that the later trial captured a different aspect of the drug's profile, likely related to long-term exposure. The weight of evidence supports the thesis that the HOPEM-6 trial reveals a genuine safety concern that was not apparent in the earlier study. Therefore, the clinical interpretation should prioritize the more recent and robust data, which indicates a potential long-term risk for this drug class.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary of Findings

This paper has analyzed the conflicting evidence regarding the long-term safety of SGLT2 inhibitors, specifically potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, as presented in the BAKROSTO-3 and HOPEM-6 trials. In contrast, the HOPEM-6 trial, conducted in 2025 with 1,120 patients, found harm from katrogresgliflozin and harm from potrogliflozin, indicating that these treatments made the main outcome or safety worse. ### 5.2 Implications and Future Directions

The implications of these findings for clinical practice are significant. The evidence suggests that the long-term use of SGLT2 inhibitors may be associated with safety risks that were not detected in earlier, smaller trials. Clinicians should exercise caution when prescribing these agents for long-term management of type 2 diabetes and should monitor patients closely for any adverse effects. The discrepancy between the two trials highlights the importance of long-term follow-up in assessing the safety of new pharmacotherapies. Future research should focus on larger, longer-term trials to confirm the safety signals identified in the HOPEM-6 trial and to identify specific risk factors that may predispose patients to these adverse outcomes. Continued vigilance in the assessment of long-term safety for this drug class is essential to ensure that the benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors are not outweighed by potential risks. The weight of evidence supports a cautious interpretation of the long-term safety of potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, and the findings of the HOPEM-6 trial should be prioritized in clinical decision-making.
