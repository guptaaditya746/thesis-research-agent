# Neutral Outcomes for Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors in 2023-2025 Diabetes Management

## Abstract

The management of type 2 diabetes has increasingly incorporated tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) as potential adjunctive therapies, driven by mechanistic hypotheses regarding inflammatory and metabolic signaling. However, the most recent large-scale randomized controlled trials, the MOTIFO-7 trial (2023) and the THOKAM-7 trial (2025), have failed to demonstrate a clear efficacy advantage for these agents over standard care. This paper analyzes the clinical evidence from these two pivotal studies, focusing on the specific outcomes for the TKIs tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib, as well as the SGLT2 inhibitor potrogliflozin. The analysis reveals a consistent pattern of null results across both trials and all three agents. In MOTIFO-7, neither tukrapurtinib nor drebrabestinib showed a clear difference on the main outcome, and potrogliflozin similarly failed to demonstrate a significant effect. The THOKAM-7 trial replicated these findings, with no measurable benefit observed for tukrapurtinib or drebrabestinib, and no clear advantage for potrogliflozin. These consistent null findings challenge the theoretical premise that TKIs offer superior metabolic benefits in this context. By examining the trial evidence in chronological order and addressing potential counter-arguments regarding study design and subgroup effects, this paper argues that the current evidence does not support the routine adoption of TKIs in type 2 diabetes management. The findings suggest that clinicians should maintain a cautious stance, prioritizing established standard care until further evidence emerges that clarifies the potential utility of these experimental agents.

## 1. Introduction

The management of type 2 diabetes has historically relied on pharmacological interventions that target specific metabolic pathways, with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) emerging as a class of interest for their potential to modulate inflammatory and metabolic signaling. In recent years, clinical attention has turned to specific agents within this class, such as tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib, which were hypothesized to offer additional therapeutic benefits beyond standard care. These experimental therapies were often evaluated in the context of established treatments, including sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors like potrogliflozin, to determine if a combined or alternative approach could yield superior metabolic outcomes.

Despite the theoretical promise of TKIs in type 2 diabetes, the most recent large-scale randomized controlled trials have failed to demonstrate a clear efficacy advantage over standard care. Specifically, the MOTIFO-7 trial conducted in 2023 and the THOKAM-7 trial conducted in 2025 represent the latest high-quality evidence in this domain. Both studies were designed as randomised, double-blind investigations involving substantial patient cohorts, aiming to rigorously test the hypothesis that TKIs could improve primary clinical outcomes. The anticipation surrounding these trials was high, given the mechanistic rationale for targeting tyrosine kinase pathways in the context of chronic metabolic inflammation.

However, the results from these pivotal studies have introduced a significant gap between mechanistic expectation and clinical evidence. In both MOTIFO-7 and THOKAM-7, the experimental TKIs did not show a statistically significant benefit on the main outcome measures when compared to the control conditions. This lack of significant efficacy challenges the prevailing narrative that TKIs are a viable next-line therapy for type 2 diabetes management. The consistency of these null findings across two separate, large-scale trials conducted in different years suggests that the issue is not merely a matter of statistical power or single-study variability, but rather a fundamental limitation in the clinical translation of this drug class.

This paper argues that recent evidence from MOTIFO-7 and THOKAM-7 suggests that TKIs do not provide a clear benefit over standard care in type 2 diabetes. By analyzing the trial evidence in chronological order, we examine how each study treated the specific agents tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib, as well as the role of potrogliflozin as a comparator or background therapy. The analysis focuses on the specific null results reported in these trials to defend the thesis that the current adoption of TKIs in type 2 diabetes management is not supported by the latest clinical data. We also address potential counter-arguments regarding the interpretation of these findings, ensuring a balanced view of the current state of evidence.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the MOTIFO-7 and THOKAM-7 trials treated the sources of interest, specifically the TKIs and the SGLT2 inhibitor. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of trial evidence should be followed, emphasizing the weight of the most recent null results. Third, it synthesizes the findings to provide a definitive perspective on the clinical utility of tyrosine kinase inhibitors in the contemporary management of type 2 diabetes.

## 2. Clinical Background

### 2.1 Standard Care and SGLT2 Inhibitors

Established metabolic pathway targeting forms the backbone of current type 2 diabetes management, with SGLT2 inhibitors playing a central role. Potrogliflozin, a representative SGLT2 inhibitor, has been widely integrated into standard care protocols due to its proven efficacy in improving glycemic control and associated metabolic benefits. In the context of the recent trials, potrogliflozin served as a key component of the therapeutic landscape, either as part of the standard care arm or as a comparator for evaluating experimental therapies. However, the role of SGLT2 inhibitors in the context of TKI combination or as a direct comparator for TKIs has not been definitively established in the recent null trials. The performance of potrogliflozin in MOTIFO-7 and THOKAM-7 provides a baseline against which the null results for TKIs can be contextualized, highlighting the stability of standard care outcomes in the face of experimental interventions.

### 2.2 Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors in Diabetes

Experimental inflammatory and metabolic signaling modulation through tyrosine kinase inhibitors represents a newer approach to type 2 diabetes management. Agents such as tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib were developed with the hypothesis that inhibiting specific tyrosine kinase pathways could improve metabolic outcomes by reducing inflammation and enhancing insulin sensitivity. Earlier theoretical models suggested that TKIs could offer a significant advantage over existing therapies, leading to their inclusion in large-scale clinical trials. However, recent large-scale trials, specifically MOTIFO-7 and THOKAM-7, have not supported this hypothesis. The null results for both tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib in these trials indicate a potential disconnect between the mechanistic rationale and the observed clinical benefit. This analysis focuses on the specific null results for these TKIs to argue against the current adoption of TKIs in type 2 diabetes management, emphasizing the lack of clear efficacy in the most recent evidence.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 The MOTIFO-7 Trial (2023)

The MOTIFO-7 trial, conducted in 2023, was a randomised, double-blind study involving 860 patients with type 2 diabetes. The primary objective was to evaluate the efficacy of experimental therapies against standard care. The trial included assessments of the tyrosine kinase inhibitors tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib, as well as the SGLT2 inhibitor potrogliflozin. The results for tukrapurtinib indicated that the agent did not produce a clear difference on the main outcome compared to the control condition. Similarly, the evaluation of drebrabestinib showed that it did not improve the primary endpoint relative to the baseline. Furthermore, the trial reported that potrogliflozin did not exhibit a significant effect on the main outcome. These findings collectively suggest that, within the context of this specific trial design and population, none of the three agents demonstrated a measurable advantage over the standard of care.

### 3.2 The THOKAM-7 Trial (2025)

The THOKAM-7 trial, conducted in 2025, followed a similar randomised, double-blind design but involved a larger cohort of 1110 patients with type 2 diabetes. This study aimed to replicate and extend the findings of earlier investigations. Regarding tukrapurtinib, the trial found that the drug did not yield a measurable benefit on the primary outcome. The assessment of drebrabestinib also resulted in a null finding, with the agent failing to demonstrate efficacy on the main endpoint. Additionally, the trial indicated that potrogliflozin did not show a clear advantage on the primary outcome. The consistency of these results with those from MOTIFO-7 reinforces the observation that these agents do not provide a clear clinical benefit in the management of type 2 diabetes.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Potential Counter-Arguments

A primary counter-argument to the thesis that TKIs lack efficacy is that the null results may be attributable to limitations in trial design rather than a true absence of therapeutic benefit. Critics might argue that the sample sizes, while substantial, may not have been sufficient to detect small but clinically meaningful differences, or that the duration of the trials was too short to capture long-term metabolic improvements. Another potential counter-argument is that TKIs may offer benefits in specific patient subgroups that were not captured by the primary outcome measures. For instance, it is possible that tukrapurtinib or drebrabestinib could be effective in patients with specific inflammatory markers or comorbidities, even if the overall population showed no benefit.

### 4.2 Response to Counter-Arguments

The argument regarding design limitations is weakened by the scale and rigor of the trials. Both MOTIFO-7 and THOKAM-7 were randomised, double-blind studies with large patient cohorts (860 and 1110 patients, respectively), which provides substantial statistical power to detect meaningful differences. The fact that both trials, conducted in different years and with different sample sizes, yielded consistent null results for the same agents suggests that the findings are robust and not an artifact of a single study's design. Regarding the subgroup argument, the primary outcomes were null for both TKIs in both trials. While subgroup analyses can sometimes reveal hidden benefits, the consistent lack of effect on the primary endpoint across two large, independent trials suggests a general lack of efficacy rather than a subgroup-specific issue. Without clear evidence of benefit in predefined subgroups, the general null findings remain the most reliable guide for clinical practice.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

The analysis of the MOTIFO-7 and THOKAM-7 trials provides compelling evidence that tyrosine kinase inhibitors do not provide a clear benefit over standard care in the management of type 2 diabetes. Both tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib showed no effect in both trials, and potrogliflozin also showed no effect in both trials. The consistency of these null results across two large, randomised, double-blind studies conducted in 2023 and 2025 strongly supports the thesis that the current adoption of TKIs in type 2 diabetes management is not supported by the latest clinical data. The mechanistic rationale for these agents, while theoretically sound, has not translated into observable clinical benefit in these pivotal trials. Clinicians should therefore be cautious in adopting TKIs for type 2 diabetes management until further evidence emerges that clarifies their potential utility. The current standard of care, which does not rely on these experimental agents, remains the most evidence-based approach for managing this chronic condition.
