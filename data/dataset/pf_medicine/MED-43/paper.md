# Safety and Efficacy Conflicts in SGLT2 Inhibitor Trials: A Case Study of TODOSTAR-5 and HEVUNUR-2

## Abstract

Conflicting safety and efficacy signals for sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors and tyrosine kinase inhibitors in type 2 diabetes trials have created uncertainty in the clinical evidence base. This paper analyzes three randomized, double-blind trials: the GOGRE-3 trial (2024), the TODOSTAR-5 trial (2022), and the HEVUNUR-2 trial (2020). The TODOSTAR-5 trial reported a benefit for potrogliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, while the HEVUNUR-2 trial found no effect. The GOGRE-3 trial reported a benefit for potrogliflozin. Additionally, the HEVUNUR-2 trial reported harm from drebrabestinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, and no effect from katrogresgliflozin, while the TODOSTAR-5 trial reported a benefit from tukrapurtinib. These divergent outcomes for overlapping treatments highlight the limitations of heterogeneous trial designs and inconsistent safety monitoring. This paper argues that standardized safety monitoring protocols are necessary to resolve such apparent contradictions. By examining the trial evidence in chronological order and defending this thesis against counter-arguments, this study provides a clear account of the clinical data and an argued view on which line of evidence should be followed, ultimately supporting the implementation of uniform monitoring standards in future trials.

## 1. Introduction

However, the interpretation of these agents' clinical profiles remains complicated by divergent findings in recent randomized trials.

A significant contradiction has emerged regarding the efficacy of potrogliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor. The TODOSTAR-5 trial, conducted in 2022, reported a clear benefit for potrogliflozin, suggesting a positive impact on primary outcomes. In contrast, the HEVUNUR-2 trial, conducted in 2020, found no effect of potrogliflozin on the main outcome. This discrepancy is particularly concerning given that both trials utilized randomized, double-blind designs, which are considered the gold standard for minimizing bias. The conflicting signals not only create uncertainty for clinicians but also raise questions about the robustness of the evidence base for this class of drugs.

The scope of this analysis encompasses three randomized, double-blind trials: GOGRE-3 (2024), TODOSTAR-5 (2022), and HEVUNUR-2 (2020). These studies assessed four specific agents in patients with type 2 diabetes. By examining the findings from these trials, we aim to identify the core conflict in the clinical evidence and explore potential reasons for the divergent outcomes. The GOGRE-3 trial, the most recent of the three, also reported a benefit for potrogliflozin, adding another layer of complexity to the interpretation of its efficacy.

The central thesis of this paper is that standardized safety monitoring protocols are required to resolve these apparent contradictions. The lack of uniformity in safety monitoring across trials may contribute to the inconsistent reporting of efficacy and safety signals. By analyzing the trial findings in chronological order and defending this thesis against counter-arguments, we aim to provide a clear framework for interpreting conflicting clinical data. This approach will help clarify the true efficacy and safety profiles of the agents under investigation.

This study contributes to the field by providing a detailed analysis of the conflicting signals in SGLT2 inhibitor trials. It highlights the need for standardized safety monitoring protocols to ensure consistent and reliable clinical evidence. By examining the specific trials and their findings, this paper offers a structured approach to resolving contradictions in the clinical data, ultimately supporting more informed decision-making in the management of type 2 diabetes.

## 2. Clinical Background

### 2.1 Heterogeneity in Trial Design and Safety Monitoring

Prior clinical approaches to evaluating SGLT2 inhibitors and tyrosine kinase inhibitors in type 2 diabetes have relied on heterogeneous trial designs and inconsistent safety monitoring, leading to conflicting signals. Standard randomized, double-blind trial designs, while robust in minimizing bias, often lack unified safety protocols. This lack of standardization allows for divergent interpretations of efficacy and safety signals, particularly for overlapping treatments like potrogliflozin. The GOGRE-3, TODOSTAR-5, and HEVUNUR-2 trials, for instance, each employed randomized, double-blind designs but did not adhere to a common safety monitoring framework. This inconsistency is evident in the conflicting findings for potrogliflozin, where TODOSTAR-5 reported a benefit, HEVUNUR-2 found no effect, and GOGRE-3 reported a benefit. These discrepancies underscore the need for standardized monitoring to ensure that efficacy and safety signals are interpreted consistently across studies. This paper analyzes the specific trial evidence from GOGRE-3, TODOSTAR-5, and HEVUNUR-2 to demonstrate how inconsistent protocols contribute to conflicting results and argues for standardized monitoring to resolve these contradictions.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 HEVUNUR-2 (2020): Baseline Findings

The HEVUNUR-2 trial, conducted in 2020 with 690 patients, provided the earliest evidence among the three studies analyzed. This randomized, double-blind trial assessed the impact of several agents on the main outcome in patients with type 2 diabetes. Regarding the SGLT2 inhibitor potrogliflozin, the trial reported no clear difference on the main outcome, indicating a neutral effect. Similarly, for the SGLT2 inhibitor katrogresgliflozin, the study found no effect on the primary endpoint. In contrast, the trial reported that the tyrosine kinase inhibitor drebrabestinib made the main outcome or safety worse, indicating a harmful effect. These findings established a baseline where one SGLT2 inhibitor showed no benefit, another showed no effect, and a tyrosine kinase inhibitor was associated with harm. The implications of these results for the early evidence base were significant, as they suggested that not all agents in these classes would demonstrate efficacy, and that safety concerns could arise with specific tyrosine kinase inhibitors. The neutral findings for potrogliflozin in this trial would later become a point of contention when subsequent studies reported different outcomes.

### 3.2 TODOSTAR-5 (2022): Conflicting Signals

The TODOSTAR-5 trial, conducted in 2022 with 750 patients, introduced conflicting signals regarding the efficacy of potrogliflozin. This randomized, double-blind study reported that potrogliflozin improved the main outcome, directly contradicting the finding of no effect in the HEVUNUR-2 trial. This discrepancy is central to the argument for standardized safety monitoring, as it highlights how different trial contexts can yield divergent results for the same agent. Additionally, the TODOSTAR-5 trial reported a benefit for the tyrosine kinase inhibitor tukrapurtinib, adding to the evidence for this class of drugs. The contradiction between TODOSTAR-5 and HEVUNUR-2 for potrogliflozin suggests that factors such as differences in trial design, patient populations, or safety monitoring protocols may have influenced the outcomes. The fact that TODOSTAR-5 found a benefit where HEVUNUR-2 found no effect underscores the need for a unified approach to monitoring and interpreting clinical data. The positive finding for tukrapurtinib further complicates the landscape, as it suggests that tyrosine kinase inhibitors may have variable effects depending on the specific agent and trial context.

### 3.3 GOGRE-3 (2024): Recent Evidence

The GOGRE-3 trial, conducted in 2024 with 1000 patients, provided the most recent evidence in this analysis. This randomized, double-blind study reported a benefit for potrogliflozin, aligning with the findings of the TODOSTAR-5 trial but still conflicting with the HEVUNUR-2 trial. The pattern of findings across the three trials shows that two trials (TODOSTAR-5 and GOGRE-3) support the benefit of potrogliflozin, while one (HEVUNUR-2) does not. This pattern suggests that the earlier neutral finding in HEVUNUR-2 may be an outlier, or that changes in trial design or patient populations over time have influenced the results. The implications of this pattern for the overall evidence base are significant, as it reinforces the need for standardized protocols to resolve the conflict. The consistent benefit reported in the two more recent trials suggests that potrogliflozin may indeed have a positive impact on the main outcome, but the contradiction with HEVUNUR-2 remains unresolved without a unified monitoring framework. The GOGRE-3 trial thus adds weight to the argument that standardized safety monitoring is essential for interpreting conflicting clinical data.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument and Response

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis is that the conflicting results for potrogliflozin may be due to differences in trial design, patient populations, or outcome measures rather than a lack of standardized safety monitoring. This argument suggests that the inherent variability in clinical trials, such as differences in inclusion criteria, dosing regimens, or primary endpoints, could explain the divergent findings without invoking the need for standardized protocols. However, this counter-argument is weakened by the fact that both TODOSTAR-5 and GOGRE-3, despite their different designs and patient populations, reported a benefit for potrogliflozin. The consistency of these positive findings across two separate trials suggests that the agent itself has a genuine effect, and that the neutral finding in HEVUNUR-2 is more likely due to specific contextual factors, such as safety monitoring practices, rather than a fundamental difference in the agent's efficacy. Furthermore, the fact that HEVUNUR-2 also reported harm from drebrabestinib and no effect from katrogresgliflozin suggests that the trial's overall context may have influenced its results. Standardized safety monitoring protocols would help identify and resolve such contradictions by ensuring that all trials use a common framework for assessing and reporting safety and efficacy signals. This would allow for a more consistent interpretation of the data and reduce the likelihood of conflicting findings arising from methodological differences. Therefore, while trial design and patient populations are important factors, the need for standardized safety monitoring remains a critical component of resolving apparent contradictions in clinical data.
