# Contradictory Findings on SGLT2 Inhibitor and Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor Safety: A Comparative Review of BRIGRAHIN-4 and BORAVEN-8

## Abstract

Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors and tyrosine kinase inhibitors are increasingly utilized in the management of type 2 diabetes, yet their long-term safety profiles remain subjects of clinical debate due to conflicting trial data. This paper examines the contradictory findings regarding potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib in two pivotal randomized, double-blind trials: the BRIGRAHIN-4 trial (2016) and the BORAVEN-8 trial (2023). The BRIGRAHIN-4 trial, involving 530 patients, reported that all three agents were associated with harm, indicating a worsening of the primary outcome or safety profile. Conversely, the more recent BORAVEN-8 trial, which enrolled 880 patients, reported a benefit for each of these treatments, demonstrating an improvement in the main outcome. This direct disagreement between the earlier and later trials creates significant uncertainty regarding the true safety and efficacy of these agents. This narrative clinical review analyzes the temporal and design differences between the two studies to interpret the discrepancy. The central argument posits that the later trial’s report of benefit should be viewed with caution given the earlier signal of harm. The contradiction itself suggests potential long-term safety concerns or inconsistencies in the trial evidence for these agents, necessitating a cautious re-evaluation of clinical guidelines and further investigation to resolve the conflicting data.

## 1. Introduction

Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors have become a cornerstone of pharmacological management for type 2 diabetes, offering significant improvements in glycemic control and associated metabolic outcomes. Despite their widespread adoption, the long-term safety profile of this drug class remains a subject of intense scrutiny within the clinical community. While initial data suggested a favorable risk-benefit ratio, recent evidence has introduced ambiguity, with conflicting trial results raising questions about the consistency of safety signals across different study contexts. This uncertainty is particularly pronounced for specific agents within the class, where divergent findings from major clinical trials have created a gap in the understanding of their true safety implications.

The core of this controversy lies in the contradictory outcomes reported for potrogliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, in two pivotal randomized, double-blind trials. The BRIGRAHIN-4 trial, conducted in 2016, reported that potrogliflozin was associated with harm, indicating that the treatment worsened the primary outcome or safety profile in its cohort of 530 patients. In stark contrast, the more recent BORAVEN-8 trial, published in 2023, reported a benefit of potrogliflozin, demonstrating that the treatment improved the main outcome in a larger cohort of 880 patients. This direct disagreement between the two studies regarding the same therapeutic agent creates a significant interpretive challenge for clinicians and researchers alike.

This paper addresses the discrepancy between these two key trials by analyzing the temporal and design differences that may account for the divergent safety and efficacy signals. The central argument posits that the conflicting data necessitates a cautious re-evaluation of the safety profile of SGLT2 inhibitors, particularly when comparing earlier and later trial designs. By examining the specific findings of BRIGRAHIN-4 and BORAVEN-8 in chronological order, this study aims to clarify the nature of the conflict and provide a reasoned perspective on which line of evidence should be prioritized in clinical decision-making.

The analysis focuses specifically on the outcomes for potrogliflozin, while also noting the broader context of other agents tested in these trials, such as katrogresgliflozin, another SGLT2 inhibitor, and tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, which also showed divergent results between the two studies. The primary objective is not to dismiss the findings of either trial but to understand the factors that may have led to the opposing conclusions. This involves a detailed review of the trial methodologies, patient populations, and the specific definitions of benefit and harm used in each study.

By synthesizing the evidence from these two trials, this paper seeks to resolve the ambiguity surrounding the safety of potrogliflozin and, by extension, the broader class of SGLT2 inhibitors. The study argues that the later trial, BORAVEN-8, provides a more robust basis for assessing benefit due to its larger sample size and more recent design, yet the earlier signal of harm from BRIGRAHIN-4 cannot be ignored without further investigation. This re-evaluation is critical for ensuring that clinical guidelines reflect the most accurate and up-to-date understanding of drug safety.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the BORAVEN-8 and BRIGRAHIN-4 trials treated the safety and efficacy of potrogliflozin, highlighting the specific points of contradiction. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of trial evidence should be followed, based on a comparative analysis of the trial designs and outcomes. Third, it frames the broader implications of these conflicting findings for the long-term safety monitoring of SGLT2 inhibitors in clinical practice.

## 2. Clinical Background

Early clinical investigations into SGLT2 inhibitors primarily focused on establishing efficacy in glycemic control for type 2 diabetes. These studies, such as the BRIGRAHIN-4 trial conducted in 2016, typically employed randomized, double-blind designs to assess primary outcomes in relatively smaller patient populations. The BRIGRAHIN-4 trial, which included 530 patients, reported that potrogliflozin was associated with harm, suggesting that the treatment worsened the main outcome or safety profile. The limitation of these early studies may lie in their smaller sample sizes and shorter follow-up durations, which could have led to the detection of safety signals that were not observed in later, larger trials. The focus on primary efficacy outcomes in these early trials may have also influenced the interpretation of safety data, with harm signals potentially being underreported or misinterpreted in the context of the primary study objectives.

More recent trials and re-analyses have shifted the focus toward long-term safety and the consistency of efficacy signals across different patient populations. This trial reported a benefit of potrogliflozin, indicating that the treatment improved the main outcome. The design of BORAVEN-8, with its larger sample size and more recent methodology, may have provided a more robust assessment of the drug's safety and efficacy profile. However, the discrepancy between the findings of BORAVEN-8 and the earlier BRIGRAHIN-4 trial raises questions about the factors that may have influenced the divergent outcomes. Later trials may have different patient selections or follow-up periods that mask or alter safety profiles, leading to reports of benefit where earlier trials reported harm. This paper analyzes the temporal and design differences between these two trials to argue for a cautious interpretation of the safety data, emphasizing the need for a comprehensive understanding of the factors that contribute to conflicting trial results.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 BRIGRAHIN-4 (2016) Findings

The BRIGRAHIN-4 trial, conducted in 2016, serves as the earlier reference point in this comparative analysis. This randomized, double-blind study enrolled 530 patients with type 2 diabetes. The trial’s primary findings indicated a negative safety and efficacy profile for the agents tested. Specifically, the study reported that potrogliflozin was associated with harm, meaning the treatment worsened the primary outcome or safety metrics in the patient cohort. This adverse signal was not isolated to a single agent; the trial also found that katrogresgliflozin resulted in harm, similarly degrading the main outcome or safety profile. Furthermore, the study reported that tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor included in the trial, was also associated with harm. The consistency of these negative findings across all three treatments suggests a systemic issue within the trial context or a genuine safety concern for these agents in the 2016 patient population. The implication of these harm signals is significant, as they establish a baseline of adverse outcomes that subsequent trials must address or explain.

### 3.2 BORAVEN-8 (2023) Findings

In contrast to the earlier study, the BORAVEN-8 trial, published in 2023, presented a markedly different picture. This randomized, double-blind trial involved a larger cohort of 880 patients with type 2 diabetes. The findings from this more recent investigation reported a positive outcome for the same agents. The trial found that potrogliflozin provided a benefit, indicating that the treatment improved the main outcome in the study population. This positive signal was extended to the other agents tested; the study reported that katrogresgliflozin also yielded a benefit, enhancing the primary outcome. Additionally, the trial found that tukrapurtinib was associated with a benefit, showing an improvement in the main outcome. The shift from harm to benefit for all three treatments between the 2016 and 2023 trials is the central anomaly of this review. The larger sample size and more recent design of BORAVEN-8 may have contributed to these positive findings, potentially reflecting improved trial methodology, different patient selection criteria, or a genuine change in the risk-benefit profile of these drugs over time.

### 3.3 The Contradiction

The juxtaposition of the findings from BRIGRAHIN-4 and BORAVEN-8 reveals a stark contradiction. For potrogliflozin, the 2016 trial reported harm, while the 2023 trial reported a benefit. This direct disagreement is not unique to potrogliflozin; the same pattern of contradiction exists for katrogresgliflozin and tukrapurtinib, where the earlier trial indicated harm and the later trial indicated benefit. This systematic reversal of outcomes across all three agents is significant and cannot be ignored. It suggests that the factors influencing the trial results—whether they be patient demographics, trial duration, or methodological differences—had a profound impact on the observed safety and efficacy profiles. The contradiction challenges the assumption that the safety profile of these drugs is stable and consistent across different trial contexts. It raises the question of whether the benefit reported in 2023 is a true reflection of the drug’s effect or an artifact of the specific conditions of the BORAVEN-8 trial. The existence of such a clear conflict necessitates a careful examination of the underlying causes and a cautious interpretation of the more recent data.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for the Later Trial

The strongest argument in favor of prioritizing the findings of the BORAVEN-8 trial is its recency and scale. As a 2023 study, it reflects more current clinical practices and potentially more refined trial methodologies. Furthermore, with a sample size of 880 patients, it is substantially larger than the 530 patients in the 2016 BRIGRAHIN-4 trial. A larger sample size generally increases the statistical power of a study, making it more likely to detect true effects and less susceptible to random variation. Proponents of the later trial might argue that the benefit reported for potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib is a more reliable indicator of the drugs’ true efficacy and safety profile. The improved patient selection and longer follow-up periods in more recent trials may have allowed for a more accurate assessment of long-term outcomes, potentially masking the short-term harms observed in the earlier study. This perspective suggests that the 2023 data should be given greater weight in clinical decision-making, as it represents a more robust and up-to-date evaluation of the treatments.

### 4.2 Rebuttal: Safety Concerns Persist

However, the earlier harm signal from BRIGRAHIN-4 cannot be dismissed without further investigation. The fact that the 2016 trial reported harm for all three agents suggests a potential safety concern that may have been overlooked or mitigated in the later trial. The difference in trial design or patient population may explain the discrepancy, but it does not eliminate the safety concern. If the 2023 trial’s patient selection or methodology inadvertently excluded patients who are at higher risk of harm, the reported benefit may not be generalizable to the broader population. The contradiction itself is a reason for caution in clinical practice. Until the factors responsible for the divergent outcomes are fully understood, it is prudent to view the benefit claims of the later trial with skepticism. The persistence of the earlier harm signal indicates that the safety profile of these drugs is not as clear-cut as the 2023 data might suggest. Therefore, the conflicting evidence should lead to a more conservative approach, with continued monitoring and further research to resolve the contradiction and ensure patient safety.

## 5. Conclusion

This paper has examined the contradictory findings regarding the safety and efficacy of SGLT2 inhibitors and tukrapurtinib in the BRIGRAHIN-4 (2016) and BORAVEN-8 (2023) trials. The central thesis is that the conflicting data between these two trials raises significant safety concerns for SGLT2 inhibitors. The BRIGRAHIN-4 trial reported harm for potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib, while the more recent BORAVEN-8 trial reported a benefit for all three agents. This direct contradiction, spanning a seven-year period and involving different sample sizes and trial designs, creates a substantial gap in the understanding of these drugs’ true safety profiles. The analysis suggests that the later trial’s report of benefit should be interpreted with caution, given the earlier signal of harm. The discrepancy may be due to differences in patient populations, trial methodologies, or follow-up durations, but the safety concern remains valid until these factors are fully elucidated. Further research is needed to resolve the contradiction and provide a clear, consistent picture of the safety and efficacy of these agents. In the interim, clinicians should be aware of the conflicting evidence and consider the potential risks when prescribing these treatments. The re-evaluation of SGLT2 inhibitor safety is an ongoing process, and the findings of this review highlight the importance of careful interpretation of trial data in the context of conflicting evidence.
