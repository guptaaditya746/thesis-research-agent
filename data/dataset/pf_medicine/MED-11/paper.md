# Early SGLT2 Inhibitor Efficacy and the 2025 Contradiction

## Abstract

The therapeutic landscape for type 2 diabetes has been shaped by the sequential introduction of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, with early trials establishing a baseline of efficacy for specific agents. Recent trial data, specifically from the NESENAM-7 trial, presents a contradiction to the established narrative of consistent benefit, creating uncertainty about which line of evidence should guide clinical practice. This paper analyzes the consistency of early SGLT2 inhibitor trial results with recent contradictory findings. The key agents under investigation are potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, both SGLT2 inhibitors, and tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor. The analysis focuses on four randomized, double-blind trials: KUSOBRER-3 (2012), KATHA-5 (2023), GOGRE-3 (2024), and NESENAM-7 (2025). The central thesis is that the KUSOBRER-3 trial established a benefit for potrogliflozin, while the KATHA-5 trial confirmed benefits for both SGLT2 inhibitors, a narrative that is complicated by the NESENAM-7 trial's contradiction of KATHA-5. By analyzing the trials in chronological order, this study argues that the earlier, consistent benefit signals are more reliable than the recent contradiction. The findings indicate that while the NESENAM-7 trial introduces a significant outlier, the broader pattern of evidence from KUSOBRER-3 and KATHA-5 supports the continued validity of SGLT2 inhibitor efficacy. This paper provides a clear account of how these trials treated the sources of evidence they assessed and offers an argued view on which line of trial evidence should be followed in the context of the 2025 contradiction.

## 1. Introduction

However, the interpretation of this evidence has become increasingly complex as newer, larger-scale trials have emerged, introducing data that challenges the previously held view of uniform therapeutic success.

Central to this evolving narrative are specific agents and the trials that have evaluated them. The key treatments under consideration include two SGLT2 inhibitors, potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, as well as a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, tukrapurtinib. The clinical evidence for these agents is derived from a series of randomized, double-blind trials conducted over the past decade. Specifically, the KUSOBRER-3 trial (2012), the KATHA-5 trial (2023), the GOGRE-3 trial (2024), and the NESENAM-7 trial (2025) form the core of the current evidentiary base. Each of these studies contributes a distinct data point to the broader understanding of how these medications perform in clinical practice.

The primary issue addressed in this study is the consistency of early SGLT2 inhibitor trial results when compared against recent contradictory findings. The KUSOBRER-3 trial initially established a benefit for potrogliflozin, setting a precedent for the efficacy of this agent. Subsequently, the KATHA-5 trial confirmed benefits for both SGLT2 inhibitors, reinforcing the positive narrative established by earlier work. However, this consistent line of evidence is complicated by the NESENAM-7 trial, which presents findings that contradict the results of the KATHA-5 trial. This contradiction creates uncertainty regarding which line of evidence should guide clinical practice and how to interpret the overall efficacy of these agents.

To resolve this uncertainty, this paper analyzes the trials in chronological order, examining how each study treated the evidence provided by its predecessors. By tracing the evolution of the data from the smaller, earlier populations to the larger, more recent cohorts, we can assess the robustness of the conflicting signals. The analysis focuses on the specific outcomes reported for each agent, highlighting where the evidence aligns and where it diverges. This temporal approach allows for a clear identification of the points at which the narrative of consistent benefit was challenged.

The central thesis of this study is that while the KUSOBRER-3 trial established a benefit for potrogliflozin and the KATHA-5 trial confirmed benefits for both SGLT2 inhibitors, the NESENAM-7 trial introduces a significant contradiction that complicates this narrative. By systematically reviewing the findings of these four trials, this paper aims to determine which line of evidence is more robust and provides a clearer guide for clinical decision-making. The following sections detail the clinical background, analyze the trial evidence in depth, and address potential counter-arguments to this interpretation.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the listed trials treated the sources of evidence they assessed. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of trial evidence should be followed in the context of the 2025 contradiction. Third, it synthesizes the findings from KUSOBRER-3, KATHA-5, GOGRE-3, and NESENAM-7 to clarify the current status of SGLT2 inhibitor efficacy in type 2 diabetes.

## 2. Clinical Background

### 2.1 Early Efficacy Trials (2012-2015)

The initial phase of clinical evaluation for SGLT2 inhibitors was characterized by randomized, double-blind trials designed to establish baseline efficacy. The KUSOBRER-3 trial, conducted in 2012, represents a key study in this early period. Enrolling 450 patients with type 2 diabetes, this trial provided the first substantial evidence regarding the therapeutic potential of potrogliflozin. The findings from this study indicated a benefit for potrogliflozin, thereby establishing a foundational signal of efficacy for this agent. Additionally, the KUSOBRER-3 trial also reported a benefit for katrogresgliflozin, suggesting that both SGLT2 inhibitors had promising profiles in this initial cohort. However, the relatively small sample size of 450 patients may have limited the ability to capture long-term safety nuances or subtle variations in efficacy that might be more apparent in larger populations. Despite these limitations, the KUSOBRER-3 trial served as the critical starting point for the evidence base, providing the initial justification for further investigation into these agents.

### 2.2 Recent Confirmatory and Contradictory Trials (2023-2025)

In the more recent period, large-scale randomized, double-blind trials have sought to confirm or refine the early findings. The KATHA-5 trial, conducted in 2023 with 850 patients, expanded the evidence base by confirming benefits for both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin. This trial also included tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, for which it found a benefit. The GOGRE-3 trial, conducted in 2024 with 1000 patients, further contributed to the body of evidence, though its specific findings are contextualized by the subsequent NESENAM-7 trial. The NESENAM-7 trial, the most recent study with 1100 patients, introduced a significant complication to the narrative. By contradicting the findings of the GOGRE-3 trial, NESENAM-7 raised questions about the consistency of SGLT2 inhibitor benefits in larger, more recent populations. This contradiction highlights the need for a careful analysis of how these later trials interact with the earlier, consistent benefit signals, particularly in determining the most robust line of evidence for clinical practice.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 KUSOBRER-3 (2012): Establishing Baseline Benefit

The KUSOBRER-3 trial, conducted in 2012, serves as the foundational study in this analysis. With a cohort of 450 patients with type 2 diabetes, this randomized, double-blind trial was designed to assess the initial efficacy of SGLT2 inhibitors. The trial reported a benefit for potrogliflozin, indicating that this agent improved the main outcome in the studied population. This finding was significant as it provided the first substantial evidence supporting the therapeutic potential of potrogliflozin in clinical practice. Furthermore, the KUSOBRER-3 trial also reported a benefit for katrogresgliflozin, suggesting that the efficacy signal was not isolated to a single agent but extended to the broader class of SGLT2 inhibitors. The dual positive findings for both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin established a baseline of efficacy that would be tested in subsequent, larger trials. The consistency of these early results created a strong initial narrative of benefit, which became the reference point for later investigations.

### 3.2 KATHA-5 (2023): Confirming Benefits

A decade later, the KATHA-5 trial, conducted in 2023 with 850 patients, provided a critical confirmation of the early findings. This randomized, double-blind study expanded the evidence base by evaluating the same SGLT2 inhibitors in a larger population. The KATHA-5 trial reported a benefit for potrogliflozin, reinforcing the efficacy signal first observed in KUSOBRER-3. Additionally, the trial confirmed a benefit for katrogresgliflozin, further solidifying the narrative of consistent therapeutic success for this class of agents. Beyond the SGLT2 inhibitors, the KATHA-5 trial also evaluated tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, and reported a benefit for this agent as well. The inclusion of tukrapurtinib broadened the scope of the trial, but the primary significance of KATHA-5 lies in its confirmation of the SGLT2 inhibitor benefits. The fact that both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin showed positive outcomes in this larger, more recent cohort strengthened the argument for the reliability of the earlier, consistent benefit signals. The KATHA-5 trial thus served as a pivotal point in the evidence timeline, bridging the early efficacy data with the subsequent, more complex findings.

### 3.3 GOGRE-3 (2024) and NESENAM-7 (2025): The Contradiction

The most recent trials, GOGRE-3 (2024) and NESENAM-7 (2025), introduce a significant complication to the established narrative. The GOGRE-3 trial, conducted with 1000 patients, contributed to the body of evidence for SGLT2 inhibitors, though its specific findings are best understood in the context of the subsequent NESENAM-7 trial. The NESENAM-7 trial, the largest of the four studies with 1100 patients, reported a result that contradicted the findings of the GOGRE-3 trial. This contradiction is the central point of contention in the current evidence base. While the earlier trials consistently reported benefits for SGLT2 inhibitors, the NESENAM-7 trial presents data that disagrees with the earlier GOGRE-3 trial, introducing uncertainty about the consistency of SGLT2 inhibitor benefits in larger, more recent populations. The NESENAM-7 trial's contradiction of the GOGRE-3 trial challenges the previously held view of uniform therapeutic success and raises questions about the robustness of the earlier, consistent benefit signals. This divergence in findings necessitates a careful analysis of the factors that may have contributed to the contradiction and an assessment of which line of evidence is more reliable.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis that the earlier, consistent benefit signals are more reliable is that the NESENAM-7 trial's contradiction of the GOGRE-3 trial suggests that the earlier findings may not be robust. Proponents of this view argue that the NESENAM-7 trial, being the most recent and largest study, provides the most current and relevant data. They contend that the contradiction indicates a potential flaw in the earlier trials or a change in the patient population or treatment context that was not captured in the initial studies. This perspective emphasizes the importance of the most recent data in guiding clinical practice and suggests that the earlier, consistent benefit signals may be outliers in a broader pattern of variability.

However, this counter-argument is weakened by the consistency of the findings in the KUSOBRER-3 and KATHA-5 trials. These two trials, conducted in different time periods and with different patient populations, consistently found benefits for both SGLT2 inhibitors. The KUSOBRER-3 trial, with 450 patients, and the KATHA-5 trial, with 850 patients, both reported positive outcomes for potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin. This consistency across different cohorts and time periods suggests that the earlier benefit signals are not isolated incidents but part of a broader pattern of efficacy. The NESENAM-7 trial's contradiction, while significant, is an outlier in this broader pattern. The fact that the earlier trials consistently reported benefits, while the more recent trials show a contradiction, suggests that the earlier, consistent benefit signals are more reliable. The NESENAM-7 trial's findings may be influenced by specific factors unique to that study, such as patient characteristics or trial design, which do not reflect the broader efficacy of SGLT2 inhibitors. Therefore, the line of evidence from KUSOBRER-3 and KATHA-5 should be followed, as it provides a more robust and consistent guide for clinical decision-making.
