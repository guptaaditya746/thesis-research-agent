# Efficacy of SGLT2 Inhibitors in Mid-2010s Randomised Controlled Trials

## Abstract

This paper presents a narrative clinical review of the efficacy of sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, specifically potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, in the management of type 2 diabetes. The analysis is restricted to two randomised, double-blind clinical trials conducted between 2015 and 2019: the SOKROS-7 trial (2015) and the LEFOKOL-5 trial (2019). The objective is to assess whether these specific agents consistently demonstrated beneficial effects on primary outcomes during this period. A review of the trial evidence reveals that both the SOKROS-7 and LEFOKOL-5 trials reported positive outcomes for both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin. The SOKROS-7 trial, involving 650 patients, indicated that both SGLT2 inhibitors improved the primary outcome. Similarly, the LEFOKOL-5 trial, which enrolled 810 patients, confirmed these beneficial effects. The consistency of these findings across two distinct time points supports the therapeutic benefit of these agents. The paper further addresses potential counter-arguments regarding the inclusion of other therapeutic classes in the trial arms and the limited number of trials analyzed. It concludes that the evidence from 2015 to 2019 robustly supports the efficacy of potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin in type 2 diabetes, providing a clear line of trial evidence for clinical consideration.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 The SOKROS-7 Trial (2015)

The SOKROS-7 trial, conducted in 2015, was a randomised, double-blind study involving 650 patients with type 2 diabetes. This trial evaluated multiple therapeutic agents, including the SGLT2 inhibitors potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, as well as the tyrosine kinase inhibitor drebrabestinib. The primary outcome of the trial was assessed for each treatment arm. The results indicated that potrogliflozin improved the primary outcome, demonstrating a clear therapeutic benefit for this SGLT2 inhibitor. Similarly, katrogresgliflozin also showed a positive effect on the primary outcome, confirming its efficacy in this patient population. Additionally, the trial reported that drebrabestinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, also improved the primary outcome. These findings establish a baseline of efficacy for the SGLT2 inhibitors in the mid-2010s, with both agents showing consistent benefits in the SOKROS-7 trial.

### 3.2 The LEFOKOL-5 Trial (2019)

The LEFOKOL-5 trial, conducted in 2019, was a randomised, double-blind study that enrolled 810 patients with type 2 diabetes. This trial also evaluated a range of therapeutic agents, including the SGLT2 inhibitors potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, and the tyrosine kinase inhibitor tukrapurtinib. The primary outcome was assessed for each treatment arm. The trial reported that potrogliflozin improved the primary outcome, reinforcing the beneficial effects observed in the earlier SOKROS-7 trial. Katrogresgliflozin also demonstrated a positive effect on the primary outcome, further supporting its efficacy. In addition, the trial found that tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, improved the primary outcome. The consistency of the results for potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin across both trials highlights the robustness of their therapeutic benefits in type 2 diabetes.

### 3.3 Synthesis of SGLT2 Inhibitor Efficacy

A synthesis of the findings from the SOKROS-7 and LEFOKOL-5 trials reveals a consistent pattern of efficacy for the SGLT2 inhibitors potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin. In both trials, conducted four years apart, these agents improved the primary outcome. The synthesis of this evidence supports the thesis that SGLT2 inhibitors consistently demonstrate a beneficial effect on primary outcomes in type 2 diabetes during the 2015–2019 period.
