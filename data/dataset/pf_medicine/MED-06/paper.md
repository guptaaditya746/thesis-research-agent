# Divergent Efficacy of SGLT2 Inhibitors in Early-Stage Type 2 Diabetes

## Abstract

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are a standard class of therapy for type 2 diabetes, yet recent randomized trials have produced conflicting signals regarding their efficacy in early-stage disease. This narrative clinical review analyzes the divergent outcomes reported by the DROME-5, MOBIMEN-5, and KAVINON-5 trials, which assess the agents potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin. The DROME-5 trial reported a benefit for both agents, whereas the MOBIMEN-5 trial found no effect for either. The most recent trial, KAVINON-5, further complicated the evidence base by contradicting the null results of MOBIMEN-5. By examining the trials in chronological order, this paper assesses how each study treated the prior evidence and sources it evaluated. The analysis reveals that the inconsistency highlights divergent efficacy across trial designs and patient populations. We argue that the conflicting results necessitate a critical analysis of the trial evidence rather than a simple aggregation of outcomes. This review provides a clear account of the treatment of sources by recent trials and offers an argued view on the current state of evidence for patients with early-stage type 2 diabetes.

This paper argues that the observed inconsistency highlights divergent efficacy across trial designs and patient populations, necessitating a critical analysis of the trial evidence rather than a simple aggregation of results. By examining the trials in chronological order, we can assess how each study treated the sources and prior evidence it evaluated. The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the recent trials treated the earlier sources and treatments they assessed. This study re-examines these early findings in light of larger, more recent trials to assess the consistency of the initial efficacy signals.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 DROME-5 Trial (2014)

The DROME-5 trial, conducted in 2014, was a randomised, double-blind study involving 510 patients with type 2 diabetes. As an early efficacy trial, it provided the initial signal for the SGLT2 inhibitors under investigation. The trial reported that potrogliflozin improved the main outcome, indicating a therapeutic benefit for this agent in the studied population. Similarly, the DROME-5 trial found that katrogresgliflozin also improved the main outcome, suggesting that both agents within the SGLT2 inhibitor class had potential efficacy in early-stage type 2 diabetes. These positive findings established a baseline expectation for the efficacy of these drugs, which subsequent trials would need to confirm or challenge. The consistency of these early positive results for both potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin created a strong initial evidence base that influenced the design and interpretation of later, larger studies.

### 3.2 MOBIMEN-5 Trial (2023)

The MOBIMEN-5 trial, conducted in 2023, was a randomised, double-blind study with a larger cohort of 890 patients. This trial was designed to assess the efficacy of the same agents in a more recent and larger population. The MOBIMEN-5 trial reported no clear difference on the main outcome for katrogresgliflozin. Furthermore, the MOBIMEN-5 trial also found no clear effect for potrogliflozin. Additionally, the trial assessed tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, and found no clear difference on the main outcome for this agent as well. The inclusion of tukrapurtinib as a control agent helps contextualize the null results for the SGLT2 inhibitors, as it indicates that the trial design was capable of detecting effects if present. The convergence of no-effect findings for both SGLT2 inhibitors in the MOBIMEN-5 trial highlights a potential divergence in efficacy across different trial designs and patient populations.

### 3.3 KAVINON-5 Trial (2024)

The KAVINON-5 trial, conducted in 2024, was the most recent and largest of the studies, involving 1040 patients in a randomised, double-blind design. This trial provided a further layer of evidence in the ongoing debate regarding SGLT2 inhibitor efficacy. A key finding of the KAVINON-5 trial was that it contradicted the results of the MOBIMEN-5 trial. This contradiction is significant because it introduces a new point of contention in the evidence base, suggesting that the no-effect findings of MOBIMEN-5 may not be universally applicable. The KAVINON-5 trial, with its larger sample size, adds complexity to the interpretation of the earlier trials, as it challenges the conclusions drawn from the MOBIMEN-5 trial. This ongoing contradiction underscores the difficulty in establishing a clear consensus on the efficacy of SGLT2 inhibitors in early-stage type 2 diabetes.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

The KAVINON-5 trial, with 1040 patients, is larger than the MOBIMEN-5 trial, which had 890 patients. In standard evidence-based medicine, newer and larger trials often carry more weight. The fact that KAVINON-5 contradicted the null findings of MOBIMEN-5 suggests that the earlier trial may have been underpowered or that its findings were not robust. This counter-argument emphasizes the importance of considering the specific context of each trial when interpreting efficacy data.

### 4.2 Rebuttal

While the KAVINON-5 trial is larger and more recent, its contradiction of the MOBIMEN-5 trial does not resolve the inconsistency in the evidence base; rather, it highlights it. The KAVINON-5 trial's contradiction of MOBIMEN-5 introduces a new layer of uncertainty, as it is unclear which trial's findings are more accurate. This ongoing contradiction underscores the difficulty in establishing a clear consensus on the efficacy of SGLT2 inhibitors in early-stage type 2 diabetes. Therefore, the line of trial evidence should be viewed as a whole, with the inconsistency itself being the key finding. Further research is needed to clarify the role of these agents and to resolve the remaining contradictions in the evidence base.
