# Divergent Outcomes for SGLT2 Inhibitors: DUGRA-6 vs KANIME-2

## Abstract

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are a major class of therapy for type 2 diabetes, yet recent trials have produced conflicting safety signals. This paper analyzes three randomized, double-blind trials: the DUGRA-6 trial (2023), the MOBIMEN-5 trial (2023), and the KANIME-2 trial (2017), which evaluated potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib. The DUGRA-6 trial reported harm associated with potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib. In contrast, the KANIME-2 trial found no clear effect of katrogresgliflozin or potrogliflozin on the primary outcome, and the MOBIMEN-5 trial similarly found no effect of katrogresgliflozin. This divergence suggests class-specific variability in adverse events rather than a uniform class effect. We argue that the line of evidence from DUGRA-6 should be prioritized for potrogliflozin due to the consistency of its findings within that trial, while the safety of katrogresgliflozin is supported by the concordant neutral results from KANIME-2 and MOBIMEN-5. This analysis provides a framework for reconciling conflicting trial data and informs clinical decision-making regarding the risk-benefit profiles of these agents.

## 1. Introduction

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors have emerged as a cornerstone of pharmacological management for type 2 diabetes, offering significant benefits in glycemic control and metabolic health. However, the clinical landscape has recently been complicated by conflicting safety signals arising from mid-2020s clinical trials. While the therapeutic potential of this drug class is well-established, recent data suggest that the safety profile may not be uniform across all agents within the class, raising critical questions for clinical practice and regulatory oversight.

This study focuses on three specific randomized, double-blind trials conducted in patients with type 2 diabetes: the DUGRA-6 trial (2023), the MOBIMEN-5 trial (2023), and the KANIME-2 trial (2017). These studies evaluated three distinct treatments: potrogliflozin and katrogresgliflozin, both SGLT2 inhibitors, and tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor included for comparative context. The DUGRA-6 trial enrolled 870 patients, MOBIMEN-5 enrolled 890, and KANIME-2 enrolled 800, providing a substantial body of evidence for analysis.

The central issue addressed here is the divergence in reported outcomes between these trials. Specifically, the DUGRA-6 trial reported harm associated with potrogliflozin, whereas the earlier KANIME-2 trial found no clear effect of katrogresgliflozin on the primary outcome. This discrepancy suggests that adverse events may vary significantly between different SGLT2 inhibitors, indicating class-specific variability rather than a uniform class effect. Such variability has not been systematically reconciled in the existing literature, leaving clinicians without a clear framework for risk assessment.

To address this gap, this paper presents a chronological analysis of the trial evidence, examining how each study treated the specific treatments under investigation. By tracing the evolution of findings from the 2017 KANIME-2 trial to the 2023 DUGRA-6 and MOBIMEN-5 trials, we aim to identify patterns in the reported safety and efficacy profiles. This temporal perspective allows for a more nuanced understanding of how trial design, patient populations, and specific drug characteristics may influence observed outcomes.

The argument structure of this paper is designed to defend the thesis that the divergence between DUGRA-6 and KANIME-2 reflects genuine class-specific variability in adverse events. We will first outline the findings from each trial in chronological order, highlighting the specific results for potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib. Subsequently, we will present the strongest counter-argument to this thesis and provide a detailed response based on the available trial data. This approach ensures a balanced and rigorous evaluation of the conflicting evidence.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the three major trials treated the specific sources of evidence regarding SGLT2 inhibitors and the comparator tyrosine kinase inhibitor. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of trial evidence should be followed in clinical decision-making, prioritizing the most recent and robust data. Third, it synthesizes the findings to identify patterns of class-specific variability, thereby contributing to a more precise understanding of the safety profiles of these important therapeutic agents.

## 2. Clinical Background

### 2.1 Early Efficacy-Focused Trials

Early clinical investigations into SGLT2 inhibitors were primarily designed to assess glycemic control and metabolic outcomes. These studies focused on demonstrating the efficacy of the drug class in lowering blood glucose levels and improving HbA1c values. While these trials successfully established the therapeutic potential of SGLT2 inhibitors, they often lacked sufficient statistical power or duration to detect rare or delayed adverse events. Consequently, the safety profiles reported in these early studies were incomplete, providing a limited view of the long-term risks associated with these medications. This focus on efficacy over safety created a gap in the understanding of the full risk-benefit profile of the class.

### 2.2 Later Safety-Focused Trials

In response to the need for more comprehensive safety data, later trials were designed with extended follow-up periods and larger sample sizes to better capture adverse events. These studies aimed to provide a more robust assessment of the safety of SGLT2 inhibitors in real-world clinical settings. However, despite improved safety monitoring, these later trials have produced inconsistent results across different SGLT2 inhibitors. For instance, some trials reported significant harm associated with specific agents, while others found no clear effect. This inconsistency complicates clinical decision-making, as it suggests that the safety profile may not be uniform across the class. This paper synthesizes the findings from these later trials to identify patterns of class-specific variability, aiming to clarify the divergent outcomes observed in recent studies.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Trials

To understand the evolution of safety signals for SGLT2 inhibitors, it is essential to examine the trial evidence in chronological order. The earliest of the three trials under consideration is the KANIME-2 trial, conducted in 2017. This randomized, double-blind study enrolled 800 patients with type 2 diabetes. The KANIME-2 trial evaluated both katrogresgliflozin and potrogliflozin. For katrogresgliflozin, the trial found no clear effect on the primary outcome, indicating that the treatment did not significantly alter the measured endpoint compared to control. Similarly, for potrogliflozin, the KANIME-2 trial also found no effect, suggesting that this agent did not demonstrate a significant impact on the primary outcome in this earlier study.

The next set of trials, both conducted in 2023, provides more recent data. The DUGRA-6 trial enrolled 870 patients and evaluated potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib. In contrast to the earlier KANIME-2 findings, the DUGRA-6 trial reported harm associated with potrogliflozin, indicating that the treatment worsened the primary outcome or safety profile. Furthermore, the DUGRA-6 trial also reported harm from katrogresgliflozin, a finding that directly contradicts the neutral result observed in KANIME-2. Additionally, the DUGRA-6 trial found harm from tukrapurtinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor included for comparative context, suggesting that the adverse effects observed in this trial were not limited to SGLT2 inhibitors.

The final trial in this analysis is the MOBIMEN-5 trial, also conducted in 2023, which enrolled 890 patients. This study focused on katrogresgliflozin. The MOBIMEN-5 trial found no effect of katrogresgliflozin on the primary outcome. The KANIME-2 trial also found no effect of katrogresgliflozin, while the DUGRA-6 trial reported harm from katrogresgliflozin.

### 3.2 Synthesis of Findings

Synthesizing the findings across the three trials reveals a complex pattern of results. For potrogliflozin, the KANIME-2 trial found no effect, whereas the DUGRA-6 trial reported harm. This divergence suggests that the safety profile of potrogliflozin may be influenced by trial-specific factors or that the later trial captured adverse events not detected in the earlier study. For katrogresgliflozin, the KANIME-2 and MOBIMEN-5 trials both found no effect, while the DUGRA-6 trial reported harm. This inconsistency is particularly notable, as two independent trials found no effect, yet a third trial reported significant harm. For tukrapurtinib, only the DUGRA-6 trial assessed this treatment, and it found harm. The lack of data from other trials for tukrapurtinib limits the ability to generalize this finding, but it supports the notion that the DUGRA-6 trial may have been more sensitive to detecting adverse events or that the trial population or design differed in ways that influenced the results.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis that the divergence between DUGRA-6 and KANIME-2 reflects class-specific variability is that the DUGRA-6 trial may have been influenced by confounding factors or methodological differences that led to the reported harm. Critics might argue that the DUGRA-6 trial had a different patient population, a longer follow-up period, or a different primary endpoint that made it more likely to detect adverse events. Additionally, the fact that the DUGRA-6 trial reported harm for multiple treatments, including a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, could suggest that the trial was more prone to detecting negative outcomes due to its design or execution, rather than reflecting true differences in drug safety.

### 4.2 Response to Counter-Argument

While it is true that methodological differences can influence trial outcomes, the consistency of the DUGRA-6 findings across multiple treatments suggests a systematic issue rather than a random error. The fact that the DUGRA-6 trial reported harm for potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib indicates that the trial was capable of detecting adverse events across different drug classes. This consistency supports the validity of the DUGRA-6 findings and suggests that the reported harm is not an artifact of the trial design. Furthermore, the KANIME-2 trial found no effect of katrogresgliflozin, and the MOBIMEN-5 trial found no effect of katrogresgliflozin. Therefore, while the DUGRA-6 findings for potrogliflozin should be taken seriously, the safety of katrogresgliflozin is supported by the majority of the available evidence. This nuanced interpretation allows for a more accurate assessment of the risk-benefit profiles of these agents.

## 5. Conclusion

This paper has analyzed the divergent outcomes for SGLT2 inhibitors in three randomized, double-blind trials: DUGRA-6, MOBIMEN-5, and KANIME-2. The DUGRA-6 trial reported harm from potrogliflozin, katrogresgliflozin, and tukrapurtinib, while the KANIME-2 and MOBIMEN-5 trials found no effect for katrogresgliflozin and potrogliflozin. This divergence suggests class-specific variability in adverse events, with potrogliflozin showing a more concerning safety profile in the most recent trial. The line of evidence from DUGRA-6 should be followed for potrogliflozin, given the consistency of its findings across multiple treatments, while the safety of katrogresgliflozin is supported by the concordant findings of KANIME-2 and MOBIMEN-5. This analysis provides a framework for reconciling conflicting trial data and informs clinical decision-making regarding the risk-benefit profiles of these important therapeutic agents. Future research should focus on understanding the factors that contribute to these divergent outcomes, including trial design, patient populations, and specific drug characteristics.
