# Early vs Late Phase Efficacy: KRORIVEL-6 and LEGES-9 Trials

## Abstract

The management of type 2 diabetes relies on the consistent efficacy of pharmacological interventions, particularly sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors. A central question in clinical pharmacology is whether the observed benefits of specific agents are robust across trials of different sizes and conducted in different years. This paper examines the consistency of SGLT2 inhibitor benefits by analyzing three randomized, double-blind trials: the DROME-5 trial (2014), the LEGES-9 trial (2016), and the KRORIVEL-6 trial (2021). These studies evaluated the SGLT2 inhibitors katrogresgliflozin and potrogliflozin, as well as the tyrosine kinase inhibitor tukrapurtinib. The analysis reveals that the KRORIVEL-6 trial reported a benefit of katrogresgliflozin, and the LEGES-9 trial reported a benefit of katrogresgliflozin. The DROME-5 trial provided early evidence for potrogliflozin. The consistent positive outcome for katrogresgliflozin across the mid-sized and large-scale trials supports the robustness of its efficacy in type 2 diabetes management. Although variations in trial scale and year could suggest potential variability, the uniformity of the positive findings for katrogresgliflozin mitigates these concerns. This paper argues that the evidence from LEGES-9 and KRORIVEL-6 provides a strong basis for confidence in the therapeutic reliability of katrogresgliflozin, offering a clear account of how these trials treated the relevant sources and defending the thesis of consistent efficacy.

## 1. Introduction

The central thesis of this study is that the KRORIVEL-6 trial and the LEGES-9 trial each demonstrated a benefit of katrogresgliflozin, supporting the robustness of its efficacy in type 2 diabetes management. The contributions of this study include a clear account of how the trials treated the relevant sources and an argued view on which line of trial evidence should be followed. By synthesizing the findings from DROME-5, LEGES-9, and KRORIVEL-6, this paper provides a focused perspective on the consistency of SGLT2 inhibitor benefits, specifically highlighting the robust efficacy of katrogresgliflozin across different clinical trial contexts.

## 2. Clinical Background

Initial randomized, double-blind trials of SGLT2 inhibitors in type 2 diabetes laid the groundwork for understanding the class's potential. Mid-sized randomized, double-blind trials assessing multiple SGLT2 inhibitors followed the early phase studies. However, its smaller sample size and earlier year limit the generalizability of its findings to larger, more recent populations. The results from LEGES-9 provide a crucial mid-point in the timeline of evidence, offering a bridge between the initial trials and the more recent, larger studies.

### 2.3 Recent Phase Evidence: KRORIVEL-6 (2021)

Large-scale randomized, double-blind trials assessing multiple drug classes represent the most recent phase of evidence. The KRORIVEL-6 trial, conducted in 2021 with 900 patients, is the most recent and largest trial in this series. It assessed katrogresgliflozin, potrogliflozin, and tukrapurtinib, providing a comprehensive view of the efficacy of these agents. However, its recency and size may introduce confounding factors related to evolving clinical standards. This study uses KRORIVEL-6 as the primary comparator to LEGES-9 to defend the thesis of robust efficacy for katrogresgliflozin across different trial contexts, leveraging the scale and recency of the data to strengthen the argument for consistency.

## 3. Analysis of the Trial Evidence

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Trial Findings

To evaluate the consistency of SGLT2 inhibitor benefits, it is necessary to examine the specific outcomes reported by each trial in chronological order. The earliest data point comes from the DROME-5 trial, conducted in 2014. This study focused on the SGLT2 inhibitor potrogliflozin and reported that the treatment improved the main outcome for patients with type 2 diabetes. This finding established the initial efficacy signal for potrogliflozin, demonstrating that the drug class could yield positive results in a randomized, double-blind setting.

The next phase of evidence is provided by the LEGES-9 trial, conducted in 2016 with 500 patients. This study expanded the scope of the investigation to include both katrogresgliflozin and potrogliflozin. The results indicated that katrogresgliflozin was associated with an improvement in the primary outcome, marking the first reported benefit for this agent in the series of trials. Additionally, LEGES-9 confirmed the earlier findings for potrogliflozin, reporting that this agent also improved the main outcome. The concurrent positive results for both SGLT2 inhibitors in LEGES-9 suggest that the efficacy signals observed in the earlier DROME-5 trial were not isolated to a single agent but were representative of the broader class, at least within the context of this mid-sized trial.

The most recent and largest trial in this analysis is the KRORIVEL-6 trial, conducted in 2021 with 900 patients. This study evaluated three agents: katrogresgliflozin, potrogliflozin, and the tyrosine kinase inhibitor tukrapurtinib. The findings from KRORIVEL-6 reported a benefit of katrogresgliflozin, indicating that the treatment improved the main outcome in this larger, more recent population. Furthermore, the trial found a benefit of potrogliflozin, consistent with the results from both DROME-5 and LEGES-9. In addition to the SGLT2 inhibitors, KRORIVEL-6 reported a benefit of tukrapurtinib, providing evidence for the efficacy of this tyrosine kinase inhibitor in the management of type 2 diabetes. The inclusion of tukrapurtinib in this trial broadens the scope of the evidence base, although the primary focus of this analysis remains on the consistency of the SGLT2 inhibitor findings.

### 3.2 Consistency of Katrogresgliflozin Efficacy

The central question of this analysis is whether the efficacy of katrogresgliflozin is robust across different trial contexts. The evidence from LEGES-9 and KRORIVEL-6 provides a strong basis for affirming this robustness. Both trials reported a benefit of katrogresgliflozin, with the treatment improving the main outcome in both instances. This consistency is notable because the two trials differ significantly in scale and timing. LEGES-9 was a mid-sized trial conducted in 2016 with 500 patients, while KRORIVEL-6 was a large-scale trial conducted in 2021 with 900 patients. The fact that the positive outcome for katrogresgliflozin was observed in both a smaller, earlier trial and a larger, more recent trial suggests that the efficacy signal is not an artifact of a specific trial design or temporal context.

This pattern of consistent benefit for katrogresgliflozin contrasts with the evidence for other agents in the series. Potrogliflozin, for instance, showed a benefit in all three trials: DROME-5, LEGES-9, and KRORIVEL-6. This uniformity across the entire timeline further supports the general reliability of SGLT2 inhibitors in this context. Tukrapurtinib, on the other hand, was only assessed in the most recent trial, KRORIVEL-6, where it also showed a benefit. However, because tukrapurtinib was not evaluated in the earlier trials, its consistency over time cannot be assessed in the same way as katrogresgliflozin or potrogliflozin. The ability to track katrogresgliflozin across two distinct trial phases, with consistent positive results, provides a clearer picture of its long-term efficacy profile.

The robustness of katrogresgliflozin’s efficacy is further supported by the fact that the positive findings were not isolated to a single trial but were replicated in a different context. The transition from the 2016 LEGES-9 trial to the 2021 KRORIVEL-6 trial represents a significant shift in both sample size and clinical environment. Despite these changes, the outcome for katrogresgliflozin remained positive. This stability across diverse trial conditions strengthens the argument that the observed benefits are genuine and reproducible, rather than being influenced by transient or context-specific factors.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Variability in Trial Size and Year

A potential counter-argument to the thesis of robust efficacy for katrogresgliflozin is that the differing trial sizes and years could introduce variability that undermines the consistency of the findings. Specifically, the LEGES-9 trial was conducted in 2016 with 500 patients, while the KRORIVEL-6 trial was conducted in 2021 with 900 patients. Critics might argue that the larger sample size of KRORIVEL-6 provides a more precise estimate of efficacy, and that the smaller sample size of LEGES-9 may be subject to greater random error. Additionally, the five-year gap between the two trials could reflect changes in clinical standards, patient populations, or background therapies, which might influence the observed outcomes. If the benefit of katrogresgliflozin were sensitive to these factors, one might expect to see a difference in the magnitude or direction of the effect between the two trials.

However, the available evidence does not support this concern. Both LEGES-9 and KRORIVEL-6 reported a benefit of katrogresgliflozin, with the treatment improving the main outcome in both cases. The consistency of this positive finding across two trials that differ significantly in scale and timing suggests that the efficacy of katrogresgliflozin is not dependent on a specific trial size or year. If the benefit were an artifact of the smaller sample size in LEGES-9, one might expect a different result in the larger KRORIVEL-6 trial. Conversely, if the benefit were influenced by the evolving clinical standards of 2021, one might expect a different result in the earlier LEGES-9 trial. The fact that the outcome was positive in both trials indicates that the efficacy signal is stable and robust.

Furthermore, the consistency of the findings for katrogresgliflozin is supported by the broader pattern of results for SGLT2 inhibitors in this series. Potrogliflozin, for example, showed a benefit in all three trials, including the earliest DROME-5 trial. This uniformity across the entire timeline suggests that the positive outcomes for SGLT2 inhibitors are not isolated incidents but are part of a consistent pattern of efficacy. The fact that katrogresgliflozin fits within this pattern, with positive results in both LEGES-9 and KRORIVEL-6, reinforces the argument for its robustness. While the differences in trial size and year are important considerations, they do not appear to have altered the fundamental finding that katrogresgliflozin provides a benefit in the management of type 2 diabetes.
