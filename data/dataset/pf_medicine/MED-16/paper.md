# The 2024 GOGRE-3 Trial: A Re-evaluation of Tukrapurtinib and Drebrabestinib Efficacy

## Abstract

Discrepancies in recent tyrosine kinase inhibitor trials for type 2 diabetes have created uncertainty regarding the clinical utility of agents such as tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib. This paper analyzes the temporal evolution of trial evidence, focusing on the RATHIRA-6 trial (2015), the KATHA-5 trial (2023), the GOGRE-3 trial (2024), and the NESENAM-7 trial (2025). The RATHIRA-6 trial reported no clear effect of tukrapurtinib or drebrabestinib on the primary outcome, suggesting a lack of therapeutic potency. In contrast, the GOGRE-3 trial confirmed the benefit of tukrapurtinib, drebrabestinib, and potrogliflozin, directly challenging the earlier null findings. Furthermore, the NESENAM-7 trial contradicted the results of the KATHA-5 trial, adding another layer of complexity to the evidentiary landscape. This analysis argues that the GOGRE-3 trial represents the current standard of evidence for the efficacy of tyrosine kinase inhibitors in type 2 diabetes. The larger sample size and more refined patient selection in the GOGRE-3 trial provide a more robust assessment of therapeutic benefit, superseding the earlier null findings. By prioritizing recent, high-quality data, clinicians can make more informed decisions regarding the use of these agents in the management of type 2 diabetes.

## 1. Introduction

Tyrosine kinase inhibitors have emerged as a promising class of therapeutic agents for the management of type 2 diabetes, yet the clinical evidence supporting their efficacy remains fragmented. While the underlying biological rationale for targeting tyrosine kinase pathways in metabolic regulation is well-established, recent trial data present a complex and sometimes conflicting picture of their clinical utility. This inconsistency poses a significant challenge for clinicians and researchers seeking to establish a definitive standard of care, as it raises questions about the reliability of earlier findings in the face of newer, more robust data.

A central point of contention in this field is the temporal discrepancy observed in the efficacy data for specific agents. The RATHIRA-6 trial, conducted in 2015, reported no clear effect of tukrapurtinib on the primary outcome in patients with type 2 diabetes. This null result suggested that the drug might lack sufficient therapeutic potency or that the trial design was insufficient to detect a meaningful benefit. However, more recent investigations have challenged this early conclusion, introducing a layer of uncertainty regarding which line of evidence should be prioritized in clinical decision-making.

The GOGRE-3 trial, published in 2024, represents a significant shift in the evidentiary landscape. This large-scale, randomized, double-blind study confirmed the benefit of tukrapurtinib, directly contradicting the earlier findings of the RATHIRA-6 trial. Furthermore, the GOGRE-3 trial also demonstrated the efficacy of drebrabestinib, another tyrosine kinase inhibitor, providing a broader perspective on the potential of this drug class. The contrast between the null result from 2015 and the positive result from 2024 highlights a temporal evolution in the understanding of these therapies, suggesting that earlier trials may have been limited by factors such as sample size or patient selection criteria.

This paper argues that the GOGRE-3 trial represents the current standard of evidence for the efficacy of tyrosine kinase inhibitors in type 2 diabetes. By analyzing the methodological differences between the early and recent trials, we aim to resolve the apparent contradiction in the data. We propose that the larger sample size and more refined patient selection in the GOGRE-3 trial provide a more robust and reliable assessment of therapeutic benefit, thereby superseding the earlier null findings.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we provide a comprehensive analysis of the trial evidence, tracing the evolution of findings from the RATHIRA-6 trial to the GOGRE-3 trial. Second, we offer a reasoned argument for why the GOGRE-3 trial should be considered the definitive source of evidence for the efficacy of tukrapurtinib and drebrabestinib. Third, we discuss the implications of this temporal shift for clinical practice, emphasizing the need to prioritize recent, high-quality data when evaluating the therapeutic potential of tyrosine kinase inhibitors in type 2 diabetes.
