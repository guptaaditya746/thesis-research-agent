# Conflicting Efficacy Signals for Third-Generation EGFR TKIs: LEGEGRUM-3 vs. GRENAM-7

## Abstract

This brief report synthesizes data from three randomized clinical trials evaluating epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with EGFR mutations. The trials included are the THAFO-3 trial (2018), the LEGEGRUM-3 trial (2020), and the GRENAM-7 trial (2022). It is important to note that the drug names (krethebrutinib, nukokrontinib, gredrertinib) and trial names are invented for the purpose of this synthetic analysis, and the data presented are not derived from real-world clinical trials. The primary objective is to examine the discrepancy between the reported benefit of the third-generation TKI krethebrutinib in the LEGEGRUM-3 trial and the lack of effect for the second-generation TKI nukokrontinib in the GRENAM-7 trial. The analysis presents the hazard ratios for the primary outcome across the available studies. The reported hazard ratio for krethebrutinib in the LEGEGRUM-3 trial was 0.78 (95% CI 0.68–0.89), while in the GRENAM-7 trial it was 0.96 (95% CI 0.77–1.19). A descriptive pooled estimate for krethebrutinib, derived from these two trials, is 0.83 (95% CI 0.74–0.93), with a heterogeneity statistic of 60.5%. These results suggest that the efficacy of EGFR TKIs is highly dependent on the specific agent and trial context, and that generalizing benefits across different generations of TKIs or trial designs may be misleading.

## Background

The management of advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations has evolved rapidly with the introduction of multiple generations of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs). While first- and second-generation agents established the foundation for targeted therapy, the development of third-generation TKIs aimed to address resistance mechanisms, particularly the T790M mutation. Recent clinical data have introduced conflicting signals regarding the efficacy of these advanced agents, creating uncertainty in clinical decision-making. This report uses a set of invented trials and drugs to explore how such conflicting signals might be interpreted in a synthetic context.

A notable discrepancy has emerged between two recent trials in this synthetic dataset. The LEGEGRUM-3 trial, conducted in 2020, reported a benefit for the third-generation TKI krethebrutinib when compared against a first-generation agent. In contrast, the subsequent GRENAM-7 trial, conducted in 2022, reported no effect for the second-generation TKI nukokrontinib. This divergence in outcomes has led to a discrepancy in the perceived value of advanced EGFR TKIs, leaving clinicians and researchers to question whether the efficacy of these drugs is consistent across different trial contexts or highly dependent on the specific agent and study design.

The LEGEGRUM-3 trial found a benefit of krethebrutinib, whereas the GRENAM-7 trial found no effect of nukokrontinib, indicating that the efficacy of EGFR TKIs is highly dependent on the specific agent and trial context. This observation suggests that generalizing the benefits of one generation of TKIs to another, or across different trial settings, may be misleading. The variability in results highlights the need for a systematic evaluation of the available evidence to clarify the true impact of these therapies.

To address this gap, this study presents a brief synthesis of the trials. By synthesizing the data from the LEGEGRUM-3, GRENAM-7, and THAFO-3 trials, the aim is to provide a clearer picture of the efficacy of EGFR TKIs in advanced NSCLC. This analysis focuses on the primary endpoints reported in these studies, allowing for a direct comparison of the effects of different TKI generations.

The primary objective of this study is to answer the question: The LEGEGRUM-3 trial (2020) reported a benefit for the third-generation TKI krethebrutinib, while the subsequent GRENAM-7 trial (2022) reported no effect for the second-generation TKI nukokrontinib, creating a discrepancy in the perceived value of advanced EGFR TKIs. By presenting the available data, the study seeks to determine whether the observed differences are due to random variation or reflect genuine differences in drug efficacy.

The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) a systematic review and synthesis of the efficacy of EGFR TKIs in advanced NSCLC; (2) a detailed comparison of the results from the LEGEGRUM-3 and GRENAM-7 trials; and (3) a discussion of the implications of these findings for clinical practice and future research.

## Methods

This study is a brief synthesis report that synthesizes data from three randomized clinical trials evaluating EGFR TKIs in advanced NSCLC with EGFR mutations. The trials included are the THAFO-3 trial, the LEGEGRUM-3 trial, and the GRENAM-7 trial. Each trial was a randomized study with a specific design and comparator arm, as detailed in the trial descriptions. The THAFO-3 trial was a randomised, double-blind, phase III trial conducted in 2018, comparing a third-generation EGFR TKI against standard platinum-based chemotherapy. The LEGEGRUM-3 trial was a randomised, double-blind, phase III trial conducted in 2020, comparing a third-generation EGFR TKI against a first-generation EGFR TKI. The GRENAM-7 trial was a randomised, open-label, phase II trial conducted in 2022, comparing a second-generation EGFR TKI against a third-generation EGFR TKI.

The primary endpoint for the THAFO-3 and LEGEGRUM-3 trials was progression-free survival, while the primary endpoint for the GRENAM-7 trial was objective response rate. The treatments evaluated include krethebrutinib, a third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor designed to target both sensitizing and T790M resistance mutations; gredrertinib, a first-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor that inhibits the kinase domain of the epidermal growth factor receptor; and nukokrontinib, a second-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor with irreversible binding to the EGFR kinase domain.

The analysis focuses on the hazard ratios for the primary outcome reported in each trial. A descriptive pooled estimate was calculated for krethebrutinib, which was evaluated in two of the three trials. The heterogeneity of the pooled estimates was assessed using the I² statistic. No new experiments were conducted; all data were derived from the reported results of the three trials. It is important to note that the drug names and trial names are invented for the purpose of this synthetic analysis, and the data presented are not derived from real-world clinical trials.

## Interpretation

The results of this synthesis report clarify the discrepancy between the LEGEGRUM-3 and GRENAM-7 trials. The LEGEGRUM-3 trial found a benefit of krethebrutinib, whereas the GRENAM-7 trial found no effect of nukokrontinib, indicating that the efficacy of EGFR TKIs is highly dependent on the specific agent and trial context. The hazard ratio for krethebrutinib in the LEGEGRUM-3 trial was 0.78, which suggests a reduction in the risk of the primary outcome. In contrast, the hazard ratio for nukokrontinib in the GRENAM-7 trial was 1.03, which suggests no difference in the risk of the primary outcome. The hazard ratio for krethebrutinib in the GRENAM-7 trial was 0.96, which suggests no clear effect. This pattern indicates that the benefit of krethebrutinib observed in the LEGEGRUM-3 trial was not replicated in the GRENAM-7 trial, where the confidence interval for the hazard ratio included the null value.

The descriptive pooled hazard ratio for krethebrutinib was 0.83 (95% CI 0.74–0.93), which suggests a modest benefit. However, the heterogeneity I² of 60.5% indicates substantial variability between the two trials, which limits the strength of the pooled estimate. The GRENAM-7 trial contradicted the LEGEGRUM-3 trial by reporting no effect for krethebrutinib, whereas the LEGEGRUM-3 trial reported a benefit. The GRENAM-7 trial also contradicted the THAFO-3 trial, which had reported a benefit for a third-generation EGFR TKI. These contradictions suggest that the efficacy of EGFR TKIs is not consistent across different trial contexts, and that factors such as trial design, comparator arm, and patient population may influence the observed outcomes.

The findings also indicate that the efficacy of EGFR TKIs is highly dependent on the specific agent. The LEGEGRUM-3 trial found a benefit of gredrertinib, a first-generation TKI, with a hazard ratio of 0.67 (95% CI 0.55–0.81). This suggests that even first-generation TKIs can show significant benefits in certain trial contexts. The GRENAM-7 trial found no effect of nukokrontinib, a second-generation TKI, with a hazard ratio of 1.03 (95% CI 0.79–1.35). This suggests that the second-generation TKI did not show a clear benefit in this trial context. The variability in results across different generations of TKIs and trial contexts highlights the need for careful interpretation of individual trial data rather than broad categorical assumptions about the efficacy of advanced EGFR TKIs.

This brief synthesis report synthesizes data from three randomized clinical trials evaluating EGFR TKIs in advanced NSCLC with EGFR mutations.
