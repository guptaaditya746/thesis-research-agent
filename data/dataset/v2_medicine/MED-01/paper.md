# Divergent Efficacy of FGF23-Targeted Therapies in Secondary Hyperparathyroidism: A Comparative Analysis of Early Trials

## Abstract

Secondary hyperparathyroidism in chronic kidney disease is managed with agents targeting mineral metabolism, including FGF23 pathways. This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes data from three randomised trials to resolve conflicting results regarding the efficacy of FGF23-targeted therapies. We included the DREBRABES-4, NAZOTO-9, and LODON-3 trials, which evaluated potrocet, a monoclonal antibody, and katrogrestinib, a receptor inhibitor. The DREBRABES-4 trial reported a benefit of potrocet on phosphate levels, whereas the NAZOTO-9 trial found no effect of katrogrestinib on vascular calcification. We pooled hazard ratios for the primary outcomes of these trials using a random-effects model. The pooled hazard ratio for potrocet was 0.83 (95% CI 0.74 to 0.94), with high heterogeneity (I² 86.6%). The pooled hazard ratio for katrogrestinib was 0.88 (95% CI 0.77 to 0.99), with moderate heterogeneity (I² 31.2%). While both treatments show trends toward benefit, the high heterogeneity for potrocet and the specific trial contexts limit definitive conclusions on mechanism-specific efficacy. The conflicting results suggest that the mechanism of FGF23 inhibition and the specific clinical outcome measured are critical factors in determining efficacy.

## Introduction

Secondary hyperparathyroidism is a common complication of chronic kidney disease (CKD), characterized by the overactivity of the parathyroid glands in response to disturbances in mineral metabolism. This condition contributes significantly to cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in the CKD population. Recent therapeutic strategies have focused on targeting the fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) pathway, a key regulator of phosphate homeostasis. Agents designed to inhibit FGF23 signaling aim to correct these metabolic abnormalities, potentially offering a new avenue for managing secondary hyperparathyroidism and its associated complications.

Despite the theoretical promise of FGF23-targeted therapies, the clinical evidence remains inconsistent. The DREBRABES-4 trial, conducted in 2014, evaluated potrocet, a monoclonal antibody that neutralizes FGF23. This study reported a benefit of potrocet in reducing serum phosphate levels in dialysis patients with secondary hyperparathyroidism. In contrast, the NAZOTO-9 trial, published in 2018, investigated katrogrestinib, a small molecule inhibitor of the FGF23 receptor. This trial found no effect of katrogrestinib on vascular calcification scores in non-dialysis CKD patients. This discrepancy creates a conflict in the evidence base, leaving clinicians uncertain about the efficacy of FGF23-targeted interventions.

A critical distinction between these trials lies in the mechanism of action of the agents tested. Potrocet functions as a monoclonal antibody that binds and neutralizes circulating FGF23, whereas katrogrestinib acts as a receptor inhibitor, blocking the downstream signaling of the FGF23 receptor. These mechanistic differences may influence the clinical outcomes observed, as neutralizing the ligand and inhibiting the receptor could have distinct effects on mineral metabolism and vascular health. Furthermore, the trials differed in their primary endpoints, with DREBRABES-4 focusing on surrogate markers like phosphate levels, while NAZOTO-9 targeted a hard clinical outcome related to vascular calcification.

To resolve this discrepancy, it is necessary to synthesize data from multiple trials that have evaluated FGF23-targeted therapies. This study aims to conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised trials, specifically including DREBRABES-4, NAZOTO-9, and the more recent LODON-3 trial. By pooling data from these studies, we can assess whether the observed differences in efficacy are attributable to the specific mechanism of FGF23 inhibition or to other trial design factors. This analysis seeks to clarify the role of FGF23-targeted agents in the management of secondary hyperparathyroidism.

The primary objective of this study is to synthesize data from DREBRABES-4, NAZOTO-9, and LODON-3 to resolve the discrepancy between the reported benefit of potrocet and the lack of effect of katrogrestinib. By comparing the outcomes of these trials, we aim to determine if different mechanisms of FGF23 inhibition yield disparate clinical outcomes in CKD patients. This comparative analysis will provide a clearer understanding of the potential utility of these therapies in clinical practice.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we provide a comprehensive synthesis of the available evidence on FGF23-targeted therapies in secondary hyperparathyroidism. Second, we compare the efficacy of monoclonal antibody and receptor inhibitor approaches, highlighting the impact of mechanism on clinical outcomes. Third, we offer a resolution to the conflicting results between the DREBRABES-4 and NAZOTO-9 trials, guiding future research and clinical decision-making.

## Methods

### Eligibility criteria

We defined inclusion criteria as randomised trials of FGF23-targeted agents in secondary hyperparathyroidism in chronic kidney disease. We identified three trials that met these criteria for this specific comparative analysis: the DREBRABES-4 trial, the NAZOTO-9 trial, and the LODON-3 trial. The DREBRABES-4 trial was a randomised, double-blind, active-controlled study conducted in 2014. It enrolled patients with secondary hyperparathyroidism on dialysis and randomised them to potrocet or a standard calcimimetic. The primary outcome was change in serum phosphate levels at 24 weeks. The NAZOTO-9 trial was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study conducted in 2018. It enrolled non-dialysis CKD patients with elevated FGF23 and randomised them to katrogrestinib or placebo. The primary outcome was reduction in vascular calcification score by imaging. The LODON-3 trial was a randomised, open-label, active-controlled study conducted in 2022. It enrolled dialysis patients with high cardiovascular risk and randomised them to potrocet or calcimimetic. The primary outcome was a composite of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure.

### Study selection and data extraction

We extracted trial characteristics including the number of patients randomised, follow-up duration, and mean age. We also extracted effect sizes, specifically hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals for the primary outcome. Data were extracted directly from the trial reports. For each trial, we recorded the specific treatment assigned and the corresponding result regarding the primary outcome. The DREBRABES-4 trial reported a benefit of potrocet and a benefit of katrogrestinib. The NAZOTO-9 trial reported no effect of katrogrestinib and no effect of potrocet. The LODON-3 trial reported no effect of katrogrestinib. Additionally, the NAZOTO-9 trial contradicted the DREBRABES-4 trial regarding the efficacy of FGF23-targeted agents.

### Synthesis

We performed a meta-analysis to pool hazard ratios for each treatment. Given the observed heterogeneity between trials, we used a random-effects model to account for the variability in effect sizes. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic. We calculated pooled values for potrocet, which was evaluated in two trials, and katrogrestinib, which was evaluated in three trials. The pooling process combined the individual trial estimates to provide an overall measure of effect for each agent. This approach allows for a summary estimate of the treatment effect while acknowledging the differences in trial designs and endpoints.

## Discussion

The pooled results indicate that both potrocet and katrogrestinib show a trend toward benefit in secondary hyperparathyroidism. For katrogrestinib, the pooled hazard ratio of 0.88 (95% CI 0.77 to 0.99) suggests a statistically significant effect, as the confidence interval does not include the null value. However, the moderate heterogeneity (I² 31.2%) indicates some variability in the effect sizes across the three trials. For potrocet, the pooled hazard ratio was 0.83 (95% CI 0.74 to 0.94), but the high heterogeneity (I² 86.6%) limits the reliability of this estimate. This high heterogeneity is driven by the conflicting results between the DREBRABES-4 trial, which reported a benefit, and the NAZOTO-9 trial, which reported no effect.

A key factor in these divergent outcomes may be the difference in mechanisms of action. Potrocet is a monoclonal antibody that neutralizes FGF23, while katrogrestinib is a receptor inhibitor. These distinct mechanisms may influence the clinical efficacy of the agents. Additionally, the primary outcomes differed between the trials. DREBRABES-4 focused on phosphate levels, a surrogate marker, whereas NAZOTO-9 focused on vascular calcification, a hard clinical outcome. The LODON-3 trial used a composite cardiovascular endpoint. These differences in endpoints make direct comparison difficult and may contribute to the observed heterogeneity. The pooling of hazard ratios across these different endpoints provides a summary measure of the reported effects, but it does not account for the biological differences between the outcomes.

The conflicting results suggest that the mechanism of FGF23 inhibition and the specific clinical outcome measured are critical factors in determining efficacy. The lack of a consistent effect across trials for both agents indicates that the clinical utility of FGF23-targeted therapies may depend on the specific patient population and the endpoint being assessed. The random-effects model used in this analysis accounts for the variability in effect sizes, but the small number of trials limits the power to detect true differences between the agents.

### Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the number of trials included is small, with only three trials meeting the eligibility criteria. This limits the generalizability of the findings. Second, the high heterogeneity in the potrocet pool limits the reliability of the pooled estimate. Third, the trials differed in their populations, with some including dialysis patients and others including non-dialysis patients. The comparators also varied, with some trials using a calcimimetic and others using a placebo. Finally, the primary outcomes differed between trials, making direct comparison difficult. These factors must be considered when interpreting the results of this meta-analysis. The use of a random-effects model helps to account for the heterogeneity, but it does not resolve the underlying differences in trial design and endpoints.

## Conclusions

The evidence for FGF23-targeted therapies in secondary hyperparathyroidism is conflicting. Potrocet showed a benefit in the DREBRABES-4 trial but no effect in the NAZOTO-9 trial, leading to high heterogeneity in the pooled estimate. Katrogrestinib showed a benefit in the DREBRABES-4 trial but no significant effect in the NAZOTO-9 and LODON-3 trials. The pooled analysis suggests a significant benefit for katrogrestinib, but the moderate heterogeneity and differences in endpoints warrant caution. These findings suggest that different mechanisms of FGF23 inhibition may yield disparate clinical outcomes. Further trials with consistent endpoints and comparable populations are needed to clarify the role of these therapies in clinical practice.
