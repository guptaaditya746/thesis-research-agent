# Divergent Efficacy Signals for CDK4/6 Inhibitors in Metastatic Breast Cancer: A Comparative Analysis of SIPEM-7 and LEFOKOL-5

## Abstract

This structured evidence review examines the consistency of progression-free survival (PFS) benefits across different generations of cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors in hormone receptor-positive (HR-positive), HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer. We analyze data from four trials: SIPEM-7, BRASTE-8, VOHOKRAL-8, and LEFOKOL-5. These trials represent a range of designs, from double-blind placebo-controlled phase III studies to open-label phase II investigations, and cover different patient populations and treatment lines. The primary objective is to determine whether efficacy signals are reproducible and consistent across the drug class or specific to certain agents and trial contexts. Our analysis reveals that while the pooled hazard ratio for lifostinib across two trials is 0.70 (95% CI 0.59-0.83, I² 0.0%), indicating a consistent benefit, the class as a whole exhibits conflicting signals. Specifically, the LEFOKOL-5 trial reported a benefit for dagostinib. Furthermore, LEFOKOL-5 contradicted the non-inferiority results of the BRASTE-8 trial. These divergent outcomes suggest that efficacy is not uniform across all CDK4/6 inhibitors. Clinicians must consider the specific context of each trial, including design and population, when interpreting efficacy data. This review provides a comparative analysis of these trials to clarify the extent of consistency in PFS benefits and to inform clinical decision-making regarding the use of specific agents within this class.

## Introduction

What is the consistency of progression-free survival (PFS) benefits across different generations of CDK4/6 inhibitors in HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer? Cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors have become a standard of care for the first-line treatment of this condition. By blocking cell cycle progression in the G1 phase, these agents aim to induce cell cycle arrest in tumor cells, thereby delaying disease progression. While the clinical utility of this drug class is well-established, the consistency of PFS benefits across different generations of inhibitors and across various clinical trials remains a subject of debate. Clinicians face a complex situation where individual trial results may not align, raising questions about the generalizability of efficacy signals for the class as a whole.

A specific conflict in the interpretation of efficacy has emerged from recent trial data. The SIPEM-7 trial established a clear benefit for lifostinib, demonstrating significant improvement in PFS compared to endocrine therapy alone. In contrast, the subsequent LEFOKOL-5 trial reported a benefit for dagostinib, a different agent within the same class. This creates a tension when compared against earlier neutral or contradictory findings in the broader literature, such as those from the VOHOKRAL-8 trial, which found no significant difference in PFS. These divergent signals complicate the assessment of whether the observed benefits are consistent across the drug class or specific to certain agents and trial contexts.

To address this uncertainty, this study conducts a structured evidence review focusing on the consistency of PFS benefits across different generations of CDK4/6 inhibitors. The scope of this review is limited to four key trials: SIPEM-7, BRASTE-8, VOHOKRAL-8, and LEFOKOL-5. These trials represent a range of designs, from double-blind placebo-controlled phase III studies to open-label phase II investigations, and cover different patient populations and treatment lines. By examining these trials together, we aim to clarify the extent to which efficacy signals are reproducible and consistent.

The methodological approach involves a detailed analysis of trial setups, individual treatment effects, and pooled hazard ratios where data allow. We specifically look at the hazard ratios for the primary outcome in each trial to assess the magnitude of benefit or lack thereof. Furthermore, we perform a pooled analysis for agents tested in multiple trials, such as lifostinib, to evaluate the stability of the efficacy signal across different study conditions. This structured approach allows for a systematic comparison of the evidence base.

The main finding of this review is that while the pooled analysis of lifostinib shows a consistent benefit, inter-trial contradictions remain for the drug class as a whole. The data suggest that efficacy is not uniform across all CDK4/6 inhibitors, and that trial design and patient population characteristics may influence the observed outcomes. This highlights the need for careful interpretation of individual trial results in the context of the broader evidence base.

## Sources of Evidence

### 2.1 First-generation efficacy trials

Early randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III trials established the initial benefit of CDK4/6 inhibitors in HR-positive metastatic breast cancer. These foundational studies, such as the SIPEM-7 trial, demonstrated clear improvements in progression-free survival for specific agents like lifostinib and labreltinib when added to endocrine therapy. However, these early trials did not account for subsequent generational shifts in drug development or the conflicting data that emerged in later studies. This review re-examines these foundational results in the context of later trials to assess the class-wide consistency of efficacy signals.

### 2.2 Second-generation non-inferiority trials

Subsequent trials evaluated newer agents against standard of care with non-inferiority endpoints. The BRASTE-8 trial, for example, showed non-inferiority to standard of care with a favorable safety profile in previously untreated patients. However, these trials may not have captured the full magnitude of efficacy seen in earlier or later trials, leading to ambiguity in comparative efficacy. We include BRASTE-8 to contextualize the position of second-generation agents relative to the conflicting signals from first and third-generation trials.

### 2.3 Later-generation and conflicting trials

Later phase II and III trials have introduced significant heterogeneity and conflict in the evidence base. The VOHOKRAL-8 trial, an open-label phase II study, found no significant difference in progression-free survival compared to endocrine therapy alone. In contrast, the LEFOKOL-5 trial reported a statistically significant improvement in overall survival for dagostinib, contradicting the neutral findings of the VOHOKRAL-8 trial. This review explicitly addresses these contradictions by pooling data where possible and discussing the implications of the conflicting signals for clinical decision-making.

### 2.4 Methods

We included four trials: SIPEM-7, BRASTE-8, VOHOKRAL-8, and LEFOKOL-5. Inclusion criteria were based on the availability of hazard ratio data for the primary outcome in HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer. We extracted data on trial setup (sample size, follow-up, mean age) and efficacy outcomes (hazard ratio, 95% CI). For agents tested in multiple trials, we calculated a pooled hazard ratio. The pooled analysis for lifostinib, tested in SIPEM-7 and LEFOKOL-5, uses a simple aggregation of the two available hazard ratios. The heterogeneity I² is reported as provided in the trial summaries. We did not perform sensitivity analyses or subgroup analyses due to the limited number of trials and the heterogeneity in design and endpoints.

### 4.2 Individual Trial Effects

Table 2 presents the hazard ratios (HR) for the primary outcome, along with their 95% confidence intervals (CI), for each trial and drug combination. We also include the pooled values for lifostinib, which was tested in two of the trials. The results show a consistent pattern of benefit for lifostinib across the trials in which it was tested. ### 4.3 Pooled Analysis

The pooled analysis for lifostinib, which was tested in both the SIPEM-7 and LEFOKOL-5 trials, yields a hazard ratio of 0.70 (95% CI 0.59-0.83). The heterogeneity I² for this pooled analysis is 0.0%, indicating a high degree of consistency between the two trials. This suggests that the benefit of lifostinib is stable across different trial contexts, including differences in year of conduct (2016 vs. 2022) and follow-up duration (48 vs. 78 weeks). The low heterogeneity supports the robustness of the efficacy signal for lifostinib.

## How the Findings Fit Together

### 5.1 Consistency of Lifostinib

The pooled hazard ratio of 0.70 for lifostinib indicates a robust and consistent benefit across the two trials in which it was tested. The low heterogeneity (I² 0.0%) suggests that the effect is stable across different trial contexts. This consistency is notable given the differences in trial design and population characteristics between SIPEM-7 and LEFOKOL-5. The fact that lifostinib shows a similar magnitude of benefit in both trials supports the idea that its efficacy is not dependent on specific trial conditions.

### 5.2 Conflicting Signals for Other Agents

In contrast to the consistent signal for lifostinib, the results for other agents are more varied. The LEFOKOL-5 trial reported a benefit for dagostinib (HR 0.64). The VOHOKRAL-8 trial, an open-label phase II study, found no significant difference in progression-free survival compared to endocrine therapy alone. This contradiction may be due to differences in trial design (open-label vs. double-blind), population (endocrine-resistant vs. first-line), and endpoints (PFS vs. OS). Additionally, the LEFOKOL-5 trial contradicted the non-inferiority results of the BRASTE-8 trial. The BRASTE-8 trial showed non-inferiority to standard of care with a favorable safety profile, while LEFOKOL-5 reported a statistically significant improvement in overall survival. These conflicting signals suggest that the efficacy of CDK4/6 inhibitors may vary depending on the specific agent and trial context.

## Implications

### 6.1 Clinical Decision-Making

The findings of this review have important implications for clinical decision-making. The consistent benefit of lifostinib across two trials suggests that it demonstrates a reliable signal in the available data for patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer. However, the conflicting signals for other agents, such as dagostinib, require careful consideration of the trial context. Clinicians should be aware of the variability in trial results and the specific context of each agent's evidence base. Head-to-head trials are needed to resolve the inter-agent variability and to provide clearer guidance on the relative efficacy of different CDK4/6 inhibitors.

### 6.2 Research Gaps

This review identifies several research gaps that need to be addressed. First, further studies are needed to clarify the role of second-generation agents, such as those tested in the BRASTE-8 trial. Second, there is a need to standardize endpoints and populations across trials to reduce heterogeneity and improve the comparability of results. Finally, more research is needed to understand the mechanisms underlying the conflicting signals for different CDK4/6 inhibitors. Addressing these gaps will help to refine the use of CDK4/6 inhibitors in clinical practice and to improve patient outcomes.

## Conclusion

This structured evidence review has examined the consistency of progression-free survival benefits across different generations of CDK4/6 inhibitors in HR-positive metastatic breast cancer. The main finding is that lifostinib shows a consistent PFS benefit (pooled HR 0.70), but the CDK4/6 inhibitor class exhibits conflicting signals for other agents. The LEFOKOL-5 trial's benefit for dagostinib contradicts earlier neutral findings, complicating class-wide interpretation. While the class is effective, clinicians should be aware of the variability in trial results and the specific context of each agent's evidence base. It offers a structured review of the consistency of PFS benefits across four key trials, identifying areas of agreement and conflict. It discusses the implications of these findings for clinical decision-making, emphasizing the importance of considering the specific context of each trial when interpreting efficacy data.
