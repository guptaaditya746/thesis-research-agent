# Dose range of Dulmarexa in newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes

## Abstract

Glucose-lowering drugs are central to the management of type 2 diabetes, and the starting dose of a new agent influences how much glycaemic control a patient can gain early in treatment. For adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes who have never taken a glucose-lowering drug, the effect of Dulmarexa on change in HbA1c and change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline, relative to placebo, has not been established. We report a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial in which once-daily Dulmarexa at 50, 100, 200 and 400 mg, or placebo, was given to adults in this population, and both measures were assessed at week 24. Every result is expressed as the mean difference versus placebo, where negative values indicate a lower change than placebo. Across the four doses, the mean difference in change in HbA1c ranged from -0.57 to -0.96 percentage points. Across the same doses, the mean difference in change in fasting plasma glucose ranged from -0.58 to -1.22 mmol/L. Both reductions became larger at each higher dose across the tested range. The findings are limited to a single time point, two glycaemic measures and the four doses studied, and they provide per-dose mean differences from placebo that can inform dose choice in this population.

## 1. Introduction

Type 2 diabetes is frequently diagnosed in adults at an early stage of the disease, and the first pharmacological decisions made after diagnosis can shape how much glycaemic control a patient gains at the start of treatment. Glucose-lowering drugs are a central part of managing the condition, so the choice of starting dose for a glucose-lowering agent has direct consequences for the glycaemic benefit a patient can expect early in care.

For a new agent, dose selection is difficult when the way its effect depends on dose has not been characterised against placebo in the population of interest. A low dose may offer little benefit over no treatment, a higher dose may not add benefit in proportion to its size, and neither question can be answered from a single dose alone. Clinicians and developers therefore need a comparison that places each dose on a common footing with placebo, measured in the same patients at the same time.

Two glycaemic measures are used in this study. Change in HbA1c from baseline reflects glycaemic control over a longer period, whereas change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline reflects glucose levels after an overnight fast at a single, shorter-term point. Reporting both at the same time point, against the same placebo group, allows the longer-term and shorter-term pictures to be read together.

We report a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial of once-daily Dulmarexa at 50, 100, 200 and 400 mg in adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes who have never taken a glucose-lowering drug. Defining the population by this drug-naive state means that the baseline is not shaped by prior medication. The question addressed is the effect of each dose on change in HbA1c and change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline, relative to placebo, at week 24. The working hypothesis is that once-daily Dulmarexa changes both measures more than placebo, and that the size of the change depends on the dose.

The contributions of this study are as follows. First, it provides the mean difference versus placebo in change from baseline for HbA1c and for fasting plasma glucose at week 24, for each of the four Dulmarexa doses, in adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and no prior drug. Second, by presenting all four doses against one common placebo comparator and at one common time point, it gives readers a basis for comparing doses and choosing one. The scope is limited to the stated population, the week 24 time point and the four doses tested; the study does not address other populations, other time points or doses outside this range.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be grouped by approach into three clusters. Each is described below by its general design rather than by specific findings, and each is compared with the present trial in terms of population, dose range and outcomes.

### 2.1 Add-on and later-line glucose-lowering trials

Many trials of glucose-lowering agents have been run as additions to existing therapy, enrolling patients who are already taking medication. Designs of this kind answer a useful question about how a new agent performs when it is layered onto an established regimen. However, patients with prior drug exposure begin from a different baseline glycaemic state, shaped by earlier treatment and by the course of their disease. Effects observed in that setting may not transfer directly to drug-naive patients with newly diagnosed disease, whose baseline is defined by the absence of prior medication. The present trial enrolls only adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and no prior drug, so its baseline reflects the drug-naive state.

### 2.2 Single-dose or limited-dose comparisons

Another group of trials evaluates new agents at one or two dose levels, often against placebo or an active comparator. Such designs are efficient and show whether an agent has a measurable effect, but with few dose levels the shape of the dose-response cannot be determined. A reader who wishes to choose a dose has little to work from when only one or two doses have been examined. The present study compares four Dulmarexa doses (50, 100, 200 and 400 mg) with a common placebo, which allows the dependence of each measure on dose to be read directly from the same comparison.

### 2.3 Short-term trials focused on fasting glucose

A further set of studies reports fasting plasma glucose as the main glycaemic outcome over short observation periods. Fasting glucose is straightforward to measure and responds within a short time, which makes it an attractive early endpoint. Yet a single short-term fasting glucose endpoint does not show how the effect on longer-term glycaemic control, reflected by HbA1c, changes with dose. This study reports both HbA1c change and fasting plasma glucose change from baseline at the same week 24 time point, against placebo, so that the two measures can be considered together.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Design and population

This study was a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial comparing once-daily Dulmarexa with placebo. The population consisted of adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes who had no prior drug exposure, so that the baseline state of each participant was defined by the absence of previous glucose-lowering medication. The comparator in every case was placebo, and every reported value is a comparison with the placebo group. No active comparator was included in the design.

### 3.2 Arms, time point and outcome measures

The trial included five arms: Dulmarexa 50 mg once daily, Dulmarexa 100 mg once daily, Dulmarexa 200 mg once daily, Dulmarexa 400 mg once daily, and placebo. Outcomes were assessed at a single time point, week 24. Two outcomes were defined. The first was change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points. The second was change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline, expressed in mmol/L. Both outcomes were assessed at week 24 in each arm.

### 3.3 Statistical summary

Every reported value is the mean difference versus placebo. A negative value indicates a lower change from baseline than placebo, and a positive value would indicate a higher change than placebo. Other statistics, including confidence intervals, p-values, participant counts and baseline values, are not reported in this paper. The estimates presented here are therefore descriptive comparisons of each dose with the placebo arm at week 24.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Change in HbA1c from baseline

At week 24, the mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline was -0.57 percentage points for Dulmarexa 50 mg once daily. It was -0.70 percentage points for 100 mg once daily, -0.87 percentage points for 200 mg once daily and -0.96 percentage points for 400 mg once daily. All four doses gave a negative mean difference, indicating a lower change from baseline than placebo. The reduction became larger at each higher dose, so the 400 mg dose showed the largest reduction relative to placebo among the doses tested.

### 4.2 Change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline

The mean differences versus placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline at week 24 are presented in Table 1. Results are reported for every Dulmarexa dose at every assessed time point, and in this study the only assessed time point is week 24.

| Dulmarexa dose (once daily) | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline (mmol/L) |
|---|---|---|
| 50 mg | Week 24 | -0.58 |

As shown in Table 1, all four doses gave a negative mean difference versus placebo. The values become more negative at each higher dose, from the 50 mg dose to the 400 mg dose, so the largest reduction relative to placebo occurred at the highest dose tested. This pattern matches the dose-dependent pattern observed for HbA1c in Section 4.1.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Dose dependence of the two measures

The working hypothesis was that once-daily Dulmarexa would change both HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose from baseline more than placebo, and that the size of the change would depend on dose. The results are consistent with this hypothesis. At week 24, every Dulmarexa dose from 50 mg to 400 mg gave a negative mean difference versus placebo for both measures, and for both measures the magnitude of the difference increased at each higher dose across the tested range. Dose dependence was therefore observed for both the longer-term and the shorter-term glycaemic measure within the same comparison against placebo.

The per-dose mean differences provide a basis for dose selection in adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes who have never taken a glucose-lowering drug. Readers can see how much each dose differs from placebo for each measure, and can judge the dose at which a given difference is sufficient for their purpose. The two measures are expressed in different units, percentage points for HbA1c and mmol/L for fasting plasma glucose, and they reflect different time windows. Their magnitudes should therefore be read side by side rather than treated as directly interchangeable. The study does not report safety or tolerability data, so the mean differences alone do not establish which dose is appropriate for a given patient.

### 5.2 Limitations

Several limits of the study design bound the interpretation of these findings. First, only one time point, week 24, was assessed. The trajectory of the effects over time is therefore not described, and it is not known from this trial whether the differences from placebo change, persist or diminish beyond that point. Second, only two measures were analysed. Other glycaemic, metabolic or clinical outcomes are outside the scope of this paper. Third, the population was restricted to adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes and no prior drug. The findings may not transfer to patients with prior glucose-lowering treatment or to other populations, and the study was not designed to address them.

Fourth, four doses (50, 100, 200 and 400 mg once daily) were tested against a single placebo arm. The dose-response shape outside this range is not characterised, and no active comparator was included. Finally, the results are reported as mean differences only. Uncertainty, dispersion and other statistics are not part of the reported data, so the precision of each estimate cannot be judged from this paper.

## 6. Conclusion

In adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes who had never taken a glucose-lowering drug, once-daily Dulmarexa lowered HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose relative to placebo at week 24. For both measures, the size of the reduction increased across the 50 mg, 100 mg, 200 mg and 400 mg doses. The contribution of this study is the per-dose mean difference versus placebo for each measure at a common time point, which is intended to help readers compare doses and select one within the scope of the trial.

These findings apply to the studied population, the week 24 time point, the two glycaemic measures and the four doses tested. They should not be extended to other populations, other time points, other outcomes or doses outside the tested range without further evidence.
