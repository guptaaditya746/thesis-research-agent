# Tavorindel added to basal insulin in type 2 diabetes: a 26-week trial

## Abstract

Adults with type 2 diabetes who take basal insulin often need additional glucose-lowering treatment, and an add-on should be judged on glycaemic control and body weight together. The effect of Tavorindel compared with placebo on change from baseline in HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose in this population is not known at week 13 or week 26. We designed a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial in adults with type 2 diabetes on basal insulin, comparing Tavorindel 5 mg, 10 mg and 20 mg once daily with placebo, with each measure assessed at week 13 and week 26. Every value is a mean difference versus placebo. At week 13, the change in HbA1c was -0.27 percentage points at 5 mg, -0.36 percentage points at 10 mg and -0.45 percentage points at 20 mg. At week 26, the corresponding HbA1c mean differences were -0.36, -0.45 and -0.58 percentage points. Fasting plasma glucose differences were the most negative at 20 mg at both time points, and body weight differences were small at every dose and time point. Results are reported for every dose at every time point. The contribution is a set of dose-specific mean differences versus placebo for HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose, presented on one grid to support dose choice. The reported data cover one population, three doses and two time points, and they do not by themselves establish effects beyond these conditions.

## 1. Introduction

Many adults with type 2 diabetes who take basal insulin do not reach their glycaemic goals on that therapy alone, so further glucose-lowering options remain relevant for this group. When an add-on is considered, two kinds of outcome matter at the same time. Glycaemic control, reflected in HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose, is the reason for adding treatment. Body weight matters because a change in weight affects how an add-on is judged by patients and clinicians. Both outcomes therefore belong in the same assessment.

An add-on therapy should be assessed on HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose and body weight together, against a placebo comparator. A placebo arm separates the effect of the study drug from changes that occur in people who continue basal insulin without any added agent. Assessing the three measures together also shows whether an improvement in one is accompanied by a change in another, rather than judging each measure in isolation.

For Tavorindel, the effect on these measures in adults with type 2 diabetes on basal insulin is not known. Specifically, the mean difference versus placebo in change from baseline in HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose at each dose under study has not been established at week 13 or at week 26. Without these values, the doses cannot be compared on the same footing, and it is not possible to see how the effect develops between the two time points. The gap is therefore scoped to this population, these three doses, these three measures and these two time points. It does not extend to other populations or durations.

This study addresses that gap with a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial. Adults with type 2 diabetes on basal insulin were randomised to Tavorindel 5 mg, 10 mg or 20 mg once daily, or to placebo. The working hypothesis is that once-daily Tavorindel changes these measures more than placebo, and that the size of the change depends on the dose. Each measure is reported as a mean difference versus placebo for each dose, at week 13 and at week 26, so that every dose is read against the same comparator.

The Results section reports these mean differences and the dose ordering they show at each time point. The Discussion then states what the reported data do and do not establish. The data cover three doses, three measures and two time points in one population. They do not by themselves establish effects beyond those conditions, such as other doses, longer treatment or other patient groups.

The contribution of this study is the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point, presented on one grid so that readers can choose a dose of Tavorindel for use with basal insulin.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this trial falls into three clusters defined by approach. Each cluster either precedes or competes with the present design. The descriptions below cover general methods only and do not attribute specific findings to any individual study.

### 2.1 Single-dose add-on comparisons in basal insulin users

A substantial body of work evaluates add-on glucose-lowering agents in people who already receive basal insulin. These studies typically compare one fixed dose of the add-on with placebo, with both arms continuing the same background insulin regimen. This design suits the question of whether an add-on has any effect beyond background therapy. Its limitation for dose selection is that a single dose against placebo does not estimate how the size of the effect changes as the dose changes. Such a study shows whether the agent works at one dose, but not how much additional effect a higher or lower dose would bring, and that is the question that governs the choice of dose. The present trial addresses this by testing three doses against one shared placebo arm.

### 2.2 Dose-ranging studies of once-daily agents

Placebo-controlled dose-ranging designs for once-daily oral glucose-lowering agents are the closest methodological precedent for the present trial. These designs allocate participants to several doses and a placebo, which allows dose-related patterns to be examined. In many of them, the primary focus is HbA1c, and fasting plasma glucose and body weight appear as secondary observations. Follow-up may also be shorter than in longer programmes, so the time course of effects is often less well characterised. The present trial keeps the dose-ranging structure but reports HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose at both week 13 and week 26 for every dose, so that the time course is visible for each measure.

### 2.3 Separate reporting of glycaemic and weight outcomes

Another group of reports analyses glycaemic and body weight outcomes and presents them as separate summaries, sometimes in different tables, different publications or on different scales. This presentation is common when outcomes are treated as secondary to one another. Its limitation is that when weight and glycaemic results are not placed side by side, the trade-off across doses is hard for a reader to see. A dose that gives a larger glycaemic effect may also carry a larger weight effect, and separate summaries make that comparison laborious. The present trial places all three measures on the same dose and time grid, each as a mean difference versus the same placebo comparator, so that the trade-off across doses can be read directly from one table.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design

The study is a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial. Participants were allocated to receive Tavorindel or placebo, with each Tavorindel dose compared against the same placebo comparator. Each measure was assessed at two time points, week 13 and week 26, so that the change from baseline can be examined at an intermediate and at a final visit within the same trial.

The analysis reported here is the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point. No further statistical procedures are reported in this paper.

### 3.2 Participants and treatment arms

The study population comprised adults with type 2 diabetes who were on basal insulin. The trial included four treatment arms: Tavorindel 5 mg once daily, Tavorindel 10 mg once daily, Tavorindel 20 mg once daily, and placebo. Placebo is the comparator for every reported value, so each Tavorindel dose is read as a difference from the same placebo arm at the same time point.

### 3.3 Outcomes and reporting conventions

Three outcomes were assessed, each as a change from baseline. The first is change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points. The second is change in body weight from baseline, expressed in kilograms (kg). The third is change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline, expressed in millimoles per litre (mmol/L).

Every value is a mean difference versus placebo. Negative values indicate a value lower than placebo, and positive values indicate a value higher than placebo. Values are reported exactly as given, with their decimals and units. Confidence intervals, p-values, patient counts and baseline values are not reported in this paper, so the statements below concern the mean differences alone.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Glycaemic control: change in HbA1c

Table 1 gives the mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline at week 13 for each Tavorindel dose. At week 13, the mean difference was -0.27 percentage points at 5 mg, -0.36 percentage points at 10 mg and -0.45 percentage points at 20 mg. The values become more negative as the dose increases.

Table 1: Mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline at week 13, by Tavorindel dose

| Tavorindel dose | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline (percentage points) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 mg daily | week 13 | -0.27 |
| 10 mg daily | week 13 | -0.36 |
| 20 mg daily | week 13 | -0.45 |

At week 26, the mean difference versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline was -0.36 percentage points for 5 mg daily, -0.45 percentage points for 10 mg daily and -0.58 percentage points for 20 mg daily. The dose ordering at week 26 matches that at week 13, with the most negative mean difference at the highest dose. Taken together, the HbA1c values show a descriptive pattern in which more negative mean differences accompany higher doses at both time points. No formal test of this ordering is reported.

### 4.2 Body weight

Table 2 gives the mean difference versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline at weeks 13 and 26 for each Tavorindel dose. At week 13, the mean differences were 0.3 kg at 5 mg daily, 0.4 kg at 10 mg daily and 0.2 kg at 20 mg daily. At week 26, they were 0.0 kg at 5 mg daily, 0.5 kg at 10 mg daily and 0.5 kg at 20 mg daily.

Table 2: Mean difference versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline at weeks 13 and 26, by Tavorindel dose

| Tavorindel dose | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in body weight from baseline (kg) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 mg daily | week 13 | 0.3 |
| 5 mg daily | week 26 | 0.0 |
| 10 mg daily | week 13 | 0.4 |
| 10 mg daily | week 26 | 0.5 |
| 20 mg daily | week 13 | 0.2 |
| 20 mg daily | week 26 | 0.5 |

The body weight differences do not follow the dose ordering seen for the glycaemic measures. At week 13, the 20 mg dose gave a lower value than the 10 mg dose, so the pattern is not monotonic in dose. At week 26, the 5 mg dose showed a mean difference of 0.0 kg, while the 10 mg and 20 mg doses both showed 0.5 kg. All body weight differences are small in absolute terms.

### 4.3 Fasting plasma glucose

Table 3 gives the mean difference versus placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline at weeks 13 and 26 for each Tavorindel dose. At week 13, the mean differences were -0.49 mmol/L at 5 mg daily, -0.51 mmol/L at 10 mg daily and -0.74 mmol/L at 20 mg daily. At week 26, they were -0.41 mmol/L, -0.49 mmol/L and -0.71 mmol/L for the same three doses, respectively.

Table 3: Mean difference versus placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline at weeks 13 and 26, by Tavorindel dose

| Tavorindel dose | Time point | Mean difference vs placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline (mmol/L) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 mg daily | week 13 | -0.49 |
| 5 mg daily | week 26 | -0.41 |
| 10 mg daily | week 13 | -0.51 |

Fasting plasma glucose differences are negative at every dose and at both time points. The largest reduction relative to placebo is seen at 20 mg daily at both week 13 and week 26. At each time point, the mean difference becomes more negative as the dose increases. Results are reported for every dose of Tavorindel at every time point.

## 5. Discussion

The trial tested two parts of the working hypothesis: whether once-daily Tavorindel changes HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose more than placebo, and whether the size of that change depends on dose. For the two glycaemic measures, the reported mean differences versus placebo are negative at every dose and time point, and they become more negative as the dose increases (Tables 1 and 3). This pattern is consistent with the dose-dependence part of the hypothesis. Because the values are descriptive mean differences, they support the direction of the effect and its ordering by dose, but they do not by themselves quantify the precision of the estimates.

The body weight results do not follow the same pattern. The differences are small at every dose and time point, and the ordering across doses is not monotonic at week 13 (Table 2). In these data, the larger glycaemic differences at the higher doses were not accompanied by a clear dose-related change in body weight. This is the point on which the trade-off between glycaemic benefit and weight is most relevant for dose choice, and the present data show no consistent weight pattern that would separate the doses on that basis.

The time course is also informative. For HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose and body weight, the values at week 26 are reported alongside those at week 13 for the same doses (Tables 2 and 3 and Section 4.1). Mean differences for fasting plasma glucose at 5 mg and 10 mg moved slightly towards zero between the two time points, whereas at 20 mg they remained close to their week 13 level. Changes of this kind can only be read descriptively with two time points, because the paper does not report intermediate visits.

Several limitations follow from the design. First, the analysis is descriptive. Only mean differences versus placebo are reported, and no confidence intervals, p-values, dispersion measures or patient counts are reported here. The uncertainty around each value cannot therefore be judged from this paper, and the dose ordering should not be read as a formal test of a dose-response relationship. Second, the design includes one placebo comparator and three fixed doses. The shape of the response between these doses, and at doses above 20 mg or below 5 mg, is not characterised. Third, the two time points, week 13 and week 26, give limited information on the time course, and no data are reported beyond 26 weeks. Fourth, the population is restricted to adults with type 2 diabetes on basal insulin. The findings do not extend to people who are not receiving basal insulin, to other patient groups or to other forms of background therapy. Finally, baseline values are not reported in this paper, which limits the reader's ability to compare the absolute levels of the measures at the start of the trial with the changes shown.

Within these limits, the data give a practical reference for dose selection. The mean differences for each measure, dose and time point are placed on one grid, so that the glycaemic gains and the body weight differences can be read together for each dose. The results do not establish effects outside the doses, time points and population studied.

## 6. Conclusion

In adults with type 2 diabetes on basal insulin, once-daily Tavorindel at 5 mg, 10 mg and 20 mg gave mean differences versus placebo that were negative for HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose at weeks 13 and 26, and that became more negative as the dose increased. Body weight differences versus placebo were small at every dose and time point, and they did not follow a consistent dose-related pattern. The dose-specific mean differences presented here give a single reference for each measure, dose and time point. They describe the reported conditions only and do not establish effects beyond the three doses, two time points and one population studied.
