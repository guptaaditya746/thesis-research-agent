# Tiravelor for blood pressure and cholesterol in type 2 diabetes with high blood pressure

## Abstract

Adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure who take metformin often carry combined cardiometabolic risk, and the effect of once-daily Tiravelor on systolic blood pressure, LDL cholesterol and HbA1c compared with placebo has not been described for this population. We report a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups in adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure on metformin, comparing Tiravelor 2 mg daily and Tiravelor 4 mg daily with placebo. Change from baseline in systolic blood pressure (mmHg), LDL cholesterol (mmol/L) and HbA1c (percentage points) was assessed at week 12 and week 24. Results are reported as mean differences versus placebo, where negative values indicate lower values than placebo. Both doses gave values below placebo for all three measures at both time points. The 4 mg dose gave more negative mean differences than the 2 mg dose at every time point for each measure, with HbA1c mean differences ranging from -0.16 to -0.22 percentage points. The report presents point estimates only. No measures of uncertainty, inferential statistics, sample sizes or safety data are given, so the observed pattern is described without an assessment of its precision, statistical reliability or clinical importance.

## 1. Introduction

Adults with type 2 diabetes frequently live with high blood pressure and raised LDL cholesterol at the same time as hyperglycaemia. Their cardiometabolic risk is therefore shaped by several measures acting together. A treatment that lowers blood glucose but leaves blood pressure or LDL cholesterol unchanged addresses only part of that risk, and a treatment aimed at blood pressure alone may leave glycaemia unaffected. The combined profile of systolic blood pressure, LDL cholesterol and HbA1c is therefore a useful lens for considering a new agent in this group of patients.

Many of these adults take metformin, and a considerable number need further treatment. Adding a second agent is a common decision point, and its value depends on what it does to each measure, not only to one. It is not clear how a once-daily agent given in this setting changes systolic blood pressure, LDL cholesterol and HbA1c at the same time. Answering this question requires a population defined by the combined presentation, namely patients who have high blood pressure as well as type 2 diabetes and who already take metformin.

Existing approaches are usually assessed for one measure at a time. Studies of add-on glucose-lowering treatment centre on HbA1c, while studies of blood-pressure treatment centre on systolic pressure, and each seldom reports the other measures on a common design. Where more than one dose is examined, the comparison with placebo is often limited to one dose or one time point. This makes it difficult to judge how the size of the effect depends on dose and whether it changes between earlier and later follow-up.

This study addresses that need for one clearly defined population. It is a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups, in which once-daily Tiravelor at 2 mg and 4 mg is compared with placebo. The working hypothesis is that Tiravelor changes systolic blood pressure, LDL cholesterol and HbA1c more than placebo, and that the size of the change depends on dose. Each measure is expressed as change from baseline and assessed at week 12 and week 24. The scope is deliberately bounded to these two doses, these two time points and these three measures.

The aim of this report is descriptive: to present the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point, so that the pattern across doses and time points is documented in one place. The report does not assess clinical importance, safety or the choice between doses, which require further information.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be grouped into three broad approaches: add-on glucose-lowering therapy in patients taking metformin, blood-pressure-focused management in diabetes, and multi-target approaches to cardiometabolic risk. Each is described below by its general approach, together with the limits that motivate the present trial.

### 2.1 Add-on glucose-lowering therapy in patients on metformin

A large body of work has studied glucose-lowering agents added to metformin. These studies typically use placebo-controlled designs in which change in HbA1c is the central measure. Their focus is mainly on glycaemia, and they usually do not report blood pressure or LDL cholesterol on the same design. As a result, the effects of such agents on other cardiometabolic measures in the same patients are often unclear from the glycaemic literature. The present trial keeps the placebo-controlled comparison typical of this work and reports HbA1c alongside systolic blood pressure and LDL cholesterol within one population.

### 2.2 Blood-pressure-focused management in diabetes

A second group of studies examines antihypertensive and other blood-pressure-lowering treatment in adults who have both diabetes and hypertension. These studies are designed around systolic blood pressure, with outcomes, follow-up schedules and analysis plans chosen for that measure. They seldom record glycaemic or lipid changes over the same time points, so the effect of blood-pressure treatment on HbA1c and LDL cholesterol in the same patients is difficult to read from them. The present trial records the three measures at the same time points so that they can be read against one another.

### 2.3 Multi-target approaches to cardiometabolic risk

A third approach comprises studies of agents intended to act on several cardiometabolic measures at once, reflecting the view that blood pressure, lipid and glycaemic risk are linked. Such studies address the combined profile directly, but they often report one or two doses and do not give the mean difference versus placebo for each dose and time point. This makes it hard to compare doses or to judge how the effect develops over time. The present trial compares two fixed doses against placebo and reports each mean difference by dose and time point.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design and population

This study is a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups, in which Tiravelor is compared with placebo. The population comprises adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure who take metformin. Participants are assigned to one of three arms: Tiravelor 2 mg taken once daily, Tiravelor 4 mg taken once daily, or placebo. Assessments are made at two time points, week 12 and week 24, relative to baseline. Trial registration, ethics approval, the randomisation method, blinding, sample size and the analysis population are not described in this report.

### 3.2 Outcomes and effect measure

Three outcomes are analysed: change from baseline in systolic blood pressure (mmHg), change from baseline in LDL cholesterol (mmol/L) and change from baseline in HbA1c (percentage points). For each outcome, dose and time point, the effect is expressed as the mean difference versus placebo, and negative values indicate a value lower than placebo. Placebo is the only comparator. Values are reported exactly as recorded, with their decimals and units. No statistical model, primary endpoint or method for handling multiplicity is specified in this report, and no additional statistics are added. The measurement methods for blood pressure and LDL cholesterol are not described here. Baseline values are not reported in this paper.

### 3.3 Intervention and scope of reporting

Tiravelor is given once daily at 2 mg or 4 mg. Its pharmacological class, formulation and the rationale for the two doses are not described in this report. Safety and tolerability data, including adverse events and discontinuations, are not reported, and background therapies other than metformin are not described.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Principal observations

In this trial, both doses gave values lower than placebo for all three measures at both time points. The 4 mg dose gave more negative mean differences than the 2 mg dose for each measure and each time point reported. This is a descriptive observation from two doses. No test of the difference between doses is reported, so the pattern cannot be presented as a demonstrated dose-dependent effect. Likewise, the direction of every reported mean difference is consistent with the working hypothesis, but the report does not include measures of uncertainty, so this consistency cannot be read as statistical evidence.

### 5.2 Interpretation for dose comparison

For systolic blood pressure, the 4 mg dose gave the more negative value at both time points. For LDL cholesterol and HbA1c, the 4 mg dose gave values that are more negative than the 2 mg values, with the differences between doses appearing small on the scales reported. The systolic blood pressure values are numerically larger than the LDL cholesterol and HbA1c values, but these measures are expressed in different units, so their magnitudes cannot be compared directly. This report does not assess whether any difference is clinically meaningful, and it reports no adverse events, discontinuations or other safety data. A judgement about the choice between doses would therefore need information on clinical importance and on harms that this report does not provide.

### 5.3 Limitations

Only mean differences versus placebo are reported. Measures of uncertainty, p-values, sample sizes, dropout and baseline values are not presented, so precision and statistical strength cannot be judged from this report. Safety, tolerability and quality of life are not reported, so the balance of benefit and harm cannot be assessed. Only two doses and two time points were assessed, so the findings say nothing about other doses, other durations of treatment or later follow-up, and no dose-response relationship can be established. The results apply only to adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure who take metformin, and their applicability to other populations or background therapies is not shown.

## 6. Conclusion

This randomised, placebo-controlled parallel-group trial examined once-daily Tiravelor at 2 mg and 4 mg in adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure who take metformin. At weeks 12 and 24, both doses gave values lower than placebo for systolic blood pressure, LDL cholesterol and HbA1c, and the 4 mg dose gave more negative mean differences than the 2 mg dose in each case reported. These are descriptive observations from point estimates alone. Their precision, the clinical importance of the differences, and the safety profile of each dose are not established by this report, and further evidence on these points is needed before the findings are used to guide dose selection.
