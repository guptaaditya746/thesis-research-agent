# Veloranide with metformin in type 2 diabetes: a 24-week trial

## Abstract

Adults with type 2 diabetes who remain on metformin often need an additional glucose-lowering agent, and such an agent must be judged on both change in glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) and change in body weight. The effect of Veloranide on these two measures relative to placebo in this population has not been established. We report a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, comparing once-daily Veloranide 25 mg and once-daily Veloranide 50 mg with placebo. Each measure was assessed at week 12 and week 24. All outcomes are reported as mean differences versus placebo, with change in HbA1c given in percentage points and change in body weight from baseline given in kilograms. For HbA1c, the mean differences for 25 mg and 50 mg were -0.35 and -0.52 percentage points at week 12, and -0.42 and -0.54 percentage points at week 24. For body weight, the mean differences for 25 mg and 50 mg were -0.3 and -0.8 kg at week 12, and -0.4 and -0.7 kg at week 24. The 50 mg dose gave the more negative mean difference on both measures at both time points. Within the studied population and the 24-week duration, the larger dose therefore gave the greater reduction relative to placebo on both measures. Only mean differences are reported here, and the findings do not extend beyond these conditions.

## 1. Introduction

Type 2 diabetes is commonly managed in stages, and metformin is often the first agent prescribed. Over time, many adults who remain on metformin do not reach their glycaemic targets and need an additional glucose-lowering treatment. Choosing such an add-on agent involves more than glycaemic effect alone. Change in glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) shows how far an agent lowers average blood glucose, while change in body weight matters because weight is a central concern for many people with type 2 diabetes and for their clinicians. An add-on agent therefore has to be judged on both measures together.

Earlier add-on work in this setting has used placebo-controlled designs in adults already taking metformin, which isolate the effect of the added agent on top of standard background therapy. However, these studies often report a single dose or a single outcome, and body weight is frequently treated as a secondary endpoint. When a new agent is tested at more than one dose, the trade-off between glycaemic effect and weight change can be spread across several tables and time points. This makes it difficult for a reader to compare doses on both measures at once.

The present study is a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial in adults with type 2 diabetes who are treated with metformin. Participants were assigned to once-daily Veloranide 25 mg, once-daily Veloranide 50 mg, or placebo. The working hypothesis is that once-daily Veloranide changes both HbA1c and body weight more than placebo, and that the size of the change depends on the dose. Each measure was assessed at week 12 and week 24, so both the dose dependence and the time course of effect can be examined.

Each outcome is reported as a mean difference versus placebo, where a negative value means a lower value than placebo. Change in HbA1c is given in percentage points and change in body weight in kilograms. Presenting every dose and time point in the same format allows the size of effect to be read directly for each measure, and allows the change between week 12 and week 24 to be seen for each dose.

The scope of the study is deliberately limited. The values are reported for the studied population and the 24-week treatment duration, and they are not extended to other patient groups, to longer treatment, or to background therapies other than metformin. The study addresses only change in HbA1c and change in body weight. Readers should interpret the results within these limits.

The contribution of this study is a dose-specific and time-specific account of the mean difference from placebo for change in HbA1c and change in body weight. These values are reported in a common format so that readers can choose a dose on both measures.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work on add-on therapy in adults with type 2 diabetes who remain on metformin can be grouped into three clusters by design approach. Each cluster is described below by its approach only.

### 2.1 Add-on glucose-lowering trials in metformin-treated adults

Placebo-controlled add-on trials enrol adults who already receive metformin and randomise them to an investigational agent or to placebo while background metformin continues. This design isolates the added effect of the agent on top of standard therapy and is the usual framework when an agent is intended for use after metformin. Such trials usually centre on change in HbA1c as the principal measure, with body weight playing a secondary role. They may also report only one dose, so the dose-specific picture is not always visible. The present trial keeps the same add-on framework but reports HbA1c and body weight as paired measures, each separately for both Veloranide doses and for placebo.

### 2.2 Dose-ranging studies of investigational agents

Dose-ranging designs test several fixed doses of a single agent against placebo, with the aim of showing how effect varies with dose. They are a common route to dose selection for new agents. Their results are often summarised at one end point, which gives a snapshot rather than a course over treatment. This limits how readers judge whether an effect strengthens, levels off or wanes over the treatment period. The present design reports each dose at week 12 and week 24, so the time dependence of each mean difference can be seen alongside its dose dependence.

### 2.3 Joint reporting of glycaemic and weight outcomes

A smaller group of studies reports glycaemic and body weight outcomes together, reflecting the view that an agent's usefulness depends on both. These studies differ in how they present the two measures, for example in units, summary statistics and table layout. When body weight is a secondary outcome, the dose-specific size of weight change relative to glycaemic change is often hard to read. The present study gives both measures as mean differences versus placebo in a common format, with the same time points for each, which allows a direct dose comparison for each measure.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design

The study was a randomised controlled trial with a parallel-group design, in which Veloranide was compared against placebo. Participants were allocated to one of three parallel arms: once-daily Veloranide 25 mg, once-daily Veloranide 50 mg, or placebo. Each arm was evaluated at two time points, week 12 and week 24, so that the effect of each arm can be compared at an earlier and a later point within the 24-week treatment period. Because the arms run in parallel, each Veloranide dose is compared with the same placebo comparator at each time point.

### 3.2 Participants and interventions

The participants were adults with type 2 diabetes who were receiving metformin. Metformin was the existing glucose-lowering therapy at the time of study entry, and the study agent was assessed in this setting. Participants were assigned to one of the following arms:

- Veloranide 25 mg, taken once daily;
- Veloranide 50 mg, taken once daily;
- placebo.

Placebo is the comparator for all reported values. No other comparator is used in the results presented here.

### 3.3 Outcome measures and reporting

Two outcomes were evaluated. The first was change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points. The second was change in body weight from baseline, expressed in kilograms. Both outcomes were assessed at week 12 and at week 24.

Every value reported in this paper is a mean difference versus placebo. A negative value means that the result was lower than placebo, and a more negative value indicates a larger reduction relative to placebo. Values are reported exactly as they were given, with their decimal places and units. No confidence intervals, p-values, patient counts or baseline values are reported in this paper, so the precision of each estimate and the size of each group cannot be judged from the values presented here.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Change in HbA1c from baseline

Table 1 presents the mean differences versus placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline at week 12 for each Veloranide dose. At week 12, the 25 mg dose gave a mean difference of -0.35 percentage points, and the 50 mg dose gave a mean difference of -0.52 percentage points.

Table 1: Mean difference vs placebo in change in HbA1c from baseline at week 12, by Veloranide dose

| Veloranide dose | Time point | Change in HbA1c from baseline vs placebo (percentage points) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 mg daily | week 12 | -0.35 |
| 50 mg daily | week 12 | -0.52 |

The week 24 values for the same measure are stated in the text. At week 24, the 25 mg dose gave a mean difference of -0.42 percentage points. At week 24, the 50 mg dose gave a mean difference of -0.54 percentage points. The more negative value at 50 mg than at 25 mg therefore holds at both time points. Each dose was more negative at week 24 than at week 12, with the change from week 12 to week 24 being larger for the 25 mg dose.

### 4.2 Change in body weight from baseline

Table 2 presents the mean differences versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline for each Veloranide dose at week 12 and week 24. Results are reported for every dose at every time point.

Table 2: Mean difference vs placebo in change in body weight from baseline, by Veloranide dose and time point

| Veloranide dose | Time point | Change in body weight from baseline vs placebo (kg) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 mg daily | week 12 | -0.3 |
| 25 mg daily | week 24 | -0.4 |
| 50 mg daily | week 12 | -0.8 |
| 50 mg daily | week 24 | -0.7 |

At week 12, the mean differences were -0.3 kg for 25 mg and -0.8 kg for 50 mg. At week 24, they were -0.4 kg for 25 mg and -0.7 kg for 50 mg. The 50 mg dose was more negative than the 25 mg dose at both time points. Between week 12 and week 24, the 25 mg mean difference became slightly more negative, whereas the 50 mg mean difference became slightly less negative.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Dose-related differences

Across both measures and both time points, the 50 mg dose gave a more negative mean difference versus placebo than the 25 mg dose. For HbA1c, the 50 mg dose was more negative than 25 mg at week 12 and at week 24. For body weight, the same ordering held at both time points. This pattern is consistent with the core hypothesis that the size of change depends on dose. The dose ordering was also the same for the glycaemic and the weight outcomes, so the two measures did not point to different doses in this study.

The reported values describe the direction and relative size of the differences between doses. They do not identify the mechanism behind these differences, and this paper does not offer an explanation for them. The interpretation is therefore limited to the mean differences versus placebo that were measured.

### 5.2 Time course over 24 weeks

Within each dose, the HbA1c mean differences were more negative at week 24 than at week 12. The gap between the two doses was similar at the two time points in the reported values, so the dose-related difference in HbA1c did not visibly widen or narrow over the period. For body weight, the pattern was different. The 25 mg mean difference was slightly more negative at week 24, while the 50 mg mean difference was slightly less negative, so weight differences moved modestly between time points.

Because each measure was assessed at only two time points, the reported values do not describe the full time course of effect. The trajectory between week 12 and week 24, and any change after week 24, cannot be determined from these values. The time course discussed here should therefore be read as a comparison of two points within a single 24-week period.

### 5.3 Limitations and scope

The population studied was adults with type 2 diabetes who were receiving metformin, and the treatment duration was 24 weeks. The findings do not extend beyond these conditions. They do not address other patient groups, longer treatment, or background therapies other than metformin, and they do not address outcomes other than change in HbA1c and change in body weight.

Only mean differences versus placebo are reported. Without confidence intervals or p-values in the reported values, the precision of each estimate cannot be judged in this paper. Differences between doses should therefore be read as the reported point values only.

The magnitudes of the reported changes are small in absolute terms, particularly for body weight. They should be read alongside the full trial report, which provides the context needed to judge their clinical relevance. The study compared two doses of Veloranide with a single placebo comparator, and it did not include other active comparators.

## 6. Conclusion

In this randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, both Veloranide doses gave more negative mean differences versus placebo for change in HbA1c and change in body weight from baseline at week 12 and week 24. The 50 mg dose gave the more negative mean difference relative to 25 mg on both measures and at both time points.

Within the studied population and the 24-week duration, the paired mean differences versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point support dose selection for adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin. This conclusion is limited to the scope described in this paper.
