# Quendarin, Quelmastin and Mirabexan added to metformin: a three-drug placebo-controlled trial

## Abstract

Adults with type 2 diabetes who remain above glycaemic targets on metformin often need a second glucose-lowering agent, and the choice between options depends on the expected effects on HbA1c as well as on body weight and fasting plasma glucose. This report describes the effects of Quendarin (QND-631), Quelmastin (QLM-331) and Mirabexan (MRB-613) on these three measures, relative to placebo, in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin. We describe a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups, in which Quendarin (QND-631) was given at 10 mg daily, Quelmastin (QLM-331) at 100 mg daily and Mirabexan (MRB-613) at 15 mg daily, each compared with a single placebo arm. Outcomes were changes from baseline at week 26. Every value is a mean difference versus placebo, where a negative value means a lower change than placebo. For HbA1c, the mean differences were -0.54 percentage points for Quendarin, -0.86 percentage points for Quelmastin and -0.54 percentage points for Mirabexan. Mean differences for body weight and fasting plasma glucose are given in Tables 1 and 2. Fasting plasma glucose differences were negative for all three agents, whereas body weight differences varied in sign. The report gives point estimates only. No statistical significance is claimed, and the values describe the doses studied at a single time point. They do not establish dose-response relationships, a ranking of the agents, or safety.

## 1. Introduction

Many adults with type 2 diabetes who take metformin do not reach their glycaemic targets on that therapy alone, and a second glucose-lowering agent is then often considered. The choice of add-on is rarely based on glycaemic control alone. HbA1c is the principal measure of glycaemic control, but the expected effects on body weight and fasting plasma glucose also bear on which agent suits a given patient, because these outcomes shape treatment fit, monitoring and the patient's experience of therapy. A decision therefore depends on how an agent is expected to move several measures at once.

Comparing add-on agents is difficult when the evidence comes from separate studies. Populations, background therapy, doses, treatment durations and measurement time points often differ between trials, and each of these can change the apparent size of an effect. When such differences are present, a difference in outcome between two agents may reflect the trial design as much as the drugs themselves, which limits what can be concluded from reading published results side by side.

This report describes three named agents, each at one fixed once-daily dose, in a randomised, placebo-controlled study with parallel groups in adults with type 2 diabetes who are taking metformin. The agents are Quendarin (QND-631) at 10 mg daily, Quelmastin (QLM-331) at 100 mg daily and Mirabexan (MRB-613) at 15 mg daily. Each is compared with the same placebo arm, so that all three are measured against a common reference within one population. The agents are identified by their development codes. This report does not describe their pharmacological class, mechanism or regulatory status.

The outcomes are the change from baseline at week 26 in HbA1c, in percentage points, in body weight, in kilograms, and in fasting plasma glucose, in mmol/L. For each outcome, the summary measure is the mean difference versus placebo, where a negative value means a lower change than placebo. Reporting HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose on the same comparative footing allows the agents to be read on glycaemic effect, weight and fasting glucose together, rather than on HbA1c alone.

The scope of the study is deliberately narrow. It reports one time point, week 26, and one dose per agent. Because drug identity and dose are not separated, the design does not test dose-response relationships, and differences between agents are not interpreted as a dose-response curve for any drug. The report does not address effects beyond week 26, safety or tolerability, or populations other than adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin.

The contribution of this report is the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, at the dose studied, for Quendarin (QND-631), Quelmastin (QLM-331) and Mirabexan (MRB-613) at week 26. These values are provided as per-dose descriptive estimates to inform dose choice in the context of the limits set out above.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Placebo-controlled add-on trials with glycaemic endpoints

Studies in this group add a single investigational agent to metformin and compare it with placebo, using change in HbA1c as the principal glycaemic endpoint. The design isolates the effect of the agent from background therapy, because the placebo arm shares the metformin background with the active arm. However, such studies often evaluate one agent at one dose, so the effect of changing dose, or of comparing several agents within one design, is left unclear. Where a single drug is studied in isolation, the results describe that drug only and offer little direct basis for reading across agents. The present design keeps the placebo-controlled logic but places three agents, each at one defined dose, against one shared placebo arm, and reports each as a mean difference versus placebo at the same time point. This arrangement allows the three agents to be read side by side within one study.

### 2.2 Weight and fasting glucose as secondary outcomes of add-on therapy

Add-on studies in this group often report body weight and fasting plasma glucose alongside glycaemic control. These measures matter to the choice of therapy, and they are collected in many trials of glucose-lowering agents. In practice, however, they are frequently treated as secondary endpoints, and their analysis may receive less emphasis than the principal glycaemic measure. As a result, weight and fasting glucose effects are often interpreted separately from HbA1c for each agent, which makes it harder to judge whether a glycaemic effect accompanies a change in weight or fasting glucose for the same drug. The present design gives HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose equal status as outcomes, each expressed in its own unit and each compared with the same placebo.

### 2.3 Indirect comparison of add-on agents across separate trials

Some work compares several add-on agents for patients on metformin by setting the results of separate trials side by side. This approach can summarise a broad range of agents, but differences in populations, background therapy, doses and durations between studies can confound comparisons of effect size. Because the comparison is indirect, an apparent difference between agents may reflect the trial rather than the drug. Adjustment methods depend on assumptions that are difficult to verify when the source trials differ in many respects. The present design does not remove dose as a difference between agents, since each agent is given at its own dose. It does, however, hold population, background therapy, placebo and time point constant across the three agents, which removes the between-study differences that complicate indirect comparison.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Trial design and population

This study is a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups. Quendarin (QND-631), Quelmastin (QLM-331) and Mirabexan (MRB-613) are each compared with placebo. The study population consists of adults with type 2 diabetes who are taking metformin. Outcomes were assessed at week 26.

### 3.2 Interventions and dosing

Three investigational arms were studied, together with a placebo arm: Quendarin (QND-631) at 10 mg daily, Quelmastin (QLM-331) at 100 mg daily and Mirabexan (MRB-613) at 15 mg daily. Each investigational agent was given once daily at a single fixed dose, and each dose was compared with the same placebo arm. Each agent was therefore studied at one dose only, and the design does not compare doses within an agent. Because every agent is paired with a different dose, drug identity and dose cannot be separated in any cross-drug comparison.

### 3.3 Outcomes and summary measure

Three outcomes were defined as change from baseline at week 26: HbA1c, in percentage points; body weight, in kg; and fasting plasma glucose, in mmol/L. The summary statistic for each outcome is the mean difference versus placebo for each drug and dose. Negative values mean a lower change than placebo, and positive values mean a greater change than placebo. HbA1c values are stated in the Results text, and body weight and fasting plasma glucose values are given in Tables 1 and 2.

### 3.4 Scope of reporting

The values in this report are descriptive point estimates of mean differences. The report does not describe estimation or testing procedures beyond the summary measure defined above. It does not report confidence intervals, p-values, patient counts, baseline characteristics or safety and tolerability findings. The hypothesis stated in the Discussion is set out for context, and it is not evaluated by a formal inferential procedure in this report.

## 4. Results

Results are reported for every dose of Quendarin (QND-631), Quelmastin (QLM-331) and Mirabexan (MRB-613) at the single time point, week 26. All values are mean differences versus placebo.

For HbA1c, Quendarin (QND-631) at 10 mg daily showed a mean difference vs placebo of -0.54 percentage points. Quelmastin (QLM-331) at 100 mg daily showed a mean difference vs placebo of -0.86 percentage points, and Mirabexan (MRB-613) at 15 mg daily showed a mean difference vs placebo of -0.54 percentage points. All three HbA1c differences are negative. The Quelmastin value is the most negative of the three, and the Quendarin and Mirabexan values are the same.

Table 1 gives the mean differences versus placebo in change in body weight from baseline at week 26. The pattern is mixed across agents. The Quelmastin difference is positive, indicating a greater change in body weight than placebo, whereas the Quendarin and Mirabexan differences are negative, indicating a lower change than placebo.

Table 2 gives the mean differences versus placebo in change in fasting plasma glucose from baseline at week 26. All three differences are negative. The Quelmastin difference is the most negative of the three, followed by the Quendarin and then the Mirabexan difference.

## 5. Discussion

The study was designed to examine whether each agent, taken once daily, changes HbA1c, body weight and fasting plasma glucose relative to placebo, and whether the size of the change differs between the agents. The results are descriptive. For HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose, the direction of every mean difference is negative, meaning a lower change than placebo for all three agents. Body weight does not follow a single direction: the Quelmastin difference is positive, whereas the Quendarin and Mirabexan differences are negative. The glycaemic and fasting glucose values are therefore in the direction described above, while the body weight values are mixed in sign.

On magnitude, the Quelmastin value at 100 mg daily is the most negative of the three for HbA1c and for fasting plasma glucose in this report. Quendarin at 10 mg daily and Mirabexan at 15 mg daily share the same HbA1c mean difference. These are single point estimates obtained at different doses, and they are not a ranking of the drugs. No uncertainty measures are reported, so it cannot be judged whether the differences between agents exceed their variability.

The body weight result for Quelmastin is positive, while the results for Quendarin and Mirabexan are negative. This report does not analyse the body weight result further and does not judge whether the Quelmastin difference is clinically meaningful for dose choice. The sign is reported as observed, and any interpretation requires uncertainty measures and design information that are not part of this report.

Dose is a major confound in the cross-drug comparison. Each drug was studied at a different dose, so drug identity and dose cannot be separated. A difference between Quelmastin and the other two agents may reflect the agent, the dose, or both. For this reason, the comparison describes the doses tested and should not be read as a ranking of the drugs themselves.

Several limitations follow from the design. Only one time point, week 26, was assessed, so the durability of the mean differences after that point is not known. Only mean differences are reported, with no uncertainty measures and no patient counts, so the precision of each value is unknown. Population characteristics and background therapy are not described beyond adults with type 2 diabetes who are taking metformin. Each agent was tested at one dose, so the results do not describe dose-response relationships and apply only to the doses studied. Safety and tolerability are outside the scope of this report, and dose selection should not rest on these values alone.

The per-dose mean differences give readers a descriptive basis for considering the three agents at the doses studied. They do not, on their own, establish clinical superiority of any agent, and they do not address safety or tolerability.

## 6. Conclusion

This randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled study compared Quendarin (QND-631) at 10 mg daily, Quelmastin (QLM-331) at 100 mg daily and Mirabexan (MRB-613) at 15 mg daily with placebo in adults with type 2 diabetes who are taking metformin, with outcomes assessed at week 26. All three agents produced negative mean differences versus placebo for HbA1c and for fasting plasma glucose. The Quelmastin values are the most negative of the three for both measures in this report. Body weight differences varied in sign across the agents, with Quelmastin positive and Quendarin and Mirabexan negative.

The contribution of this report is the per-dose mean difference versus placebo for each measure at week 26. These values are descriptive point estimates. They are limited to a single time point, to mean differences without uncertainty measures, and to the doses and population studied. Safety and tolerability are not addressed, and the values do not establish dose-response relationships or a ranking of the agents.
