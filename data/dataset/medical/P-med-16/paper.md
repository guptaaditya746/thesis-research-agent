# MRB-613 added to metformin: effects on HbA1c, blood pressure and LDL cholesterol

## Abstract

Adults with type 2 diabetes who remain on metformin often need additional treatment, and their management usually also includes blood pressure and LDL cholesterol. This paper reports a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial of MRB-613 in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, comparing MRB-613 15 mg daily and MRB-613 30 mg daily with placebo at weeks 12 and 24. Three measures were evaluated: change from baseline in HbA1c (percentage points), change from baseline in systolic blood pressure (mmHg) and change from baseline in LDL cholesterol (mmol/L). Every result is a mean difference versus placebo, and negative values indicate lower values than placebo. At week 12, the 15 mg and 30 mg doses gave HbA1c differences of -0.40 and -0.57 percentage points, systolic blood pressure differences of -3.9 and -3.4 mmHg, and LDL cholesterol differences of -0.19 and -0.27 mmol/L. At week 24, the corresponding values were -0.49 and -0.55 percentage points for HbA1c, -3.3 and -5.2 mmHg for systolic blood pressure, and -0.26 and -0.31 mmol/L for LDL cholesterol. The 30 mg dose gave larger HbA1c and LDL cholesterol differences than the 15 mg dose at both time points, whereas the blood pressure ordering varied with time. All values are point estimates describing the two doses and two time points studied in this population. No measures of uncertainty are reported, and the results should be read within those limits.

## 1. Introduction

Adults with type 2 diabetes who remain on metformin often need additional treatment to reach their glycaemic goals. Most of these adults also carry substantial cardiovascular risk, so glycaemic control is rarely the only target. Blood pressure and LDL cholesterol commonly need attention at the same time, and a treatment decision in this population is therefore judged against several measures rather than one.

Add-on therapies for metformin-treated patients are usually evaluated on a single primary outcome, typically change in HbA1c from baseline. Blood pressure and lipid measures are often recorded only as secondary or safety endpoints. Their size and direction relative to placebo can then remain unclear to a clinician who is weighing a choice. A reader who wants to know how an add-on agent affects glycaemia alongside blood pressure and lipids must often assemble that answer from separate summaries.

This paper reports a randomised, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial of MRB-613 in adults with type 2 diabetes who are taking metformin. Participants were allocated to MRB-613 15 mg daily, MRB-613 30 mg daily or placebo. The trial evaluates three measures together: change from baseline in HbA1c, change from baseline in systolic blood pressure and change from baseline in LDL cholesterol.

For each measure, the trial reports the mean difference versus placebo at week 12 and at week 24. Reporting both dose levels and both time points allows the reader to compare the size of each difference across doses and across time, and to see whether the pattern changes between the two visits. Each measure is kept on its own scale, so comparisons are made within each measure rather than by combining them into a single score.

The aim is not to rank MRB-613 against other agents or to recommend a single dose. The aim is to give readers the size of each difference so that a dose can be chosen with its trade-offs in view. Conclusions are limited to the population, doses and time points studied, and the trial does not assess time points beyond week 24.

The expected contribution of this study is the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point, presented so that readers can choose a dose. The study is confined to adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin and to the two MRB-613 doses and two time points described here.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this trial falls into three groups, each described by its approach rather than by its findings.

### 2.1 Add-on glucose-lowering trials on metformin

Placebo-controlled add-on studies in adults already taking metformin form the most common design for this question. Such studies usually take change in HbA1c from baseline as the single primary outcome, which suits the evaluation of glucose-lowering efficacy. Where blood pressure or lipid measures are recorded, they tend to be secondary or safety measures. This design is well suited to glycaemic efficacy but is less often arranged to show several cardiometabolic changes on equal footing. The present trial names HbA1c, systolic blood pressure and LDL cholesterol as its evaluation metrics, each expressed as a mean difference versus placebo.

### 2.2 Multi-measure cardiometabolic assessment

Some studies record glycaemic and cardiovascular-risk measures together, reflecting the combined risk carried by people with diabetes. When several measures are recorded, results are often summarised in narrative form, with the direction of effect described for each and the magnitude given only in part. This makes it difficult to compare the size of differences across measures, or to see how those differences change with dose. The present design reports a numeric mean difference for each measure, dose and time point, arranged in tables, so that magnitudes can be compared directly.

### 2.3 Dose-comparison designs for add-on agents

Parallel-group trials that compare two fixed doses of an add-on agent against placebo are a standard way to examine whether an effect depends on dose. These designs are often reported at a single time point, such as the end of treatment or one intermediate visit. A single time point shows the dose effect at that moment but not how it develops over treatment, and differences between doses can look different early and later. The present trial reports both week 12 and week 24 for each dose, so the dose effect can be read at two time points.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Design and study population

The study was a randomised controlled trial with a parallel-group design, in which MRB-613 was compared against placebo. The study population was adults with type 2 diabetes who were taking metformin. Participants were allocated to one of three arms: MRB-613 15 mg daily, MRB-613 30 mg daily or placebo. The drug is referred to throughout by its development code, MRB-613, and by no other name.

### 3.2 Outcomes, time points and reporting

Three evaluation metrics were measured, each as a change from baseline: change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points; change in systolic blood pressure from baseline, expressed in mmHg; and change in LDL cholesterol from baseline, expressed in mmol/L. Each measure was analysed and reported separately, and no composite score was formed.

Each measure was assessed at two time points, week 12 and week 24, for each MRB-613 dose and for placebo.

Every reported value is the mean difference versus placebo. Negative values mean that the result was lower than placebo, and positive values would mean higher than placebo. Values are reported exactly as they were obtained, with their decimal places and units. No other statistics are reported in this paper.

## 4. Results

The results for the three measures are presented in turn. Table 1, Table 2 and Table 3 give the mean differences versus placebo for HbA1c, systolic blood pressure and LDL cholesterol, respectively, by MRB-613 dose and time point. Where a value is not shown in a table, it is stated in the text.

### 4.1 Change in HbA1c

Table 1 reports the change in HbA1c from baseline for MRB-613 versus placebo at week 12. At this time point, the mean difference versus placebo was -0.40 percentage points for MRB-613 15 mg daily and -0.57 percentage points for MRB-613 30 mg daily.

Table 1: Change in HbA1c from baseline for MRB-613 versus placebo, by dose and time point (mean difference vs placebo)

| Dose of MRB-613 | Time point | Change in HbA1c vs placebo (percentage points) |
|---|---|---|
| 15 mg daily | Week 12 | -0.40 |
| 30 mg daily | Week 12 | -0.57 |

At week 24, the mean difference versus placebo was -0.49 percentage points for MRB-613 15 mg daily. For MRB-613 30 mg daily, the mean difference versus placebo at week 24 was -0.55 percentage points. The 30 mg differences were larger in magnitude than the 15 mg differences at both time points, and the 15 mg difference increased in magnitude from week 12 to week 24, while the 30 mg difference was similar at the two visits.

### 4.2 Change in systolic blood pressure

Table 2 reports the change in systolic blood pressure from baseline for MRB-613 versus placebo at both time points and both doses. All four mean differences were negative, indicating lower values than placebo.

Table 2: Change in systolic blood pressure from baseline for MRB-613 versus placebo, by dose and time point (mean difference vs placebo)

| Dose of MRB-613 | Time point | Change in systolic blood pressure vs placebo (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|
| 15 mg daily | Week 12 | -3.9 |
| 15 mg daily | Week 24 | -3.3 |
| 30 mg daily | Week 12 | -3.4 |
| 30 mg daily | Week 24 | -5.2 |

The pattern differed between the two doses. For the 15 mg dose, the largest mean difference was at week 12 (-3.9 mmHg), and the week 24 value was -3.3 mmHg. For the 30 mg dose, the largest mean difference was at week 24 (-5.2 mmHg), compared with -3.4 mmHg at week 12. Thus the dose giving the larger difference changed with time point, and the two doses were close at week 12.

### 4.3 Change in LDL cholesterol

Table 3 reports the change in LDL cholesterol from baseline for MRB-613 versus placebo at both time points and both doses. All four mean differences were negative.

Table 3: Change in LDL cholesterol from baseline for MRB-613 versus placebo, by dose and time point (mean difference vs placebo)

| Dose of MRB-613 | Time point | Change in LDL cholesterol vs placebo (mmol/L) |
|---|---|---|
| 15 mg daily | Week 12 | -0.19 |
| 15 mg daily | Week 24 | -0.26 |
| 30 mg daily | Week 12 | -0.27 |
| 30 mg daily | Week 24 | -0.31 |

Both doses showed larger mean differences at week 24 than at week 12. The 30 mg dose showed a larger mean difference than the 15 mg dose at each time point, with -0.27 mmol/L against -0.19 mmol/L at week 12 and -0.31 mmol/L against -0.26 mmol/L at week 24.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Interpretation of dose and time effects

The hypothesis was that MRB-613 changes HbA1c, systolic blood pressure and LDL cholesterol more than placebo, and that the size of the change depends on dose. Across Tables 1 to 3, every mean difference versus placebo was negative, so each reported value was lower than placebo for each measure, dose and time point studied. This is consistent with the first part of the hypothesis, although the comparison is descriptive and no statistical testing is discussed here.

For HbA1c and LDL cholesterol, the 30 mg dose gave larger magnitudes than the 15 mg dose at both time points. This order is consistent with the second part of the hypothesis, that the size of the change depends on dose. The blood pressure results do not show a uniform dose order. At week 12, the 15 mg difference (-3.9 mmHg) was slightly larger in magnitude than the 30 mg difference (-3.4 mmHg), whereas at week 24 the 30 mg difference (-5.2 mmHg) was larger than the 15 mg difference (-3.3 mmHg). The dose relationship for blood pressure therefore depends on the time point, and the dose-related pattern is clearest for HbA1c and LDL cholesterol.

The time pattern also differed across measures. The LDL cholesterol differences were larger at week 24 than at week 12 for both doses, and the 15 mg HbA1c difference was larger at week 24 than at week 12. The 30 mg HbA1c difference was similar at both time points. Reporting both visits makes these differences visible, which a single time point would not have shown.

### 5.2 Limitations

Only point estimates of the mean difference versus placebo are reported. No measures of uncertainty are presented, so the precision of each value cannot be judged from this paper, and the size of the differences should be read as descriptive.

Only two doses of MRB-613 and two time points, week 12 and week 24, were studied. The results therefore do not support conclusions about other doses, about longer treatment durations, or about time points beyond week 24.

The results apply to adults with type 2 diabetes who are taking metformin. They should not be extended to other populations, to people not taking metformin, or to other treatment combinations without further evaluation.

This paper does not report patient numbers, baseline values or secondary outcomes. Without these, the reader cannot assess the composition of the groups or the wider effects of MRB-613 beyond the three measures described here.

## 6. Conclusion

MRB-613 at 15 mg and 30 mg daily, added to metformin in adults with type 2 diabetes, produced mean differences versus placebo in HbA1c, systolic blood pressure and LDL cholesterol at both week 12 and week 24. All reported differences were negative, indicating lower values than placebo. For HbA1c and LDL cholesterol, the 30 mg dose generally showed larger differences than the 15 mg dose, while the blood pressure ordering varied with time point. These findings are limited to the two doses, two time points and population studied, and they are reported as point estimates without measures of uncertainty.

By giving the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point, this paper provides the information needed to compare doses for the studied population. Further evaluation is needed before the findings are applied to other populations, doses or treatment durations.
