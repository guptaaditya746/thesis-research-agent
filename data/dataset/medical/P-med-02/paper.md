# Brenoxalin added to metformin in adults with type 2 diabetes: mean differences versus placebo in HbA1c and body weight at weeks 36 and 52

## Abstract

Background: For adults with type 2 diabetes taking metformin, the effects of Brenoxalin on the change in HbA1c from baseline and on the change in body weight from baseline, relative to placebo, are reported here together at matched time points. Design: This report presents results from a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial. Participants were allocated to Brenoxalin 20 mg daily, Brenoxalin 40 mg daily, or placebo. Measures: Change in HbA1c from baseline (percentage points) and change in body weight from baseline (kg), each expressed as the mean difference versus placebo at week 36 and week 52. Results: For HbA1c, the mean differences at week 36 were -0.78 percentage points (20 mg daily) and -0.98 percentage points (40 mg daily), and at week 52 they were -0.82 percentage points and -1.05 percentage points, respectively. For body weight, the mean differences at week 36 were -1.2 kg (20 mg daily) and -1.7 kg (40 mg daily), and at week 52 they were -1.2 kg and -1.8 kg, respectively. Conclusion: In these point estimates, both doses were lower than placebo on both measures, with larger differences at 40 mg daily than at 20 mg daily at both time points. No measures of uncertainty, patient counts or baseline values are reported, so the precision of these differences cannot be judged and they should be regarded as descriptive.

## 1. Introduction

For adults with type 2 diabetes who are already taking metformin, treatment decisions depend on more than one outcome. Glycaemic control, measured as the change in HbA1c from baseline, is the central target of therapy. Body weight is also a clinically important consideration, because an additional agent that improves glycaemic control but adds weight, or that leaves weight unaddressed, may suit some patients less well than others. An add-on agent for this population should therefore be considered on both measures together rather than on glycaemic control alone.

For adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, the effect of Brenoxalin on the change in HbA1c from baseline and on the change in body weight from baseline, relative to placebo, is the question addressed here. A placebo comparison is needed to separate the effect of the agent from the background treatment and from the natural course of the disease. Dose adds a further question. A reader choosing between doses needs to know whether the size of the difference from placebo differs between doses, and whether that difference changes between the two time points reported.

This report addresses that question using a randomised, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial. Participants were adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, allocated to Brenoxalin 20 mg daily, Brenoxalin 40 mg daily, or placebo. Both measures were assessed at week 36 and at week 52. Presenting both time points for each dose allows the comparison against placebo to be read across time as well as across dose.

Our working hypothesis is that Brenoxalin, taken once daily, changes both HbA1c and body weight more than placebo, and that the size of the change depends on the dose. The two measures are treated separately, so that a dose pattern present for one measure but not the other can be reported as such rather than merged into a single summary.

The contribution of this study is the mean difference versus placebo for each measure, dose and time point. For HbA1c and for body weight, the report gives the difference for Brenoxalin 20 mg and 40 mg daily at week 36 and week 52, so that readers can compare doses directly and consider both measures together. The scope is deliberately limited: the study addresses these two measures, these two doses and these two time points in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, and it makes no claim beyond that population or those endpoints.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this report can be grouped into three general approaches. Each is described below only by its design, and no specific finding is attributed to any prior study.

### 2.1 Placebo-controlled add-on trials in metformin-treated adults

Add-on agents given to patients who are already receiving metformin, compared with placebo, form the most direct design precedent for this report. Such trials provide the placebo comparison needed to separate a drug effect from background therapy. Their emphasis, however, may fall mainly on glycaemic endpoints, with body weight treated as a secondary or exploratory outcome. Where both measures are collected, they may not be presented at matched time points, which makes it harder to read the glycaemic and weight effects together. The present report addresses this by stating HbA1c and body weight as its two study measures, each expressed as a mean difference versus placebo at matched time points.

### 2.2 Multi-dose comparisons of glucose-lowering agents

Designs that compare two or more doses of an antidiabetic agent against placebo show how dose relates to the size of the effect. These designs are informative because they allow the response to be examined across doses rather than assuming that it is uniform. A limitation is that dose effects may be summarised at a single endpoint, so the time course of the difference between doses remains unclear. Two doses may look similar at one time point and differ at another, and a single summary cannot show this. The present report gives the mean difference versus placebo for two doses, 20 mg and 40 mg daily, at two time points, which allows a dose-by-time reading of both measures.

### 2.3 Extended-duration follow-up of antidiabetic therapy

Longer-duration follow-up phases indicate whether an effect is maintained over a longer interval. Results from such phases may, however, be reported in aggregate or without per-dose contrasts against placebo. This limits how a reader can select a dose, because the information needed for that choice is not separated by dose. The present report gives per-dose mean differences versus placebo at week 36 and week 52 for both measures, so that the later time point can be read against the earlier one for each dose.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design and participants

This report describes a randomised controlled trial with parallel groups, in which Brenoxalin was compared with placebo. The study population was adults with type 2 diabetes who were taking metformin. Results are reported at week 36 and week 52. Patient counts and baseline values are not part of the reported results and are not given in this paper. The parent trial design, the randomisation procedure, the blinding arrangements, the trial registration and the handling of dropouts are not described in this report.

### 3.2 Interventions and time points

Three arms were included: Brenoxalin 20 mg once daily, Brenoxalin 40 mg once daily, and placebo. Each measure was assessed at two time points, week 36 and week 52. Results are reported for every Brenoxalin dose at every time point. In all analyses the comparator is placebo, so every value in this report is a difference relative to placebo and no active comparator is included.

### 3.3 Outcome measures and reporting

Two outcome measures were analysed. The first is the change in HbA1c from baseline, expressed in percentage points. The second is the change in body weight from baseline, expressed in kilograms. For each measure, dose and time point, the reported statistic is the mean difference versus placebo. Negative values indicate a value lower than placebo. The estimation method, any adjustment for baseline, and any measures of uncertainty or dispersion are not reported here, so no confidence intervals, p-values or measures of dispersion are presented. Values are given with their units and with the decimal places at which they were obtained.

At week 52, the mean difference versus placebo in the change in HbA1c from baseline was -0.82 percentage points with Brenoxalin 20 mg daily and -1.05 percentage points with Brenoxalin 40 mg daily. Both doses were lower than placebo at both time points, and the 40 mg daily arm showed the larger difference at each time point. For both doses, the difference at week 52 was larger than at week 36.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Interpretation of dose and time

The results are consistent with the working hypothesis that Brenoxalin changes both HbA1c and body weight relative to placebo, with larger differences at 40 mg daily than at 20 mg daily. For both measures and at both time points, the 40 mg daily arm showed a larger mean difference from placebo than the 20 mg daily arm. These are point estimates, and no formal test of the dose contrast is reported here, so the dose pattern should be read as descriptive. The pattern appeared for HbA1c and for body weight alike, although, without measures of precision, it cannot be determined whether the body-weight differences exceed measurement variability. The two measures are therefore interpreted separately.

The time course differed between the two measures. For HbA1c, the difference from placebo was larger at week 52 than at week 36 for both doses. For body weight, the difference at 20 mg daily was the same at both time points, whereas at 40 mg daily it was slightly larger at week 52. These descriptive patterns show that the two time points differ for most comparisons. Two time points cannot, however, characterise the course of the effect between or beyond them, and the report does not indicate whether the differences would continue to change or level off.

The dose statements rest on two doses only. Any relationship between dose and effect across a wider dose range cannot be inferred from these data. The per-dose, per-time-point presentation was chosen so that a reader can see each comparison directly rather than relying on a single pooled summary.

### 5.2 Limitations

The report gives mean differences versus placebo only. No confidence intervals, p-values, patient counts or baseline values are reported, so the precision of each difference and its clinical relevance cannot be judged from this report. Readers should interpret the differences as point estimates without a stated margin of error. The estimation method, any adjustment for baseline and the handling of missing data are not described, and no formal test of the dose contrast is presented.

Only two doses of Brenoxalin and one comparator (placebo) are included. No active comparator is reported, so the findings cannot be placed against other glucose-lowering or weight-related treatments in this population. The population is restricted to adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, and the findings should not be extended to other groups without further data.

The parent trial, its primary and secondary endpoints, and whether week 36 was a pre-specified endpoint are not described in this report. The reasons for selecting week 36 and week 52, and whether other time points were analysed, are also not described. The measures reported are HbA1c and body weight only. Safety and tolerability data are not reported here, and no other glycaemic measures are described. These omissions limit the evidence available for dose selection, and the report does not support a dose recommendation on the basis of these differences alone.

Future reports would be more useful if they included uncertainty measures, patient counts, baseline values, the estimation method and safety data alongside the mean differences, so that the precision and the evidential weight of each comparison could be judged directly.

## 6. Conclusion

In adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, Brenoxalin 20 mg daily and Brenoxalin 40 mg daily gave mean differences versus placebo in both HbA1c and body weight at week 36 and week 52. In these point estimates, the 40 mg daily differences were larger than the 20 mg daily differences at both time points for both measures. The contribution of this report is a set of per-measure, per-dose and per-time-point mean differences versus placebo. Because the report gives no measures of uncertainty, no safety data and covers only two doses, two time points and two measures, these differences should be read as descriptive and within the limitations described above.
