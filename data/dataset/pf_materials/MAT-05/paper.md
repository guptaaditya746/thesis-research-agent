# Hardness and Fatigue: Cross-Property Correlations in Alloys

## Abstract

In titanium alloy development, hardness is frequently used as a proxy for mechanical performance, including fatigue resistance. This paper examines whether improvements in hardness for one alloy, Sapifin-2, reliably predict fatigue life improvements in a different alloy, Stetrugel-6. The analysis synthesizes findings from four recent studies: the Putro Lab 2021 study, which assessed hardness; the Bopor Lab 2021 study, which evaluated corrosion resistance; and the Tofere Lab 2025 and Fulel Lab 2025 studies, which measured fatigue life. The Putro Lab 2021 study reported an improvement in hardness for both Sapifin-2 and Stetrugel-6. However, the Fulel Lab 2025 study reported no change in fatigue life for Stetrugel-6, while reporting an improvement for Sapifin-2. Similarly, the Tofere Lab 2025 study reported an improvement in fatigue life for Sapifin-2. These findings indicate a disconnect between hardness gains and fatigue outcomes in Stetrugel-6. The paper argues that hardness improvements in Sapifin-2 do not necessarily correlate with fatigue life improvements in Stetrugel-6. This divergence suggests that single-property correlations are insufficient for predicting fatigue behavior across different alloy compositions. The evidence supports a cautious approach to extrapolating mechanical property correlations, emphasizing the necessity of direct fatigue testing for accurate performance assessment in titanium alloys.

## 1. Introduction

In the development and qualification of titanium alloys, hardness is frequently employed as a convenient proxy for broader mechanical performance, including resistance to fatigue failure. This practice is rooted in the empirical observation that harder materials often exhibit higher yield strengths, which can, in some contexts, correlate with improved fatigue life. However, the reliability of this proxy relationship is not universal, particularly when comparing distinct alloy compositions or when the underlying microstructural mechanisms governing crack initiation and propagation differ significantly. For the specific titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, the assumption that hardness gains translate directly into fatigue benefits remains a subject of debate, especially in light of recent, seemingly conflicting experimental evidence.

The central issue addressed in this study is whether improvements in hardness for one alloy, specifically Sapifin-2, can be used to reliably predict fatigue life improvements in a different alloy, Stetrugel-6. Recent investigations have produced data that challenges the straightforward extrapolation of hardness metrics across these two materials. Specifically, earlier hardness assessments indicated positive trends for both alloys, while more recent fatigue-specific testing has revealed a disconnect between these hardness gains and actual fatigue performance in Stetrugel-6. This discrepancy suggests that the hardness-fatigue correlation may be alloy-dependent and potentially misleading when applied across different material systems without careful consideration of the specific testing methodologies and microstructural contexts.

This paper argues that hardness improvements in Sapifin-2 do not necessarily correlate with fatigue life improvements in Stetrugel-6. The evidence suggests that while Sapifin-2 may benefit from increased hardness in terms of fatigue resistance, Stetrugel-6 does not exhibit a similar response, indicating that the mechanical properties of these two alloys are governed by different underlying factors. Consequently, relying solely on hardness data to predict fatigue behavior in Stetrugel-6 based on observations from Sapifin-2 is not supported by the available experimental record. The divergence in outcomes highlights the limitations of single-property correlations in complex alloy systems.

To support this thesis, the analysis proceeds in a chronological manner, examining the sequence of studies that have contributed to the current understanding of these alloys. The discussion begins with the 2021 hardness assessments, which established the baseline mechanical properties for both Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2. It then moves to the 2025 fatigue life investigations, which employed different testing methods to evaluate the long-term durability of these materials. By tracing the evolution of the evidence from hardness-focused to fatigue-focused assessments, the paper identifies the points where the correlation between the two properties breaks down. This chronological approach allows for a clear delineation of how the interpretation of the data has shifted as more specific fatigue data became available.

The structure of the argument is designed to first present the factual findings from the relevant studies, then to synthesize these findings into a coherent narrative that supports the proposed thesis. Following the chronological analysis, the paper addresses potential counter-arguments that might suggest a continued validity of the hardness-fatigue link. These counter-arguments are evaluated against the specific data points from the 2025 fatigue tests, which provide the most direct evidence regarding the fatigue performance of Stetrugel-6. By systematically addressing these opposing views, the paper aims to provide a robust defense of the position that hardness is not a reliable predictor of fatigue life for Stetrugel-6 in this context.

The primary contribution of this study is a clear account of how the available experimental evidence treats the relationship between hardness and fatigue life in Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2. It provides an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be followed when assessing the mechanical performance of these alloys, specifically advocating for the prioritization of direct fatigue testing over indirect hardness-based predictions for Stetrugel-6. This analysis offers a critical perspective on the use of proxy properties in materials science, emphasizing the need for direct validation of fatigue performance in alloy development and qualification processes.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Hardness Assessment Approaches

Hardness assessment in titanium alloys has traditionally relied on micro-Vickers testing, a method that provides a localized measure of resistance to plastic deformation. This approach is widely used due to its simplicity and the ability to obtain data from small sample sizes. However, a significant limitation of this methodology is that it may not capture the complex microstructural factors that govern fatigue crack initiation and propagation. Hardness is a static property that reflects the material's resistance to indentation, whereas fatigue life is a dynamic property influenced by cyclic loading, microstructural heterogeneity, and defect sensitivity. In the context of the alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, hardness data from earlier studies, such as those conducted in 2021, indicated improvements in both materials. Yet, these measurements do not inherently account for the specific mechanisms that lead to fatigue failure, which can vary significantly between different alloy compositions. This paper examines whether such hardness data can be extrapolated to predict fatigue behavior, highlighting the inherent limitations of relying on single-property correlations to infer complex mechanical performance.

### 2.2 Fatigue Life Assessment Approaches

Fatigue life assessment typically involves dynamic testing methods, such as bending fatigue and rotating beam tests, which subject the material to cyclic loads until failure. These methods are more directly relevant to the service conditions of many engineering components but are sensitive to the specific test configuration used. For instance, bending fatigue and rotating beam tests can yield different results for the same material due to variations in stress distribution, surface finish effects, and the nature of the applied load. This sensitivity complicates cross-study comparisons, as differences in test methodology can obscure underlying material properties. In the case of Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, recent studies from 2025 employed both bending and rotating beam methods to evaluate fatigue life. The findings from these tests revealed that while Sapifin-2 showed improved fatigue performance, Stetrugel-6 did not exhibit a corresponding change, despite earlier hardness gains. This paper compares these fatigue findings to assess the consistency of the results and their relevance to the broader question of hardness-fatigue correlation, emphasizing the need for careful interpretation of fatigue data in the context of specific testing protocols.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Hardness Evidence (2021)

The foundational data for this analysis comes from the Putro Lab 2021 study, which employed micro-Vickers hardness testing to evaluate the mechanical properties of the two titanium alloys. This study provided the initial baseline for comparing the static mechanical response of Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2. The results indicated a positive trend in hardness for both materials. Specifically, the Putro Lab 2021 study reported an improvement in hardness for alloy Sapifin-2. Concurrently, the same study reported an improvement in hardness for alloy Stetrugel-6. These parallel improvements suggest that, at the level of static indentation resistance, both alloys responded similarly to the conditions or treatments assessed in 2021. This simultaneous enhancement in hardness might initially lead one to expect a corresponding parallel improvement in other mechanical properties, such as fatigue life, for both alloys. However, as the subsequent sections will demonstrate, this assumption does not hold when direct fatigue testing is considered.

### 3.2 Corrosion Resistance Context (2021)

To provide a broader context for the mechanical properties of Stetrugel-6, it is relevant to consider the Bopor Lab 2021 study, which focused on corrosion resistance using electrochemical impedance testing. This study did not assess hardness or fatigue directly but provided insight into the stability of Stetrugel-6 in corrosive environments. The Bopor Lab 2021 study reported no change in corrosion resistance for alloy Stetrugel-6. This finding is significant because it indicates that the properties of Stetrugel-6 are not uniformly improving across all metrics. While hardness increased, corrosion resistance remained static. This divergence within the 2021 data set already hints at the complexity of Stetrugel-6's material behavior, suggesting that improvements in one property do not automatically translate to improvements in others. This lack of uniformity in property enhancement sets the stage for the more pronounced disconnect observed in the 2025 fatigue data.

### 3.3 Fatigue Life Evidence (2025)

The most critical evidence for the thesis comes from the 2025 studies, which directly measured fatigue life. Two distinct studies were conducted: the Fulel Lab 2025 study, which used a rotating beam test, and the Tofere Lab 2025 study, which used a bending fatigue test. These methods, while both assessing fatigue, differ in their stress application and may yield different sensitivities to material defects. The Fulel Lab 2025 study reported no change in fatigue life for alloy Stetrugel-6. This is a crucial finding, as it directly contradicts the expectation that the hardness improvements noted in 2021 would lead to better fatigue performance. In contrast, the Fulel Lab 2025 study reported an improvement in fatigue life for alloy Sapifin-2. This result aligns with the hardness data for Sapifin-2, suggesting a potential correlation for this specific alloy. To verify the consistency of this finding, the Tofere Lab 2025 study was also considered. The Tofere Lab 2025 study reported an improvement in fatigue life for alloy Sapifin-2. The fact that both 2025 studies, using different methodologies, reported improved fatigue life for Sapifin-2 reinforces the reliability of this result. However, the absence of a similar improvement in Stetrugel-6, despite its hardness gains, highlights a fundamental difference in how these two alloys respond to mechanical enhancements. The disconnect between the hardness data and the fatigue data for Stetrugel-6 is the central point of contention in this analysis.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

A plausible counter-argument to the thesis is that hardness is a fundamental mechanical property that is intrinsically linked to fatigue resistance. Proponents of this view might argue that since both Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2 are titanium alloys, and both showed hardness improvements in the Putro Lab 2021 study, it is logical to expect that these improvements would translate to better fatigue performance in both materials. This argument relies on the general principle that harder materials are more resistant to plastic deformation, which can delay crack initiation. Furthermore, the fact that Sapifin-2 showed both hardness and fatigue improvements supports the idea that the hardness-fatigue link is valid. Therefore, one might argue that the lack of fatigue improvement in Stetrugel-6 is an anomaly or a result of experimental error, rather than a fundamental disconnect. This counter-argument suggests that the hardness data should be given more weight, and that the fatigue data for Stetrugel-6 might be misleading due to the specific test conditions used in 2025.

### 4.2 Rebuttal from the Findings

This counter-argument is rebutted by the direct evidence from the Fulel Lab 2025 study, which reported no change in fatigue life for alloy Stetrugel-6 despite the documented hardness improvements. The consistency of this finding with the corrosion resistance data from the Bopor Lab 2021 study, which also showed no change, further supports the idea that Stetrugel-6's properties are not uniformly responsive to the factors that improved its hardness. Moreover, the fact that both the Fulel Lab 2025 and Tofere Lab 2025 studies reported improved fatigue life for Sapifin-2, using different test methods, indicates that the fatigue gain in Sapifin-2 is robust and not an artifact of a specific testing protocol. This reinforces the conclusion that the difference in fatigue outcomes between the two alloys is real and not due to methodological inconsistencies. The lack of fatigue improvement in Stetrugel-6, despite hardness gains, demonstrates that hardness is not a reliable predictor of fatigue life across different alloy compositions. The microstructural mechanisms governing fatigue crack initiation and propagation in Stetrugel-6 appear to be distinct from those in Sapifin-2, such that improvements in one property do not necessarily translate to improvements in the other. Therefore, the thesis stands: hardness improvements in Sapifin-2 do not necessarily correlate with fatigue life improvements in Stetrugel-6.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the relationship between hardness and fatigue life in the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, focusing on the evidence from four recent studies. The analysis revealed that while the Putro Lab 2021 study reported hardness improvements in both alloys, the subsequent fatigue testing in 2025 showed a clear divergence in outcomes. The Fulel Lab 2025 study reported no change in fatigue life for Stetrugel-6, whereas it reported an improvement for Sapifin-2. This finding was corroborated by the Tofere Lab 2025 study, which also reported an improvement in fatigue life for Sapifin-2. The Bopor Lab 2021 study, which assessed corrosion resistance, reported no change for Stetrugel-6, further indicating that this alloy's properties are not uniformly improving. These findings support the thesis that hardness improvements in Sapifin-2 do not necessarily correlate with fatigue life improvements in Stetrugel-6. The evidence suggests that the mechanical properties of these two alloys are governed by different underlying factors, and that single-property correlations are insufficient for predicting fatigue behavior across different alloy compositions. Researchers should not assume cross-alloy correlations between hardness and fatigue life without direct fatigue testing. The evidence supports a cautious approach to extrapolating mechanical property correlations in titanium alloys, emphasizing the need for direct validation of fatigue performance in alloy development and qualification processes.
