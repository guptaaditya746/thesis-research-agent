# Wear Rate Stability of Ceramic Coatings: A Comparative Analysis of 2024 and 2013 Studies

## Abstract

The reliability of tribological data for ceramic coatings is critical for industrial application, yet consistency across different testing methodologies and time periods remains a significant challenge. This paper addresses the discrepancy in reported performance for the ceramic coating Bumom-7, where recent studies indicate no change in wear rate under specific conditions, conflicting with earlier reports of improvement in related properties. In contrast, the coating Kogal-8 demonstrates a stable improvement trend across the examined period. By analyzing four studies conducted between 2012 and 2024, this work evaluates the coherence of these findings. The analysis reveals that while Kogal-8 shows consistent positive outcomes in thermal conductivity, oxidation resistance, and wear rate, Bumom-7 presents a mixed profile. Earlier investigations reported improvements in oxidation resistance and thermal conductivity for Bumom-7, but the most recent tribological assessment found no change in its wear rate. This divergence suggests that the stability of Bumom-7 is not uniform across all material properties or testing regimes. The paper argues that the line of evidence supporting Kogal-8’s stable improvement is more consistent and reliable for predicting long-term performance. Conversely, the conflicting reports for Bumom-7 highlight the need for caution when extrapolating property-specific improvements to overall tribological stability. The findings underscore the importance of methodological consistency and the distinction between different material properties when evaluating coating durability. It is important to note that this analysis is based on a limited dataset of four studies, and the conclusions are qualitative in nature, reflecting the consistency of reported outcomes rather than statistical significance.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of tribological data for ceramic coatings is essential for their industrial application, yet consistency across different testing methodologies and time periods remains a significant challenge. As manufacturers seek to optimize coating performance for high-stress environments, the ability to trust historical data alongside recent measurements is critical for predicting long-term durability. This study focuses on the comparative analysis of wear rate stability for two specific ceramic coatings, Bumom-7 and Kogal-8, to evaluate the coherence of their reported performance trajectories.

A central conflict in the existing literature concerns the reported performance of Bumom-7. Data spanning from 2012 to 2024 reveals inconsistencies in how this material’s properties have been characterized. While some earlier reports indicated specific improvements in related material properties, recent tribological assessments suggest a different picture regarding its mechanical wear behavior. This discrepancy creates uncertainty about the true performance trajectory of Bumom-7, making it difficult to determine whether its stability is a genuine material characteristic or an artifact of varying test conditions.

In contrast, Kogal-8 presents a more consistent narrative. Across the examined time frame, Kogal-8 demonstrates a stable improvement trend in its tested properties. This consistency provides a useful benchmark for evaluating the reliability of data for other materials in the same family. By comparing the erratic reporting of Bumom-7 against the steady progression of Kogal-8, this study highlights the importance of methodological consistency in materials science research.

The thesis of this paper is that recent evidence suggests Bumom-7’s wear rate is stable under specific conditions, but this finding conflicts with earlier reports of improvement in related properties. Meanwhile, Kogal-8’s improvement remains consistent across different testing contexts. This divergence suggests that the reliability of wear rate data depends heavily on the specific methodology employed and the temporal context of the measurement.

To resolve this ambiguity, this study analyzes the available studies in chronological order. By examining how each study treated the materials and what specific properties were measured, we can determine which line of evidence is more reliable. This approach allows for a nuanced understanding of how property-specific tests and direct tribological assessments interact to form a complete picture of material stability.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how different studies treated the ceramic coatings Bumom-7 and Kogal-8. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be followed for predicting wear rate stability. Third, it establishes a framework for evaluating the consistency of tribological data across different testing methodologies and time periods. It is important to note that this analysis is based on a limited dataset of four studies, and the conclusions are qualitative in nature, reflecting the consistency of reported outcomes rather than statistical significance.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Early Characterization Studies (2012-2014)

Early research on ceramic coatings primarily focused on thermal and oxidation properties rather than direct tribological performance. Studies conducted between 2012 and 2014 employed methods such as laser flash analysis, steady-state heat flow, and isothermal oxidation to assess material stability. These investigations provided foundational data on how coatings like Bumom-7 and Kogal-8 behaved under thermal stress and oxidative environments. However, these property-specific tests did not directly measure wear rate under mechanical stress. Consequently, they may have missed the specific tribological behavior that is critical for industrial applications. This study integrates these early findings with recent wear rate data to provide a comprehensive view of material stability, addressing the gap between property-specific tests and overall performance consistency.

### 2.2 Recent Tribological Assessments (2024)

More recent work has shifted focus to direct wear rate measurement, using methods such as ball-on-plate tests to evaluate mechanical durability. The 2024 studies provide direct data on how Bumom-7 and Kogal-8 perform under tribological conditions. However, these assessments may not fully account for the variability observed in earlier, property-specific tests. This limitation could lead to potential overconfidence in the stability of materials like Bumom-7, whose earlier characterization showed mixed results. By comparing the 2024 wear rate data with the 2012-2014 property data, this study evaluates whether the recent stability claim holds up against the broader historical context of material performance. This comparative approach ensures that the interpretation of wear rate stability is grounded in a complete understanding of the material's behavior across different testing regimes.

### 2.3 Limitations of the Dataset

It is crucial to acknowledge the limitations of the dataset used in this analysis. The study is based on a small number of four studies conducted between 2012 and 2024, which is insufficient for drawing generalizable conclusions about material stability. The data is qualitative in nature, relying on reported outcomes (improvement, no change, degradation) rather than quantitative values, error bars, or statistical significance. This lack of quantitative data limits the ability to perform rigorous statistical analysis or to determine the magnitude of the reported changes. Furthermore, the studies were conducted by different labs using different methodologies, which may introduce variability in the results. The potential for protocol variations, sample preparation differences, and environmental conditions between the different labs and years is not accounted for in this analysis. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of this paper. The conclusions are therefore preliminary and should be viewed as a qualitative assessment of the consistency of reported outcomes rather than a definitive statement about the material properties of Bumom-7 and Kogal-8.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of the Studies

To evaluate the consistency of the reported data, the available studies are examined in chronological order, focusing on the specific property tested and the outcome for each material. The earliest study in this dataset is the Legrer Lab 2012 study, which utilized laser flash analysis to assess thermal conductivity. In this investigation, the coating Kogal-8 was found to exhibit an improvement in thermal conductivity. No data regarding Bumom-7 is reported in this specific study.

Following this, the Budrol Lab 2013 study employed isothermal oxidation to evaluate oxidation resistance. This study provided data for both materials. The results indicated that the coating Bumom-7 showed an improvement in oxidation resistance. Similarly, the coating Kogal-8 also demonstrated an improvement in oxidation resistance in this test. This finding is notable as it represents the first instance in the dataset where Bumom-7 is reported to have a positive performance outcome.

The next study, the Brusizal Lab 2014 study, used steady-state heat flow to measure thermal conductivity. In this assessment, the coating Bumom-7 was reported to have an improvement in thermal conductivity. This study did not report on Kogal-8. Thus, by 2014, Bumom-7 had been associated with positive outcomes in two different property tests: oxidation resistance in 2013 and thermal conductivity in 2014.

The most recent study, the Zekistam Lab 2024 study, shifted the focus to direct tribological performance by measuring wear rate using a ball-on-plate test. This study tested both materials. For the coating Bumom-7, the study reported no change in wear rate. In contrast, the coating Kogal-8 was reported to have an improvement in wear rate. This 2024 finding is critical because it is the only direct measurement of the property in question (wear rate) for Bumom-7 in this dataset.

### 3.2 Consistency and Inconsistency in the Findings

The chronological analysis reveals a stark contrast in the consistency of the reported performance for the two coatings. Kogal-8 demonstrates a high degree of consistency. It was reported to have an improvement in thermal conductivity in 2012, an improvement in oxidation resistance in 2013, and an improvement in wear rate in 2024. Across three different years and three different properties, the direction of the result for Kogal-8 is uniformly positive. This suggests a stable and reliable performance trajectory for this material.

Bumom-7, however, presents a more complex and inconsistent picture. In 2013, it was reported to have an improvement in oxidation resistance. In 2014, it was reported to have an improvement in thermal conductivity. However, in the 2024 study, which directly measured wear rate, no change was reported for Bumom-7. This creates a discrepancy. The earlier studies suggest that Bumom-7 is a material that improves in specific properties, but the most recent and direct tribological assessment indicates that its wear rate is stable rather than improving. The absence of a reported improvement in wear rate, despite earlier positive findings in related properties, raises questions about the transferability of these improvements to mechanical wear behavior.

The gap between the earlier reports of improvement in oxidation resistance and thermal conductivity, and the recent finding of no change in wear rate, suggests that the performance of Bumom-7 may be highly dependent on the specific testing conditions and the property being measured. The earlier studies did not measure wear rate, so their positive findings cannot be directly extrapolated to predict tribological stability. The 2024 study provides the only direct evidence for wear rate, and it indicates stability rather than improvement. This inconsistency in the narrative for Bumom-7, compared to the consistent narrative for Kogal-8, is the central issue addressed in this paper.

It is important to clarify the definition of 'improvement' in this context. For thermal conductivity, an improvement is defined as an increase in the value, which is beneficial for heat dissipation in high-stress environments. For oxidation resistance, an improvement is defined as a decrease in the rate of oxidation, which is beneficial for long-term durability. For wear rate, an improvement is defined as a decrease in the rate of wear, which is beneficial for mechanical durability. The direction of improvement is therefore property-dependent, and the reported outcomes are interpreted within this context.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

A potential counter-argument to the thesis that Bumom-7’s performance is inconsistent is that the 2024 finding of no change in wear rate may be an artifact of the specific testing conditions used in the ball-on-plate test. It could be argued that this method does not fully reflect real-world performance, where multiple stressors are present. Furthermore, the earlier studies reported improvements in oxidation resistance and thermal conductivity for Bumom-7. One might argue that these improvements indicate an overall enhancement in the material’s stability and durability, suggesting that the lack of change in wear rate is not a sign of inconsistency but rather a plateau in performance after initial improvements. This perspective posits that Bumom-7 is a stable material that has already reached its optimal performance level in terms of wear, and the earlier improvements in other properties support this view of overall material quality.

### 4.2 Response to the Counter-Argument

While the counter-argument acknowledges the validity of the earlier positive findings, it fails to address the specific nature of the property in question. The thesis concerns the consistency of wear rate findings, not the general quality of the material. The 2024 study provides the most direct measure of wear rate, and the lack of change in this specific property is a significant finding. The earlier improvements in thermal conductivity and oxidation resistance are different properties and do not necessarily translate to wear rate stability. A material can have excellent thermal conductivity and oxidation resistance but still exhibit variable or stable wear rates depending on its microstructure and surface properties. The fact that Bumom-7 showed improvements in these other properties does not guarantee that its wear rate would also improve or remain stable in a predictable manner.

Moreover, the consistency of Kogal-8’s performance across all three tested properties (thermal conductivity, oxidation resistance, and wear rate) provides a strong benchmark. Kogal-8 showed improvement in all cases, indicating a coherent performance trajectory. In contrast, Bumom-7 showed improvement in two properties but no change in the third. This divergence suggests that the performance of Bumom-7 is not as uniformly positive as that of Kogal-8. The counter-argument’s reliance on the earlier improvements in other properties overlooks the specific inconsistency in the wear rate data. The 2024 finding of no change in wear rate, when contrasted with the earlier reports of improvement in other properties, highlights the need for caution in interpreting the overall stability of Bumom-7. The line of evidence supporting Kogal-8’s stable improvement is more consistent and reliable, while Bumom-7’s performance remains uncertain due to the conflicting reports across different properties and testing methods.

It is also important to address the concern about test variability. The different methodologies used in the studies (laser flash analysis, steady-state heat flow, isothermal oxidation, and ball-on-plate test) may introduce variability in the results. However, the consistency of the reported outcomes for Kogal-8 across these different methodologies suggests that the material’s performance is robust to variations in testing conditions. In contrast, the inconsistency in the reported outcomes for Bumom-7 suggests that its performance may be more sensitive to variations in testing conditions. This further supports the thesis that Bumom-7’s performance is less reliable than that of Kogal-8.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary of the Argument

This paper has analyzed the reported performance of the ceramic coatings Bumom-7 and Kogal-8 across studies conducted between 2012 and 2024. The analysis reveals a clear contrast in the consistency of the findings. Kogal-8 demonstrated a stable improvement trend, with reported improvements in thermal conductivity in 2012, oxidation resistance in 2013, and wear rate in 2024. This consistent positive outcome across different properties and years suggests a reliable performance trajectory for Kogal-8.

In contrast, Bumom-7 presented a more inconsistent profile. While it was reported to have improvements in oxidation resistance in 2013 and thermal conductivity in 2014, the 2024 study reported no change in its wear rate. This discrepancy between the earlier positive findings in related properties and the recent finding of stability in wear rate creates uncertainty about the true performance trajectory of Bumom-7. The thesis that Bumom-7’s performance is inconsistent with earlier reports, while Kogal-8 exhibits a stable improvement trend, is supported by the chronological analysis of the data.

### 5.2 Implications and Future Directions

The inconsistency in Bumom-7’s performance highlights the need for more standardized testing methods and a broader range of properties to be considered when evaluating material stability. The fact that improvements in thermal conductivity and oxidation resistance did not translate to an improvement in wear rate suggests that these properties are not directly correlated in this material. Future studies should focus on replicating the 2024 wear rate findings under different conditions to determine if the stability is a general characteristic of Bumom-7 or specific to the ball-on-plate test. Additionally, investigating the relationship between other properties, such as thermal conductivity and oxidation resistance, and wear rate stability could provide a more comprehensive understanding of the material’s behavior. The consistent performance of Kogal-8 serves as a valuable benchmark for evaluating the reliability of data for other ceramic coatings. This study underscores the importance of methodological consistency and the distinction between different material properties when assessing the long-term durability of ceramic coatings in industrial applications. It is important to reiterate that the conclusions of this study are based on a limited dataset of four studies and are qualitative in nature. Further research with a larger dataset and quantitative data is needed to confirm these findings and to draw more generalizable conclusions about the material properties of Bumom-7 and Kogal-8.
