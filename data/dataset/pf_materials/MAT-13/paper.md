# Hardness Assessment of Titanium Alloys via Indentation Methods

## Abstract

The mechanical reliability of titanium alloys and ceramic coatings is frequently assessed through indentation-based hardness testing, yet consistency across different techniques remains a point of contention. This paper addresses the ambiguity in reported hardness trends for the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, as well as the ceramic coating Kogal-8, by analyzing the consistency of findings from three key studies conducted between 2017 and 2024. Specifically, we examine the Thukepel Lab 2017 study, which focused on fatigue life via cyclic loading; the Nafir Lab 2018 study, which utilized micro-Vickers hardness testing; and the Rosemam Lab 2024 study, which employed scratch test methodologies. A significant conflict exists in the literature regarding Sapifin-2, where earlier micro-Vickers data indicated an improvement in hardness, while recent scratch test data reported a degradation. This discrepancy creates uncertainty regarding the true mechanical response of the alloy. We argue that the scratch test method, by simulating dynamic wear conditions, provides a more relevant assessment of surface integrity for engineering applications. The apparent degradation of Sapifin-2 in the 2024 study is interpreted as a reflection of the method's sensitivity to surface conditions and interface failure, rather than a direct contradiction of the static hardness improvements reported in 2018. By contextualizing the static micro-Vickers data with dynamic scratch test results, we propose a unified interpretation of the mechanical behavior of these alloys. The analysis reveals that while Stetrugel-6 consistently shows improved hardness across all methodologies, the mechanical response of Sapifin-2 is method-dependent. We conclude that the line of evidence from recent nanoindentation and scratch tests should be followed for evaluating surface durability, as it provides a more current and robust assessment of the mechanical response for these materials in engineering applications.

## 1. Introduction

The mechanical reliability of titanium alloys and ceramic coatings is frequently assessed through indentation-based hardness testing, yet consistency across different techniques remains a point of contention. While hardness is a fundamental property for predicting wear resistance and structural integrity, the methods used to quantify it—ranging from static micro-indentation to dynamic scratch testing—often yield divergent results for the same material. This variability creates ambiguity in the engineering characterization of surface-treated alloys, where the interplay between bulk properties and surface integrity is critical.

This study focuses on the hardness assessment of three specific materials: the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, and the ceramic coating Kogal-8. The primary objective is to evaluate the consistency of hardness measurements for these materials across different indentation methodologies. By examining the reported trends for these alloys, we aim to clarify whether observed changes in hardness reflect genuine material improvements or are artifacts of the testing protocol employed.

A significant conflict exists in the literature regarding the mechanical response of Sapifin-2. Earlier investigations using micro-Vickers hardness testing reported an improvement in hardness for this alloy, suggesting enhanced surface durability. However, more recent studies employing scratch test methodologies have reported a degradation in hardness for the same material. This discrepancy between the 2018 and 2024 findings creates a paradox: does Sapifin-2 exhibit a robust mechanical response, or is its performance unstable under different loading conditions? Similar questions arise for Stetrugel-6, where both methodologies have generally indicated improvements, but the magnitude and consistency of these changes warrant closer scrutiny.

The central thesis of this paper is that the scratch test method, by simulating dynamic wear conditions, provides a more relevant assessment of surface integrity for engineering applications. We argue that the apparent degradation of Sapifin-2 in recent scratch tests may be influenced by methodological sensitivities rather than a true decline in bulk hardness. By contextualizing the static micro-Vickers data with dynamic scratch test results, we propose a unified interpretation of the mechanical behavior of these alloys.

To resolve this inconsistency, this paper outlines a chronological analysis of three key studies. We examine the 2017 fatigue life study, the 2018 micro-Vickers hardness study, and the 2024 scratch test study, analyzing how each approach treated the materials in question. This structured review allows us to identify the specific conditions under which hardness values were recorded and to assess the reliability of each method for these specific alloys.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we provide a clear account of how the available studies treated the hardness properties of Stetrugel-6, Sapifin-2, and Kogal-8. Second, we analyze the methodological differences between static and dynamic indentation techniques to explain the observed discrepancies. Third, we offer an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be followed for engineering applications, advocating for a consistent interpretation of the mechanical response based on the totality of the evidence.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Static Micro-Indentation Approaches

Static micro-indentation, particularly the micro-Vickers method, has long been the standard for assessing the hardness of metallic alloys and coatings. This technique involves applying a constant load to a diamond indenter and measuring the resulting impression to calculate hardness. The primary advantage of this method is its ability to provide a stable, reproducible measure of bulk hardness, as it minimizes the effects of dynamic loading and friction. For materials like Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, micro-Vickers testing has historically been used to establish baseline hardness values and to detect improvements resulting from surface treatments or alloying modifications. However, a key limitation of static methods is their inability to capture surface integrity changes that occur under dynamic loading conditions. Since many real-world applications involve sliding or impact loads, the static hardness value may not fully represent the material's performance in service. This limitation can lead to inconsistent hardness values for surface-treated alloys, where the surface layer may behave differently from the bulk material under dynamic stress.

### 2.2 Dynamic Scratch Testing Approaches

Dynamic scratch testing offers an alternative approach to hardness assessment by simulating the conditions of wear and abrasion. In this method, a hard tip is dragged across the material surface under a controlled load, and the force required to maintain a constant depth or the resulting groove profile is analyzed to determine hardness. This technique is particularly useful for evaluating the surface integrity and coating adhesion of materials, as it directly measures the resistance to plastic deformation under sliding contact. For alloys such as Sapifin-2, scratch testing can reveal how the material responds to the combined effects of pressure and friction. However, dynamic methods are sensitive to surface roughness and coating adhesion, which can confound the interpretation of bulk hardness improvements. If the surface is rough or if the coating is not perfectly adhered, the scratch test may measure the failure of the interface rather than the hardness of the material itself. By analyzing the 2024 scratch test data alongside 2018 micro-Vickers data, this paper argues for a consistent interpretation of the mechanical response, taking into account the specific sensitivities of each method.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 The Thukepel Lab 2017 Study

The earliest of the three studies under consideration is the Thukepel Lab 2017 study, which focused on the fatigue life of the materials under investigation. This study employed cyclic loading as its primary method for assessing mechanical performance. The primary finding of this study was that it reported an improvement with alloy Stetrugel-6. This result provides a baseline for the mechanical behavior of Stetrugel-6, indicating that the alloy exhibits enhanced durability under cyclic stress conditions. While this study did not directly measure hardness, the improvement in fatigue life suggests a robust mechanical response that is consistent with the hardness improvements observed in subsequent studies. The Thukepel Lab 2017 study thus serves as an initial indicator of the potential for Stetrugel-6 to exhibit improved mechanical properties, setting the stage for the more direct hardness assessments conducted in later years.

### 3.2 The Nafir Lab 2018 Study

The Nafir Lab 2018 study shifted the focus from fatigue life to direct hardness assessment, utilizing the micro-Vickers method. This study examined the hardness of three materials: alloy Stetrugel-6, alloy Sapifin-2, and coating Kogal-8. The findings of this study were uniformly positive regarding the mechanical response of these materials. Specifically, the study reported an improvement with alloy Stetrugel-6, confirming the trend observed in the 2017 fatigue life study. Additionally, the study reported an improvement with alloy Sapifin-2, suggesting that this alloy also benefits from the conditions or treatments under investigation. Furthermore, the study reported an improvement with coating Kogal-8, indicating that the ceramic coating also exhibits enhanced hardness properties. The consistency of these improvements across the three materials in the Nafir Lab 2018 study provides a strong baseline for the mechanical response of Stetrugel-6, Sapifin-2, and Kogal-8 under static loading conditions. This study is particularly important for establishing the baseline hardness values for Sapifin-2, which will be contrasted with the findings of the 2024 study.

### 3.3 The Rosemam Lab 2024 Study

The most recent of the three studies is the Rosemam Lab 2024 study, which focused on hardness assessment using the scratch test method. This study examined the hardness of alloy Stetrugel-6 and alloy Sapifin-2. The findings of this study were mixed. For alloy Stetrugel-6, the study reported an improvement with the material, consistent with the findings of the 2017 and 2018 studies. However, for alloy Sapifin-2, the study reported degradation with the material. This finding stands in direct contrast to the improvement reported in the Nafir Lab 2018 study. The discrepancy for Sapifin-2 is the central point of contention in this paper.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Sapifin-2 Degradation

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis that both Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2 show a robust mechanical response is the finding from the Rosemam Lab 2024 study that reported degradation with alloy Sapifin-2. This finding directly contradicts the improvement reported in the Nafir Lab 2018 study, creating a significant inconsistency in the data. Proponents of the degradation argument would point to the scratch test method as a more realistic simulation of wear conditions, suggesting that the static micro-Vickers method may not capture the true mechanical response of the material under dynamic loading. The scratch test method is sensitive to surface roughness and coating adhesion, which could lead to a lower measured hardness if the surface is not perfectly prepared or if the coating is not fully adhered. This sensitivity could explain the degradation reported in the 2024 study, as the scratch test may be measuring the failure of the surface layer rather than the bulk hardness of the material.

### 4.2 Reconciling the Evidence

Despite the counter-argument, we maintain that the line of evidence from recent nanoindentation and scratch tests should be followed, as it provides a more current and robust assessment of the mechanical response. The consistent improvement for Stetrugel-6 across all three studies (Thukepel Lab 2017, Nafir Lab 2018, and Rosemam Lab 2024) supports the robustness of the mechanical response for this alloy. For Sapifin-2, the improvement reported in the 2018 study and the degradation reported in the 2024 study can be reconciled by considering the methodological differences between the two studies. The scratch test method is more sensitive to surface conditions, which may have led to the degradation reported in the 2024 study. However, the overall trend for Stetrugel-6 and the 2018 data for Sapifin-2 support a robust mechanical response. We argue that the apparent degradation of Sapifin-2 in recent scratch tests may be influenced by methodological sensitivities rather than a true decline in bulk hardness. By contextualizing the static micro-Vickers data with dynamic scratch test results, we propose a unified interpretation of the mechanical behavior of these alloys. The consistency of the data for Stetrugel-6 and the improvement reported for Sapifin-2 in the 2018 study support the thesis that both alloys exhibit a robust mechanical response.

By contextualizing the static micro-Vickers data with dynamic scratch test results, we propose a unified interpretation of the mechanical behavior of these alloys. This interpretation supports the robustness of the mechanical response for the titanium alloys and provides a clear account of how the available studies treated the hardness properties of Stetrugel-6, Sapifin-2, and Kogal-8.
