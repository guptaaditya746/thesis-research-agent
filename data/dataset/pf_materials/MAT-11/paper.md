# Oxidation and Corrosion Stability of Ceramic Coatings in Recent Trials

## Abstract

The deployment of advanced ceramic coatings in high-temperature and corrosive environments relies on the consistent reliability of specific material formulations. This paper evaluates the reliability of the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, alongside the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6, by synthesizing evidence from three recent laboratory studies. The analysis examines the Stomestim Lab 2023 study, which assessed oxidation resistance via isothermal methods; the Krebro Lab 2023 study, which evaluated corrosion resistance using electrochemical impedance; and the Stipuma Lab 2025 study, which tested corrosion resistance through immersion. The Stomestim Lab 2023 study reported no change in oxidation resistance for Kogal-8, Bumom-7, and Stetrugel-6. The Krebro Lab 2023 study indicated no change in corrosion resistance for Bumom-7. Conversely, the Stipuma Lab 2025 study reported no change for Kogal-8 but observed degradation in corrosion resistance for Bumom-7. Despite this isolated report of degradation in the 2025 immersion test, the overall body of evidence supports the thesis that both coatings exhibit stable performance under the conditions tested in the 2023 studies. The preponderance of data indicates that Kogal-8 and Bumom-7 maintain their integrity under the majority of tested conditions, with the 2025 finding representing a specific condition-dependent observation rather than a systemic failure. This synthesis clarifies the reliability profile of these materials, suggesting that the 2023 experimental lines provide the most robust indicators of inherent material stability, while the 2025 data highlights specific vulnerabilities under immersion conditions.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Oxidation Resistance Assessment

Prior work in this domain has primarily focused on the oxidation resistance of ceramic coatings and alloys, often utilizing isothermal oxidation testing as the standard methodological approach. These tests provide a baseline for high-temperature stability by exposing materials to constant elevated temperatures over extended periods. While isothermal tests may not fully capture the dynamic effects of thermal cycling, they are essential for establishing the fundamental oxidative stability of a material. The Stomestim Lab 2023 study serves as a foundational reference in this area, providing critical data on the oxidation behavior of Kogal-8, Bumom-7, and Stetrugel-6. This study established a baseline of stability, indicating that these materials do not exhibit significant oxidative degradation under the tested conditions. This baseline is crucial for interpreting subsequent corrosion data, as it confirms that the materials' integrity is not compromised by high-temperature exposure alone.

### 2.2 Corrosion Resistance Assessment

The assessment of corrosion resistance has been approached through two primary methodological clusters: electrochemical impedance spectroscopy and immersion testing. Electrochemical methods offer high sensitivity to surface changes and can detect early-stage corrosion mechanisms, while immersion tests simulate real-world exposure to corrosive environments over time. A key limitation in this area is that different testing methods may yield divergent results due to varying environmental exposures and measurement sensitivities. The Krebro Lab 2023 study, which employed electrochemical impedance, and the Stipuma Lab 2025 study, which utilized immersion testing, represent the two primary lines of corrosion evidence for these materials. The Krebro Lab 2023 study provided initial insights into the electrochemical stability of Bumom-7, while the Stipuma Lab 2025 study introduced a conflicting data point regarding the long-term immersion performance of the same coating. This divergence highlights the complexity of evaluating corrosion resistance and underscores the need for a comprehensive analysis that considers both methodological approaches.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Oxidation Resistance: Stomestim Lab 2023

The Stomestim Lab 2023 study was conducted to evaluate the oxidation resistance of the materials under investigation. Utilizing an isothermal oxidation method, this study provided the earliest data point in the current sequence of evidence. The primary objective was to determine whether the ceramic coatings and the titanium alloy exhibited any degradation in their protective properties when subjected to high-temperature oxidative environments.

The findings of the Stomestim Lab 2023 study were consistent across all three materials tested. For the ceramic coating Kogal-8, the study reported no change in oxidation resistance, indicating that the material maintained its structural and chemical integrity under the specified thermal conditions. Similarly, for the ceramic coating Bumom-7, the study reported no change in oxidation resistance, suggesting that this formulation also possesses inherent stability against oxidative degradation. Furthermore, the study examined the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6 and reported no change in its oxidation resistance. This uniformity in results across different material classes—ceramic coatings and a metallic alloy—establishes a robust baseline for high-temperature stability. The absence of reported degradation in any of the materials during the 2023 isothermal tests implies that the fundamental oxidative mechanisms do not compromise the integrity of Kogal-8, Bumom-7, or Stetrugel-6 under the conditions tested. This baseline is critical for the subsequent analysis of corrosion data, as it isolates corrosion as the primary variable of concern in later studies.

### 3.2 Corrosion Resistance: Krebro Lab 2023 and Stipuma Lab 2025

Following the establishment of oxidative stability, the focus shifted to corrosion resistance. Two distinct studies addressed this property: the Krebro Lab 2023 study and the Stipuma Lab 2025 study. These investigations employed different methodological approaches, which is essential for understanding the divergent outcomes observed for Bumom-7.

The Krebro Lab 2023 study utilized electrochemical impedance to assess corrosion resistance. This method is particularly sensitive to surface changes and early-stage corrosion mechanisms. In this study, the focus was on the ceramic coating Bumom-7. The Krebro Lab 2023 study reported no change in corrosion resistance for Bumom-7. This finding aligns with the oxidative stability observed in the Stomestim Lab 2023 study, reinforcing the notion that Bumom-7 maintains its protective capabilities under electrochemical evaluation. The consistency between the 2023 oxidation and 2023 corrosion data for Bumom-7 suggests a stable performance profile during that period.

In contrast, the Stipuma Lab 2025 study employed an immersion test method to evaluate corrosion resistance. This approach simulates prolonged exposure to corrosive environments, potentially revealing long-term degradation pathways that electrochemical methods might not capture. The Stipuma Lab 2025 study assessed both Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. For the ceramic coating Kogal-8, the study reported no change in corrosion resistance, confirming its stability across different testing methodologies and timeframes. However, for the ceramic coating Bumom-7, the Stipuma Lab 2025 study reported degradation in corrosion resistance. This finding stands in direct contrast to the results of the Krebro Lab 2023 study. The divergence between the 2023 electrochemical data (indicating stability) and the 2025 immersion data (indicating degradation) for Bumom-7 is the central point of contention in the current literature. It suggests that the performance of Bumom-7 may be sensitive to the specific environmental conditions and measurement techniques employed, with immersion testing potentially exposing vulnerabilities not apparent in electrochemical impedance measurements.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Bumom-7 Instability

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis of stable performance for Bumom-7 rests on the findings of the Stipuma Lab 2025 study. This study explicitly reported degradation in corrosion resistance for Bumom-7 under immersion conditions. Proponents of this view argue that the recency and specificity of the 2025 immersion test provide the most relevant data for assessing long-term reliability in real-world corrosive environments. The immersion test, by simulating continuous exposure, is often considered a more rigorous indicator of material durability than electrochemical methods, which may capture only transient or surface-level phenomena. Therefore, the report of degradation in 2025 suggests that Bumom-7 may suffer from instability over extended periods, challenging the assumption of its long-term integrity.

However, this counter-argument is refuted by the broader context of the available evidence. The Krebro Lab 2023 study, which used a valid and sensitive electrochemical method, reported no change in corrosion resistance for coating Bumom-7. Additionally, the Stomestim Lab 2023 study reported no change with coating Bumom-7. Thus, there are three reports of stability (two in 2023 for oxidation and electrochemical corrosion, and one in 2025 for Kogal-8's corrosion, which serves as a control for the method's validity) versus one report of degradation for coating Bumom-7 in 2025. The preponderance of evidence supports the thesis of overall stability. The single report of degradation in the 2025 immersion test is an outlier when compared to the consistent stability reported in 2023. It is more plausible that the 2025 degradation is method-specific or an anomaly related to the particular conditions of the immersion test, rather than a systemic failure of the coating Bumom-7 formulation. The consistency of the 2023 data across different properties (oxidation and electrochemical corrosion) provides a stronger foundation for assessing the material's inherent reliability than a single conflicting data point from a different methodological cluster.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Final Assessment

This paper has analyzed the recent experimental evidence regarding the oxidation and corrosion stability of the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, as well as the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6. Kogal-8 demonstrated consistent stability across all tests, with no change in oxidation resistance reported in 2023 and no change in corrosion resistance reported in 2025. Bumom-7 showed stability in oxidation resistance in 2023 and in electrochemical corrosion resistance in 2023. The only conflicting data point is the report of degradation in corrosion resistance for Bumom-7 in the 2025 immersion test.

The weight of evidence supports the reliability of both coatings. The consistent stability reported in the 2023 studies for both oxidation and electrochemical corrosion indicates that the fundamental properties of these materials are robust. The 2025 report of degradation for Bumom-7 should be viewed as a specific condition-dependent observation rather than a general failure. It highlights the importance of considering the testing methodology when interpreting material performance. The line of experimental evidence from 2023 (Stomestim and Krebro) should be followed as the primary indicator of material stability, with the 2025 Stipuma result serving as a cautionary note regarding specific immersion environments. In conclusion, Kogal-8 and Bumom-7 exhibit stable performance, with no significant degradation in the majority of tested scenarios, supporting their potential for use in high-temperature and corrosive applications.
