# Wear Rate Variability in Ceramic Coatings

## Abstract

Recent literature presents conflicting data on the performance of ceramic coatings Bumom-7 and Kogal-8, creating uncertainty for engineers selecting materials for durable surface applications. This paper argues that these divergent results are not indicative of inherent material superiority or inferiority, but are instead highly sensitive to the specific tribological test method employed. By conducting a critical review of four key studies—Legrer Lab 2012, Dikrur Lab 2022, Kinal Lab 2022, and Krebro Lab 2023—we analyze how methodological choices drive reported outcomes. The Dikrur Lab 2022 study, utilizing axial fatigue testing, reported improvements in fatigue life for both Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. In contrast, the Kinal Lab 2022 study, employing ball-on-plate testing, reported wear rate degradation for Bumom-7 and no change for Kogal-8. Additionally, the Legrer Lab 2012 study reported an improvement in thermal conductivity for Kogal-8 via laser flash analysis, while the Krebro Lab 2023 study found no change in corrosion resistance for Kogal-8 using electrochemical impedance. These findings demonstrate that performance metrics are context-dependent. The evidence suggests that the observed contradictions arise from the different failure modes probed by each test method rather than from inconsistent material properties. Consequently, the interpretation of coating performance must account for the specific experimental context, and future research should clearly specify the relevance of the test method to the intended application to avoid misleading conclusions about material viability.

## 1. Introduction

Ceramic coatings have become a standard solution in engineering applications where surface durability is paramount. Their widespread adoption is driven by the expectation that these materials provide consistent protection against mechanical degradation. However, recent literature presents a confusing picture regarding the performance of specific ceramic variants, namely Bumom-7 and Kogal-8. While these materials are often discussed in the same context, the reported outcomes for them vary significantly across different experimental settings, raising questions about the reliability of the data and the intrinsic properties of the coatings themselves.

The core of this conflict lies in divergent findings from recent studies. For instance, one line of research indicates that Kogal-8 offers improved fatigue life, suggesting it is a superior choice for cyclic loading applications. Conversely, other studies report that Bumom-7 exhibits wear rate degradation, implying it may be unsuitable for abrasive environments. These contradictory conclusions create a dilemma for engineers and researchers who must select a coating based on potentially misleading or context-dependent data. The discrepancy suggests that the observed performance is not solely a function of the material's chemical composition or microstructure.

This paper argues that the divergence in results is attributable to the sensitivity of coating performance to the specific tribological test method employed, rather than inherent material superiority or inferiority. The scope of this analysis is limited to the ceramic coatings Bumom-7 and Kogal-8, focusing on four specific laboratory studies: Dikrur Lab 2022, Kinal Lab 2022, Legrer Lab 2012, and Krebro Lab 2023. By examining these studies, we aim to demonstrate how methodological differences, such as the choice between axial fatigue testing and ball-on-plate wear testing, drive the conflicting conclusions reported in the literature.

The argument proceeds by analyzing the studies in chronological order to trace the evolution of the evidence. We will first look at the foundational data provided by earlier studies and then examine how subsequent investigations have interpreted or contradicted these findings. This approach allows us to isolate the impact of the test method on the reported properties. By contrasting the outcomes of fatigue-focused tests with those of wear-focused tests, we can better understand why a coating that performs well in one scenario may appear to fail in another.

This study contributes a clear account of how the various studies treated the source materials and an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be followed. Specifically, we provide a structured analysis of the reported improvements, degradations, and null results for Bumom-7 and Kogal-8. By synthesizing these findings, we offer a framework for interpreting ceramic coating performance that accounts for the specific failure modes being tested, thereby resolving the apparent contradictions in the existing literature.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Fatigue Life Assessment via Axial Loading

Prior work on ceramic coatings has frequently focused on assessing fatigue life under axial loading conditions. This methodology is designed to isolate fatigue mechanisms, providing insights into how coatings withstand cyclic stress without immediate surface failure. Studies employing axial fatigue testing, such as those conducted by Dikrur Lab in 2022, have reported improvements in fatigue life for certain ceramic variants, including Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. The primary advantage of this approach is its ability to quantify the resistance to crack initiation and propagation under repeated loading. However, a significant limitation of this method is that it may not capture the abrasive or adhesive wear components that dominate in other tribological scenarios. By focusing exclusively on fatigue, these tests can potentially lead to an overestimation of general durability, as they do not account for the material loss that occurs through friction and contact wear. This narrow focus means that a coating performing well in an axial fatigue test may not necessarily exhibit the same level of performance in a mixed-mode loading environment.

### 2.2 Wear Rate Assessment via Ball-on-Plate Testing

In contrast to fatigue-focused assessments, another major cluster of prior work emphasizes wear rate assessment via ball-on-plate testing. This tribological method is specifically designed to evaluate surface wear mechanisms, such as abrasion and adhesion, under controlled contact conditions. Studies like the one by Kinal Lab in 2022 have utilized this approach to report degradation in wear rate for Bumom-7, while noting no change for Kogal-8. The ball-on-plate test provides a direct measure of material removal, which is critical for applications where surface integrity is paramount. However, this method has its own limitations; it may not fully account for subsurface fatigue initiation, which can be a precursor to failure in other contexts. Consequently, the performance profile observed in ball-on-plate tests can differ significantly from that seen in fatigue-focused tests. By comparing the outcomes of this method with axial fatigue tests, it becomes evident that the 'best' coating depends heavily on the specific failure mode being tested, highlighting the need for a multi-method approach to fully characterize ceramic coating performance.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Chronological Review of Studies

To understand the trajectory of the evidence, it is necessary to examine the studies in chronological order, beginning with the earliest data point. The Legrer Lab 2012 study provided initial context by investigating thermal conductivity using laser flash analysis. In this investigation, the study reported an improvement in thermal conductivity with coating Kogal-8. While this finding addresses a different physical property than the tribological focus of the current debate, it establishes that Kogal-8 exhibits distinct material characteristics that can be enhanced or observed through specific measurement techniques. This early work suggests that the material's response to testing is not uniform across all properties, setting the stage for the more complex tribological findings that followed.

The focus then shifts to the tribological properties in the Dikrur Lab 2022 study, which employed axial fatigue testing to assess fatigue life. Under these conditions, the study reported an improvement in fatigue life with coating Kogal-8. Notably, the same study also reported an improvement in fatigue life with coating Bumom-7. This dual positive outcome is significant because it indicates that, under the specific stress conditions of axial fatigue, both ceramic variants demonstrated enhanced performance relative to the baseline. The consistency of these positive results for both materials suggests that the axial fatigue method effectively captures a mode of performance where both coatings are viable, potentially masking differences that might emerge under other loading conditions.

In contrast, the Kinal Lab 2022 study utilized ball-on-plate testing to evaluate wear rate, a method that emphasizes surface abrasion and adhesion. The results from this study diverge sharply from those of the Dikrur Lab 2022 study. Specifically, the Kinal Lab 2022 study reported degradation in wear rate with coating Bumom-7, indicating that the material performed worse under these abrasive conditions. Conversely, for coating Kogal-8, the study reported no change in wear rate. This shift in outcomes for Bumom-7—from an improvement in fatigue life to a degradation in wear rate—when the test method changes from axial fatigue to ball-on-plate, is central to the argument that performance is method-dependent. The fact that Kogal-8 showed no change in wear rate, despite showing improved fatigue life in the other study, further complicates the picture, suggesting that the material's response is not linearly transferable between different failure modes.

Finally, the Krebro Lab 2023 study expanded the scope of properties tested by examining corrosion resistance using electrochemical impedance. In this context, the study reported no change in corrosion resistance with coating Kogal-8. This finding, while not directly tribological, reinforces the broader theme that the performance of Kogal-8 is highly context-specific. The lack of change in corrosion resistance, combined with the previous findings of improved thermal conductivity and variable tribological performance, illustrates that 'performance' is a multi-faceted concept. The Krebro Lab 2023 study does not contradict the tribological findings but rather adds another dimension to the material's profile, showing that its advantages or disadvantages are not universal but are tied to the specific physical property and measurement technique being employed.

### 3.2 Methodological Sensitivity and Mechanistic Divergence

Synthesizing the findings from the four studies reveals a clear pattern of methodological sensitivity that can be explained by the distinct mechanical mechanisms probed by each test. The Dikrur Lab 2022 study, using axial fatigue, found that both Kogal-8 and Bumom-7 improved fatigue life. However, when the Kinal Lab 2022 study applied ball-on-plate testing, Bumom-7 showed degradation in wear rate, while Kogal-8 showed no change. This divergence cannot be easily explained by material inconsistency, as the same materials were tested in both studies. Instead, the difference in outcomes aligns with the different failure modes probed by each method.

Axial fatigue testing isolates subsurface crack initiation and propagation under cyclic stress. A coating that performs well in this test demonstrates a high resistance to the nucleation and growth of cracks under repeated loading. In contrast, ball-on-plate testing focuses on surface material removal through friction, abrasion, and adhesion. A coating that resists crack propagation may still be susceptible to abrasive wear if its surface hardness or tribological compatibility is insufficient. This mechanistic distinction explains the observed discrepancy for Bumom-7: it possesses the structural integrity to withstand cyclic fatigue (as shown by the Dikrur Lab 2022 study) but lacks the surface durability to resist abrasive wear (as shown by the Kinal Lab 2022 study). The methodological context, therefore, acts as a filter that reveals specific aspects of the material's performance while obscuring others.

The inclusion of the Legrer Lab 2012 and Krebro Lab 2023 studies further supports this interpretation by demonstrating that the material's response is not limited to mechanical properties. The Legrer Lab 2012 study reported an improvement in thermal conductivity for Kogal-8, a property unrelated to mechanical wear but indicative of the material's broader physical behavior. Similarly, the Krebro Lab 2023 study reported no change in corrosion resistance for Kogal-8. These findings demonstrate that the material's performance is not a single scalar value but a vector of properties that respond differently to different stimuli. The fact that Kogal-8 showed improved thermal conductivity but no change in corrosion resistance or wear rate (in the Kinal study) suggests that its 'superiority' is not absolute but is contingent on the specific property being measured. This multi-faceted nature of performance underscores the importance of the test method in defining what 'good' performance means.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Material Intrinsic Properties

A plausible counter-argument to the thesis of methodological sensitivity is that the divergent results are due to inherent, intrinsic properties of Bumom-7 and Kogal-8 that are simply being revealed by different tests. One might argue that Bumom-7 is inherently a poor wear-resistant material, and the Dikrur Lab 2022 study's positive result was an anomaly or a result of specific testing conditions that do not reflect general performance. Similarly, one could argue that Kogal-8 is a superior material overall, and the 'no change' in wear rate reported by Kinal Lab 2022 is merely a neutral result that does not negate its overall quality.

However, this argument is weakened by the specific pattern of results. If Bumom-7 were inherently poor in all mechanical aspects, one would expect it to show degradation or no improvement in the axial fatigue test as well. Yet, the Dikrur Lab 2022 study reported an improvement in fatigue life with coating Bumom-7. This suggests that Bumom-7 possesses certain mechanical strengths, specifically in resisting cyclic fatigue, that are not present in its wear resistance. The shift from improvement to degradation when the test method changes from axial fatigue to ball-on-plate indicates that the material's performance is not a fixed attribute but is modulated by the type of stress applied. Therefore, the methodological context is a more parsimonious explanation for the divergent results than a static, intrinsic material property.

Furthermore, the case for Kogal-8 as an inherently superior material is also complicated by the data. While it showed improved fatigue life in the Dikrur Lab 2022 study, it showed no change in wear rate in the Kinal Lab 2022 study. If Kogal-8 were universally superior, one might expect it to show improvement in both tribological metrics. The fact that it did not show improvement in wear rate suggests that its advantages are specific to certain failure modes, such as fatigue, rather than being a general enhancement in all tribological properties. This specificity again points to the test method as the key variable determining the observed outcome.

### 4.2 Addressing Confounding Variables and Generalizability

Critics may suggest that variations in sample preparation, surface roughness, or environmental conditions between the four labs could explain the differences, rather than the test method itself. While these are valid confounding variables in any comparative study, the core argument here relies on the contrast between the *types* of tests applied to the *same* materials. The divergence between the axial fatigue results and the ball-on-plate results for Bumom-7 is too stark to be attributed solely to minor variations in preparation; it aligns precisely with the known differences in the mechanical mechanisms probed by these two methods. By focusing on the methodological difference, we isolate the most significant variable in the observed divergence.

Regarding generalizability, while this analysis focuses on Bumom-7 and Kogal-8, the principle of methodological sensitivity is not unique to these materials. In materials science, it is well-established that performance metrics are context-dependent. A material that excels in one property (e.g., fatigue life) may not excel in another (e.g., wear resistance). The specific case of Bumom-7 and Kogal-8 serves as a clear illustration of this broader principle. The argument is therefore generalizable to other ceramic coatings and materials where multiple performance metrics are relevant to the application. The key takeaway is that researchers and engineers must always consider the specific test method when interpreting performance data, as the method itself shapes the outcome.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has argued that the divergent wear rate and fatigue life results for ceramic coatings Bumom-7 and Kogal-8 are best explained by the sensitivity of coating performance to the specific tribological test method employed. The analysis of the four studies—Legrer Lab 2012, Dikrur Lab 2022, Kinal Lab 2022, and Krebro Lab 2023—demonstrates that the reported outcomes are not consistent across different testing conditions. The Dikrur Lab 2022 study, using axial fatigue, reported improvements for both materials, while the Kinal Lab 2022 study, using ball-on-plate testing, reported degradation for Bumom-7 and no change for Kogal-8. These contrasting outcomes for the same materials highlight the critical role of the test method in defining performance.

The evidence suggests that 'best' performance is context-dependent. A coating that excels in fatigue life may not necessarily excel in wear resistance, and vice versa. Therefore, engineers and researchers must interpret the results of any single study with caution, recognizing that the findings are specific to the failure mode being tested. The Legrer Lab 2012 and Krebro Lab 2023 studies further reinforce this point by showing that other properties, such as thermal conductivity and corrosion resistance, also vary in their response to the materials, independent of the tribological findings.

In conclusion, the line of experimental evidence should be followed with an understanding that the test method is a critical variable in interpreting coating performance. Future studies should clearly specify the test method and its relevance to the intended application, allowing for a more nuanced comparison of materials. By acknowledging the methodological dependency of performance metrics, the field can move beyond contradictory conclusions and towards a more accurate, context-aware understanding of ceramic coating behavior.
