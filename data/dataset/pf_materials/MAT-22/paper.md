# Oxidation Resistance and Wear Rate Discrepancies in Titanium Alloys: A Methodological Framework for Interpreting Conflicting Data

## Abstract

This paper addresses a conflict in the experimental literature regarding the high-temperature performance of titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, as well as the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. The discrepancy centers on the stability of oxidation resistance versus wear rate. The Begrur Lab 2017 study, utilizing thermogravimetric analysis, reported no change in oxidation resistance for Stetrugel-6, Sapifin-2, and Kogal-8. Conversely, the Diper Lab 2016 study, employing reciprocating slider tests, reported degradation in the wear rate for Stetrugel-6, Sapifin-2, and Bumom-7. This divergence suggests that while chemical stability may be maintained, mechanical integrity is compromised under stress. The analysis argues that the evidence points to potential instability in high-temperature performance, driven primarily by wear degradation despite stable oxidation resistance. By synthesizing these findings, this review recommends following the Diper Lab 2016 line of evidence for wear rate assessments, as mechanical failure poses a more immediate risk to service life than the observed chemical stability. The paper provides a critical framework for interpreting conflicting data in materials science, emphasizing the necessity of integrating chemical and mechanical degradation pathways to accurately forecast material reliability in demanding industrial applications.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of titanium alloys in high-temperature applications is fundamentally dependent on their ability to maintain consistent oxidation resistance and wear performance over time. As these materials are increasingly deployed in demanding environments, the predictability of their degradation mechanisms becomes a critical factor for engineering design and safety. However, the current body of experimental evidence presents a complex picture, with conflicting reports regarding the stability of these key properties for specific alloy variants. This discrepancy creates uncertainty for practitioners who rely on historical data to forecast material behavior under sustained thermal and mechanical stress.

A specific conflict has emerged in the literature concerning the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2. The Begrur Lab 2017 study, which utilized thermogravimetric analysis, found no change in the oxidation resistance of both alloys. In contrast, the Diper Lab 2016 study, employing reciprocating slider tests, reported degradation in the wear rate for Stetrugel-6. This divergence in findings highlights a potential gap in understanding how different testing methodologies capture the multifaceted nature of material degradation. The inconsistency is not merely academic; it directly impacts the assessment of whether these alloys can be trusted for long-term service in high-temperature regimes.

The scope of this analysis is focused on the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, as well as the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. While these specific entities serve as the primary case study, they represent a significant subset of the high-performance alloys and protective layers used in industrial applications. By concentrating on these specific materials, the study aims to clarify the conflicting reports and provide a coherent interpretation of the available data. The analysis will examine how each study treated these sources, looking at the specific properties tested and the methods employed to measure them. This case study is intended to illustrate a broader methodological issue: how to reconcile conflicting qualitative data from different testing paradigms.

The central thesis of this work is that the evidence suggests potential instability in high-temperature performance, particularly regarding wear, despite stable oxidation resistance. While the oxidation data from the 2017 study indicates a degree of chemical stability, the wear data from the 2016 study points to mechanical vulnerability. This suggests that a holistic view of material performance must integrate both chemical and mechanical degradation pathways. The apparent stability in oxidation does not necessarily guarantee overall reliability if the material is susceptible to wear under mechanical stress.

This study contributes a clear account of how the existing studies treated the relevant materials and an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be followed. This paper provides a critical review that helps resolve the ambiguity surrounding the performance of alloy Stetrugel-6 and alloy Sapifin-2. The analysis offers a framework for interpreting conflicting data in materials science, emphasizing the importance of considering multiple degradation mechanisms when assessing material reliability.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Oxidation Resistance Assessment

The assessment of oxidation resistance in high-temperature materials has traditionally relied on thermogravimetric analysis, a method that measures mass changes in a material as a function of temperature and time. This approach provides a direct quantification of the chemical stability of alloys and coatings under oxidative conditions. The Begrur Lab 2017 study employed this methodology to evaluate the oxidation resistance of the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, as well as the ceramic coating Kogal-8. The findings from this study indicated that there was no clear change in the tested property for these materials, suggesting a high degree of chemical stability under the specific conditions of the test. However, a limitation of this approach is that it may not capture dynamic wear mechanisms that occur under mechanical stress. Oxidation resistance, while a critical factor, is only one component of overall material performance in high-temperature applications. A material may exhibit stable oxidation behavior but still suffer from significant mechanical degradation due to wear, which is not directly measured by thermogravimetric analysis.

### 2.2 Wear Rate Evaluation

Wear rate evaluation is another critical aspect of assessing material performance in high-temperature environments. Reciprocating slider tests are a common method for measuring wear, as they simulate the mechanical stresses that materials experience in service. The Diper Lab 2016 study used this method to evaluate the wear rate of the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, as well as the ceramic coating Bumom-7. The results from this study reported degradation in the wear rate for these materials, indicating that they were susceptible to mechanical wear under the test conditions. A limitation of this approach is that it may not account for oxidative degradation that precedes or accompanies wear. Wear and oxidation are often synergistic processes, where the formation of oxide layers can influence the wear mechanism, and vice versa. By focusing solely on wear rate, this method may overlook the chemical changes that contribute to overall material degradation. This study cross-references wear data with oxidation resistance results to identify potential synergistic effects, providing a more comprehensive view of high-temperature performance.

### 2.3 Test Conditions and Methodological Divergence

A critical aspect of interpreting the conflicting data is the difference in test conditions between the two studies. The Begrur Lab 2017 study utilized thermogravimetric analysis, which typically involves exposing the material to a controlled atmosphere at elevated temperatures without significant mechanical load. This method isolates the chemical reaction between the material and the environment. In contrast, the Diper Lab 2016 study employed reciprocating slider tests, which involve applying a mechanical load and sliding motion to the material surface, often at high temperatures. This method captures the combined effects of thermal and mechanical stress. The divergence in results is likely due to the fact that the TGA method does not account for the mechanical stresses that drive wear, while the reciprocating slider method does not fully isolate the chemical stability of the material. This methodological divergence is a key factor in the conflicting reports and highlights the need for a more integrated testing approach.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Begrur Lab 2017 Study

The Begrur Lab 2017 study focused exclusively on the property of oxidation resistance, utilizing thermogravimetric analysis as its primary measurement tool. This method is well-suited for detecting chemical changes in material mass due to oxidation but does not directly measure mechanical integrity. The study evaluated the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, along with the ceramic coating Kogal-8. For the alloy Stetrugel-6, the study reported no change in oxidation resistance, indicating that the material maintained its chemical stability under the tested conditions. Similarly, for the alloy Sapifin-2, the findings showed no change in oxidation resistance, suggesting that this alloy also exhibited stable chemical behavior. The study also examined the ceramic coating Kogal-8, reporting no change in its oxidation resistance. These consistent findings across the tested materials suggest that, from a purely chemical perspective, these materials do not undergo significant oxidative degradation under the specific conditions of the thermogravimetric tests. However, this stability is limited to the chemical domain and does not provide insight into how these materials perform under mechanical stress.

### 3.2 Diper Lab 2016 Study

In contrast to the chemical focus of the 2017 study, the Diper Lab 2016 study investigated the mechanical property of wear rate using reciprocating slider tests. This method simulates the friction and mechanical stress that materials experience in high-temperature service environments. The study evaluated the titanium alloys Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, as well as the ceramic coating Bumom-7. For the alloy Stetrugel-6, the study reported degradation in wear rate, indicating that the material suffered from increased mechanical wear under the test conditions. This finding is particularly significant as it suggests that Stetrugel-6 may be mechanically vulnerable despite its chemical stability. Similarly, for the alloy Sapifin-2, the study reported degradation in wear rate, pointing to a similar mechanical vulnerability. The study also examined the ceramic coating Bumom-7, reporting degradation in its wear rate. These findings collectively suggest that the materials tested in the Diper Lab 2016 study are susceptible to mechanical wear, which could compromise their structural integrity in high-temperature applications.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

A potential counter-argument to the thesis of potential instability is that the lack of change in oxidation resistance, as reported by the Begrur Lab 2017 study, suggests overall stability of the materials. Proponents of this view might argue that since oxidation is a primary degradation mechanism in high-temperature environments, the absence of oxidative degradation implies that the materials are reliable and stable. This argument relies on the assumption that chemical stability is the dominant factor in determining material performance in high-temperature applications. However, this view overlooks the critical role of mechanical integrity in ensuring long-term service life. Wear degradation, as reported by the Diper Lab 2016 study, indicates that the materials are susceptible to mechanical failure, which can lead to catastrophic breakdown even if the chemical composition remains stable. In high-temperature applications, mechanical stress is often a significant factor, and the ability of a material to withstand wear is crucial for its reliability. Therefore, while the stable oxidation resistance is a positive indicator, it does not negate the risks associated with mechanical wear. The evidence from the Diper Lab 2016 study suggests that the materials may fail due to wear before any significant oxidative degradation occurs, making the wear rate a more critical factor in assessing their overall stability.

### 4.2 Addressing the Synergistic Effects of Tribo-Oxidation

A further point of contention is the potential for synergistic effects between oxidation and wear, often referred to as tribo-oxidation. In high-temperature environments, the formation of oxide layers can influence the wear mechanism, and vice versa. The current data treats oxidation and wear as independent pathways. However, the discrepancy between the two studies may be a result of the synergistic effects of tribo-oxidation, which are not fully captured by either method alone. The reciprocating slider test, by applying mechanical stress, may accelerate the oxidation process, leading to a higher wear rate than would be observed in a static environment. Conversely, the thermogravimetric analysis, by not applying mechanical stress, may underestimate the rate of oxidation in a real-world scenario where the material is subjected to wear. This highlights the need for a more integrated testing approach that accounts for the coupling of chemical and mechanical degradation pathways.
