# Oxidation and Fatigue Studies on Titanium Alloys and Coatings

## Abstract

This paper addresses a methodological challenge in the reported degradation profiles of titanium alloys and ceramic coatings, specifically contrasting the findings of the Krestos Lab 2025 study and the Hozus Lab 2013 study. The central issue concerns the integration of independent observations regarding the stability of the ceramic coating Kogal-8 and the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6 under different testing conditions. The Krestos Lab 2025 study reported a degradation in the oxidation resistance of Kogal-8, whereas the Hozus Lab 2013 study reported no change in the fatigue life of Stetrugel-6. To resolve the apparent tension in these data streams, this review conducts a chronological analysis of four laboratory studies: Stamen 2019, Hozus 2013, Zekistam 2024, and Krestos 2025. These investigations involve two titanium alloys, Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, and two ceramic coatings, Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. By examining the progression of experimental evidence from 2013 to 2025, the review evaluates the robustness of the reported findings. The analysis indicates that the evidence supports the thesis that Kogal-8 exhibits degradation in oxidation resistance, while Stetrugel-6 maintains stable fatigue life. This conclusion highlights the necessity for material-specific degradation models rather than generalized assumptions about material class performance. The review further demonstrates that the stability of Bumom-7 and the fatigue behavior of Sapifin-2 align with the broader pattern of differentiated material responses, reinforcing the need for precise, property-specific assessments in high-stress applications.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of titanium alloys and ceramic coatings in high-stress applications depends on consistent degradation profiles, yet integrating findings from disparate test methods presents a significant challenge for material selection. Engineers and researchers rely on predictable performance metrics to ensure safety and longevity in structural components, but discrepancies in experimental outcomes can lead to suboptimal material selection. This uncertainty is particularly acute when comparing different classes of materials, such as metallic alloys and ceramic coatings, which are often used in complementary roles within the same system. The core issue is not a direct scientific contradiction between the stability of a metal and the degradation of a ceramic, but rather the difficulty in synthesizing independent observations into a coherent framework for system-level reliability.

A significant methodological gap exists between recent and earlier studies concerning the stability of these material classes. Specifically, the Krestos Lab 2025 study indicates a degradation in the oxidation resistance of the ceramic coating Kogal-8, while the Hozus Lab 2013 study found no change in the fatigue life of the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6. This contrast creates uncertainty about whether the observed instability is a general trend in these material families or an isolated phenomenon specific to certain test conditions or material variants. Resolving this tension is essential for establishing a coherent framework for material evaluation that accounts for the specific degradation modes of each component.

This paper defines its scope as a critical review of four specific laboratory studies: Stamen 2019, Hozus 2013, Zekistam 2024, and Krestos 2025. These investigations involve two titanium alloys, Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2, and two ceramic coatings, Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. By focusing on this limited set of materials and studies, the review aims to provide a detailed and focused analysis of the reported findings without the noise of broader, less comparable datasets. The selection of these specific studies allows for a direct comparison of methodologies and outcomes across different time periods and testing protocols.

The central issue identified in the literature is the divergence between the reported degradation in Kogal-8 oxidation resistance and the reported stability in Stetrugel-6 fatigue life. While these properties are distinct, their simultaneous evaluation is crucial for understanding the overall durability of composite systems. The thesis of this study is that the evidence supports the view that Kogal-8 exhibits degradation in oxidation resistance, whereas Stetrugel-6 maintains stable fatigue life. This necessitates a differentiated approach to material selection, where the specific degradation modes of each material class are considered independently rather than assuming a uniform behavior across all tested materials.

To address this issue, the study employs a chronological analysis of the studies' findings. By examining the progression of experimental evidence from 2013 to 2025, the review determines which line of experimental evidence is more robust and consistent. This method allows for the identification of trends and potential methodological shifts that may have influenced the reported outcomes. The analysis integrates data from wear rate assessments and thermal/mechanical stability tests to provide a comprehensive view of material degradation.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the four reviewed studies treated the specific materials in question. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be followed, resolving the apparent tension between the Krestos and Hozus findings. Third, it establishes a differentiated framework for material selection that accounts for the distinct degradation profiles of titanium alloys and ceramic coatings, preventing the erroneous transfer of stability data from one material class to another.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Wear Rate Assessment

Mechanical wear testing has been a primary method for evaluating the surface durability of materials. Studies such as those conducted by the Stamen Lab in 2019 and the Zekistam Lab in 2024 have employed techniques like reciprocating slider and ball-on-plate tests to measure wear rates. These methods focus on surface degradation under mechanical stress, providing valuable insights into the friction and abrasion characteristics of materials. The reciprocating slider test, used in the Stamen Lab 2019 study, simulates linear motion contact, while the ball-on-plate test, used in the Zekistam Lab 2024 study, evaluates point contact wear. However, a significant limitation of this approach is that it does not directly address oxidation or fatigue. Consequently, wear rate studies leave gaps in understanding long-term thermal and cyclic stability, which are critical for applications involving high temperatures or repeated loading. This review integrates wear data with oxidation and fatigue findings to provide a holistic view of material degradation, bridging the gap between surface-level mechanical performance and bulk material stability.

### 2.2 Thermal and Mechanical Stability Assessment

Thermal analysis and cyclic loading tests are essential for assessing the stability of materials under extreme conditions. The Krestos Lab 2025 study utilized differential thermal analysis to evaluate oxidation resistance, while the Hozus Lab 2013 study employed bending fatigue tests to measure fatigue life. Differential thermal analysis allows for the precise measurement of thermal events associated with oxidation, providing a direct indicator of chemical stability at elevated temperatures. Bending fatigue tests, on the other hand, isolate the mechanical response to cyclic loading, offering detailed insights into how materials respond to repeated stress. However, individual studies often do not cross-reference findings across different material families or test methods. This isolation can lead to fragmented understanding, where the stability of one material class is not directly compared to the degradation of another. This review synthesizes these isolated findings to resolve the apparent tension between degradation in coatings and stability in alloys, offering a more integrated perspective on material performance.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Chronological Review of Studies

The analysis begins with the earliest study in the dataset, the Hozus Lab 2013 study, which focused on the fatigue life of titanium alloys using bending fatigue tests. This investigation examined both Stetrugel-6 and Sapifin-2. The results indicated that the fatigue life of Stetrugel-6 remained unchanged under the tested conditions. Similarly, the study reported no change in the fatigue life of Sapifin-2. These findings establish a baseline of mechanical stability for these titanium alloys under cyclic loading, suggesting that neither alloy exhibited significant degradation in fatigue performance during the period of observation.

Subsequently, the Stamen Lab 2019 study addressed the wear rate of ceramic coatings using a reciprocating slider method. This study specifically evaluated the coating Kogal-8. The findings revealed a degradation in the wear rate of Kogal-8, indicating that the material experienced increased surface loss under mechanical stress. This result contrasts with the stability observed in the titanium alloys, highlighting a potential vulnerability in the ceramic coating under mechanical abrasion.

The Zekistam Lab 2024 study continued the investigation into wear rates but employed a different methodology, the ball-on-plate test. This study focused on the ceramic coating Bumom-7. The results showed no change in the wear rate of Bumom-7, suggesting that this coating maintained its surface integrity under the specific mechanical conditions of the test. This finding provides a point of comparison with the earlier results for Kogal-8, indicating that not all ceramic coatings exhibit the same susceptibility to wear degradation.

Finally, the Krestos Lab 2025 study examined the oxidation resistance of ceramic coatings using differential thermal analysis. This investigation included both Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. The study reported a degradation in the oxidation resistance of Kogal-8, confirming the instability previously observed in its wear rate. In contrast, the study found no change in the oxidation resistance of Bumom-7. This result aligns with the wear rate findings from the Zekistam Lab 2024 study, reinforcing the notion that Bumom-7 exhibits greater stability than Kogal-8 across different testing modalities.

### 3.2 Synthesis of Findings

Synthesizing the findings from the four studies reveals distinct patterns of material behavior. For the ceramic coating Kogal-8, the evidence points toward consistent instability. The Stamen Lab 2019 study reported degradation in its wear rate, and the Krestos Lab 2025 study reported degradation in its oxidation resistance. This convergence of negative outcomes across different properties and testing methods suggests that Kogal-8 is prone to degradation under both mechanical and thermal stresses. The consistency of these findings strengthens the argument that Kogal-8 is a less stable material compared to its counterparts.

In contrast, the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6 demonstrates stability in mechanical cyclic loading. The Hozus Lab 2013 study reported no change in its fatigue life, indicating that the alloy maintains its structural integrity under repeated stress. While this study did not assess oxidation resistance, the stability in fatigue life is a critical indicator of long-term performance in structural applications. The absence of reported degradation in this key property supports the view that Stetrugel-6 is a reliable material for applications involving cyclic loading.

The ceramic coating Bumom-7 presents a profile of stability similar to that of Stetrugel-6. The Zekistam Lab 2024 study reported no change in its wear rate, and the Krestos Lab 2025 study reported no change in its oxidation resistance. This dual stability in both mechanical and thermal properties distinguishes Bumom-7 from Kogal-8 and aligns it more closely with the stable behavior observed in the titanium alloys. The contrast between Kogal-8 and Bumom-7 highlights the importance of material-specific evaluation, as coatings within the same family can exhibit vastly different degradation profiles.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Potential Discrepancies

A primary counter-argument to the thesis is that the different test methods used in the studies—wear rate, oxidation resistance, and fatigue life—are not directly comparable. Critics may argue that degradation in one property does not necessarily imply degradation in another, and that the stability of Stetrugel-6 in fatigue does not preclude instability in other areas. However, the thesis does not rely on cross-property generalization. Instead, it focuses on the specific properties reported by the respective studies. The degradation in Kogal-8 oxidation resistance is a specific finding from the Krestos Lab 2025 study, and the stability in Stetrugel-6 fatigue life is a specific finding from the Hozus Lab 2013 study. The review acknowledges that these properties are distinct but argues that the consistency of findings within each material's tested properties supports the thesis. For Kogal-8, the degradation in both wear and oxidation suggests a broader instability, while for Stetrugel-6, the stability in fatigue is a positive indicator of mechanical reliability.

Another potential counter-argument is that the Krestos Lab 2025 study is more recent than the Hozus Lab 2013 study, and therefore its findings should take precedence. This argument assumes that newer studies are inherently more accurate or relevant. However, the studies test different materials and properties. The Krestos Lab 2025 study focused on the oxidation resistance of ceramic coatings, while the Hozus Lab 2013 study focused on the fatigue life of titanium alloys. The recency of the Krestos study does not invalidate the specific finding of the Hozus study regarding Stetrugel-6. Both studies are valid within their respective scopes, and the review integrates their findings to provide a comprehensive view of material performance. The thesis does not claim that one study is superior to the other but rather that their combined evidence supports a differentiated approach to material selection.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Final Assessment

This review has analyzed the findings of four laboratory studies concerning the degradation of titanium alloys and ceramic coatings. The Krestos Lab 2025 study reported degradation with coating Kogal-8, while the Hozus Lab 2013 study reported no change with alloy Stetrugel-6. The chronological analysis of the studies supports this thesis, revealing a pattern of instability in Kogal-8 and stability in Stetrugel-6.

The evidence indicates that Kogal-8 exhibits degradation in both wear rate, as reported by the Stamen Lab 2019 study, and oxidation resistance, as reported by the Krestos Lab 2025 study. This consistent degradation across different properties suggests that Kogal-8 is a less stable material for high-stress applications. In contrast, Stetrugel-6 maintains stable fatigue life, as reported by the Hozus Lab 2013 study, indicating its reliability under cyclic loading. The stability of Bumom-7 in both wear and oxidation further supports the need for material-specific assessments, as coatings within the same family can exhibit different degradation profiles.

The line of evidence supports the thesis that a differentiated approach to material selection is necessary. Engineers and researchers should consider the specific degradation modes of each material class independently, rather than assuming uniform behavior across all tested materials. This approach ensures that material selection is based on a comprehensive understanding of the performance characteristics of each material, leading to more reliable and durable structural components. Future work should continue to explore the degradation profiles of these materials under various conditions to further refine the framework for material evaluation.
