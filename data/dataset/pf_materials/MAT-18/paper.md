# Wear and Oxidation Performance of Ceramic Coatings

## Abstract

The reliability of ceramic coatings in industrial applications depends on consistent performance metrics for wear and oxidation resistance. Conflicting or ambiguous reports from specific laboratory studies have created uncertainty regarding the true performance of the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. This paper presents a critical review of the impact of the Kronor Lab 2013 and Grebrom Lab 2015 studies on the perceived performance of these materials. The central thesis is that the Kronor Lab 2013 study reported degradation in the wear rate for Kogal-8, while the Grebrom Lab 2015 study reported no change in the oxidation resistance for Bumom-7. To evaluate the reliability of these findings, this review analyzes the chronological progression of evidence, incorporating subsequent investigations by Stomestim Lab 2023 and Brudes Lab 2024. The analysis reveals a consistent pattern of stable oxidation and corrosion resistance for Bumom-7 across multiple testing methodologies, including thermogravimetric analysis, isothermal oxidation, and immersion tests. In contrast, the reported wear degradation for Kogal-8 remains a distinct and uncontradicted finding within the available literature. By examining the specific methods and results of these four studies, this paper argues that the line of evidence supporting stable chemical performance for Bumom-7 and degraded mechanical wear performance for Kogal-8 is the most reliable interpretation. This distinction is crucial for accurate material selection and the prediction of service life in industrial applications where both mechanical and chemical stresses are present.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of ceramic coatings in industrial applications hinges on consistent performance metrics, particularly regarding wear and oxidation resistance. These properties determine the service life and safety of components operating under mechanical stress or high-temperature conditions. However, the scientific literature occasionally presents conflicting or ambiguous reports that create uncertainty regarding the true performance of specific coating families. This uncertainty can hinder the adoption of promising materials and complicate the selection of appropriate protective layers for critical infrastructure.

This study focuses on a critical review of the impact of two specific investigations on the perceived performance of two ceramic coatings: Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. The scope is limited to analyzing how these earlier studies have shaped the current understanding of these materials. By isolating these two cases, we aim to clarify the discrepancies that have emerged in the broader discourse on ceramic coating durability.

The specific properties in question are the wear rate for Kogal-8 and the oxidation resistance for Bumom-7. These metrics are central to the functional integrity of the coatings. Wear rate indicates how quickly the material erodes under friction, while oxidation resistance reflects the material's ability to withstand chemical degradation at elevated temperatures. Discrepancies in these areas can lead to incorrect assumptions about material longevity and failure modes.

The central thesis of this review is that the Kronor Lab 2013 study reported degradation in the wear rate for Kogal-8, while the Grebrom Lab 2015 study reported no change in the oxidation resistance for Bumom-7. These two findings represent the core of the current debate. The former suggests a potential flaw in the mechanical durability of Kogal-8, whereas the latter implies a stable, albeit unimproved, chemical profile for Bumom-7. Understanding the context of these reports is essential for interpreting their validity.

To address this issue, this paper employs an analysis of the studies listed in the relevant records, read against the sources they deal with. This method involves examining the specific data points and conclusions drawn by each laboratory. By comparing the findings across different time points and testing methodologies, we determine which line of evidence should be followed. This approach allows for a nuanced evaluation of whether the reported issues are inherent material flaws or artifacts of specific testing conditions.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the Kronor Lab 2013 and Grebrom Lab 2015 studies treated the sources Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be prioritized in future assessments. Third, it clarifies the distinction between mechanical wear degradation and chemical oxidation stability in these specific ceramic coating families.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Mechanical Wear Assessment

Prior work on ceramic coatings has extensively focused on mechanical wear properties, utilizing methodologies such as pin-on-disc testing and tribological analysis. These studies aim to quantify the rate at which material is lost under friction, providing critical data for predicting service life. However, a significant limitation in earlier studies is the tendency to isolate wear from other environmental factors. This isolation can lead to potential misinterpretation of degradation signals, particularly in specific coating families like Kogal-8. When wear is tested in a vacuum or controlled atmosphere without considering thermal or chemical interactions, the resulting data may not reflect real-world performance. This review re-examines the specific wear rate data from the Kronor Lab 2013 study to clarify whether the reported degradation is a genuine material flaw or a testing artifact resulting from isolated conditions.

### 2.2 Oxidation and Corrosion Resistance

The second cluster of prior work focuses on chemical and thermal stability, specifically oxidation resistance. Methodologies in this area typically include thermogravimetric analysis and isothermal oxidation tests, which measure mass changes due to chemical reactions at high temperatures. A common limitation in these studies is the difficulty in distinguishing between 'no change' and 'improvement' when the baseline performance of the material is already high. This ambiguity is particularly evident in studies on Bumom-7, where high initial stability can mask subtle variations. For instance, the Grebrom Lab 2015 study reported no change in oxidation resistance, a finding that may be interpreted differently depending on the baseline expectations. This review analyzes the 'no change' findings for Bumom-7 in the context of subsequent studies, such as those by Stomestim Lab 2023 and Brudes Lab 2024, to validate the stability of the material and determine if the lack of change indicates robustness or a plateau in performance.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Studies

To determine the reliability of the performance metrics for Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, it is necessary to examine the findings of the relevant laboratory studies in chronological order. The earliest of the four studies under consideration is the Kronor Lab 2013 study, which utilized a pin-on-disc test to assess wear rate. In this investigation, the researchers observed a worsening of the wear rate for the coating Kogal-8, indicating a degradation in mechanical performance under friction. Simultaneously, the same study evaluated the oxidation resistance of the coating Bumom-7 and found no clear change in this property, suggesting that the material maintained its baseline chemical stability under the tested conditions.

Following this, the Grebrom Lab 2015 study employed thermogravimetric analysis to investigate oxidation resistance. The results from this study indicated that the coating Bumom-7 exhibited no change in oxidation resistance, a finding that aligns with the earlier observation from the Kronor Lab 2013 study. Additionally, the Grebrom Lab 2015 study assessed the coating Kogal-8 and reported no change in its oxidation resistance, providing a contrast to the wear degradation previously noted for this material. This distinction highlights that the issues with Kogal-8 are specific to mechanical wear rather than chemical stability.

More recent investigations have further clarified the performance of these coatings. The Stomestim Lab 2023 study used isothermal oxidation to test the oxidation resistance of the coating Bumom-7. The findings from this study confirmed that there was no change in the oxidation resistance of Bumom-7, reinforcing the consistency of the material's chemical stability over time and across different testing methods. Finally, the Brudes Lab 2024 study conducted an immersion test to evaluate the corrosion resistance of the coating Bumom-7. The results showed no change in corrosion resistance, further supporting the conclusion that Bumom-7 possesses robust chemical durability.

Synthesizing these findings reveals a clear pattern. For the coating Bumom-7, the evidence from four distinct studies, spanning from 2013 to 2024 and utilizing different methodologies (pin-on-disc, thermogravimetric analysis, isothermal oxidation, and immersion test), consistently reports no change in oxidation or corrosion resistance. This uniformity across time and method strongly suggests that the chemical stability of Bumom-7 is a reliable and inherent property of the material. In contrast, the evidence for the coating Kogal-8 is more nuanced. While its oxidation resistance was reported as unchanged in the Grebrom Lab 2015 study, its wear rate was reported as degraded in the Kronor Lab 2013 study. No subsequent study in this review has contradicted the specific finding of wear degradation for Kogal-8, making it a distinct and persistent characteristic of this material's mechanical performance.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Potential Misinterpretations

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that the consistent reports of "no change" for Bumom-7 might imply a lack of performance or a plateau in development, rather than true stability. Critics might argue that if a material does not improve, it may not be suitable for advanced applications where enhanced performance is required. Furthermore, one could argue that the wear degradation of Kogal-8 is an outlier, possibly resulting from specific testing conditions in the Kronor Lab 2013 study that do not reflect general performance.

However, the findings from the reviewed studies refute these interpretations. The term "no change" in the context of oxidation and corrosion resistance, as reported by the Grebrom Lab 2015, Stomestim Lab 2023, and Brudes Lab 2024 studies, indicates that the material maintained its baseline performance without degradation. Given that these studies utilized different methods—thermogravimetric analysis, isothermal oxidation, and immersion tests—the consistency of the results across these varied conditions supports the interpretation of stable performance rather than a lack of capability. The material is performing as expected under the tested stresses, which is a critical requirement for industrial coatings.

Regarding Kogal-8, the argument that the wear degradation is an outlier is weakened by the fact that no subsequent study in this review has replicated the Kronor Lab 2013 test to show different results. The specific finding of degradation in wear rate is a distinct data point that has not been contradicted by later work on this specific metric. While the oxidation resistance of Kogal-8 was reported as stable, the mechanical wear issue remains a valid concern based on the available evidence. Therefore, the thesis that Kogal-8 exhibits degraded wear performance while Bumom-7 exhibits stable chemical performance is supported by the totality of the evidence presented in the four studies.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Final Assessment

This review has analyzed the impact of the Kronor Lab 2013 and Grebrom Lab 2015 studies on the perceived performance of the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. The central thesis, that the Kronor Lab 2013 study reported degradation in the wear rate for Kogal-8 while the Grebrom Lab 2015 study reported no change in the oxidation resistance for Bumom-7, is supported by the chronological analysis of the evidence. The inclusion of subsequent studies by Stomestim Lab 2023 and Brudes Lab 2024 further validates the stability of Bumom-7's chemical properties.

The analysis confirms that the line of evidence supporting stable oxidation and corrosion resistance for Bumom-7 is robust, as it is consistent across multiple testing methodologies and time periods. Conversely, the evidence for Kogal-8 indicates a specific vulnerability in mechanical wear, as reported by the Kronor Lab 2013 study. In future applications and research, it is advisable to follow this line of evidence: treating Bumom-7 as a chemically stable coating and Kogal-8 as a material with potential limitations in mechanical durability. This distinction is essential for accurate material selection and the prediction of service life in industrial settings.
