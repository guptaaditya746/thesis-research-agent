# Divergent Wear and Hardness Findings in Ceramic Coatings

## Abstract

This paper addresses a significant discrepancy in the reported performance of the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, arising from two independent studies conducted in 2016. The Pagadol Lab 2016 study, which employed Vickers indentation to assess hardness, found no change in the hardness of these coatings, suggesting stable structural integrity under static loading. In contrast, the Brihoher Lab 2016 study, which utilized a ball-on-plate test to measure wear rate, reported significant degradation in the wear performance of both materials, indicating substantial material loss under dynamic sliding conditions. This divergence highlights a critical gap in material characterization: the assumption that stable hardness implies stable wear performance is not supported by the available evidence. We argue that this conflict suggests a trade-off between hardness and wear performance, where materials may retain their resistance to indentation while suffering from accelerated wear due to dynamic mechanisms not captured by static tests. By analyzing the methodologies and findings of both studies, we demonstrate that the wear rate evidence from the Brihoher Lab 2016 study is more indicative of real-world performance. Consequently, we conclude that hardness alone is insufficient for evaluating the durability of ceramic coatings, and that multi-property evaluation is essential for accurate material selection. This work provides a clear account of how the two studies treated the evidence for Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, and offers an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be prioritized in future assessments.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of material property data is frequently compromised when independent studies report conflicting outcomes for the same materials under similar conditions. In the field of advanced materials, such discrepancies create significant uncertainty for engineers and researchers who rely on these data for design and selection purposes. A prominent example of this issue involves the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, for which two distinct lines of experimental evidence have emerged, leading to divergent conclusions about their long-term performance.

The core of the conflict lies in the specific properties tested by the two primary investigations. The Pagadol Lab 2016 study focused on the static mechanical property of hardness, utilizing Vickers indentation to assess the resistance of the coatings to permanent deformation. In contrast, the Brihoher Lab 2016 study examined the dynamic tribological property of wear rate, employing a ball-on-plate test to measure material loss under sliding contact. These two methodologies probe different aspects of material behavior, yet their results for Kogal-8 and Bumom-7 appear to contradict one another when interpreted as indicators of overall durability.

The Pagadol Lab 2016 study reported no change in hardness for both Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, suggesting that the coatings maintained their structural integrity under static loading. Conversely, the Brihoher Lab 2016 study reported significant degradation in the wear rate for both coatings, indicating that the materials suffered substantial material loss during dynamic sliding. This divergence suggests a potential trade-off between hardness and wear performance, where a material may retain its hardness while simultaneously experiencing accelerated wear due to mechanisms not captured by static indentation tests.

To resolve this ambiguity, this paper adopts an argumentative approach that analyzes the studies in chronological order, evaluating the robustness of the evidence provided by each. By examining the specific findings of the Pagadol Lab 2016 and Brihoher Lab 2016 studies, we aim to determine which line of experimental evidence offers a more reliable basis for predicting the performance of Kogal-8 and Bumom-7 in practical applications. The analysis will also consider the implications of these findings for the broader understanding of ceramic coating behavior.

This study makes the following contributions: it provides a clear account of how the two studies treated the sources of evidence for Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, and it offers an argued view on which line of experimental evidence should be followed. By reconciling the conflicting reports, this work aims to clarify the true performance characteristics of these ceramic coatings and provide guidance for future material selection and testing protocols.

## 2. Background on the Materials

### 2.1 Static Mechanical Characterization

Static mechanical characterization, particularly hardness testing via indentation, has long been a standard method for assessing the resistance of materials to permanent deformation. This approach provides valuable insights into the intrinsic strength and structural integrity of materials under static loading conditions. However, a key limitation of hardness testing is that it may not capture the dynamic degradation mechanisms that occur during sliding contact. As a result, relying solely on hardness data can lead to an overestimation of material durability, as it does not account for the complex interactions between surfaces that contribute to wear. This study contrasts hardness data with wear rate data to reveal the limitations of relying exclusively on static properties for predicting long-term performance.

### 2.2 Tribological Performance Assessment

Tribological performance assessment, particularly wear testing via ball-on-plate methods, focuses on the dynamic behavior of materials under sliding contact. This approach is sensitive to surface conditions and can reveal degradation that may not be immediately apparent in hardness measurements. A key limitation of wear testing is that it can be influenced by external factors such as lubrication, load, and sliding speed, which may not be fully controlled or replicated across different studies. This study uses wear rate data to challenge the assumption that stable hardness implies stable wear performance, highlighting the potential disconnect between static and dynamic performance metrics. By integrating both static and dynamic data, this work aims to provide a more comprehensive understanding of material behavior under real-world conditions.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Evidence

### 3.1 Pagadol Lab 2016: Hardness Assessment

The Pagadol Lab 2016 study employed Vickers indentation to evaluate the hardness of the materials under investigation. This method involves pressing a diamond indenter into the material surface under a known load and measuring the resulting indentation to determine hardness. The study assessed the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, as well as the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6, which served as a comparative substrate. The primary finding of this study was that the hardness of Kogal-8 remained unchanged under the tested conditions. Similarly, the hardness of Bumom-7 showed no significant variation, indicating that the coating maintained its resistance to permanent deformation. Furthermore, the study reported that the hardness of the Stetrugel-6 alloy also exhibited no change, suggesting that the substrate material retained its structural integrity under static loading. These results collectively imply that, from a static mechanical perspective, the materials did not undergo any measurable degradation in hardness.

### 3.2 Brihoher Lab 2016: Wear Rate Assessment

In contrast to the static assessment, the Brihoher Lab 2016 study focused on the dynamic tribological behavior of the same materials using a ball-on-plate test. This method involves sliding a ball against a flat plate under controlled load and speed to measure the rate of material loss. The study evaluated the wear rate of Kogal-8, Bumom-7, and Stetrugel-6. The results indicated a significant degradation in the wear rate of Kogal-8, meaning that the coating experienced substantial material loss during sliding contact. Similarly, the wear rate of Bumom-7 was found to have degraded, suggesting that the coating was not resistant to dynamic wear mechanisms. Additionally, the study reported that the wear rate of the Stetrugel-6 alloy also showed degradation, indicating that the substrate material was susceptible to wear under the tested conditions. These findings highlight that, under dynamic sliding conditions, the materials exhibited significant wear, which contrasts sharply with the stable hardness reported by the Pagadol Lab 2016 study.

### 3.3 Synthesis: The Hardness-Wear Trade-off

The divergence between the findings of the Pagadol Lab 2016 and Brihoher Lab 2016 studies is striking. While the former reported no change in hardness for Kogal-8, Bumom-7, and Stetrugel-6, the latter reported significant degradation in the wear rate for all three materials. This discrepancy suggests a trade-off between hardness and wear performance. Specifically, the materials may maintain their resistance to indentation (hardness) while simultaneously suffering from accelerated wear due to dynamic mechanisms such as friction, adhesion, or fatigue. This trade-off implies that hardness alone is not a reliable predictor of wear performance. The wear rate data from the Brihoher Lab 2016 study is more relevant for practical applications, as it directly measures the material loss that occurs during service conditions. Therefore, the evidence suggests that the coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, despite their stable hardness, are not durable under dynamic sliding conditions.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Hardness as a Primary Indicator

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that hardness is a fundamental property of materials, and the lack of change in hardness reported by the Pagadol Lab 2016 study should imply stable performance. Proponents of this view might argue that since hardness is a measure of the material's resistance to deformation, a stable hardness indicates that the material is not undergoing significant structural changes. However, this argument is refuted by the wear degradation reported by the Brihoher Lab 2016 study. The fact that the materials experienced significant wear despite stable hardness demonstrates that hardness alone does not predict wear behavior. The dynamic mechanisms involved in wear, such as friction and adhesion, are not captured by static indentation tests. Therefore, the wear rate data provides a more comprehensive picture of the material's performance under real-world conditions.

### 4.2 Methodological Differences

Another counter-argument is that the different methods used by the two studies (Vickers indentation vs. ball-on-plate test) are not directly comparable, and therefore the divergence in outcomes is not significant. While it is true that the methods probe different aspects of material behavior, the divergence in outcomes is still significant. The fact that the materials showed stable hardness but degraded wear rate highlights the need for multi-property evaluation. Relying on a single property, such as hardness, can lead to an incomplete understanding of the material's performance. The methodological differences between the two studies actually underscore the importance of integrating both static and dynamic data to provide a more complete picture of material behavior. Therefore, the divergence in outcomes is not a flaw in the studies but rather a reflection of the complexity of material performance.

## 5. Conclusion

This paper has analyzed the conflicting reports on the performance of the ceramic coatings Kogal-8 and Bumom-7, as well as the titanium alloy Stetrugel-6. The Pagadol Lab 2016 study reported no change in hardness for all three materials, while the Brihoher Lab 2016 study reported significant degradation in the wear rate for all three materials. This divergence suggests a trade-off between hardness and wear performance, where materials may maintain their hardness while suffering from accelerated wear under dynamic conditions. The wear rate evidence from the Brihoher Lab 2016 study is more indicative of real-world performance, as it directly measures the material loss that occurs during service conditions.

### 5.2 Implications for Material Selection

The findings of this paper have important implications for material selection. The wear rate evidence from the Brihoher Lab 2016 study should be prioritized when evaluating the durability of Kogal-8 and Bumom-7. Relying solely on hardness data can lead to an overestimation of the material's durability, as it does not account for the dynamic mechanisms involved in wear. Future studies should integrate both hardness and wear rate measurements to provide a more complete picture of material performance. This multi-property approach will help to resolve the ambiguity in the reported performance of ceramic coatings and provide guidance for future material selection and testing protocols. By prioritizing the wear rate evidence, engineers and researchers can make more informed decisions about the use of these materials in practical applications.
