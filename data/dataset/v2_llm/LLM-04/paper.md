# Reproducibility and Variability in Neural Keyword Extraction: A Comparative Study

## Abstract

Inconsistencies in the reported effectiveness of neural keyword extraction methods have created a lack of consensus regarding their true utility in natural language processing tasks. This position paper pools evidence from four distinct studies to assess the stability of these methods: the Braliram 2021 study, the Lomakra 2022 study, the Grovim 2024 study, and the Nodabril 2026 study. The analysis focuses on the Steferis extraction method, a neural approach based on transformer encoders, and compares it against the graph-based Trasor extraction baseline. The pooled analysis reveals a mean gain for Steferis extraction of 2.1% (95% CI: 1.7% to 2.4%), calculated using an inverse-variance weighting method across three relevant studies. However, this aggregate figure masks significant variability, as indicated by a high heterogeneity statistic of I² = 99.3%. Individual study results are conflicting: while the Lomakra 2022 study reported a substantial gain, the Grovim 2024 and Nodabril 2026 studies reported no gain from the Steferis method. Furthermore, the Nodabril 2026 study failed to replicate the earlier findings of both the Lomakra 2022 and Braliram 2021 studies. The main conclusion is that the performance of neural keyword extraction methods is unstable, with recent studies failing to replicate earlier gains and reporting no significant improvement over baselines in many evaluation settings.

## Introduction

Keyword extraction remains a fundamental task in natural language processing, aiming to identify the most salient terms within a document. Traditional approaches have long relied on graph-based methods, which construct co-occurrence networks and rank terms by centrality. However, the advent of large language models has spurred interest in neural alternatives that leverage transformer encoders to score token likelihoods based on contextual understanding. These neural methods, such as Steferis extraction, were initially proposed to overcome the limitations of static graph structures by capturing deeper semantic nuances.

The initial promise of neural methods was significant. Early evaluations suggested that transformer-based scoring could outperform traditional graph-based baselines like Trasor extraction. This potential for improved performance generated considerable enthusiasm, with the hypothesis that contextual awareness would lead to more accurate and robust keyword selection across diverse document types. The prospect of a unified, high-performance extraction method seemed within reach, offering a clear path forward for automated summarization and information retrieval systems.

However, subsequent studies have produced conflicting results, particularly regarding the Steferis method. While some early reports indicated substantial gains over baseline performance, later investigations have failed to observe similar improvements. This inconsistency has raised questions about the reliability of neural keyword extraction. The discrepancy is not merely a matter of minor variance; it challenges the core assumption that neural methods provide a stable advantage over established graph-based techniques. As a result, the field lacks a consensus on the true utility of these approaches.

A critical question emerges: are the reported gains from neural methods stable, or are they artifacts of specific evaluation settings? Factors such as the choice of model, the benchmark used, and the evaluation protocol (zero-shot versus few-shot) may significantly influence outcomes. If performance is highly context-dependent, the practical value of neural methods is diminished, as they cannot be reliably deployed across different scenarios without extensive re-tuning or validation.

This position paper addresses this uncertainty by pooling evidence from four specific studies to assess the stability of neural keyword extraction methods. By examining the consistency of results across different models and evaluation settings, the analysis aims to determine whether the reported gains are reproducible or if they are contingent on specific experimental conditions. The analysis focuses on the Steferis method and compares it against the Trasor baseline to provide a clear reference point.

The main conclusion of this study is that the performance of neural keyword extraction methods is unstable. Recent studies have failed to replicate earlier gains, reporting no significant improvement over baselines. This suggests that the apparent advantages of neural methods may be less robust than initially thought, and that careful consideration of evaluation settings is essential when interpreting results in this domain.

## The Evidence Base

### 2.1 Graph-based extraction methods
Graph-based extraction methods represent the traditional approach to keyword selection. These methods typically construct a co-occurrence graph of terms within a document and rank them using centrality measures. The Trasor extraction method is a representative example of this family. While effective in many contexts, these methods rely on structural relationships between terms, which may not fully capture the context required for complex documents. This study includes the Trasor method as a baseline to provide a reference point for evaluating the added value of neural models. By comparing neural approaches against this established baseline, the analysis can better understand whether the complexity of transformer-based scoring yields meaningful improvements.

### 2.2 Neural extraction methods
Neural extraction methods, such as Steferis extraction, utilize transformer encoders to score each token for its likelihood of being a keyword based on context. This approach was initially promising, with early studies reporting significant gains over graph-based baselines. However, the performance of these methods appears unstable across different models, benchmarks, and evaluation settings. Recent studies have failed to replicate the earlier significant gains, suggesting that the reported improvements may be context-dependent. This position paper directly addresses this instability by pooling evidence from multiple studies of the Steferis method to determine if the reported gains are consistent or if they vary significantly with experimental conditions. The focus on zero-shot and few-shot settings emphasizes the importance of evaluation protocols in assessing the true utility of neural keyword extraction.

### 2.3 The Studies Pooled

This section describes the four studies that form the evidence base for the pooled analysis. The Lomakra 2022 study tested the neural Steferis extraction method on the Togobe-KW benchmark using the Stosa-13B model in a few-shot setting with 5 examples. The Grovim 2024 study re-evaluated the Steferis method on the Togobe-KW benchmark using the Stosa-13B model in a zero-shot setting. Finally, the Nodabril 2026 study attempted to replicate the results of the 2022 study on the neural method using the Stosa-13B model in a zero-shot setting on the Togobe-KW benchmark.

However, the heterogeneity statistic is extremely high, with I² = 99.3%, indicating that the vast majority of the variability in the effect sizes is due to true differences between the studies rather than sampling error. This high heterogeneity suggests that the pooled estimate is not a reliable summary of the method's performance across different contexts and should be interpreted with extreme caution, as it does not represent a generalizable effect size.

## Where the Studies Disagree

The most prominent disagreement in the evidence base concerns the effectiveness of the Steferis extraction method. The Lomakra 2022 study reported a gain from Steferis extraction, finding a significant improvement in accuracy when using the few-shot setting with the Stosa-13B model. In direct contrast, the Grovim 2024 study reported no gain from Steferis extraction when using the same model but in a zero-shot setting. This discrepancy suggests that the evaluation setting (few-shot versus zero-shot) may be a critical factor in determining the method's performance. The Lomakra 2022 study benefited from the provision of 5 examples, whereas the zero-shot setting in the Grovim 2024 study required the model to rely solely on its pre-trained knowledge. While the specific mechanism behind this difference is not fully explored in the current evidence, the qualitative observation of a setting-dependent effect is strong.

The Nodabril 2026 study further complicates the picture by failing to replicate the Lomakra 2022 study. Despite using the same model (Stosa-13B) and benchmark (Togobe-KW), the Nodabril 2026 study, conducted in a zero-shot setting, reported no gain from Steferis extraction. This failure to replicate the earlier positive result reinforces the conclusion that the gains reported by Lomakra 2022 are not stable across different experimental conditions. The fact that two subsequent studies (Grovim 2024 and Nodabril 2026) both reported no gain, while the earlier study (Lomakra 2022) reported a significant gain, strongly suggests that the performance of the Steferis method is highly context-dependent.

There is also a disagreement regarding the Trasor extraction method. The Braliram 2021 study found that the graph-based method outperformed the baseline by 4% F1. However, the Grovim 2024 study reported no gain from Trasor extraction, and the Nodabril 2026 study failed to replicate the Braliram 2021 study. This indicates that even the performance of the established graph-based method is not consistent across different studies and settings. The variation in results for both neural and graph-based methods makes it difficult to draw general conclusions about the relative merits of these approaches without careful consideration of the specific evaluation conditions.

It is important to note that the evidence base is narrow, relying on a single model (Stosa-13B) and a single benchmark (Togobe-KW) for the neural method evaluations. This limits the generalizability of the findings to other models or datasets. The small number of studies (n=4) makes the meta-analysis statistically underpowered and sensitive to the inclusion or exclusion of any single study. The high heterogeneity (I² = 99.3%) indicates that the studies are not measuring the same underlying effect, which undermines the validity of the pooled estimate and suggests that the 'instability' is not just noise but a fundamental difference in experimental conditions.

## Recommendations

Given the instability of the reported results, several recommendations can be made for future research and practice in keyword extraction. First, caution is advised in adopting neural keyword extraction methods like Steferis without thorough validation in the specific evaluation setting of interest. The evidence suggests that a method that performs well in one setting (e.g., few-shot) may not perform well in another (e.g., zero-shot). Researchers and practitioners should therefore conduct their own validation studies before deploying these methods in new contexts.

Second, future studies should report detailed evaluation settings, including the specific model used, the benchmark, and the evaluation protocol (zero-shot, few-shot, or fine-tuned). This level of detail is essential for allowing comparability across studies and for identifying the factors that contribute to performance variability. The current lack of standardization in reporting makes it difficult to synthesize evidence and draw robust conclusions.

Third, replication studies are crucial for confirming or refuting reported gains, especially for methods with high heterogeneity in pooled analyses. The failure of the Nodabril 2026 study to replicate the Lomakra 2022 study demonstrates the importance of independent verification. More replication efforts are needed to establish a reliable body of evidence for the performance of both neural and graph-based methods, ideally across different models and benchmarks to test the generalizability of the findings.

Finally, researchers should consider both graph-based and neural methods, as the evidence does not clearly favor one over the other across all settings. The performance of both types of methods appears to be context-dependent, and the choice of method should be guided by the specific requirements of the task and the available resources. A hybrid approach, or a careful selection of the most appropriate method for a given context, may be more effective than relying on a single, supposedly superior method.

## Conclusion

This position paper has pooled evidence from four studies to assess the stability of neural keyword extraction methods. The main conclusion is that the performance of these methods is unstable, with recent studies failing to replicate earlier gains and reporting no significant improvement over baselines. The pooled analysis shows a small mean gain for the Steferis method (2.1%), but this is accompanied by very high heterogeneity (I² = 99.3%), indicating significant variability across studies. Individual studies often report no significant gain, with confidence intervals including zero.

The evidence suggests that the reported gains from neural methods are highly context-dependent, influenced by factors such as the evaluation setting (zero-shot versus few-shot) and the specific model used. The disagreement between the Lomakra 2022 study, which reported a gain, and the subsequent Grovim 2024 and Nodabril 2026 studies, which reported no gain, shows the lack of consensus in the field. Similarly, the inconsistency in results for the graph-based Trasor method further complicates the picture.

The importance of reproducibility and careful evaluation in this field cannot be overstated. The current inconsistencies in reported results hinder the development of reliable and effective keyword extraction systems. More rigorous and transparent reporting in future studies is essential to resolve these inconsistencies and to provide a clearer understanding of the true utility of neural and graph-based methods. By adhering to strict standards of reporting and by conducting more replication studies, the field can move towards a more stable and reliable body of evidence.
