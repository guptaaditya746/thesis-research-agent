# Zero-Shot Prompting in Code and Math: A Replication Analysis

## Abstract

The reproducibility of zero-shot prompting results in code generation and arithmetic reasoning remains a contentious issue in large language model research. This paper presents a critical analysis of six studies that evaluated the efficacy of Kabaso prompting, a specific prompting technique, across different model scales and benchmarks. The central thesis is that Kabaso prompting exhibits significant variability, with later investigations failing to replicate the gains reported in earlier work, suggesting a high sensitivity to model size and task complexity. The analysis examines the Netras 2020, Brastom 2021, Dedrola 2021, Grodrokris 2021, Braliram 2023, and Lomakra 2023 studies, detailing how each treated the method and its predecessors. We find that while early studies using larger models such as Nimel-13B reported positive outcomes, subsequent studies using smaller models like Thipom-7B or different benchmarks consistently failed to observe similar benefits. Furthermore, direct replication attempts of the Netras 2020 study by Dedrola 2021 and Grodrokris 2021 did not yield the originally reported results. These findings indicate that the observed gains are not robust properties of the prompting method itself but are contingent on specific experimental conditions. Consequently, the reliability of zero-shot prompting gains is undermined by this context-dependency, necessitating a more cautious interpretation of results that accounts for the interplay between model capacity and task structural requirements.

## 1. Introduction

Zero-shot prompting has emerged as a dominant paradigm for leveraging large language models in specialized tasks, particularly in domains such as code generation and arithmetic reasoning. By relying on the model's pre-trained capabilities without additional fine-tuning or in-context examples, this approach promises efficient deployment and broad applicability. However, the consistency of reported gains from these techniques remains a subject of significant debate within the research community. While initial reports suggested substantial improvements over baseline performance, subsequent investigations have yielded mixed results, raising questions about the reliability of these methods across different experimental conditions.

The specific method under scrutiny in this analysis is Kabaso prompting. Kabaso prompting is a family of prompting techniques that utilize structured zero-shot instructions to guide the model's reasoning process. Unlike few-shot methods that rely on in-context examples, Kabaso prompting attempts to elicit complex behaviors through carefully crafted natural language directives alone. Existing literature presents conflicting evidence regarding the efficacy of this approach. Some studies report significant improvements in task accuracy, while others fail to replicate these gains, suggesting a lack of robustness across different model scales and task complexities. This discrepancy is particularly pronounced when comparing results from larger, more capable models against smaller, resource-constrained variants, as well as when evaluating performance on distinct benchmarks such as mathematical reasoning versus code synthesis.

This variability in outcomes indicates that the observed benefits of zero-shot prompting are highly sensitive to specific model architectures and the inherent difficulty of the target tasks. The inconsistency challenges the generalizability of zero-shot prompting gains, implying that what appears to be a universal improvement in one context may not hold in another. This sensitivity suggests that the method's effectiveness is not an intrinsic property of the prompting strategy itself but is instead contingent on the interaction between the model's capacity and the task's structural requirements.

To address these concerns, this paper provides a critical analysis of the reported results from various studies involving Kabaso prompting. By examining the outcomes across different model sizes, benchmarks, and evaluation settings, we aim to clarify the conditions under which zero-shot prompting yields reliable benefits. The analysis focuses on identifying patterns in replication failures and inconsistent gains, offering a nuanced view of the method's practical utility.

In summary, this study contributes a clear account of how different empirical investigations have treated the claims of early exploratory work. It argues for a more cautious interpretation of zero-shot prompting results, emphasizing the need to consider model scale and task complexity when evaluating the efficacy of techniques like Kabaso prompting. The findings suggest that the line of evidence supporting universal gains is less robust than previously assumed, and that future research should prioritize replication across diverse model configurations to establish true generalizability.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Early Exploratory Studies

Initial demonstrations of prompting efficacy often relied on specific model configurations and high-capacity benchmarks to establish baseline gains. These early studies typically focused on larger models, such as those in the 13B to 70B parameter range, and utilized benchmarks designed to test complex reasoning or code generation capabilities. The primary goal was to showcase the potential of zero-shot and few-shot techniques to enhance performance without extensive fine-tuning. However, these studies often operated under controlled conditions that may have overestimated the generalizability of the observed gains. By focusing on specific model versions and task types, they created a narrative of consistent improvement that did not necessarily account for the variability introduced by smaller models or different task complexities. This initial optimism set the stage for subsequent investigations that sought to verify these findings under broader conditions.

### 2.2 Replication and Robustness Studies

Attempts to verify and extend initial findings have revealed significant inconsistencies in the reported results. Replication studies, which aim to re-run earlier setups to confirm the original claims, have often encountered subtle differences in evaluation settings or model versions that lead to ambiguous conclusions. For instance, some studies failed to replicate the gains reported in earlier work, suggesting that the initial improvements were not robust across different experimental contexts. These replication failures highlight the sensitivity of zero-shot prompting methods to specific model characteristics and task requirements. By analyzing the outcomes of these replication attempts, it becomes clear that the inconsistency is not merely due to experimental error but reflects a fundamental dependency on model size and task complexity. This paper argues that the variability in results underscores the need for a more nuanced understanding of when and why zero-shot prompting techniques like Kabaso are effective, rather than assuming their universal applicability.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Studies (2020-2021)

The initial wave of investigations into Kabaso prompting, conducted between 2020 and 2021, primarily utilized the Nimel-13B model and reported positive outcomes. The Brastom 2021 study examined the technique on the Vofal-Math benchmark using the same Nimel-13B model, but in a few-shot setting. This investigation also concluded that Kabaso prompting yielded an improvement in performance over the baseline, extending the reported benefits to arithmetic reasoning tasks.

However, the consistency of these early positive results was challenged within the same period. The Dedrola 2021 study, which also used the Nimel-13B model on the Donar-Code benchmark but in a few-shot setting, reported a gain from Kabaso prompting. Despite this positive outcome for the method itself, the Dedrola 2021 study simultaneously failed to replicate the specific results reported by the Netras 2020 study. This discrepancy suggests that even when the general direction of improvement is observed, the specific magnitude or conditions of the gain may not be stable across different evaluation protocols. Furthermore, the Grodrokris 2021 study attempted to verify the Netras 2020 findings using a larger model, Nodrana-70B, on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study also failed to replicate the Netras 2020 study, indicating that scaling up the model size did not guarantee the reproduction of the initial code generation gains, particularly when the benchmark domain shifted to question answering.

### 3.2 Later Studies (2023)

The later investigations, conducted in 2023, shifted focus to a smaller model, Thipom-7B, and consistently failed to demonstrate the benefits observed in the earlier, larger-model studies. The Braliram 2023 study evaluated Kabaso prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark using the Thipom-7B model in a zero-shot setting. This study reported no clear difference from the baseline score, indicating that the method did not enhance performance in this context. Similarly, the Lomakra 2023 study assessed the technique on the Vofal-Math benchmark, also using the Thipom-7B model in a zero-shot setting. This investigation likewise reported no gain from Kabaso prompting, finding no significant improvement over the baseline performance.

The synthesis of these later findings reveals a stark contrast with the earlier positive reports. While the 2020 and 2021 studies using Nimel-13B generally reported gains, the 2023 studies using Thipom-7B consistently failed to observe such benefits. This pattern suggests that the efficacy of Kabaso prompting is not uniform across model scales. The failure to observe gains in the smaller Thipom-7B model, across both code and math benchmarks, implies that the method's effectiveness may be contingent on a certain level of model capacity. Additionally, the shift from few-shot to zero-shot settings in the later studies, combined with the change in model size, further complicates the interpretation of the results, highlighting the interplay between evaluation settings and model characteristics.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Potential Explanations

A primary counter-argument to the thesis of high sensitivity to model size and task complexity is that the observed variability may stem from experimental errors or subtle differences in implementation details rather than a fundamental flaw in the method. Proponents of this view might argue that the failure to replicate gains in later studies could be attributed to inconsistencies in data preprocessing, evaluation metrics, or specific prompt formulations, rather than an inherent lack of robustness in Kabaso prompting. If this were the case, the method might still be considered effective, provided that these experimental variables are carefully controlled.

However, the pattern of results across the six studies challenges this explanation. The consistent failure to replicate gains in later studies, particularly when using different model sizes (Nimel-13B versus Thipom-7B) and distinct benchmarks (Donar-Code, Vofal-Math, Mupadem-QA), suggests a systematic issue rather than random experimental error. If the variability were solely due to implementation inconsistencies, one would expect some degree of success in replication across different contexts, or at least a random distribution of failures. Instead, the data shows a clear trend: studies using the larger Nimel-13B model reported gains, while those using the smaller Thipom-7B model did not. Furthermore, the direct replication attempts by Dedrola 2021 and Grodrokris 2021, which aimed to verify the Netras 2020 study, both failed to reproduce the original results, despite using similar or larger models. This convergence of evidence from multiple independent studies, spanning different years, models, and benchmarks, supports the view that the variability is not an artifact of experimental noise but reflects a genuine dependency on model capacity and task characteristics. Therefore, the thesis that zero-shot prompting gains are not robust and are highly dependent on specific model and task conditions remains well-supported by the available evidence.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has provided a critical analysis of the reported results from six studies evaluating Kabaso prompting in code generation and arithmetic reasoning tasks. The analysis reveals significant variability in the efficacy of the method, with early studies using larger models reporting gains, while later studies using smaller models consistently failed to observe similar benefits. Direct replication attempts of the initial positive findings also failed to reproduce the reported results, further undermining the claim of robustness. These findings support the thesis that the effectiveness of zero-shot prompting techniques like Kabaso is highly sensitive to model size and task complexity, rather than being a universal property of the method.

The implications of these results are significant for the research community. Researchers should be cautious in generalizing zero-shot prompting gains across different model scales and task domains. The observed context-dependency suggests that the practical utility of such techniques is limited to specific conditions, and that claims of broad applicability require rigorous validation across diverse experimental settings. Future work should prioritize replication studies that systematically vary model size and task complexity to better understand the boundaries of these methods' effectiveness. By acknowledging the sensitivity of zero-shot prompting to these factors, the field can develop more reliable guidelines for the deployment of large language models in specialized tasks.
