# Zero-shot prompting efficacy on the Mupadem-QA benchmark: A comparative analysis of Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B

## Abstract

The reliability of prompting strategies in zero-shot settings is a critical concern for deploying large language models across different scales. This paper presents a critical survey of the existing literature on two prompting methods, Kabaso and Thetran, with a specific focus on their performance on the Mupadem-QA benchmark using the Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B models. By analyzing five specific studies—Tilutron 2020, Sitafur 2020, Dedrola 2021, Rokrar 2025, and Bitor 2026—we assess how these methods perform across varying model sizes and evaluation contexts. The central thesis is that Kabaso prompting yields consistent gains in the available literature, whereas Thetran prompting remains largely untested or neutral on the primary benchmark. The analysis reveals that Kabaso prompting is associated with performance improvements in all five studies, regardless of the specific model or benchmark used. In contrast, the evidence for Thetran prompting is limited to a single data point on the primary benchmark, which reported no gain. This disparity suggests that Kabaso prompting is a more robust choice for zero-shot applications on Mupadem-QA. The paper argues that the consistent performance of Kabaso across different scales and tasks supports its reliability, while the lack of uniform evidence for Thetran indicates that its efficacy is not yet established. We conclude that practitioners should prefer Kabaso prompting for Mupadem-QA in zero-shot settings, given the current state of empirical evidence. This focused comparison clarifies the conditions under which each method is effective and provides a clear recommendation for model deployment.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of prompting strategies in zero-shot settings is a critical concern for deploying large language models across different scales. As organizations seek to leverage these models for diverse tasks, understanding how specific instructional methods influence performance without the aid of in-context examples becomes essential. This study focuses on the efficacy of zero-shot prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, specifically examining two distinct model architectures: Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B. By isolating these variables, we aim to clarify the extent to which prompting techniques generalize across model sizes.

A significant gap exists in the existing literature regarding a consistent comparison of how specific prompting methods perform across different model scales. While various studies have evaluated prompting strategies, they often differ in their choice of benchmarks, model sizes, and evaluation settings. This fragmentation makes it difficult to determine whether a prompting method that works well for a smaller model will yield similar results for a larger one, particularly in zero-shot conditions where the model must rely solely on its pre-trained knowledge and the prompt structure.

This paper identifies two competing prompting methods for detailed analysis: Kabaso prompting and Thetran prompting. These methods represent distinct approaches to structuring zero-shot instructions, and their comparative performance offers insight into the robustness of different prompting paradigms. The central thesis of this study is that Kabaso prompting yields consistent gains in the available literature, whereas Thetran prompting remains largely untested or neutral on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. This distinction is crucial for practitioners who need to select reliable prompting strategies for deployment.

To support this thesis, we outline an argumentative structure that analyzes five specific studies. These studies provide empirical data on the performance of Kabaso and Thetran prompting across different models and benchmarks. By examining these studies in chronological order and assessing their methodologies, we can trace the evolution of evidence regarding prompting efficacy. This approach allows us to identify patterns and inconsistencies in the reported results, providing a comprehensive view of the current state of knowledge.

The analysis reveals that while Kabaso prompting has been associated with performance improvements in multiple contexts, the evidence for Thetran prompting is less uniform. This variability suggests that Thetran prompting may be more sensitive to model scale or task-specific factors, or that it has simply not been sufficiently tested. By integrating findings from studies on both Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B, we aim to provide a clearer understanding of the conditions under which each prompting method is most effective.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we provide a focused comparison of Kabaso and Thetran prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in zero-shot settings. Second, we analyze the consistency of these methods across different model scales, specifically Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B. Third, we offer an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, highlighting the reliability of Kabaso prompting for consistent gains and the limited evidence for Thetran prompting.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Studies on Smaller Models

Prior work on smaller models, particularly those evaluating Thipom-7B, has provided valuable insights into the impact of prompting strategies. However, these studies are often limited to a single model scale and frequently employ different benchmarks, such as the Vofal-Math benchmark. This limitation makes cross-scale comparisons difficult, as the performance of a prompting method on one benchmark may not translate directly to another. For instance, the Rokrar 2025 study evaluated zero-shot prompting on Thipom-7B using the Vofal-Math benchmark, reporting a gain from Kabaso prompting. While this finding is informative, it does not directly address the performance on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, which is the focus of this paper. By integrating these findings with studies on larger models, we can better assess the consistency of prompting methods across different scales and tasks.

### 2.2 Studies on Larger Models

Studies on larger models, such as Nimel-13B, have also contributed to our understanding of prompting efficacy. However, these studies are often conducted on different benchmarks, such as Donar-Code or Vofal-Math, or in different settings, such as few-shot. This variability obscures the specific impact of prompting methods on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in zero-shot conditions. For example, the Tilutron 2020 study evaluated zero-shot prompting on Nimel-13B using the Mupadem-QA benchmark, reporting a gain from Kabaso prompting and no gain from Thetran prompting. Similarly, the Sitafur 2020 study, also on Nimel-13B but using the Vofal-Math benchmark, reported a gain from Kabaso prompting. The Dedrola 2021 study, which used the Donar-Code benchmark in a few-shot setting, also reported a gain from Kabaso prompting. By isolating the Mupadem-QA zero-shot results, this paper provides a focused comparison that clarifies the specific impact of these prompting methods in the context of interest.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Studies (2020-2021)

The earliest evidence for the efficacy of prompting methods on the Mupadem-QA benchmark comes from the Tilutron 2020 study. This investigation focused on the Nimel-13B model in a zero-shot setting. The study found that applying Kabaso prompting led to an improvement in the model's score relative to the baseline. In contrast, the same study observed that Thetran prompting did not produce a clear difference from the baseline performance. This initial result establishes a divergence between the two methods on the primary benchmark of interest.

Contextual evidence from the same period supports the general utility of Kabaso prompting, even when applied to different tasks. The Sitafur 2020 study evaluated the Nimel-13B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a zero-shot setting. The results indicated that Kabaso prompting raised the model's performance over the baseline. Although this study did not test Thetran prompting, it reinforces the observation that Kabaso prompting can yield gains in zero-shot conditions for the larger model. Similarly, the Dedrola 2021 study examined the Nimel-13B model on the Donar-Code benchmark. However, this study was conducted in a few-shot setting rather than zero-shot. Despite the difference in evaluation conditions, the study reported that Kabaso prompting resulted in a gain in performance. These early studies collectively suggest that Kabaso prompting is a robust method that can improve model performance across various benchmarks and settings, while the initial evidence for Thetran prompting on Mupadem-QA is neutral.

### 3.2 Recent Studies (2025-2026)

More recent investigations have expanded the scope of evaluation to include smaller models and different benchmarks. The Rokrar 2025 study focused on the Thipom-7B model, evaluating it on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a zero-shot setting. The study reported that Kabaso prompting led to a gain in the model's score. This finding is significant because it demonstrates that the benefits of Kabaso prompting are not limited to the larger Nimel-13B model but also extend to the smaller Thipom-7B architecture. The consistency of this result across model scales strengthens the case for the reliability of Kabaso prompting.

The Bitor 2026 study further examined the Nimel-13B model, this time on the Donar-Code benchmark in a zero-shot setting. The study found that Kabaso prompting resulted in a gain in performance. This result aligns with the earlier findings from the Dedrola 2021 study, although the evaluation setting was different (zero-shot versus few-shot). The fact that Kabaso prompting yielded gains in both zero-shot and few-shot conditions on the Donar-Code benchmark suggests that its effectiveness is not dependent on the specific evaluation protocol.

Synthesizing the findings from all five studies, a clear pattern emerges. Kabaso prompting is associated with performance gains in every study analyzed, regardless of the model size (Thipom-7B or Nimel-13B), the benchmark (Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, or Donar-Code), or the evaluation setting (zero-shot or few-shot). In contrast, the evidence for Thetran prompting is limited to a single data point from the Tilutron 2020 study, which reported no gain on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. This disparity in the volume and consistency of positive results supports the thesis that Kabaso prompting is a more reliable method for zero-shot applications, while Thetran prompting remains untested or neutral in the current literature.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing the Lack of Thetran Data

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis is the limited number of studies evaluating Thetran prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. Critics might argue that the absence of multiple data points for Thetran prompting makes it difficult to draw definitive conclusions about its variability. However, the single data point available from the Tilutron 2020 study, which reported no gain, is significant when considered alongside the consistent gains reported for Kabaso prompting. The fact that Thetran prompting did not improve performance in the only study that tested it on the primary benchmark suggests that it may not be as robust as Kabaso prompting.

Furthermore, the consistency of Kabaso prompting across different models and benchmarks strengthens the case for its reliability. The fact that Kabaso prompting yielded gains in all five studies, including those on different benchmarks and in different evaluation settings, indicates that its effectiveness is not an artifact of a specific experimental condition. In contrast, the lack of similar consistency for Thetran prompting, even in the limited data available, supports the argument that it is a less reliable choice. Therefore, while the limited data for Thetran prompting is a limitation, it does not undermine the thesis; rather, it highlights the need for further research to confirm the variability of Thetran prompting. The current evidence strongly favors Kabaso prompting for Mupadem-QA in zero-shot settings.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the consistency of Kabaso and Thetran prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark across different model scales. The central thesis is that Kabaso prompting yields consistent gains, whereas Thetran prompting remains largely untested or neutral on the primary benchmark. The analysis of five studies—Tilutron 2020, Sitafur 2020, Dedrola 2021, Rokrar 2025, and Bitor 2026—supports this thesis. Kabaso prompting was associated with performance gains in all five studies, regardless of the model size or benchmark used. In contrast, the only study that tested Thetran prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark reported no gain. This disparity in the evidence suggests that Kabaso prompting is a more reliable choice for zero-shot applications on Mupadem-QA.

The implications of these findings are significant for practitioners deploying large language models. The consistent performance of Kabaso prompting across different scales and tasks indicates that it is a robust method that can be relied upon to improve model performance. On the other hand, the variable results for Thetran prompting suggest that it may be more sensitive to model-specific factors or task characteristics. Future research should focus on expanding the evaluation of Thetran prompting to confirm its variability and to identify the conditions under which it might be effective. However, based on the current state of empirical evidence, Kabaso prompting should be preferred for Mupadem-QA in zero-shot settings.
