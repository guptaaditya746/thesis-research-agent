# Conflicting Evidence on Prompting and Decoding in Zero-Shot QA

## Abstract

The rapid adoption of large language models has spurred the development of various prompting and decoding strategies aimed at enhancing performance in complex reasoning tasks, particularly in zero-shot multi-hop question answering. Despite the growing popularity of specific techniques, the empirical literature presents contradictory evidence regarding their efficacy. This paper critically examines the reported outcomes of four specific methods: Kabaso prompting, Thetran prompting, Ragrer decoding, and Gofabran decoding. We focus on two key evaluation studies: the Vitraran 2024 study, which utilized the Thipom-7B model on the Donar-Code benchmark in a fine-tuned setting, and the Vives 2024 study, which employed the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. A central conflict emerges regarding Kabaso prompting, where the Vitraran 2024 study reported a performance gain, while the Vives 2024 study found no clear improvement over the baseline. Furthermore, the Vives 2024 study reported gains from Ragrer and Gofabran decoding but no gain from Thetran prompting, contrasting with the positive findings for Thetran prompting in the Vitraran 2024 study. This paper argues that these inconsistencies highlight a fundamental instability in the utility of these methods in zero-shot settings. By analyzing the specific conditions under which these methods were tested, we demonstrate that their effectiveness is highly contingent on model architecture and evaluation context, rather than being inherent properties of the techniques themselves. This analysis underscores the need for caution in the application of these methods and calls for more standardized evaluation protocols to ensure reliable performance assessments.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Prompting Strategies for Multi-Hop Reasoning

Prompting-based methods have emerged as a primary approach for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models in multi-hop question answering tasks. These strategies involve modifying the input prompt to guide the model toward more accurate and coherent responses. Two prominent examples are Kabaso prompting and Thetran prompting. Kabaso prompting is designed to structure the input in a way that encourages the model to break down complex questions into simpler sub-questions, thereby facilitating a step-by-step reasoning process. Thetran prompting, on the other hand, focuses on providing contextual cues that help the model identify relevant information within the prompt. 

However, the effectiveness of these prompting strategies may be sensitive to model architecture and evaluation settings. This sensitivity can lead to inconsistent results when these methods are transferred across different models or benchmarks. For instance, a prompting strategy that works well for one model may not yield the same benefits for another, particularly if the models have different training data or architectural designs. This paper analyzes the specific outcomes of Kabaso and Thetran prompting in two distinct studies to identify the conditions under which these methods fail or succeed. By examining the differences in model size, benchmark complexity, and evaluation settings, we aim to provide a clearer understanding of the factors that influence the performance of prompting strategies.

### 2.2 Decoding Techniques for Enhanced Generation

Decoding-based methods offer an alternative approach to improving the quality of model outputs by altering the way tokens are selected during the generation process. Two notable techniques in this category are Ragrer decoding and Gofabran decoding. Ragrer decoding modifies the probability distribution over the vocabulary to favor tokens that are more likely to contribute to a coherent and accurate response. Gofabran decoding, similarly, adjusts the decoding process to reduce the likelihood of generating irrelevant or contradictory information. 

Despite their potential benefits, decoding strategies may not consistently improve reasoning accuracy in zero-shot settings. In some cases, these techniques may introduce noise or bias that affects the final answer quality, particularly when the model is already performing well on a given task. This paper examines the reported effects of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding to determine if they provide reliable gains in zero-shot QA tasks. By comparing the results from different studies, we seek to establish whether these decoding techniques offer a robust improvement over baseline models or if their effectiveness is limited to specific contexts. This analysis is crucial for understanding the role of decoding strategies in enhancing the performance of large language models for complex reasoning tasks.

### 2.3 Evaluation Settings and Benchmarks

The evaluation of prompting and decoding strategies is heavily influenced by the specific setting and benchmark used. In this context, the zero-shot setting is particularly relevant, as it assesses the model's ability to perform a task without any prior examples or fine-tuning specific to that task. This setting is critical for understanding the inherent generalization capabilities of large language models and the robustness of the applied techniques. The two studies analyzed in this paper utilize different benchmarks and models, which introduces variability in the results. The Vitraran 2024 study employed the Thipom-7B model on the Donar-Code benchmark, operating in a fine-tuned setting. In contrast, the Vives 2024 study used the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, evaluating the methods in a zero-shot setting. These differences in model scale, benchmark complexity, and evaluation protocol are essential to consider when interpreting the reported outcomes. The fine-tuned setting in the Vitraran 2024 study allows the model to adapt to the specific characteristics of the Donar-Code benchmark, potentially amplifying the effects of the prompting strategies. Conversely, the zero-shot setting in the Vives 2024 study tests the methods' ability to generalize without such adaptation, providing a stricter test of their utility. Understanding these contextual factors is vital for assessing the stability and reliability of the methods in question.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Vitraran 2024 Study

The Vitraran 2024 study evaluated the effectiveness of prompting strategies using the Thipom-7B model on the Donar-Code benchmark. This study was conducted in a fine-tuned setting, meaning the model was adapted to the specific characteristics of the benchmark before evaluation. The primary focus was on assessing the impact of Kabaso prompting and Thetran prompting on the model's performance. The results indicated that both prompting strategies contributed to an improvement in the model's score relative to the baseline. Specifically, the study found that the application of Kabaso prompting led to a higher performance metric, suggesting that the structured input facilitated better reasoning. Similarly, the use of Thetran prompting also resulted in a positive shift in the model's output quality, indicating that the contextual cues provided by this method were beneficial in the fine-tuned context. These findings suggest that, under the specific conditions of the Vitraran 2024 study, both prompting methods were effective in enhancing the model's ability to handle multi-hop questions.

### 3.2 The Vives 2024 Study

In contrast, the Vives 2024 study examined the same set of methods but under different conditions. This study utilized the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark and was conducted in a zero-shot setting. The results from this study presented a more nuanced picture. Regarding prompting strategies, the study found that Kabaso prompting did not yield a clear improvement over the baseline performance. This lack of gain suggests that the structured input provided by Kabaso prompting did not translate into better reasoning in the zero-shot context for the Nodrana-70B model. Similarly, Thetran prompting also failed to produce a significant difference from the baseline, indicating that the contextual cues were not effective in this setting. However, the study reported positive outcomes for the decoding strategies. Ragrer decoding was found to improve the model's performance, suggesting that the modification of the probability distribution during generation was beneficial. Likewise, Gofabran decoding also resulted in a gain, indicating that the adjustment to reduce irrelevant information contributed to higher accuracy. These findings highlight a divergence in the effectiveness of prompting versus decoding strategies in the zero-shot setting.

### 3.3 The Conflict in Kabaso Prompting Results

The most significant conflict in the literature arises from the divergent outcomes reported for Kabaso prompting. The Vitraran 2024 study reported a gain from this method, whereas the Vives 2024 study reported no gain. This direct contradiction is particularly striking because it involves the same method being evaluated in two different studies. The differences in the experimental setups are substantial: the Vitraran 2024 study used a smaller model (Thipom-7B) in a fine-tuned setting on the Donar-Code benchmark, while the Vives 2024 study used a larger model (Nodrana-70B) in a zero-shot setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. These variations in model architecture, evaluation setting, and benchmark complexity likely contribute to the inconsistent results. The fact that Kabaso prompting was effective in one context but not in another suggests that its utility is not inherent to the method itself but is highly dependent on the specific conditions under which it is applied. This instability raises concerns about the reliability of Kabaso prompting as a general-purpose technique for enhancing multi-hop reasoning in large language models.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

A primary counter-argument to the thesis of method instability is that the differences in results are attributable to the distinct models and benchmarks used in the two studies, rather than to the methods themselves. Proponents of this view might argue that the Thipom-7B model and the Nodrana-70B model have different architectural properties and training data, which could influence how they respond to prompting and decoding strategies. Similarly, the Donar-Code and Mupadem-QA benchmarks may differ in complexity, question type, and evaluation metrics, leading to different outcomes. From this perspective, the conflict in results is not a sign of instability in the methods but rather a reflection of the variability in the experimental conditions. This argument is valid in that it acknowledges the importance of context in evaluating the effectiveness of these techniques. However, it does not fully address the core issue of whether these methods can be relied upon in practical applications where the specific model and benchmark may vary.

### 4.2 Rebuttal Based on Findings

While the differences in models and benchmarks are indeed significant, they do not negate the concern regarding the instability of the methods. The fact that Kabaso prompting produced a gain in one study and no gain in another, despite the differences in setup, suggests that the method lacks robustness. If a technique is to be considered useful in a broad range of applications, it should demonstrate consistent performance across different models and settings. The inconsistency in the results for Kabaso prompting, and the varying outcomes for Thetran prompting, indicate that these methods are not reliably effective in zero-shot settings. Furthermore, the positive results for Ragrer and Gofabran decoding in the Vives 2024 study highlight the need for a more comprehensive evaluation of decoding strategies. The instability of these methods is a significant concern for their practical application, as it implies that users cannot predict the outcome of applying these techniques without extensive testing on specific models and benchmarks. This unpredictability undermines the utility of these methods in real-world scenarios where rapid deployment and consistent performance are essential.
