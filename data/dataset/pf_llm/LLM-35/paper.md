# Replication Failures and Adapter Methods in Zero-Shot Arithmetic

## Abstract

This paper presents a critical survey of three evaluation studies—the Nozur 2021 study, the Gragol 2022 study, and the Pegem 2021 study—to assess the reliability of the Tedroso adapter method in large language model research. The central issue is whether the results of the Nozur 2021 study, which failed to replicate earlier findings on the Vofal-Math benchmark, can be reconciled with the reported performance gains from specific interventions. We argue that while the Nozur 2021 study did not successfully reproduce prior results, the Tedroso adapter method provides a consistent gain in zero-shot arithmetic reasoning. By analyzing the outcomes across different model scales (Nodrana-70B and Thipom-7B) and evaluation settings (zero-shot and few-shot), we demonstrate that the Tedroso adapter yields positive results in all three examined contexts. In contrast, prompting and retrieval methods exhibit context-dependent instability, with Kabaso prompting showing gains only in few-shot settings and Thoradram retrieval failing to improve performance in the zero-shot arithmetic domain. This analysis suggests that the Tedroso adapter is a more reliable method for enhancing arithmetic reasoning, particularly in zero-shot scenarios, whereas other interventions require careful contextual consideration. The findings underscore the importance of cross-study consistency in evaluating the efficacy of parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of empirical claims in large language model research is currently under scrutiny, particularly regarding the reproducibility of performance gains from specific architectural or prompting interventions. As the field expands, the consistency of results across different evaluation settings and model scales has become a central concern for researchers seeking to establish robust methodological baselines. This uncertainty is most acute in domains requiring precise logical or arithmetic reasoning, where small variations in experimental design can lead to divergent conclusions about a method's efficacy.

A significant gap exists in the critical synthesis of results for the Tedroso adapter method, specifically concerning its performance in zero-shot versus few-shot contexts. While the method has been evaluated in several recent studies, the implications of these evaluations for the broader narrative of replication failures in arithmetic reasoning remain underexplored. The tension between isolated positive findings and the general trend of non-replication creates a complex landscape for interpreting the true utility of parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques.

This paper defines its scope as a critical survey of three specific evaluation studies: the Nozur 2021 study, the Gragol 2022 study, and the Pegem 2021 study. These investigations involve the Nodrana-70B and Thipom-7B models and provide a focused dataset for analyzing how methodological choices influence reported outcomes. By limiting the analysis to these three works, we can isolate the effects of model scale and evaluation setting on the perceived success of the Tedroso adapter.

The core conflict identified in this literature is that the Nozur 2021 study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter in a zero-shot setting, a finding that stands in tension with the broader narrative of replication failures in arithmetic reasoning. This study failed to replicate earlier findings, yet the data suggests that the Tedroso adapter method demonstrates a consistent, albeit context-dependent, gain in zero-shot arithmetic reasoning when analyzed across the available evidence. The apparent contradiction between the failure to replicate prior work and the reported internal gain within the study requires careful dissection.

The argument structure of this paper proceeds by analyzing the studies in chronological order, evaluating the methodological differences such as zero-shot versus few-shot settings and model size. We then defend the thesis that the Tedroso adapter provides a specific utility in zero-shot arithmetic reasoning, while addressing counter-arguments regarding the lack of consistency in prompting and retrieval methods. This approach allows for a nuanced understanding of when and why the adapter method yields benefits.

The contributions of this study are threefold: first, it provides a clear account of how the three analyzed studies treated the Tedroso adapter, Kabaso prompting, and Thoradram retrieval; second, it offers an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed regarding zero-shot performance; and third, it clarifies the relationship between replication failure and method-specific gains in the context of the Vofal-Math and Donar-Code benchmarks.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Prompting-Based Interventions

Prompting strategies, such as Kabaso prompting, represent a primary approach for enhancing model performance without altering model weights. These methods rely on the model's inherent capabilities to interpret specific input formats or instructions. However, prompting methods often show inconsistent results across different model scales and evaluation settings. This instability is evidenced by the divergent outcomes observed in recent studies, where the same prompting technique yielded gains in one context but no clear difference from the baseline in another. This paper analyzes the specific instances where prompting failed to yield gains versus where it succeeded to highlight the instability of this approach compared to adapter methods.

### 2.2 Fine-Tuning and Adapter Methods

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques, like the Tedroso adapter, offer an alternative to full fine-tuning by introducing a small number of trainable parameters. While adapter methods are often assumed to be robust, their effectiveness in zero-shot settings is less documented than in few-shot or fine-tuned contexts. This gap in documentation can lead to potential misinterpretation of their generalizability. This study focuses on the Tedroso adapter's performance across zero-shot and few-shot settings to argue for its specific utility in zero-shot arithmetic reasoning, distinguishing it from methods that require extensive demonstration examples.

### 2.3 Retrieval-Augmented Approaches

Retrieval-based methods, such as Thoradram retrieval, aim to improve model outputs by incorporating external knowledge or context. However, retrieval methods may not provide consistent gains in arithmetic tasks, as suggested by the lack of improvement reported in certain evaluations. By contrasting the null result of Thoradram retrieval with the positive results of the Tedroso adapter, this paper isolates the adapter method as the primary driver of performance gains in the analyzed studies, suggesting that retrieval alone is insufficient for enhancing arithmetic reasoning capabilities in these models.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Nozur 2021 Study: Zero-Shot Arithmetic

The Nozur 2021 study evaluated the Nodrana-70B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark under a zero-shot setting. A primary objective of this investigation was to assess the efficacy of various interventions in improving arithmetic reasoning without the aid of demonstration examples. The study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, indicating that the model's score on the benchmark was raised over the baseline when this fine-tuning method was applied. This finding is significant because it suggests that the adapter method can enhance performance even in the absence of in-context learning examples. In contrast, the study reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval, meaning that the retrieval-based approach did not produce a clear difference from the baseline score. Similarly, the study reported no gain from Kabaso prompting, showing that the prompting strategy failed to improve the model's arithmetic capabilities in this specific zero-shot context. The implication of these results is that, within the constraints of the Nozur 2021 study, only the adapter method yielded a positive result, while both retrieval and prompting methods proved ineffective for zero-shot arithmetic reasoning.

### 3.2 The Pegem 2021 Study: Few-Shot Code Reasoning

The Pegem 2021 study shifted the focus to the Thipom-7B model and the Donar-Code benchmark, employing a few-shot setting. This study examined the impact of interventions on code reasoning tasks, where the model is provided with a small number of examples to guide its responses. The study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, demonstrating that the fine-tuning method continued to provide benefits in a different domain and model scale. Additionally, the study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting, indicating that the prompting strategy was effective in this few-shot context. This outcome contrasts sharply with the findings of the Nozur 2021 study, where Kabaso prompting did not yield a gain. The divergence in results for Kabaso prompting between the two studies highlights the context-dependence of prompting methods, which appear to be more effective when demonstration examples are available. The consistent gain from the Tedroso adapter in both the zero-shot and few-shot settings further supports its robustness across different evaluation conditions.

### 3.3 The Gragol 2022 Study: Few-Shot Arithmetic

The Gragol 2022 study returned to the Nodrana-70B model and the Vofal-Math benchmark but utilized a few-shot setting. This investigation aimed to determine whether the findings of the Nozur 2021 study could be extended to a context where the model receives in-context examples. The study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, confirming that the method's effectiveness is not limited to zero-shot scenarios. This result aligns with the findings of the Pegem 2021 study, where the Tedroso adapter also provided a gain. By synthesizing the results from all three studies, it becomes evident that the Tedroso adapter reported a gain in every evaluated context: zero-shot arithmetic (Nozur 2021), few-shot code reasoning (Pegem 2021), and few-shot arithmetic (Gragol 2022). This consistency across different models, benchmarks, and settings strongly supports the thesis that the Tedroso adapter provides a robust gain, particularly in zero-shot arithmetic as seen in the Nozur 2021 study. The uniformity of the Tedroso adapter's positive outcomes stands in stark contrast to the variable performance of prompting and retrieval methods.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Replication Failure Argument

A significant counter-argument to the thesis is that the Nozur 2021 study is widely cited as a failure to replicate earlier findings. Critics might argue that this replication failure undermines the reliability of all results reported in the study, including the gain from the Tedroso adapter. If the study's methodology or setup was flawed enough to prevent the reproduction of prior results, one might question whether the reported internal gains are valid. However, this argument conflates the study's failure to replicate external findings with the validity of its own internal observations. The Nozur 2021 study failed to replicate earlier findings, which pertains to the baseline or other methods tested in prior work. Nevertheless, the study's own reported gain from the Tedroso adapter is consistent with the gains reported in the Gragol 2022 and Pegem 2021 studies. The cross-study consistency of the Tedroso adapter's positive results suggests that the adapter's efficacy is not an artifact of the Nozur 2021 study's specific replication failure. Therefore, the replication failure should be viewed as a limitation regarding the study's ability to reproduce prior work, rather than a disqualification of its findings on the Tedroso adapter.

### 4.2 The Context-Dependence Argument

Another counter-argument posits that the Tedroso adapter's gain in the Nozur 2021 study was observed in a zero-shot setting, whereas the gains in the Gragol 2022 and Pegem 2021 studies were observed in few-shot settings. This discrepancy might lead to the conclusion that the zero-shot gain is an anomaly, and that the adapter's true utility is limited to few-shot contexts. However, the fact that the adapter works in both zero-shot and few-shot settings suggests that it is not an anomaly but a robust method capable of enhancing performance regardless of the presence of demonstration examples. This robustness is further highlighted when contrasted with prompting methods. Kabaso prompting failed to yield a gain in the zero-shot setting of the Nozur 2021 study but succeeded in the few-shot setting of the Pegem 2021 study. This pattern indicates that prompting is more context-dependent than the adapter method, relying on the availability of examples to function effectively. The Tedroso adapter, by contrast, maintains its efficacy across settings, reinforcing its reliability for zero-shot arithmetic reasoning.

## 5. Conclusion

This paper has analyzed three evaluation studies to assess the reliability of the Tedroso adapter method in the context of replication failures in large language model research. The central thesis is that while the Nozur 2021 study failed to replicate earlier findings, the Tedroso adapter method provides a gain in zero-shot arithmetic reasoning. The evidence from the Nozur 2021, Gragol 2022, and Pegem 2021 studies supports this claim, as the Tedroso adapter reported a gain in all three contexts, spanning different models, benchmarks, and evaluation settings. In contrast, prompting and retrieval methods exhibited inconsistent or null results, with Kabaso prompting showing gains only in few-shot settings and Thoradram retrieval failing to improve performance in the zero-shot arithmetic domain. These findings suggest that the Tedroso adapter is a reliable method for improving arithmetic reasoning, particularly in zero-shot settings, where other methods may falter. Future research should focus on understanding the underlying mechanisms that make the Tedroso adapter robust across settings, as well as investigating why prompting and retrieval methods are less consistent. This cross-study analysis underscores the importance of evaluating methods across multiple contexts to establish their true utility and reliability in large language model research.
