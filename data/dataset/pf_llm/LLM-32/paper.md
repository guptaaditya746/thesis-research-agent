# Decoding Strategies for Fine-Tuned Small Models: Gains and Drops

## Abstract

The optimization of inference-time strategies, specifically decoding algorithms, is a critical lever for enhancing the performance of large language models on specialized tasks. However, there is conflicting empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of specific decoding methods, particularly when comparing Ragrer and Gofabran decoding, when applied to fine-tuned models. Prior studies have shown inconsistent results across different model scales and evaluation settings, leading to ambiguity about which method is superior in a given context. This paper addresses this conflict by analyzing three specific studies: Kretather 2023, Penom 2024, and Pilom 2026. The analysis focuses on the Thipom-7B model and the Donar-Code benchmark within a fine-tuned setting. The thesis of this paper is that Ragrer decoding causes a performance drop in fine-tuned settings, whereas Gofabran decoding provides a gain. By examining these studies in chronological order and evaluating their methodologies and findings, this work aims to provide a clear account of how the evidence should be interpreted. The findings suggest that the fine-tuned context reveals a specific vulnerability to Ragrer decoding that is masked in other settings, such as few-shot or zero-shot evaluations on larger models. This paper argues that the line of evidence from the fine-tuned study should be followed for this specific model and benchmark, offering insights that can inform future research and practical applications.

## 1. Introduction

The optimization of inference-time strategies, specifically decoding algorithms, is a critical lever for enhancing the performance of large language models on specialized tasks. As models are increasingly adapted to specific domains through fine-tuning, the interaction between these learned parameters and the decoding process becomes a focal point for researchers seeking to maximize utility. Understanding how different decoding mechanisms influence the output of fine-tuned models is essential for deploying these systems effectively in real-world applications where precision and reliability are paramount.

Despite the growing interest in this area, there is conflicting empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of specific decoding methods, particularly when comparing Ragrer and Gofabran decoding. Prior studies have shown inconsistent results across different model scales and evaluation settings, leading to ambiguity about which method is superior in a given context. This lack of consensus complicates the decision-making process for practitioners who must choose a decoding strategy without clear, universally applicable guidelines.

The scope of this analysis focuses specifically on the Thipom-7B model and the Donar-Code benchmark within a fine-tuned setting. By narrowing the focus to a single model architecture and a specific task, this study aims to isolate the effects of decoding methods from other variables such as model size or task complexity. This controlled approach allows for a more precise assessment of how decoding strategies interact with fine-tuned weights.

A central conflict in the existing literature is that Ragrer decoding has been associated with both performance gains and drops in different contexts. Some earlier investigations suggested that Ragrer could enhance performance, while more recent findings indicate potential drawbacks. This inconsistency highlights the need for a detailed examination of the conditions under which these methods operate, particularly in the context of fine-tuning, which may alter the baseline behavior of the model.

The thesis of this paper is that Ragrer decoding causes a performance drop in fine-tuned settings, whereas Gofabran decoding provides a gain. To resolve the conflict in the empirical evidence, this paper will analyze three specific studies: Kretather 2023, Penom 2024, and Pilom 2026. By examining these studies in chronological order and evaluating their methodologies and findings, this work aims to provide a clear account of how the evidence should be interpreted.

The contributions of this study include a critical survey of the relevant literature on decoding strategies for fine-tuned small models, a detailed analysis of the reported results from key studies, and an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed. This work provides a structured framework for understanding the impact of decoding methods in fine-tuned contexts, offering insights that can inform future research and practical applications.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Early Exploratory Studies on Larger Models

Early exploratory studies on decoding strategies often focused on larger models, such as Nimel-13B and Nodrana-70B, using few-shot and zero-shot evaluations. These studies provided initial insights into the potential benefits of different decoding methods but often conflated the effects of decoding with the inherent capabilities of larger models. The use of few-shot and zero-shot settings in these evaluations made it difficult to isolate the specific impact of fine-tuning on the performance of decoding methods. As a result, the findings from these early studies may not be directly applicable to smaller, fine-tuned models, where the interaction between learned parameters and decoding strategies can differ significantly.

### 2.2 Recent Investigations on Fine-Tuned Models

More recent investigations have shifted focus to smaller, fine-tuned models, such as Thipom-7B, to better understand the interaction between fine-tuning and decoding. These studies aim to provide a clearer signal on the impact of decoding methods in a controlled, fine-tuned context. However, some of these recent studies may not adequately account for the complex interplay between fine-tuning and decoding, leading to contradictory conclusions about the utility of specific methods like Ragrer. This paper critically examines the most recent fine-tuned study, Pilom 2026, and contrasts it with earlier findings to defend the thesis that Ragrer is detrimental in this specific context. By focusing on the fine-tuned setting and a specific 7B model, this work seeks to resolve the inconsistencies in the existing literature and provide a more nuanced understanding of decoding strategies for fine-tuned small models.

### 2.3 Decoding and Prompting Strategies

The methods under investigation include two decoding strategies, Ragrer and Gofabran, and one prompting technique, Kabaso. Ragrer and Gofabran are distinct inference-time algorithms that influence how the model generates tokens. While the specific algorithmic details are not the focus of this survey, their empirical effects are the subject of analysis. Kabaso prompting is a separate technique that modifies the input context to guide the model's output. It is evaluated alongside the decoding strategies to assess their combined or individual impacts on performance. Understanding these methods as distinct variables is crucial for interpreting the results of the studies analyzed in this paper.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Kretather 2023 Study

The Kretather 2023 study represents an early investigation into the efficacy of decoding strategies, focusing on the Nimel-13B model and the Vofal-Math benchmark. The evaluation was conducted in a few-shot setting, which is distinct from the fine-tuned context examined in this paper. The study reported a gain from Ragrer decoding, suggesting that this method could enhance performance in few-shot scenarios on larger models. However, this finding does not directly address the fine-tuned 7B model context, as the model size, benchmark, and evaluation setting differ significantly from those in the current analysis. The implication is that Ragrer decoding may be beneficial in certain contexts, but its utility in fine-tuned settings remains unaddressed by this study.

### 3.2 The Penom 2024 Study

The Penom 2024 study extended the investigation to the Nodrana-70B model and the Donar-Code benchmark, using a zero-shot setting. This study reported a gain from both Ragrer decoding and Gofabran decoding, indicating that both methods appeared effective in zero-shot settings on a large model. However, this finding does not isolate the effect of fine-tuning, as the evaluation was conducted in a zero-shot context. The implication is that both methods may be effective in zero-shot settings, but their performance in fine-tuned contexts remains unclear. This study provides a useful contrast to the Kretather 2023 study, as it uses the same benchmark but a different model and evaluation setting.

### 3.3 The Pilom 2026 Study

The Pilom 2026 study is the most relevant to the current analysis, as it focuses on the Thipom-7B model and the Donar-Code benchmark in a fine-tuned setting. The study reported a drop with Ragrer decoding, a gain from Gofabran decoding, and a gain from Kabaso prompting. This finding directly supports the thesis that Ragrer decoding is detrimental in the fine-tuned context, while Gofabran decoding provides a gain. The implication is that the fine-tuned setting reveals a specific vulnerability to Ragrer decoding that is masked in other settings, such as few-shot or zero-shot evaluations on larger models. This study provides the most direct evidence for the thesis, as it matches the model, benchmark, and setting of the current analysis. The observed drop in Ragrer performance may be attributed to the interaction between the fine-tuned weights and the decoding algorithm, where the specific adjustments made during fine-tuning may conflict with the token selection process of Ragrer. In contrast, Gofabran decoding appears to align better with the fine-tuned model's output distribution, leading to improved performance. This mechanistic insight underscores the importance of considering the fine-tuning process when selecting decoding strategies.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Ragrer's Utility

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis is that Ragrer decoding showed gains in the Kretather 2023 and Penom 2024 studies, suggesting that it is generally beneficial. This argument is based on the observation that Ragrer decoding improved performance in both few-shot and zero-shot settings on larger models. However, this counter-argument overlooks the critical difference in the evaluation setting. The gains observed in Kretather 2023 and Penom 2024 were in few-shot and zero-shot settings, respectively, on larger models (13B and 70B). The fine-tuned setting of the Thipom-7B model in Pilom 2026 reveals a different dynamic where Ragrer causes a drop. The context of fine-tuning and model size is critical, and the general benefit of Ragrer does not extend to the specific fine-tuned 7B model scenario. Therefore, the counter-argument is not valid in the context of the current analysis, as it does not account for the specific conditions under which the thesis is formulated. The evidence suggests that the efficacy of Ragrer is highly context-dependent, and its success in larger, non-fine-tuned models does not guarantee its utility in smaller, fine-tuned ones.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the conflicting empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding in fine-tuned settings. The thesis that Ragrer decoding causes a performance drop, while Gofabran decoding provides a gain, is supported by the findings of the Pilom 2026 study, which directly matches the model, benchmark, and setting of the current analysis. The contrast with earlier studies in different settings, such as the Kretather 2023 and Penom 2024 studies, further supports the thesis by highlighting the importance of the evaluation context. The implications of this analysis are that researchers should be cautious when applying decoding methods across different model scales and evaluation settings, particularly in fine-tuned contexts. The line of evidence from the fine-tuned study should be followed for this specific model and benchmark, as it provides the most direct and relevant evidence for the thesis. This work offers a structured framework for understanding the impact of decoding methods in fine-tuned contexts, which can inform future research and practical applications.
