# Adapter Methods and Replication Challenges in Few-Shot Evaluation

## Abstract

This paper presents a critical survey of reported results in language model research, focusing on the reliability of adapter methods in few-shot settings and the replicability of earlier zero-shot findings within the Nodrana-70B model family. To support this argument, we analyze the reported outcomes of four key studies conducted on the Nodrana-70B model: the Grodrokris 2021 and Nozur 2021 studies, which established zero-shot baselines, and the Gragol 2022 and Bririro 2026 studies, which evaluated few-shot adaptation techniques. Our analysis reveals a contrast in empirical stability as reported in these studies. The Gragol 2022 study reported a failure to replicate the zero-shot results of both Grodrokris 2021 and Nozur 2021, while simultaneously reporting gains from the Tedroso adapter and Thoradram retrieval. This pattern is reinforced by the Bririro 2026 study, which also reported gains from the Tedroso adapter and Thoradram retrieval. The evidence suggests that, within this specific model family and benchmark set, few-shot adapter methods offer a more robust empirical foundation for performance improvement than zero-shot prompting strategies. By systematically examining the reported treatment of sources and methods across these studies, this paper argues that researchers working with the Nodrana-70B model should prioritize few-shot adapter methods like Tedroso for reliable performance gains, while treating zero-shot prompting results with caution due to the reported replication challenges.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of empirical findings in large language model research is a subject of ongoing scrutiny, particularly regarding the reproducibility of zero-shot performance claims and the efficacy of parameter-efficient adaptation techniques. As the field moves toward more complex evaluation protocols, a critical gap has emerged in the systematic comparison of consistency across different adaptation strategies. Specifically, there is a lack of rigorous analysis contrasting the stability of adapter methods in few-shot settings against the replicability of earlier zero-shot prompting studies. This paper addresses this gap by focusing on the Nodrana-70B model family and its associated benchmarks, providing a structured critique of how these methods perform under independent re-evaluation as reported in the literature.

The core conflict in this domain lies in the tension between reported gains from recent adapter methods and the failure to replicate earlier zero-shot results. While recent few-shot studies have highlighted the benefits of techniques such as the Tedroso adapter and Thoradram retrieval, these findings stand in contrast to the instability observed in zero-shot baselines. For instance, the Gragol 2022 study reported a failure to replicate the zero-shot results reported by Grodrokris 2021 and Nozur 2021, despite demonstrating clear gains from the Tedroso adapter in a few-shot context. This discrepancy suggests that the reliability of performance claims may depend heavily on the evaluation setting, with few-shot adapter methods potentially offering a more stable foundation for empirical progress than zero-shot prompting within this specific context.

This study argues that the Tedroso adapter method provides consistent gains in few-shot settings. This thesis positions the Tedroso adapter as a more reliable path for performance improvement, contrasting it with the volatile nature of zero-shot prompting strategies as observed in the reported results. By examining the specific failures of replication in early studies, this paper highlights the unreliability of zero-shot baselines and underscores the need for a more nuanced understanding of methodological consistency in language model evaluation.

The argument structure of this paper follows a chronological analysis of four key studies: Grodrokris 2021, Nozur 2021, Gragol 2022, and Bririro 2026. This temporal progression allows for a clear demonstration of the pattern of adapter reliability versus zero-shot instability. By analyzing how each study treated the sources and methods it assessed, this paper constructs a coherent narrative that supports the thesis. The analysis reveals that while zero-shot results have proven difficult to replicate in the reported outcomes, the Tedroso adapter has consistently shown positive effects in few-shot evaluations across different benchmarks and time periods.

This study makes three primary contributions. First, it provides a clear account of how the four key studies treated the methods and benchmarks they assessed, offering a detailed map of the empirical landscape as reported. Second, it argues for the superior consistency of the Tedroso adapter method in few-shot settings compared to zero-shot prompting strategies within the Nodrana-70B model family. Third, it identifies the specific failures of replication in early zero-shot studies, thereby highlighting the need for more robust evaluation protocols in future research. By synthesizing these findings, this paper offers a critical perspective on the current state of adapter methods and their role in advancing language model research.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Early Zero-Shot Prompting Evaluations

Early studies in this domain focused on zero-shot prompting evaluations of large language models, with a particular emphasis on the Nodrana-70B model family. The Grodrokris 2021 study and the Nozur 2021 study were among the first to report specific performance levels in zero-shot settings, using benchmarks such as the Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math, respectively. These studies established initial baselines for zero-shot performance, but their findings have not been consistently replicated in subsequent independent re-runs as reported in the literature. The Gragol 2022 study, for example, reported a failure to replicate the results of both Grodrokris 2021 and Nozur 2021, suggesting potential instability or context-dependency in zero-shot prompting results. This lack of replicability raises questions about the reliability of zero-shot baselines and the need for more robust evaluation protocols.

### 2.2 Few-Shot Adapter and Retrieval Methods

In contrast to the instability observed in zero-shot evaluations, recent few-shot adapter and retrieval methods have shown promise in providing consistent performance gains. The Tedroso adapter and Thoradram retrieval are two such methods that have been evaluated in few-shot settings. The Gragol 2022 study reported a gain from both the Tedroso adapter and Thoradram retrieval, while the Bririro 2026 study also reported gains from these methods. These findings suggest that adapter methods may offer a more stable and reliable path for performance improvement in few-shot settings. However, the consistency of these methods across different benchmarks and time periods has not been systematically argued against the backdrop of the zero-shot replication crisis. This paper addresses this gap by analyzing the specific failures of replication in early zero-shot studies and contrasting them with the more stable few-shot adapter results, thereby highlighting the potential of the Tedroso adapter as a reliable method for performance gains.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Zero-Shot Studies (2021)

The empirical landscape for the Nodrana-70B model was initially defined by two zero-shot evaluations conducted in 2021. The Grodrokris 2021 study evaluated the model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, establishing a baseline for zero-shot performance in question-answering tasks. Concurrently, the Nozur 2021 study assessed the same model on the Vofal-Math benchmark, providing a reference point for zero-shot mathematical reasoning. These two studies served as the foundational zero-shot baselines for the Nodrana-70B model. Their reported results were subsequently subject to independent replication attempts in later few-shot studies, setting the stage for the observed discrepancies in empirical reliability.

### 3.2 Few-Shot Studies and Replication Failures (2022)

The Gragol 2022 study marked a shift toward few-shot evaluation settings, specifically assessing the Nodrana-70B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark. A critical aspect of this study was its attempt to replicate the earlier zero-shot findings. The study reported a failure to replicate the results reported by the Grodrokris 2021 study, indicating that the zero-shot performance on Mupadem-QA was not consistent under the new evaluation conditions. Furthermore, the Gragol 2022 study also reported a failure to replicate the results of the Nozur 2021 study, suggesting that the zero-shot baseline on Vofal-Math was similarly unstable. Despite these replication failures, the study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, demonstrating that the fine-tuning method improved the model's score over the baseline in the few-shot setting. Additionally, the study reported a gain from Thoradram retrieval, indicating that the retrieval-based method also contributed to performance improvements. The contrast between the failure to replicate zero-shot results and the success of few-shot adaptation methods highlights a significant divergence in reliability between the two evaluation paradigms.

### 3.3 Recent Few-Shot Consistency (2026)

The Bririro 2026 study extended the few-shot evaluation to the Donar-Code benchmark, again using the Nodrana-70B model. This study provided further evidence for the consistency of adapter methods in few-shot settings. It reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, confirming that the fine-tuning method continued to yield positive performance changes in a new benchmark context. Similarly, the study reported a gain from Thoradram retrieval, reinforcing the effectiveness of retrieval-augmented approaches in few-shot evaluations. The consistency of the Tedroso adapter's positive impact across both the Gragol 2022 and Bririro 2026 studies supports the thesis that this method offers a reliable path for performance gains in few-shot settings, in stark contrast to the instability observed in zero-shot prompting.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Zero-Shot Stability

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that the zero-shot findings from the 2021 studies (Grodrokris 2021 and Nozur 2021) might still be valid, and the replication failures observed in the Gragol 2022 study are attributable to experimental error or minor setup differences rather than fundamental instability in zero-shot prompting. Proponents of this view might argue that zero-shot performance is inherently sensitive to prompt formatting and context, and that the Gragol 2022 study did not perfectly match the original conditions. However, this explanation is weakened by the consistent failure to replicate both the Grodrokris 2021 and Nozur 2021 results within the same study. If the failures were due to isolated experimental errors, one might expect at least one of the two replications to succeed. The simultaneous failure of both replications suggests a systematic issue with the reliability of zero-shot baselines in this specific context.

Furthermore, the consistent success of the Tedroso adapter in both the 2022 and 2026 few-shot studies provides a strong counterpoint. The Tedroso adapter reported gains in both the Gragol 2022 and Bririro 2026 studies, across different benchmarks (Vofal-Math and Donar-Code). This consistency in positive outcomes for the adapter method, contrasted with the inconsistency in zero-shot replication, supports the argument that few-shot adapter methods are more reliable. The evidence favors following the line of empirical evidence from few-shot adapter methods, such as Tedroso, over the zero-shot prompting findings, which have proven difficult to replicate under independent evaluation. It is important to note that this argument is limited to the Nodrana-70B model family and the specific benchmarks evaluated; it does not claim that zero-shot prompting is universally unreliable, but rather that in this specific evaluation context, few-shot adapter methods have demonstrated greater consistency.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the reported results of four key studies on the Nodrana-70B model to evaluate the reliability of adapter methods versus zero-shot prompting. The analysis reveals that the Tedroso adapter method provided consistent gains in few-shot settings across the Gragol 2022 and Bririro 2026 studies. In contrast, the zero-shot findings from the Grodrokris 2021 and Nozur 2021 studies were not replicated by the Gragol 2022 study, indicating significant instability in zero-shot performance claims within this model family.

The implications of these findings are clear for future research in language model evaluation, particularly for those working with the Nodrana-70B model. Researchers should prioritize few-shot adapter methods like Tedroso for reliable performance gains, as these methods have demonstrated consistent positive effects across different benchmarks and time periods in the reported studies. Conversely, zero-shot prompting results should be treated with caution due to the persistent replication challenges observed in this analysis. The argument supports the thesis that the Tedroso adapter method is a more consistent and reliable approach in the current landscape of language model evaluation for this specific model family, offering a robust foundation for empirical progress compared to the volatile nature of zero-shot prompting strategies.
