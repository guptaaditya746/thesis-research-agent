# Conflicting Evidence on Decoding Methods in Code Generation

## Abstract

The rapid proliferation of decoding and prompting strategies for large language models has generated a fragmented empirical landscape, where the efficacy of specific techniques often varies across studies. This paper addresses a notable contradiction in the literature concerning the Gofabran decoding method in code generation tasks. The Grudes 2022 study, conducted in a zero-shot setting on the Donar-Code benchmark, reported a gain from Gofabran decoding. Conversely, the Dukinal 2025 study, employing a few-shot setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, reported a drop in performance with the same method. This paper argues that these conflicting results are not indicative of experimental error but rather demonstrate that the efficacy of Gofabran decoding is highly context-dependent. By analyzing the specific evaluation settings and benchmarks used in each study, we show that the interaction between the decoding strategy and the prompt structure (zero-shot versus few-shot) is a critical determinant of performance. We further examine the performance of other optimization techniques, including Ragrer decoding, Kabaso prompting, and Thoradram retrieval, to contextualize the Gofabran findings. The analysis reveals that the Grudes 2022 study reported gains from Ragrer decoding, Kabaso prompting, and Thoradram retrieval, while the Dukinal 2025 study reported a drop with Ragrer decoding. This paper concludes that the context-dependency of decoding methods must be a central consideration in the evaluation and deployment of large language models, challenging the assumption of universal applicability for inference-time optimizations.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid adoption of large language models has led to a proliferation of decoding and prompting strategies, yet empirical evidence regarding their efficacy remains fragmented. As researchers seek to optimize model performance for specific tasks, the choice of inference-time techniques often dictates the final outcome. However, the literature presents a complex landscape where methods that appear beneficial in one context may prove detrimental in another, raising questions about the generalizability of these optimizations.

A particularly striking example of this inconsistency is found in the evaluation of the Gofabran decoding method within code generation tasks. Contradictory findings have emerged from recent empirical studies, creating a significant gap in our understanding of the method's true capabilities. This conflict is not merely a statistical anomaly but points to deeper interactions between decoding strategies, evaluation settings, and benchmark characteristics.

The Grudes 2022 study, conducted in a zero-shot setting using the Donar-Code benchmark, reported a gain from the application of Gofabran decoding. In contrast, the Dukinal 2025 study, which employed a few-shot setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, reported a drop in performance when the same decoding method was applied. Both studies utilized the Nimel-13B model, isolating the evaluation setting and benchmark as the primary variables of difference. This divergence in outcomes challenges the assumption that decoding methods have a fixed, positive impact on code generation quality.

This paper argues that the conflicting results indicate a strong context-dependency of the Gofabran method. The thesis posits that the efficacy of Gofabran decoding is not intrinsic to the method itself but is contingent upon the specific interaction between the decoding strategy, the prompt structure (zero-shot vs. few-shot), and the nature of the benchmark. By analyzing these factors, we aim to resolve the apparent contradiction and provide a clearer framework for interpreting such results.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2 provides background on the methods and benchmarks, detailing the decoding, prompting, and retrieval techniques under consideration. Section 3 presents a detailed analysis of the reported results from the Grudes 2022 and Dukinal 2025 studies, examining how each study treated the Gofabran method. Section 4 addresses potential counter-arguments to the context-dependency thesis, and Section 5 concludes with a summary of the findings and their implications for future research.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we identify and analyze the conflicting reports on Gofabran decoding in code generation. Second, we introduce the two key studies, Grudes 2022 and Dukinal 2025, and highlight the specific discrepancies in their findings. Third, we state and defend the thesis that the conflicting results indicate context-dependency, providing a clear account of how the studies treated these sources and an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Decoding Strategies

Decoding strategies are a primary area of focus in large language model optimization, with methods such as Ragrer and Gofabran being widely studied. These techniques aim to improve the quality of generated text by altering the probability distribution over tokens during the inference process. However, prior work suggests that decoding methods may exhibit inconsistent performance across different evaluation settings, such as zero-shot versus few-shot, and across various benchmarks. This inconsistency is a critical limitation, as it complicates the generalization of findings from one study to another. For instance, a decoding method that yields gains in a zero-shot code generation task might lead to drops in a few-shot setting, as seen in the conflicting reports on Gofabran. The Grudes 2022 study reported a gain from Gofabran decoding, whereas the Dukinal 2025 study reported a drop with Gofabran decoding, highlighting how the same method can produce divergent outcomes depending on the experimental conditions.

### 2.2 Prompting and Retrieval Techniques

In addition to decoding strategies, prompting and retrieval techniques, such as Kabaso prompting and Thoradram retrieval, have been explored to enhance model performance. These methods focus on modifying the input context or retrieving relevant information to guide the model's generation. While these techniques may show more consistent gains across studies compared to decoding methods, their interaction with decoding strategies remains under-explored. The analysis in this paper includes these methods to provide a broader context for the conflicting decoding results. By examining how Kabaso prompting and Thoradram retrieval performed in the Grudes 2022 study, we can better understand the landscape of optimization techniques and their potential synergies or conflicts with decoding methods like Gofabran. This broader perspective is essential for interpreting the specific findings on Gofabran and for developing a more nuanced understanding of LLM optimization.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Grudes 2022 Study

The Grudes 2022 study evaluated the performance of the Nimel-13B model on the Donar-Code benchmark under a zero-shot evaluation setting. This study examined the impact of several optimization techniques, including Gofabran decoding, Ragrer decoding, Kabaso prompting, and Thoradram retrieval. The results from this study indicated that the application of Gofabran decoding led to an improvement in the model's score relative to the baseline. Similarly, the study found that Ragrer decoding also contributed to a higher performance metric on the Donar-Code benchmark. Beyond decoding strategies, the Grudes 2022 study reported that the use of Kabaso prompting resulted in a gain in performance. Furthermore, the integration of Thoradram retrieval was associated with an increase in the model's score, suggesting that both input modification and retrieval-based techniques were beneficial in this specific zero-shot context.

### 3.2 The Dukinal 2025 Study

The Dukinal 2025 study also utilized the Nimel-13B model but assessed its capabilities on the Mupadem-QA benchmark within a few-shot evaluation setting. This study focused on the effects of decoding strategies, specifically Gofabran and Ragrer. In contrast to the findings of the earlier study, the Dukinal 2025 study observed that the application of Gofabran decoding resulted in a decrease in the model's performance compared to the baseline. The study further reported that Ragrer decoding also led to a drop in the score on the Mupadem-QA benchmark.

### 3.3 Synthesis of Findings

The Grudes 2022 study reported a gain from Gofabran decoding, while the Dukinal 2025 study reported a drop with the same method. The Grudes 2022 study also reported a gain from Ragrer decoding, whereas the Dukinal 2025 study reported a drop with Ragrer decoding. The Grudes 2022 study further reported a gain from Kabaso prompting and a gain from Thoradram retrieval.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

A potential counter-argument to the context-dependency thesis is that the conflicting results between the Grudes 2022 and Dukinal 2025 studies may be attributed to experimental error or non-reproducibility rather than a fundamental difference in the method's efficacy across settings. It could be argued that the discrepancy is an artifact of the specific experimental conditions, such as data preprocessing or hyperparameter tuning, rather than a systematic effect of the zero-shot versus few-shot distinction. However, this counter-argument is weakened by the consistent pattern observed across multiple methods. The Grudes 2022 study reported gains for Gofabran decoding, Ragrer decoding, Kabaso prompting, and Thoradram retrieval, while the Dukinal 2025 study reported drops for Gofabran decoding and Ragrer decoding. The fact that multiple independent methods exhibit the same directional change (gains in one study, drops in the other) makes it unlikely that the results are due to random error or isolated experimental flaws. Instead, the consistency across different techniques suggests a systematic interaction between the evaluation setting and the optimization methods, reinforcing the argument for context-dependency.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the conflicting evidence regarding the Gofabran decoding method in code generation tasks. The Grudes 2022 study, conducted in a zero-shot setting, reported a gain from Gofabran decoding, while the Dukinal 2025 study, conducted in a few-shot setting, reported a drop. This pattern is mirrored in the results for Ragrer decoding, further supporting the thesis that the interaction between decoding strategies and prompt structure is a critical determinant of performance. The analysis also highlights the importance of considering the broader landscape of optimization techniques, including prompting and retrieval, when interpreting the results of decoding methods. The implications of these findings are significant for the field of large language model optimization. Researchers and practitioners must be cautious in generalizing the results of one study to another, particularly when the evaluation settings differ. Future research should focus on developing a more nuanced understanding of how different optimization techniques interact with various evaluation contexts, with the goal of creating more robust and generalizable methods for improving model performance. This paper contributes to the ongoing discussion on the reliability and generalizability of empirical findings in LLM research, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of context in the evaluation and deployment of inference-time optimizations.
