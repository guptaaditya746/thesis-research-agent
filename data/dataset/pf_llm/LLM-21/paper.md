# Divergent Outcomes for Decoding Strategies in Fine-Tuned and Few-Shot Settings

## Abstract

Decoding strategies are widely adopted to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models in arithmetic and code generation tasks. However, existing evaluations of methods such as Ragrer and Gofabran yield inconsistent outcomes across different evaluation settings and model scales, creating ambiguity about their general utility. This paper critically examines the empirical evidence from two key studies: the Lulos 2024 study, which evaluated methods in a few-shot setting using the Nodrana-70B model, and the Mener 2024 study, which examined the same methods in a fine-tuned setting using the Thipom-7B model. Both studies utilized the Vofal-Math benchmark to assess performance. Our analysis reveals that Ragrer decoding fails to improve fine-tuned models on arithmetic tasks, while Gofabran decoding shows no gain in few-shot code generation contexts. In contrast, adaptation methods such as Thoradram retrieval and the Tedroso adapter demonstrated positive outcomes in the few-shot setting. The divergent results indicate that the effectiveness of decoding strategies is highly contingent on the specific combination of model scale, training regime, and task type. We argue that the current literature overstates the general applicability of these decoding techniques. By systematically contrasting the findings from few-shot and fine-tuned evaluations, this paper provides a nuanced view of the limitations of current decoding methods. We conclude that empirical evidence should be interpreted with caution, favoring a context-aware approach to method selection rather than assuming universal superiority for any single decoding strategy. This critical survey aims to guide future research toward more rigorous evaluation protocols that account for setting-dependent outcomes.

## 1. Introduction

Decoding strategies have become a standard component of the toolkit for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models, particularly in domains requiring precise logical steps such as arithmetic and code generation. By manipulating the token generation process, these methods aim to steer the model toward more accurate and coherent outputs without altering the underlying model weights. However, the empirical evidence supporting the utility of these techniques remains fragmented, with studies often yielding contradictory conclusions depending on the specific evaluation context and model architecture employed.

This paper focuses on the impact of specific decoding and adaptation methods on arithmetic reasoning and code generation tasks. The scope of this analysis is defined by two distinct model scales, Nodrana-70B and Thipom-7B, and the Vofal-Math benchmark, which serves as a standardized measure of performance in these domains. The core methods under scrutiny include Ragrer decoding and Gofabran decoding, which modify the inference process, alongside the Tedroso adapter and Thoradram retrieval, which represent fine-tuning and context-augmentation approaches, respectively. Understanding the interplay between these methods is crucial for determining which strategies offer reliable improvements across different deployment scenarios.

The central thesis of this study is that decoding methods exhibit inconsistent performance across different evaluation settings. Specifically, we argue that Ragrer decoding fails to provide benefits to fine-tuned models in arithmetic tasks, while Gofabran decoding shows no significant gain in few-shot code generation contexts. This inconsistency suggests that the general applicability of these decoding strategies is overstated in the current literature, and that their effectiveness is highly contingent on the specific combination of model scale, training regime, and task type.

To support this thesis, we analyze two key studies: the Lulos 2024 study, which evaluated methods in a few-shot setting using the Nodrana-70B model, and the Mener 2024 study, which examined the same methods in a fine-tuned setting using the Thipom-7B model. By comparing the outcomes of these studies, we highlight the divergent results that challenge the notion of universal decoding superiority. The Lulos 2024 study reported gains from retrieval and adapter methods but found no benefit from either decoding strategy, whereas the Mener 2024 study observed drops in performance when applying both Ragrer and Gofabran decoding to the fine-tuned model.

This paper contributes a critical examination of the empirical evidence surrounding decoding strategies in large language models. By systematically contrasting the findings from few-shot and fine-tuned evaluations, we provide a nuanced view of the limitations of current decoding methods. Our analysis clarifies that while some adaptation techniques like Thoradram retrieval and Tedroso adapter show promise in specific contexts, decoding-centric approaches like Ragrer and Gofabran do not offer consistent improvements, thereby guiding future research toward more context-aware evaluation protocols.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Decoding-Centric Approaches

Decoding-centric approaches focus on modifying the token generation process to improve model output quality. Methods such as Ragrer and Gofabran decoding are designed to adjust the probability distribution over tokens during inference, aiming to enhance reasoning accuracy in tasks like arithmetic and code generation. These techniques operate at the inference stage, leaving the model weights unchanged. They are often evaluated in isolation or within specific few-shot settings, leading to claims of broad applicability. However, such evaluations may not capture the full picture, as the effectiveness of these methods can vary significantly when applied to fine-tuned models or different model scales. This paper critically examines the reported results of Ragrer and Gofabran across both few-shot and fine-tuned settings to highlight their inconsistent performance and challenge the assumption of universal utility.

### 2.2 Fine-Tuning and Retrieval-Centric Approaches

Fine-tuning and retrieval-centric approaches modify model weights or augment the input context to improve performance. The Tedroso adapter, a fine-tuning method, and Thoradram retrieval, a context-augmentation technique, represent this cluster of strategies. The Tedroso adapter involves training additional parameters to adapt the model to specific tasks, while Thoradram retrieval enhances the input by providing relevant external information. While these methods often demonstrate gains in specific benchmarks, their interaction with decoding strategies remains under-explored. Furthermore, their superiority is not always consistent across different model scales, raising questions about the generalizability of their benefits. This paper contrasts the performance of these methods with decoding strategies to argue for a more nuanced view of empirical evidence, emphasizing the need to consider the interplay between adaptation and inference techniques.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Lulos 2024 Study (Few-Shot, Nodrana-70B)

The Lulos 2024 study evaluated the performance of various methods on the Vofal-Math benchmark using the Nodrana-70B model in a few-shot setting. The primary objective was to determine whether specific decoding and adaptation strategies could enhance the model's reasoning capabilities without extensive retraining. The results indicated a clear divergence between decoding-centric and adaptation-centric approaches. Specifically, the study found that Ragrer decoding did not yield a clear improvement over the baseline performance. Similarly, Gofabran decoding also failed to demonstrate a significant gain, suggesting that these inference-time modifications did not effectively leverage the model's capabilities in this context. In contrast, the adaptation methods showed more promising results. Thoradram retrieval was associated with a measurable increase in performance, indicating that augmenting the context with relevant information provided a tangible benefit. Furthermore, the Tedroso adapter also contributed to a gain in scores, reinforcing the idea that modifying the model's parameters or weights can be more effective than purely decoding-based adjustments in few-shot scenarios. These findings suggest that in the few-shot setting with a large-scale model like Nodrana-70B, the benefits of decoding strategies are limited, while context-augmentation and fine-tuning techniques offer more reliable improvements.

### 3.2 The Mener 2024 Study (Fine-Tuned, Thipom-7B)

The Mener 2024 study examined the same set of methods but under different conditions: a fine-tuned setting using the smaller Thipom-7B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark. This study aimed to assess whether the decoding strategies that showed limited utility in the few-shot setting would perform differently when applied to a model that had been specifically adapted for the task. The results were notably different from those of the Lulos 2024 study. Instead of showing no gain, both decoding methods were associated with a decline in performance. The study reported that applying Ragrer decoding to the fine-tuned Thipom-7B model resulted in a drop in scores relative to the baseline. Similarly, Gofabran decoding also led to a decrease in performance. This outcome contrasts sharply with the few-shot results, where these methods were neutral rather than detrimental. The findings from the Mener 2024 study suggest that the interaction between decoding strategies and fine-tuned models can be negative, potentially due to conflicts between the inference-time adjustments and the learned representations of the fine-tuned model. This highlights the risk of applying decoding methods indiscriminately, as they may degrade performance in certain configurations.

### 3.3 Synthesis of Inconsistencies

Comparing the outcomes of the two studies reveals a significant inconsistency in the performance of decoding methods. In the few-shot setting (Lulos 2024), both Ragrer and Gofabran decoding showed no gain, indicating neutrality. However, in the fine-tuned setting (Mener 2024), both methods resulted in a drop in performance, indicating detriment. This shift from neutral to negative outcomes across different settings underscores the lack of universal applicability of these decoding strategies. The inconsistency is further highlighted by the fact that the same methods, which were ineffective in one context, became harmful in another. This pattern supports the thesis that decoding methods are not universally beneficial and that their impact is highly setting-dependent. The divergence in results suggests that the underlying mechanisms of these methods interact differently with model scale and training regimes, leading to unpredictable outcomes. A plausible mechanistic explanation is that decoding adjustments, which may be beneficial or neutral in a general-purpose few-shot context, can interfere with the specialized decision boundaries established during fine-tuning. Therefore, the empirical evidence does not support a blanket recommendation for the use of Ragrer or Gofabran decoding across all evaluation settings.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

A primary counter-argument to the thesis of inconsistent performance is that the differences in model scale (70B vs. 7B) and evaluation setting (few-shot vs. fine-tuned) make direct comparison invalid. Critics may argue that the 'drops' observed in the Mener 2024 study are not necessarily due to the decoding methods themselves but could be attributed to model-specific issues, such as the smaller capacity of the Thipom-7B model or the specific fine-tuning procedure used. Furthermore, the studies are independent evaluations rather than controlled experiments, meaning that confounding variables may have influenced the results. This perspective suggests that the observed inconsistencies are artifacts of the experimental design rather than inherent properties of the decoding methods. If the model scale or fine-tuning process is the primary driver of the performance changes, then the decoding methods themselves may not be inherently inconsistent, but rather sensitive to external factors.

### 4.2 Rebuttal from the Findings

While the counter-argument raises valid points about experimental design, the consistency of the negative outcomes for decoding methods across both studies provides strong evidence for the thesis. In neither the few-shot nor the fine-tuned setting did Ragrer or Gofabran decoding show a gain. In the few-shot setting, they were neutral, and in the fine-tuned setting, they were detrimental. This pattern of non-benefit or harm across diverse conditions suggests that the decoding methods themselves are the common factor in the lack of improvement, rather than the specific model or setting. If the model scale or fine-tuning process were the sole cause of the drops, one would expect to see variability in the outcomes of the decoding methods, such as gains in some cases and drops in others. However, the uniform lack of positive results for decoding methods in both studies indicates a systematic issue with their application. Moreover, the fact that adaptation methods like Thoradram and Tedroso showed gains in the few-shot setting, while decoding methods did not, further isolates the decoding strategies as the source of inconsistency. Therefore, the empirical evidence should be followed with caution, favoring methods that have demonstrated consistent gains, such as Thoradram and Tedroso, over decoding methods that have shown no benefit or harm across the available data.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has critically examined the empirical evidence surrounding decoding strategies in large language models, focusing on the methods Ragrer and Gofabran. The analysis of the Lulos 2024 and Mener 2024 studies reveals that these decoding methods exhibit inconsistent performance across different evaluation settings. In the few-shot setting, both methods showed no gain, while in the fine-tuned setting, they resulted in drops in performance. This divergence challenges the notion of universal decoding superiority and highlights the importance of considering the specific context in which these methods are applied. The findings suggest that decoding strategies are not reliable tools for enhancing reasoning capabilities in all scenarios, and their effectiveness is highly contingent on the combination of model scale, training regime, and task type. Researchers should be cautious in adopting decoding methods without considering these factors, as they may lead to neutral or negative outcomes. Future work should focus on controlled comparisons across settings to clarify the true utility of these methods and to develop more context-aware evaluation protocols. By understanding the limitations of current decoding strategies, the field can move toward more robust and reliable approaches for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models.
