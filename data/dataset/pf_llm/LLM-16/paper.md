# Divergent Outcomes for Retrieval and Prompting in Large-Scale Zero-Shot Evaluation

## Abstract

The reliability of auxiliary techniques for large language models remains a subject of debate, particularly regarding the consistency of their performance across diverse benchmarks and evaluation settings. This paper examines the efficacy of Thoradram retrieval and Thetran prompting on the Nodrana-70B model, alongside the prompting strategy known as Kabaso. By critically analyzing three recent studies from 2026—Bivam, Thonir, and Fodra—we investigate the conditions under which these methods yield gains or fail to improve upon baseline performance. Our analysis reveals that Thoradram retrieval produces inconsistent results on the Vofal-Math benchmark, failing to provide a clear advantage in both mathematical and question-answering contexts. Furthermore, we demonstrate that Thetran prompting does not offer a zero-shot gain on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, despite showing potential in few-shot mathematical settings. The evidence suggests that the effectiveness of these methods is highly dependent on the specific interaction between the model, the task, and the evaluation protocol. We argue that general claims of efficacy for these techniques are not supported by the current empirical landscape. Instead, the data points to method-specific and setting-specific limitations that must be accounted for in practical deployment. This paper provides a structured account of these divergent outcomes, offering a framework for interpreting the reliability of retrieval and prompting strategies in large-scale evaluation.

## 1. Introduction

The integration of retrieval-augmented generation and advanced prompting strategies is central to enhancing the performance of large language models, particularly in complex reasoning tasks. As models like Nodrana-70B become more prevalent in specialized applications, the community has increasingly turned to auxiliary techniques such as Thoradram retrieval and Thetran prompting to push performance boundaries. These methods aim to bridge the gap between static model weights and dynamic task requirements, offering a flexible path to improved accuracy without extensive retraining. However, the empirical landscape surrounding these techniques remains fragmented, with recent studies yielding conflicting signals about their true efficacy.

A significant gap exists in the current literature regarding the reliability of specific techniques when applied across different benchmarks and evaluation settings. While some approaches show promise in controlled environments, their generalizability to diverse tasks such as mathematical reasoning or question answering is not well-established. This lack of consensus is particularly acute for the Nodrana-70B model, where the interplay between model scale, task complexity, and auxiliary method design creates a complex evaluation space. Without a clear understanding of when and why these methods succeed or fail, practitioners risk deploying suboptimal configurations that may not deliver expected gains.

This paper focuses on the Nodrana-70B model and three specific methods: Thoradram retrieval, Thetran prompting, and Kabaso prompting. The central conflict identified in recent 2026 studies is the inconsistent reporting of outcomes for these methods. For instance, while some evaluations suggest that retrieval can enhance mathematical reasoning, others indicate that it may introduce noise or fail to align with the specific demands of the task. Similarly, prompting strategies that appear effective in few-shot settings may not translate to zero-shot scenarios, raising questions about their robustness.

Our thesis is that Thoradram retrieval is inconsistent on the Vofal-Math benchmark, and Thetran prompting offers no zero-shot gain on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. To support this argument, we analyze three key studies from 2026: Bivam, Thonir, and Fodra. By examining how each study treated the methods and benchmarks, we aim to provide a coherent account of the empirical evidence. This analysis allows us to determine whether the observed inconsistencies are fundamental limitations of the methods or artifacts of specific evaluation settings.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we provide a clear account of how the three key studies treated the sources of Thoradram, Thetran, and Kabaso, highlighting the divergent outcomes reported. Second, we argue for a specific view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, emphasizing the importance of evaluation setting in interpreting results. Third, we offer a framework for understanding the conditions under which retrieval and prompting methods may fail to yield gains, thereby guiding future research and practical deployment of these techniques.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Retrieval-Augmented Approaches

Retrieval-augmented methods aim to enhance model performance by incorporating external knowledge into the generation process. Among these, Thoradram retrieval is a specific approach that has gained attention for its potential to improve reasoning tasks by providing relevant context to the model. It operates by retrieving external information that is intended to supplement the model's internal knowledge. However, the effectiveness of such methods is not uniform across all tasks. In particular, mathematical benchmarks like Vofal-Math require precise logical reasoning, which may not always align with the type of information retrieved. This misalignment can introduce noise, leading to inconsistent outcomes. This paper analyzes the specific outcomes of Thoradram on Vofal-Math to determine if its inconsistency is a fundamental limitation or a setting-dependent artifact.

### 2.2 Prompting Strategies

Prompting strategies focus on optimizing the input format to elicit better performance from the model. Thetran and Kabaso prompting are two such approaches that have been explored in recent studies. Thetran prompting is a technique designed to structure the input in a way that leverages in-context examples, while Kabaso prompting represents an alternative prompting paradigm. While these methods can be effective in certain contexts, their generalizability across different task types and evaluation settings is a concern. For example, a prompting strategy that works well in a few-shot setting may not yield gains in a zero-shot scenario. This paper examines the differential performance of Thetran and Kabaso across Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math to isolate the conditions under which prompting fails to yield gains, providing insights into the limitations of these strategies.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Bivam 2026 Study

The Bivam 2026 study evaluated the Nodrana-70B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark under a few-shot setting. This study is significant as it provides a baseline for understanding how auxiliary methods perform in a mathematical reasoning context where the model is provided with examples. The findings from this study reveal a mixed picture regarding the utility of the tested methods. Specifically, the study found that Thoradram retrieval did not result in a clear improvement over the baseline performance. This suggests that, in this particular configuration, the external context provided by the retrieval mechanism did not align sufficiently with the requirements of the mathematical tasks to produce a measurable benefit.

In contrast, the same study observed a positive effect from Thetran prompting. The use of this specific prompting strategy led to a higher score on the Vofal-Math benchmark compared to the baseline, indicating that the input format optimization was effective in the few-shot mathematical setting. However, the study also reported that Kabaso prompting did not yield a gain. This divergence between Thetran and Kabaso within the same study and setting highlights the sensitivity of prompting strategies to their specific design and implementation. The implication is that while prompting can be beneficial in few-shot math, the choice of strategy is critical, and not all prompting methods are equally effective.

### 3.2 The Thonir 2026 Study

The Thonir 2026 study shifted the focus to the Mupadem-QA benchmark and employed a zero-shot setting. Notably, this study utilized the Nimel-13B model rather than the Nodrana-70B, providing a comparative context that introduces model size as a variable. The primary objective was to assess the robustness of prompting strategies in a question-answering context without the aid of in-context examples. The results indicated that Thetran prompting did not provide a gain in this zero-shot QA setting. This finding is crucial as it contrasts with the positive outcome observed for Thetran in the few-shot mathematical setting of the Bivam 2026 study. It suggests that the effectiveness of Thetran is not inherent to the method itself but is contingent upon the evaluation setting and the task type.

Furthermore, the Thonir 2026 study reported that Kabaso prompting also failed to produce a gain on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in the zero-shot setting. This consistent lack of improvement for both prompting strategies in this specific context reinforces the idea that zero-shot QA may present unique challenges that these particular prompting methods are not equipped to address. The use of a smaller model, Nimel-13B, adds another layer of complexity, as it is unclear whether the lack of gain is due to the method's limitations or the model's capacity. However, the absence of gain for both methods suggests a systemic issue with the application of these prompting strategies in zero-shot QA.

### 3.3 The Fodra 2026 Study

The Fodra 2026 study returned to the Nodrana-70B model but applied it to the Mupadem-QA benchmark under a few-shot setting. This study is particularly relevant for assessing the consistency of Thoradram retrieval across different domains. The findings showed that Thoradram retrieval did not result in a gain on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. This outcome mirrors the result from the Bivam 2026 study, where Thoradram also failed to provide a benefit on the Vofal-Math benchmark. The repetition of this null result across two different benchmarks (math and QA) and two different studies strengthens the argument that Thoradram retrieval is inconsistent in its ability to enhance model performance. It suggests that the retrieval mechanism may not be effectively leveraging external knowledge to improve the model's outputs in these specific tasks.

### 3.4 Synthesis of Findings

Synthesizing the findings from the three studies reveals a clear pattern of inconsistency and setting-dependence. Thoradram retrieval is the most problematic method, as it failed to provide a gain in both the Vofal-Math benchmark (Bivam 2026) and the Mupadem-QA benchmark (Fodra 2026). This lack of efficacy across different task types and studies indicates that Thoradram is not a reliable enhancement for the Nodrana-70B model in these contexts. The inconsistency is not limited to a single benchmark or setting, suggesting a fundamental limitation in how the retrieval process interacts with the model's reasoning capabilities.

Thetran prompting presents a more nuanced picture. It showed a gain in the few-shot mathematical setting (Bivam 2026) but failed to provide a gain in the zero-shot QA setting (Thonir 2026). This divergence supports the thesis that Thetran's effectiveness is highly dependent on the evaluation setting. The method appears to be more suited to few-shot scenarios where in-context examples can guide the model, but it lacks the robustness to transfer its benefits to zero-shot conditions. This setting-dependence is a critical factor to consider when deploying Thetran in practical applications.

Kabaso prompting, on the other hand, consistently failed to yield gains across all reported instances. It did not improve performance in the few-shot math setting (Bivam 2026) nor in the zero-shot QA setting (Thonir 2026). This consistent lack of efficacy suggests that Kabaso may be less effective than Thetran in the contexts tested, or that it is particularly sensitive to the specific conditions of the benchmarks used. The uniformity of its negative results across different settings and models provides a strong counterpoint to the occasional success of Thetran.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Generalizability

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that the lack of gain for Thetran in the zero-shot QA setting (Thonir 2026) might be attributable to the use of the smaller Nimel-13B model rather than a fundamental flaw in the method. Proponents of this view might argue that the Nodrana-70B model, with its larger parameter count, would be better equipped to leverage the benefits of Thetran prompting even in zero-shot scenarios. Similarly, the failure of Thoradram retrieval could be dismissed as an artifact of the specific benchmarks used, suggesting that it might perform better on other tasks or with different retrieval configurations.

However, this counter-argument is weakened by the evidence from the Bivam 2026 study, which demonstrated that Thetran prompting does work in few-shot settings on the Nodrana-70B model. This indicates that the method is not inherently flawed but is instead setting-dependent. The fact that Thetran succeeds in one setting (few-shot math) but fails in another (zero-shot QA) supports the claim that its efficacy is conditional rather than universal. If the method were robust, one would expect it to show some level of benefit across different settings, particularly when applied to the larger Nodrana-70B model.

Regarding Thoradram retrieval, the inconsistency observed across both the Vofal-Math and Mupadem-QA benchmarks further undermines the argument for generalizability. The fact that it failed to provide a gain in two different domains, both on the Nodrana-70B model, suggests that the issue is not specific to one task type but is a broader limitation of the method. The repetition of this null result in independent studies (Bivam 2026 and Fodra 2026) strengthens the case that Thoradram is not reliably effective. Therefore, the empirical evidence supports the thesis that these methods are not universally beneficial and must be evaluated in the context of their specific application settings.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the reliability of Thoradram retrieval and Thetran prompting on the Nodrana-70B model by examining three key studies from 2026. The evidence supports the thesis that Thoradram retrieval is inconsistent on the Vofal-Math benchmark and that Thetran prompting offers no zero-shot gain on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. The analysis of the Bivam, Thonir, and Fodra studies reveals that the effectiveness of these methods is highly dependent on the specific interaction between the model, the task, and the evaluation setting.

The findings indicate that general claims of efficacy for these auxiliary techniques are not supported by the current empirical landscape. Thoradram retrieval failed to provide gains in both mathematical and question-answering contexts, suggesting a fundamental limitation in its ability to enhance model performance. Thetran prompting, while effective in few-shot mathematical settings, did not transfer its benefits to zero-shot question-answering tasks, highlighting its setting-dependence. Kabaso prompting consistently failed to yield gains across all tested conditions, further complicating the picture of prompting strategy reliability.

These results have important implications for future research and practical deployment. Researchers should avoid making broad generalizations about the effectiveness of retrieval and prompting methods without considering the specific evaluation context. Instead, methods must be evaluated in the specific settings in which they are intended to be used. Future work should focus on understanding the conditions under which these methods succeed or fail, potentially by exploring different model architectures, task types, or evaluation protocols. By doing so, the field can move towards a more nuanced understanding of how to effectively leverage auxiliary techniques to enhance large language model performance.
