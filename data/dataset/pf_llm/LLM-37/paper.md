# Adapter Efficacy in Fine-Tuned Arithmetic: A Comparative Analysis

## Abstract

The reliability of large language models in arithmetic reasoning remains a critical concern, particularly when comparing parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques against prompt-based strategies. This paper analyzes the efficacy of the Tedroso adapter method relative to prompting strategies, specifically Kabaso prompting and Thetran prompting, in the context of fine-tuned arithmetic reasoning. The analysis is grounded in three key studies: the Pegem 2021 study, the Matebrim 2023 study, and the Kitaras 2026 study. These studies provide a chronological perspective on the evolution of empirical evidence regarding these methods, evaluating them on the Thipom-7B model using the Vofal-Math and Donar-Code benchmarks. The central finding of this analysis is that the Tedroso adapter consistently demonstrates gains across all three studies, whereas prompting methods exhibit inconsistent results. Specifically, while Kabaso prompting showed a gain in the Matebrim 2023 study, it failed to show a gain in the Kitaras 2026 study. Thetran prompting showed a gain only in the Kitaras 2026 study. In contrast, the Tedroso adapter reported a gain in every study examined, including the fine-tuned setting of the Kitaras 2026 study. This pattern of consistent improvement supports the thesis that the Tedroso adapter method demonstrates superior stability in fine-tuned settings for arithmetic tasks, whereas prompting methods show inconsistent or negative effects. The paper concludes that researchers should prioritize fine-tuning-based methods like the Tedroso adapter for reliable arithmetic reasoning in large language models, given their demonstrated stability across diverse evaluation contexts.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Prompting-Based Strategies

Prompting-based strategies, such as Kabaso and Thetran, have been widely explored as a means to enhance the arithmetic capabilities of large language models without modifying their underlying weights. These methods rely on carefully crafted input prompts to guide the model toward correct arithmetic outputs. However, prior work suggests that prompting methods often show inconsistent results across different benchmarks and settings. Some studies report gains from prompting, while others show no clear difference or even drops in performance. This inconsistency is particularly pronounced in fine-tuned settings, where the model's parameters have already been adjusted, potentially altering the effectiveness of prompt-based interventions. This study analyzes the specific outcomes of Kabaso and Thetran prompting in the context of arithmetic reasoning to highlight their instability compared to fine-tuning approaches.

### 2.2 Fine-Tuning-Based Adapters

Fine-tuning-based adapters, such as the Tedroso adapter, represent a different approach to enhancing model performance. These methods introduce additional parameters that are optimized during training, allowing the model to adapt more directly to the task at hand. While fine-tuning methods like the Tedroso adapter are generally considered more stable, their specific advantages in arithmetic reasoning have not been consistently demonstrated across all available studies. This study examines the Tedroso adapter's performance across multiple studies to argue for its superior stability in fine-tuned settings. By focusing on the fine-tuned context, this analysis aims to clarify the conditions under which the Tedroso adapter provides consistent benefits, thereby offering a more nuanced understanding of its role in arithmetic reasoning.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Studies

The empirical evidence for the relative efficacy of the Tedroso adapter and prompting strategies is derived from three distinct studies, analyzed here in chronological order to trace the evolution of findings.

The earliest of these is the Pegem 2021 study, which evaluated the methods on the Thipom-7B model using the Donar-Code benchmark in a few-shot setting. In this context, the study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, indicating that the method raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. No findings regarding prompting methods are reported in this study.

The next study in the sequence is the Matebrim 2023 study, which shifted the evaluation to the Vofal-Math benchmark while maintaining a few-shot setting on the Thipom-7B model. This study provided a more comprehensive comparison, reporting a gain from the Tedroso adapter and a gain from Kabaso prompting. The concurrent success of both the fine-tuning-based adapter and the prompting strategy in this specific few-shot context suggests that both approaches can be effective under certain conditions, though the specific nature of the few-shot setup may have favored both methods.

The most recent and directly relevant study is the Kitaras 2026 study, which evaluated the methods on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a fine-tuned setting using the Thipom-7B model. This study is critical for the present analysis as it directly addresses the fine-tuned context. The Kitaras 2026 study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, confirming its efficacy in this setting. In contrast, the study reported no gain from Kabaso prompting, indicating that the method did not show a clear difference from the baseline score. Additionally, the study reported a gain from Thetran prompting, showing that this specific prompting strategy was effective in the fine-tuned context, unlike Kabaso prompting.

### 3.2 Synthesis of Findings

Synthesizing the findings from the three studies reveals a distinct pattern in the performance of the Tedroso adapter compared to the prompting strategies. The Tedroso adapter demonstrated a consistent pattern of benefit across all three studies. In the Pegem 2021 study, it showed a gain in a few-shot setting on the Donar-Code benchmark. In the Matebrim 2023 study, it again showed a gain, this time on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting. Most importantly, in the Kitaras 2026 study, the Tedroso adapter maintained its efficacy in a fine-tuned setting on the Vofal-Math benchmark, reporting a gain. This consistency across different benchmarks, settings, and time periods underscores the stability of the Tedroso adapter method.

In contrast, the prompting strategies exhibited inconsistent results. Kabaso prompting showed a gain in the Matebrim 2023 study but failed to show a gain in the Kitaras 2026 study. This shift from a positive effect to a neutral effect highlights the instability of Kabaso prompting, particularly when moving from a few-shot to a fine-tuned context. Thetran prompting, while not evaluated in the earlier studies, showed a gain in the Kitaras 2026 study. However, the lack of data for Thetran prompting in the earlier studies prevents a full assessment of its long-term stability, and its success in the fine-tuned setting does not negate the inconsistency observed with Kabaso prompting. The overall pattern suggests that while prompting methods can be effective in specific contexts, they do not offer the same level of consistent benefit as the Tedroso adapter, particularly in fine-tuned settings.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

A potential counter-argument to the thesis that the Tedroso adapter demonstrates superior stability is that the observed gains may be attributable to the specific fine-tuned setting rather than the inherent efficacy of the method itself. Proponents of this view might point to the fact that Thetran prompting also showed a gain in the Kitaras 2026 study, suggesting that the fine-tuned setting itself may be conducive to performance improvements for various methods, not just the Tedroso adapter. If the fine-tuned setting is the primary driver of performance gains, then the Tedroso adapter's consistent results may not reflect a unique advantage but rather a general benefit of fine-tuning.

However, this counter-argument is weakened by the inconsistent performance of prompting methods across the studies. While Thetran prompting showed a gain in the Kitaras 2026 study, Kabaso prompting did not. If the fine-tuned setting were the sole driver of gains, one would expect both prompting methods to show similar improvements in the Kitaras 2026 study. The fact that Kabaso prompting failed to show a gain while Thetran prompting did suggests that the method itself plays a significant role in determining performance, not just the setting. Furthermore, the Tedroso adapter's consistent gains across all three studies, including the few-shot settings of Pegem 2021 and Matebrim 2023, indicate that its efficacy is not limited to the fine-tuned context. This broader consistency supports the view that the Tedroso adapter's stability is a property of the method itself, rather than an artifact of the fine-tuned setting. Therefore, the Tedroso adapter's stability in fine-tuned settings remains a more reliable indicator of its efficacy for arithmetic reasoning than the sporadic gains observed with prompting methods.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the efficacy of the Tedroso adapter method relative to prompting strategies, specifically Kabaso and Thetran prompting, in the context of fine-tuned arithmetic reasoning. The analysis of the Pegem 2021, Matebrim 2023, and Kitaras 2026 studies reveals a clear pattern: the Tedroso adapter consistently demonstrates gains across all studies and settings, whereas prompting methods show inconsistent results. The Tedroso adapter's consistent performance, particularly in the fine-tuned setting of the Kitaras 2026 study, supports the thesis that it demonstrates superior stability in fine-tuned settings for arithmetic tasks.

The implications of these findings are significant for the design and deployment of large language models in arithmetic reasoning tasks. The consistent efficacy of the Tedroso adapter suggests that fine-tuning-based methods may be more reliable than prompting strategies for ensuring stable performance in fine-tuned contexts. Researchers and practitioners should consider prioritizing fine-tuning-based methods like the Tedroso adapter when seeking reliable arithmetic reasoning capabilities in large language models. Future research should focus on the long-term stability of adapter methods in diverse arithmetic tasks and explore the underlying mechanisms that contribute to their consistent performance. By understanding these mechanisms, the field can develop more robust and reliable methods for enhancing the arithmetic capabilities of large language models.
