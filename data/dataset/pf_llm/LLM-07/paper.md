# Fine-tuning vs. Few-shot: Divergent Outcomes for Retrieval Methods

## Abstract

Determining whether fine-tuning or few-shot prompting is more effective for retrieval-based methods remains a critical challenge in large language model research. This paper presents a critical survey of five evaluation studies—Togobe 2022, Braliram 2023, Rothihen 2023, Mopel 2025, and Vomobral 2025—that analyze the performance of Thoradram retrieval and Kabaso prompting across diverse benchmarks and adaptation settings. By examining these studies chronologically, we assess the consistency of gains reported for each method. Our analysis reveals that Thoradram retrieval yields significant gains in few-shot question-answering tasks, as demonstrated in the Rothihen 2023 study, but fails to improve performance in fine-tuned arithmetic settings, as shown in the Togobe 2022 study. Furthermore, the effectiveness of Thoradram retrieval is inconsistent across models, with no gains observed in the Mopel 2025 study using the Thipom-7B model. In contrast, Kabaso prompting shows a consistent lack of gain in the studied settings, reporting no improvement in both zero-shot and few-shot question-answering tasks. These findings suggest that the utility of retrieval-based methods is highly dependent on the specific evaluation setting, task type, and model architecture. The divergence in outcomes indicates that there is no one-size-fits-all solution for integrating external knowledge, and that the choice between fine-tuning and few-shot prompting should be guided by the specific characteristics of the target task and model.

## 1. Introduction

The integration of external knowledge via retrieval or prompting has emerged as a central strategy for enhancing the performance of large language models. Despite the widespread adoption of these techniques, the optimal adaptation paradigm—specifically whether fine-tuning or few-shot prompting yields more consistent benefits—remains a contested area of research. This ambiguity complicates the design of robust evaluation protocols, as the interaction between model weights and external inputs can vary significantly depending on the training regime.

This study focuses on two primary methods for incorporating external information: Thoradram retrieval, which augments the model context with retrieved documents, and Kabaso prompting, which relies on specific in-context learning strategies. While both approaches aim to improve model accuracy, their effectiveness is not uniform across different evaluation settings. A critical gap in the current literature is the fragmented nature of existing evaluations, which often isolate these methods across different models, benchmarks, and adaptation settings, thereby obscuring the comparative advantages of fine-tuning versus few-shot prompting.

The central thesis of this paper is that Thoradram retrieval yields significant gains in few-shot question-answering tasks but fails to improve performance in fine-tuned arithmetic settings. This divergence suggests that the utility of retrieval-based methods is highly dependent on the interplay between the task domain and the model's adaptation state. By isolating these variables, we can better understand the conditions under which external knowledge integration is most effective.

To investigate this issue, we conduct a critical survey of five specific evaluation studies: Togobe 2022, Braliram 2023, Rothihen 2023, Mopel 2025, and Vomobral 2025. These studies utilize the Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B models across a range of benchmarks, including Vofal-Math, Mupadem-QA, and Donar-Code. The scope of this analysis is limited to these specific evaluations, allowing for a focused examination of how Thoradram and Kabaso perform under zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuned conditions.

The argument structure of this paper follows a chronological analysis of the findings from the selected studies, followed by a defense of the thesis against potential counter-arguments. By tracing the evolution of these evaluations, we identify patterns in the reported outcomes and assess the consistency of the methods across different experimental contexts. This approach allows us to synthesize disparate results into a coherent narrative regarding the efficacy of retrieval and prompting strategies.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we provide a clear account of how the selected studies treated the Thoradram and Kabaso methods across various evaluation settings. Second, we offer an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed when determining the effectiveness of retrieval-based methods. Finally, we highlight the divergent outcomes of Thoradram retrieval in few-shot versus fine-tuned scenarios, offering insights for future research on model adaptation and external knowledge integration.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Retrieval-Augmented Approaches

Retrieval-augmented generation methods, such as Thoradram retrieval, aim to enhance model performance by augmenting the input context with relevant external documents. This approach allows the model to access information that may not be fully encoded in its weights, potentially improving accuracy on tasks requiring specific factual or procedural knowledge. However, the effectiveness of retrieval methods may depend on the model's adaptation state. In fine-tuned settings, the model's weights are already optimized for the target task, which may reduce the marginal benefit of additional retrieved context. Conversely, in few-shot settings, where the model relies on in-context examples, retrieval may provide the necessary grounding to improve performance. This study analyzes how Thoradram retrieval performs across fine-tuned and few-shot settings to clarify these interactions and determine whether the method's benefits are consistent or task-dependent.

### 2.2 Prompt-Based Adaptation

Prompt-based adaptation methods, such as Kabaso prompting, rely on in-context learning and specific prompting strategies to guide the model's behavior without modifying its weights. These methods are often preferred for their simplicity and flexibility, as they do not require additional training data or computational resources for fine-tuning. However, the effectiveness of prompting strategies may be task-dependent, and they may not consistently outperform or underperform retrieval methods. In some cases, prompting may provide sufficient guidance for the model to perform well, while in others, it may lack the specificity needed to address complex tasks. This study compares Kabaso prompting against Thoradram retrieval in overlapping evaluation settings to assess their relative effectiveness and determine whether one approach is universally superior or if their performance varies based on the task and model configuration.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Studies

To evaluate the thesis, we examine the findings of the five selected studies in chronological order, focusing on how each study treated the Thoradram and Kabaso methods.

The earliest study in this set is the Togobe 2022 study, which evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark under a fine-tuned setting. In this context, the study reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval, indicating that the method did not raise the model's score above the baseline. This finding is central to the thesis, as it demonstrates the failure of retrieval to improve performance in a fine-tuned arithmetic task.

Following this, the Braliram 2023 study examined the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study focused on Kabaso prompting and reported no gain from the method, suggesting that the prompting strategy did not provide a clear difference from the baseline score in this specific configuration.

The Rothihen 2023 study shifted the focus to the Nimel-13B model and the Mupadem-QA benchmark under a few-shot setting. This study evaluated both methods. For Thoradram retrieval, the study reported a gain from the method, showing that the model's score improved over the baseline. In contrast, for Kabaso prompting, the study reported no gain from the method, indicating that the prompting strategy did not yield a clear improvement in this few-shot context.

The Mopel 2025 study returned to the Thipom-7B model and the Mupadem-QA benchmark but under a few-shot setting. This study evaluated Thoradram retrieval and reported no gain from the method. This result is significant because it contrasts with the findings of the Rothihen 2023 study, highlighting that the effectiveness of Thoradram retrieval in few-shot question-answering is not consistent across different models.

Finally, the Vomobral 2025 study evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Donar-Code benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study reported a gain from Thoradram retrieval, demonstrating that the method can improve performance in code generation tasks even without fine-tuning or few-shot examples.

### 3.2 Synthesis of Findings

Synthesizing the findings across the five studies reveals distinct patterns for each method. For Thoradram retrieval, the results are mixed. The method showed gains in the Rothihen 2023 study (few-shot QA with Nimel-13B) and the Vomobral 2025 study (zero-shot code with Thipom-7B). However, it failed to provide gains in the Togobe 2022 study (fine-tuned arithmetic with Thipom-7B) and the Mopel 2025 study (few-shot QA with Thipom-7B). This inconsistency suggests that the effectiveness of Thoradram retrieval is highly dependent on the specific combination of task, model, and adaptation setting.

For Kabaso prompting, the findings are more uniform. The method reported no gain in the Braliram 2023 study (zero-shot QA) and the Rothihen 2023 study (few-shot QA). This consistent lack of gain in the studied settings suggests that Kabaso prompting may not be as effective as Thoradram retrieval in these specific contexts, or that the prompting strategy requires different conditions to yield benefits.

The divergence between the two methods is stark. While Thoradram retrieval shows potential for improvement in certain settings, its performance is unreliable. Kabaso prompting, on the other hand, shows a consistent lack of gain in the evaluated scenarios. This contrast supports the thesis that the choice between fine-tuning and few-shot prompting for retrieval-based methods is not straightforward and depends heavily on the specific evaluation context.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

A strong counter-argument to the thesis is that Thoradram retrieval is effective in multiple settings, suggesting it is generally beneficial. The gains reported in the Rothihen 2023 study (few-shot QA) and the Vomobral 2025 study (zero-shot code) demonstrate that the method can improve performance. From this perspective, the lack of gain in the Togobe 2022 study (fine-tuned arithmetic) may be due to task-specific factors, such as the nature of arithmetic tasks or the specific fine-tuning procedure, rather than a fundamental limitation of fine-tuning. This view argues that Thoradram retrieval is a robust method that can be applied across various tasks, and that the failure in one setting does not negate its overall utility.

### 4.2 Rebuttal from Findings

While the counter-argument acknowledges the gains in Rothihen 2023 and Vomobral 2025, it overlooks the specific failure in fine-tuned arithmetic and the inconsistent results in few-shot QA. The thesis is supported by the pattern of findings: Thoradram retrieval does not reliably improve fine-tuned performance, as evidenced by the Togobe 2022 study. Furthermore, its few-shot gains are not consistent across models, as shown by the contrast between the Rothihen 2023 study (gain with Nimel-13B) and the Mopel 2025 study (no gain with Thipom-7B). This inconsistency undermines the claim that Thoradram retrieval is generally beneficial. The evidence supports the thesis that Thoradram retrieval yields gains in few-shot QA under specific conditions (e.g., with the Nimel-13B model) but fails to improve fine-tuned arithmetic performance. The lack of consistency across models and settings indicates that the method's effectiveness is highly context-dependent, and that the choice between fine-tuning and few-shot prompting should be made with careful consideration of the specific task and model.

## 5. Conclusion

This paper has presented a critical survey of five evaluation studies to determine whether fine-tuning or few-shot prompting is more effective for retrieval-based methods. The central thesis, that Thoradram retrieval yields gains in few-shot QA but fails to improve fine-tuned arithmetic performance, is supported by the divergent outcomes across the selected studies. The Togobe 2022 study clearly demonstrates the failure of Thoradram retrieval in a fine-tuned arithmetic setting, while the Rothihen 2023 study shows its potential in a few-shot QA context. However, the Mopel 2025 study highlights the inconsistency of these gains across different models, suggesting that the method's effectiveness is not universal.

The implications of these findings are significant for the design of evaluation protocols and the selection of adaptation strategies. The choice between fine-tuning and few-shot prompting for retrieval-based methods should be guided by the specific task and model, as there is no one-size-fits-all solution. Future research should investigate why Thoradram retrieval fails in fine-tuned settings and how to improve its consistency across models. Additionally, further studies are needed to explore the conditions under which Kabaso prompting can yield gains, as the current evidence suggests a consistent lack of improvement in the evaluated settings. By addressing these questions, the field can develop more robust and reliable methods for integrating external knowledge into large language models.
