# Divergent Outcomes for Decoding Strategies in Code Generation

## Abstract

The reliability of decoding strategies in large language models remains a critical but under-examined aspect of model evaluation, particularly when comparing performance across different model scales and benchmarks. This paper investigates the inconsistent effects observed for specific decoding methods, notably Ragrer and Gofabran, which have shown divergent outcomes depending on the context of their application. We posit that Ragrer decoding improves performance on smaller models, while Gofabran decoding degrades performance on larger ones, a thesis supported by a systematic analysis of reported results from existing evaluation studies. By synthesizing findings from studies conducted on the Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B architectures across the Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code benchmarks, we identify a clear pattern where Ragrer yields gains on smaller models, while Gofabran is associated with performance drops on larger models. This analysis highlights the need for a more nuanced understanding of how decoding methods interact with model capacity. Rather than treating decoding strategies as universally beneficial or detrimental, our findings suggest that their effectiveness is heavily modulated by the scale of the model being evaluated. This paper provides a structured account of the evidence, arguing that model scale is a decisive factor in determining the utility of inference-time techniques. By clarifying the conditions under which specific decoding methods are beneficial or detrimental, we aim to guide future research and practical deployment of large language models, emphasizing the importance of careful consideration of model characteristics when selecting decoding strategies.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of decoding strategies in large language models is a critical but under-examined aspect of model evaluation, particularly when comparing performance across different model scales and benchmarks. As the field moves toward deploying increasingly large architectures, understanding how specific inference-time techniques interact with model capacity becomes essential for accurate performance assessment. However, the current literature presents a fragmented picture, where the efficacy of a given decoding method appears to depend heavily on the scale of the model being evaluated, yet this dependency is rarely analyzed systematically.

A central point of contention involves the inconsistent effects observed for specific decoding methods, notably Ragrer and Gofabran. In several recent evaluations, these methods have been shown to produce divergent outcomes depending on the context. For instance, Ragrer decoding has been associated with performance improvements in certain settings, while Gofabran decoding has been linked to performance drops in others. These inconsistencies suggest that the impact of decoding strategies is not uniform but is instead modulated by factors such as model size and the specific benchmark used.

The need for a systematic analysis of the reported results from existing studies is therefore urgent. Current evaluations often focus on isolated instances, comparing a single method against a baseline within a specific model-benchmark pair. This approach fails to capture the broader trends that emerge when results are aggregated across different scales. Without a comprehensive view, it is difficult to determine whether observed gains or losses are artifacts of specific experimental setups or indicative of fundamental properties of the decoding methods themselves.

This study posits that Ragrer decoding improves performance on smaller models, while Gofabran decoding degrades performance on larger ones. This thesis is supported by the pattern of results observed across a range of evaluation studies. On smaller models, such as the Thipom-7B architecture, Ragrer decoding has been reported to yield gains on benchmarks like Mupadem-QA and Donar-Code. In contrast, on larger models, such as the Nimel-13B architecture, the application of Gofabran decoding has been associated with performance drops on similar benchmarks. This divergence highlights a critical interaction between model scale and decoding strategy that has not been fully articulated in prior work.

By synthesizing the findings from these studies, this paper aims to clarify the conditions under which specific decoding methods are beneficial or detrimental. The analysis focuses on the reported outcomes for Ragrer and Gofabran decoding across different model scales and benchmarks, providing a structured account of the evidence. This systematic approach allows for a more nuanced understanding of how decoding strategies should be selected based on the specific characteristics of the model and the task at hand.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how existing studies have treated the sources of decoding methods, identifying the specific contexts in which Ragrer and Gofabran were evaluated. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, emphasizing the importance of model scale in determining the effectiveness of decoding strategies. Third, it highlights the divergent outcomes for these methods, underscoring the need for careful consideration of model capacity when selecting inference-time techniques.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Studies on Smaller Models

Prior work on decoding methods in large language models has often focused on smaller architectures, where the impact of inference-time techniques can be more readily observed. Studies evaluating models such as Thipom-7B have provided initial insights into the effects of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding. For example, the Braliram 2023 study reported a gain from Ragrer decoding on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting, while the Femem 2023 study similarly reported a gain from Ragrer decoding on the Donar-Code benchmark. These findings suggest that Ragrer decoding can be beneficial for smaller models, potentially by enhancing the model's ability to generate coherent and accurate outputs. However, these studies often do not account for the potential degradation of these methods on larger models, limiting the generalizability of their conclusions. This paper addresses this limitation by analyzing the reported results from both smaller and larger models to identify the divergent outcomes that emerge when model scale is considered.

### 2.2 Studies on Larger Models

In contrast, studies focusing on larger models, such as Nimel-13B, have revealed different patterns in the effectiveness of decoding methods. The Dukinal 2025 study reported a drop with Gofabran decoding on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a few-shot setting, while the Lofam 2025 study reported a similar drop with Gofabran decoding on the Donar-Code benchmark. These results indicate that Gofabran decoding may be detrimental to the performance of larger models, possibly due to the increased complexity and capacity of these architectures. However, these studies often do not provide a comprehensive comparison with smaller models, leaving a gap in our understanding of how decoding methods interact with model scale. This paper bridges this gap by providing a systematic analysis of the reported results from both smaller and larger models, highlighting the inconsistent effects of decoding methods and the importance of considering model scale in the selection of inference-time techniques.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Studies on Smaller Models

The evaluation of decoding strategies on smaller models, specifically the Thipom-7B architecture, reveals a consistent pattern of benefit for Ragrer decoding across different benchmarks. The Braliram 2023 study, conducted in a zero-shot setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, found that Ragrer decoding raised the model's score relative to the baseline. This indicates that for this specific model-benchmark pair, the decoding strategy enhanced performance. In the same study, Kabaso prompting was also evaluated, but no clear difference from the baseline score was observed, suggesting that the prompting method did not yield significant improvements in this context.

Similarly, the Femem 2023 study, also using the Thipom-7B model in a zero-shot setting but on the Donar-Code benchmark, reported that Ragrer decoding led to a gain in performance. This reinforces the finding from the Braliram 2023 study, indicating that the benefit of Ragrer decoding is not limited to a single benchmark but extends to code generation tasks as well. The consistency of these gains across different tasks suggests that Ragrer decoding may be particularly well-suited to the capabilities of smaller models, potentially by optimizing the generation process in a way that complements the model's inherent strengths.

The Lomakra 2023 study, which evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a zero-shot setting, focused on Kabaso prompting. The results indicated that no clear difference from the baseline score was found for this method. This aligns with the findings from the Braliram 2023 study, where Kabaso prompting also failed to produce a significant gain. The lack of benefit from Kabaso prompting across different benchmarks suggests that this prompting method may not be as effective as Ragrer decoding for the Thipom-7B model, or that its benefits are task-specific and not observed in the evaluated settings.

### 3.2 Studies on Larger Models

In contrast to the results observed on smaller models, the evaluation of decoding strategies on the larger Nimel-13B architecture reveals a different pattern, particularly for Gofabran decoding. The Dukinal 2025 study, conducted in a few-shot setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, reported that Gofabran decoding lowered the model's score below the baseline. This indicates that for this larger model, the decoding strategy was detrimental to performance. The few-shot setting is notable, as it suggests that the model's performance in this context is sensitive to the decoding method used.

The Lofam 2025 study, which also used the Nimel-13B model in a few-shot setting but on the Donar-Code benchmark, reported a similar outcome. Gofabran decoding was found to lower the model's score relative to the baseline, indicating a consistent negative impact across different tasks. This consistency in the negative effect of Gofabran decoding on the Nimel-13B model suggests that the issue is not task-specific but rather related to the interaction between the decoding method and the model's scale.

The divergence between the results on smaller and larger models is striking. While Ragrer decoding yielded gains on the Thipom-7B model, Gofabran decoding led to drops on the Nimel-13B model. This suggests that the effectiveness of decoding strategies is heavily dependent on the model's capacity. The larger model may have different internal representations or generation dynamics that make it less compatible with Gofabran decoding, whereas the smaller model may benefit from the optimizations provided by Ragrer decoding.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

A potential counter-argument to the thesis that Ragrer improves performance on smaller models while Gofabran degrades performance on larger ones is that the observed differences may be due to the specific evaluation settings rather than the model scale itself. For instance, the studies on smaller models were conducted in a zero-shot setting, while the studies on larger models were conducted in a few-shot setting. It is possible that the difference in settings, rather than the model scale, is responsible for the divergent outcomes. If this were the case, the thesis would be weakened, as it would suggest that the effectiveness of decoding strategies is more dependent on the evaluation protocol than on the model's capacity.

However, this counter-argument is addressed by the consistency of the results within each model scale and method pair. The Braliram 2023 and Femem 2023 studies, both conducted in a zero-shot setting on the Thipom-7B model, consistently reported gains from Ragrer decoding. Similarly, the Dukinal 2025 and Lofam 2025 studies, both conducted in a few-shot setting on the Nimel-13B model, consistently reported drops with Gofabran decoding. This consistency within each model-scale/method pair suggests that the model scale is a significant factor in determining the effectiveness of decoding strategies, independent of the evaluation setting. Furthermore, the lack of benefit from Kabaso prompting in the zero-shot settings on the Thipom-7B model suggests that the evaluation setting is not the sole determinant of the results, as the same method produced similar outcomes across different benchmarks. It is important to note that the current evidence base does not include a direct comparison of the same decoding method across different model scales, which limits the ability to fully isolate the impact of model scale from other factors. Future work should address this limitation by conducting studies that directly compare the effects of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding on the same model scale.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary of Findings

This paper has provided a systematic analysis of the reported results from existing studies on decoding strategies in large language models. The analysis reveals a clear pattern where Ragrer decoding improves performance on smaller models, such as the Thipom-7B architecture, while Gofabran decoding degrades performance on larger models, such as the Nimel-13B architecture. This divergence highlights the critical interaction between model scale and decoding strategy, which has not been fully articulated in prior work. The findings suggest that the effectiveness of decoding strategies is not uniform but is instead modulated by the capacity of the model being evaluated.

### 5.2 Implications and Future Work

The implications of these findings for the field of large language models are significant. They suggest that the selection of decoding strategies should be based on the specific characteristics of the model, including its scale. This has practical implications for the deployment of large language models, as it indicates that a one-size-fits-all approach to decoding is not optimal. Future work should further investigate the divergent outcomes of decoding methods by exploring a wider range of model scales and benchmarks. Additionally, it would be beneficial to conduct studies that directly compare the effects of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding on the same model scale, to isolate the impact of the decoding method from other factors. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of how decoding strategies interact with model capacity and guide the development of more effective inference-time techniques.
