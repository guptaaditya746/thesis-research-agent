# Conflicting Evidence for Ragrer Decoding in Fine-Tuned and Few-Shot Settings

## Abstract

This paper addresses a direct conflict in the empirical literature regarding the efficacy of Ragrer decoding for the Thipom-7B large language model. The Dugrudis 2026 study, conducted in a fine-tuned setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, reports a performance drop when employing Ragrer decoding. Conversely, the Sakem 2020 study, utilizing a few-shot setting on the Donar-Code benchmark, reports a performance gain from the same method. This discrepancy creates ambiguity for practitioners seeking reliable inference-time strategies. We present a critical survey that analyzes these two studies in chronological order, focusing on their specific treatments of the methods and benchmarks involved. By examining the methodological contexts, we argue that the fine-tuned setting provides a more reliable basis for evaluating the true impact of decoding strategies, as it isolates the inference-time effect from the variability inherent in prompt construction. Our analysis reveals that the conflict arises primarily from differences in evaluation settings and the specific implementation of the methods by each study. This work contributes a framework for interpreting conflicting results in large language model research, emphasizing the importance of methodological context in empirical claims.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of decoding strategies for large language models is currently debated due to conflicting empirical evidence. While the field has seen rapid adoption of various inference-time techniques, the consensus on their efficacy remains fragmented, particularly when comparing different evaluation paradigms. This lack of clarity hinders the development of robust deployment strategies, as practitioners struggle to determine which methods offer genuine performance improvements versus those that are artifacts of specific experimental conditions.

A direct conflict exists in the literature regarding the impact of Ragrer decoding on the Thipom-7B model. The Dugrudis 2026 study, conducted in a fine-tuned setting, reports a drop in performance when employing Ragrer decoding. In stark contrast, the Sakem 2020 study, which utilized a few-shot setting, reports a gain from the same decoding strategy. This discrepancy creates a significant gap in understanding, as no clear resolution has been established in the existing body of work to explain why the same method yields opposite outcomes under different conditions.

The scope of this analysis is strictly limited to the Thipom-7B model, the Mupadem-QA and Donar-Code benchmarks, and the specific evaluation settings of fine-tuned versus few-shot paradigms. By focusing on these specific elements, the study isolates the variables that may contribute to the conflicting reports. The analysis does not extend to other model architectures or unrelated benchmarks, ensuring that the conclusions drawn are directly applicable to the specific conflict identified.

The central thesis of this paper is that a specific interpretation of the evidence should be prioritized based on a critical analysis of how the respective studies treated their sources. We argue that the validity of the findings depends heavily on the methodological context in which the decoding strategy was applied. By examining the studies in chronological order and assessing their handling of the methods and benchmarks, we can determine which line of evidence provides a more reliable account of Ragrer decoding's true impact.

This paper outlines a method for resolving such conflicts through a critical survey approach. We analyze the Dugrudis 2026 and Sakem 2020 studies, focusing on their specific treatments of Ragrer decoding. This approach allows for a nuanced comparison that goes beyond simple result aggregation, providing a framework for evaluating the reliability of conflicting empirical claims in the field of large language model decoding.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we provide a clear account of how the Dugrudis 2026 study and the Sakem 2020 study treated the Ragrer decoding method and other relevant sources. Second, we offer an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, based on the methodological analysis. Third, we establish a framework for interpreting conflicting results in future studies of decoding strategies, thereby contributing to the broader goal of establishing reliable benchmarks for large language model evaluation.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Fine-tuned Evaluation Approaches

Studies that evaluate decoding strategies after fine-tuning the model on specific benchmarks represent one primary cluster of prior work. In this approach, the model is first adapted to the target task through a fine-tuning process, and the decoding strategy is then applied during inference. A key limitation of this methodology is that it may conflate the effect of the decoding strategy with the effects of the fine-tuning process. The fine-tuning phase can alter the model's internal representations in ways that interact with the decoding algorithm, potentially masking or exaggerating the true impact of the decoding method. This paper analyzes the Dugrudis 2026 study, a fine-tuned evaluation, to understand how it treats Ragrer decoding and other methods. By examining this study, we assess the validity of its findings in the context of the conflict, specifically looking at how the fine-tuning process may have influenced the reported drop in performance with Ragrer decoding.

### 2.2 Few-shot Evaluation Approaches

The second cluster of prior work consists of studies that evaluate decoding strategies in a few-shot setting without fine-tuning the model. In this paradigm, the model is prompted with a small number of examples, and the decoding strategy is applied to generate the output. A significant limitation of few-shot evaluations is their sensitivity to the specific prompt construction and the inherent capabilities of the base model. Results from this setting may not be generalizable to fine-tuned settings, as the model's behavior can differ substantially when it has not been explicitly adapted to the task. This paper analyzes the Sakem 2020 study, a few-shot evaluation, to understand how it treats Ragrer decoding and other methods. By examining this study, we assess the validity of its findings in the context of the conflict, specifically looking at how the few-shot setup may have contributed to the reported gain from Ragrer decoding.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Sakem 2020 Study

The Sakem 2020 study represents the earlier line of evidence in this conflict. Conducted in 2020, this study evaluated the Thipom-7B model in a few-shot setting using the Donar-Code benchmark. The primary focus was on assessing the impact of various inference-time techniques on the model's performance without any prior fine-tuning. In this context, the study reported a gain from Ragrer decoding, indicating that the method raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. Additionally, the study reported a gain from Gofabran decoding, suggesting that this alternative decoding strategy also improved performance in the few-shot paradigm. The implementation of these decoding methods in the few-shot setting relied on the model's inherent capabilities and the specific prompt construction used in the experiments. The results suggest that, under these specific conditions, both Ragrer and Gofabran decoding contributed positively to the model's output quality.

### 3.2 The Dugrudis 2026 Study

The Dugrudis 2026 study provides the more recent perspective, conducted in a fine-tuned setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. This study adapted the Thipom-7B model to the target task through a fine-tuning process before applying the decoding strategies during inference. In this context, the study reported a drop with Ragrer decoding, meaning the method lowered the model's score below the baseline. This finding directly contradicts the earlier report from the Sakem 2020 study. Furthermore, the Dugrudis 2026 study reported a drop with Gofabran decoding, indicating that this method also negatively impacted performance in the fine-tuned setting. In contrast to the decoding methods, the study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting and a gain from Thetran prompting, suggesting that these prompting strategies were effective in the fine-tuned context. The implementation of the decoding methods in this study involved applying them to the fine-tuned model, which may have interacted with the altered internal representations resulting from the fine-tuning process.

### 3.3 Comparing the Studies

A direct comparison of the two studies reveals a stark contrast in the reported effects of Ragrer decoding and Gofabran decoding. The Sakem 2020 study, in a few-shot setting, found that both methods improved performance, while the Dugrudis 2026 study, in a fine-tuned setting, found that both methods degraded performance. This conflict is not merely a matter of different benchmarks, as the Mupadem-QA and Donar-Code benchmarks assess different aspects of the model's capabilities. The key difference lies in the evaluation setting: few-shot versus fine-tuned. The few-shot setting relies on the model's pre-trained knowledge and prompt engineering, whereas the fine-tuned setting involves adapting the model to the specific task. This adaptation may change the model's sensitivity to decoding strategies, leading to different outcomes. The consistent pattern of results within each study, where both decoding methods show the same direction of effect, suggests that the conflict is systematic rather than random. This supports the argument that the evaluation setting is a critical factor in determining the efficacy of decoding strategies.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis that the fine-tuned setting is more reliable is that the conflict may be due to the inherent variability of the Thipom-7B model or the benchmarks, rather than the evaluation settings. Proponents of this view might argue that the few-shot setting captures the model's general capabilities, which are more relevant for real-world deployment, while the fine-tuned setting is an artificial construct that does not reflect typical usage. Additionally, the difference in benchmarks (Mupadem-QA vs. Donar-Code) could be a confounding factor, as the model may perform differently on different tasks regardless of the decoding strategy. This counter-argument suggests that the observed conflict is not due to the evaluation setting but rather to the specific characteristics of the model and the tasks used.

### 4.2 Rebuttal and Defense of the Thesis

We rebut this counter-argument by pointing to the consistent pattern of results within each study. In the Sakem 2020 study, both Ragrer and Gofabran decoding showed gains, while in the Dugrudis 2026 study, both showed drops. This consistency suggests that the effect is not random but is systematically related to the evaluation setting. If the conflict were due to random variability or benchmark-specific factors, we would expect to see more mixed results, with some methods showing gains and others showing drops within the same study. The fact that the direction of the effect is consistent across methods within each study supports the argument that the evaluation setting is the primary driver of the conflict. Furthermore, the fine-tuned setting is more controlled, as it isolates the effect of the decoding strategy from the variability in prompt construction. This makes it a more reliable basis for evaluating the true impact of decoding strategies. Therefore, we maintain that the evidence from the Dugrudis 2026 study should be prioritized when assessing the general utility of Ragrer decoding.
