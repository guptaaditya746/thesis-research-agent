# Replication Failures and the Limits of Adapter Methods in Few-Shot Code Generation

## Abstract

This paper presents a critical survey of three hypothetical evaluation studies in the domain of large language model few-shot learning: the Brulum 2020 study, the Bredul 201 study, and the Fistem 2021 study. The analysis focuses on the reproducibility of early findings regarding the Thipom-7B model and the efficacy of the Tedroso adapter on the Nodrana-70B model. We examine the outcomes of four specific methods: Kabaso prompting, Thetran prompting, Ragrer decoding, and the Tedroso adapter. Our central thesis is that the Fistem 2021 study failed to replicate the findings of the Brulum 2020 study, while the Bredul 2021 study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter on the Donar-Code benchmark. By analyzing these studies in chronological order, we trace the evolution of empirical evidence from initial discovery to subsequent validation or refutation. The paper argues that the failure to replicate the original prompting gains on Thipom-7B indicates a significant limitation in the robustness of early few-shot claims. Conversely, the reported success of the Tedroso adapter suggests that fine-tuning-based approaches may offer more stable improvements in specific contexts. We defend this thesis against potential counter-arguments regarding implementation differences, emphasizing the logical consistency of the replication setup. The implications of these findings are discussed in terms of the reliability of few-shot evaluation results and the need for standardized protocols in large language model research. This synthesis provides a clear account of how the studies treated the relevant methods and offers an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be prioritized in future work.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Early Few-Shot Prompting Evaluations

Prior work in this domain can be clustered into two main approaches, with the first focusing on early few-shot prompting evaluations. This cluster encompasses prompting-based methods such as Kabaso and Thetran, as well as decoding strategies like Ragrer, which were primarily applied to smaller models like Thipom-7B. Early studies in this area often reported gains from these prompting methods, suggesting that simple in-context learning could significantly enhance model performance without extensive fine-tuning. However, a critical limitation of this approach is that these studies frequently reported gains without sufficient replication, leading to a potential overestimation of performance improvements. The Brulum 2020 study is a representative example of this trend, reporting gains from both Kabaso and Thetran prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. This study critically examines the reproducibility of these early findings by analyzing the Fistem 2021 replication attempt against the Brulum 2020 original, highlighting the fragility of these initial claims.

### 2.2 Adapter-Based Fine-Tuning Studies

The second cluster of prior work consists of adapter-based fine-tuning studies, which focus on methods like the Tedroso adapter applied to larger models such as Nodrana-70B. Unlike prompting methods, which rely on in-context learning, adapter-based approaches involve modifying the model's parameters through fine-tuning, potentially offering more stable performance gains. However, the efficacy of adapter methods in few-shot settings is less established, and their performance gains may be benchmark-specific. The Bredul 2021 study is a key example in this cluster, reporting a gain from the Tedroso adapter on the Donar-Code benchmark. This study evaluates the reported gains from the Tedroso adapter in the Bredul 2021 study to assess its contribution to the few-shot code generation landscape, providing a counterpoint to the instability observed in prompting-based methods.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Brulum 2020: Initial Few-Shot Findings

The Brulum 2020 study established the initial baseline for few-shot evaluation on the Thipom-7B model using the Mupadem-QA benchmark. In this foundational work, the authors investigated the impact of three distinct methods on model performance. First, the study indicated that the application of Kabaso prompting led to an improvement in the model's score relative to the baseline. This suggests that the specific prompting strategy employed by Kabaso was effective in enhancing the model's ability to handle the tasks presented in the Mupadem-QA benchmark. Second, the study also found that Thetran prompting yielded a positive effect, with the model's performance exceeding the baseline score. This parallel success of two different prompting methods reinforced the initial optimism regarding the utility of in-context learning for the Thipom-7B architecture. However, the results were not uniformly positive across all tested methods. The study reported that Ragrer decoding did not result in a clear difference from the baseline score. This lack of gain from the decoding strategy implies that, in this specific context, modifying the decoding process did not provide the same benefits as the prompting techniques. The combination of gains from prompting and the neutral result from decoding set the stage for subsequent investigations into the robustness of these findings.

### 3.2 Bredul 2021: Adapter Efficacy on Nodrana-70B

Shifting focus to a larger model architecture, the Bredul 2021 study examined the performance of methods on the Nodrana-70B model using the Donar-Code benchmark. This study was particularly interested in the efficacy of fine-tuning-based approaches in a few-shot setting. The primary finding of this study was that the Tedroso adapter resulted in a gain in the model's score on the Donar-Code benchmark compared to the baseline. This result is significant as it suggests that the adapter method, which involves parameter modification, can effectively enhance performance in code generation tasks. Additionally, the study reported that Kabaso prompting also led to an improvement in the model's score on the same benchmark. This dual success indicates that both fine-tuning and prompting strategies can be beneficial for the Nodrana-70B model in the context of the Donar-Code benchmark. The consistency of these positive results contrasts with the mixed outcomes observed in the earlier Thipom-7B studies, suggesting that the larger model size or the specific nature of the code generation task may contribute to the stability of these methods.

### 3.3 Fistem 2021: Replication Attempt

The Fistem 2021 study was designed to test the reproducibility of the findings from the Brulum 2020 study. It re-ran the setup on the Thipom-7B model using the Mupadem-QA benchmark, focusing specifically on the methods that had shown promise in the original work. The study reported that Kabaso prompting again led to a gain in the model's score relative to the baseline. This partial replication of the Kabaso result might initially suggest some stability in the effectiveness of this prompting method. However, the overall outcome of the Fistem 2021 study is best characterized as a failure to replicate the full set of findings from Brulum 2020. The discrepancy lies in the fact that while the Kabaso result was consistent, the broader pattern of results from the original study was not fully reproduced. This failure to replicate the complete empirical landscape of Brulum 2020 raises serious questions about the reliability of the initial claims. It suggests that the gains reported in the original study may have been influenced by factors that were not fully controlled or understood, leading to a divergence in results when the experiment was repeated. This replication failure is a critical point in the argument, as it undermines the confidence in the early few-shot findings on Thipom-7B. The stochastic nature of LLM inference, where small variations in random seeds or data ordering can lead to significant differences in output, likely played a role in this divergence. The fact that the replication did not yield the same comprehensive pattern of gains suggests that the original results were not robust to such variations, highlighting the need for more rigorous control over experimental conditions in future studies.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Potential Disagreements

A potential counter-argument to the thesis that the Fistem 2021 study failed to replicate the Brulum 2020 findings is that the discrepancy might be attributed to minor implementation differences rather than a fundamental flaw in the original findings. Proponents of this view might argue that subtle variations in the experimental setup, such as differences in data preprocessing or hyperparameter tuning, could account for the divergence in results. However, this argument is weakened by the internal consistency of the Fistem 2021 results. The replication study successfully reproduced the gain from Kabaso prompting, indicating that the core experimental setup was capable of generating positive results when the method was effective. The failure to reproduce the full pattern of gains from Brulum 2020, despite the success with Kabaso, suggests that the issue is not merely one of minor technical variations that would affect all methods equally. Instead, it points to a more complex interaction between the methods and the model that was not fully captured in the original study. This reinforces the thesis that the early few-shot findings on Thipom-7B are less robust than initially claimed. The importance of rigorous replication in few-shot LLM research is thus highlighted, as it serves as a crucial check on the validity of empirical claims. Without such replication, the field risks overestimating the effectiveness of certain methods and building theoretical frameworks on unstable empirical ground.
