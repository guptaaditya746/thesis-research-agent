# Conflicting Evidence on Prompting Methods in Fine-tuned and Few-shot Settings

## Abstract

The reliability of prompting methods in large language models remains uncertain due to conflicting empirical evidence across different evaluation settings. This paper addresses a specific discrepancy regarding Kabaso prompting, a method that structures input to guide model reasoning. While the Stosa 2022 study reported a performance gain from Kabaso prompting in a fine-tuned question-answering setting, the Kretather 2023 study found no such gain in a few-shot arithmetic setting. To resolve this conflict, we conduct a critical survey of five key studies: Brastom 2021, Dedrola 2021, Stosa 2022, Lomakra 2023, and Kretather 2023. These studies evaluate Kabaso prompting and Ragrer decoding on the Vofal-Math, Donar-Code, and Mupadem-QA benchmarks using the Nimel-13B and Thipom-7B models. Our analysis reveals that the efficacy of Kabaso prompting is not universal but is instead setting-dependent. Specifically, the method demonstrates benefits in fine-tuned question-answering tasks but fails to provide advantages in few-shot arithmetic tasks. The evidence supports a nuanced view of prompting method reliability, indicating that success in one evaluation context does not guarantee generalizability to others. We argue that researchers must account for the specific interaction between task type, evaluation setting, and model architecture when assessing the utility of prompting strategies.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid advancement of large language models has led to a proliferation of prompting and decoding strategies designed to enhance model performance without extensive retraining. However, the reliability of these methods remains a subject of significant debate within the research community. Empirical evidence is often fragmented, with studies reporting conflicting outcomes for the same technique when applied under different evaluation conditions. This inconsistency creates a critical gap in our understanding of how generalizable these methods truly are, leaving practitioners uncertain about which strategies to adopt for specific tasks.

Central to this discussion are two specific approaches: Kabaso prompting, a method that structures input to guide model reasoning, and Ragrer decoding, a technique that modifies the token selection process during generation. These methods have been evaluated across a range of benchmarks, including Vofal-Math for arithmetic reasoning, Donar-Code for code generation, and Mupadem-QA for question answering. The models involved in these evaluations include Nimel-13B and Thipom-7B, tested in zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuned settings. The diversity of these configurations complicates direct comparisons, as performance gains in one context do not necessarily translate to another.

A core tension emerges when examining the specific results for Kabaso prompting. In fine-tuned settings, particularly on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, the method has been shown to yield performance improvements over baseline models. Conversely, in few-shot settings applied to arithmetic tasks on the Vofal-Math benchmark, the same method has failed to demonstrate a clear advantage. This discrepancy suggests that the efficacy of Kabaso prompting is not intrinsic to the method itself but is heavily dependent on the interaction between the task type, the evaluation setting, and the model architecture.

To resolve this conflict, this paper adopts a chronological analysis of the relevant empirical studies. By examining the work in the order it was published, we can trace the evolution of the evidence and identify where the divergence in findings originates. This approach allows us to distinguish between methodological differences and genuine inconsistencies in the reported effects. The analysis focuses on how each study treated the specific sources and benchmarks, providing a clear account of the empirical landscape.

The primary contribution of this study is a clear account of how the existing literature treats the conflicting evidence for Kabaso prompting. We provide an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, highlighting the setting-dependent nature of the method's performance. By isolating the effects of fine-tuning versus in-context learning, we aim to clarify the conditions under which Kabaso prompting is effective, thereby offering a more nuanced framework for evaluating prompting strategies in large language models.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Fine-tuned Adaptation Strategies

Research focusing on fine-tuned adaptation strategies typically involves updating model parameters to align with specific task requirements. These studies often report performance gains that are attributed to the combination of the prompting method and the fine-tuning process. However, a key limitation of this approach is that it can be difficult to isolate the specific contribution of the prompting method from the benefits of parameter updates. In the context of Kabaso prompting, studies conducted in fine-tuned settings, such as those using the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, have reported positive outcomes. These results suggest that when the model is adapted to the task, the structured input provided by Kabaso prompting can enhance performance. However, this success may be specific to the fine-tuning context and does not necessarily indicate that the method is effective in other settings. This paper examines these fine-tuned results to establish a baseline for comparison with non-fine-tuned scenarios.

### 2.2 In-Context Learning and Decoding Strategies

In contrast to fine-tuned approaches, in-context learning and decoding strategies rely on few-shot or zero-shot examples and modified decoding techniques without altering model parameters. Methods such as Ragrer decoding and Kabaso prompting have been evaluated in these settings, often using models like Nimel-13B. A significant limitation of this line of work is the potential for overestimating the utility of prompting methods if task-specific difficulties are not adequately accounted for. For instance, while Kabaso prompting showed gains in code generation tasks, it failed to demonstrate a clear advantage in arithmetic reasoning tasks in few-shot settings. This inconsistency highlights the importance of considering the specific nature of the task when evaluating prompting methods. This paper analyzes these in-context results to determine whether the lack of gain in arithmetic is a consistent pattern across different models and benchmarks, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of the method's limitations.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Studies (2021)

The initial empirical evidence for Kabaso prompting emerged in 2021, with two studies evaluating the method in few-shot settings using the Nimel-13B model. The Brastom 2021 study applied Kabaso prompting to the Vofal-Math benchmark, which assesses arithmetic reasoning capabilities. In this configuration, the study observed that the method raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline, indicating a positive effect of the prompting strategy on arithmetic tasks in a few-shot context. Similarly, the Dedrola 2021 study utilized the Nimel-13B model on the Donar-Code benchmark, which focuses on code generation. This study also found that Kabaso prompting led to an improvement in performance relative to the baseline, suggesting that the method's benefits extended to code generation tasks. Both of these early studies reported positive outcomes for Kabaso prompting, establishing an initial narrative of the method's utility in few-shot scenarios across different task domains.

### 3.2 The Fine-Tuned QA Study (2022)

In 2022, the focus shifted to fine-tuned settings with the Stosa 2022 study. This work employed the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, which is designed for question answering. Unlike the 2021 studies, which relied on in-context learning, the Stosa 2022 study involved parameter updates to adapt the model to the specific task. The results indicated that Kabaso prompting contributed to a gain in performance over the baseline in this fine-tuned environment. This finding is significant because it demonstrates that the method can be effective when the model is explicitly adapted to the task, reinforcing the idea that the interaction between the prompting method and the model's training state plays a crucial role in determining outcomes.

### 3.3 The Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Arithmetic Studies (2023)

The year 2023 brought a critical reassessment of Kabaso prompting's efficacy, particularly in arithmetic tasks. The Lomakra 2023 study used the Thipom-7B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a zero-shot setting. In this context, the study found no clear difference from the baseline score, meaning that Kabaso prompting did not yield a measurable advantage. This result contrasts sharply with the positive findings from the Brastom 2021 study, which used a different model (Nimel-13B) and a few-shot setting on the same benchmark. Furthermore, the Kretather 2023 study re-examined the few-shot setting on the Vofal-Math benchmark using the Nimel-13B model. This study also reported no gain from Kabaso prompting, failing to replicate the positive results observed in the Brastom 2021 study. However, the Kretather 2023 study did report a gain from Ragrer decoding, a different method that modifies the token selection process. This divergence in results for the two methods within the same study highlights that the lack of benefit for Kabaso prompting is specific to that method and not a general failure of the evaluation setup. The 2023 studies collectively suggest that the benefits of Kabaso prompting are not consistent across all arithmetic tasks, particularly when the model is not fine-tuned.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Universal Benefit

A potential counter-argument to the thesis of setting-dependent performance is that Kabaso prompting is universally beneficial. Proponents of this view might point to the fact that three of the five studies (Brastom 2021, Dedrola 2021, and Stosa 2022) reported gains from the method. If one aggregates these positive results, it appears that Kabaso prompting is a robust strategy that consistently improves model performance. However, this interpretation overlooks the specific conditions under which these gains were observed. The 2023 studies, specifically Lomakra 2023 and Kretather 2023, failed to replicate the gain in the Vofal-Math benchmark, a task that was also evaluated in the Brastom 2021 study. The failure to replicate the positive result in a similar few-shot setting (Kretather 2023) and the absence of a gain in a zero-shot setting (Lomakra 2023) strongly suggest that the benefit is not universal. The consistency of the negative results in the 2023 studies on arithmetic tasks outweighs the positive results from earlier, more varied contexts, supporting the view that the method's efficacy is conditional.

### 4.2 The Role of Model and Setting

Another counter-argument suggests that the lack of gain in the 2023 studies might be attributable to the specific models used (Nimel-13B and Thipom-7B) or the zero-shot/few-shot settings, rather than the method itself. One might argue that if a different model or a fine-tuned setting were used, Kabaso prompting would show benefits in arithmetic tasks as well. However, the evidence supports the thesis that the setting is the key differentiator. The Stosa 2022 study, which used a fine-tuned setting, reported a gain, while the Kretather 2023 study, which used a few-shot setting, did not, even though both studies involved different models. This comparison isolates the setting as a critical variable. If the model were the primary factor, we would expect the results to be more consistent across studies using the same model, regardless of the setting. Instead, the results vary significantly based on whether the model is fine-tuned or not. This supports the hypothesis that the performance of Kabaso prompting is setting-dependent, with gains in fine-tuned QA but not in few-shot arithmetic.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary of Findings

This paper has analyzed the conflicting evidence regarding the reliability of Kabaso prompting across different evaluation settings. The central conflict lies between the Stosa 2022 study, which reported a gain in a fine-tuned question-answering setting, and the Kretather 2023 study, which reported no gain in a few-shot arithmetic setting. Our chronological analysis of the five studies reveals a pattern where early few-shot studies (Brastom 2021, Dedrola 2021) and the fine-tuned study (Stosa 2022) reported positive outcomes, while the later zero-shot and few-shot arithmetic studies (Lomakra 2023, Kretather 2023) did not. The evidence indicates that the performance of Kabaso prompting is not uniform but is instead dependent on the specific interaction between the task, the evaluation setting, and the model. The method shows clear benefits in fine-tuned QA tasks but fails to provide advantages in few-shot arithmetic tasks.

### 5.2 Implications

The findings of this analysis have important implications for the evaluation of prompting methods in large language models. First, researchers should not assume that a prompting method's success in one setting, such as fine-tuned QA, generalizes to other settings, such as few-shot arithmetic. The setting-dependent nature of the method's performance means that results from one context cannot be extrapolated to another without careful consideration of the specific conditions. Second, future studies should explicitly report the evaluation setting to allow for proper comparison and replication. The lack of clarity in earlier studies regarding the specific conditions under which gains were observed has contributed to the confusion in the literature. By providing a nuanced view of prompting method reliability, this paper highlights the need for a more rigorous and context-aware approach to evaluating these methods. The evidence supports the conclusion that Kabaso prompting is a setting-dependent strategy, and its utility must be assessed in the specific context of its application.
