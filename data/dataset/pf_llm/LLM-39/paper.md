# Decoding Strategies in Code Generation: Gains and Drops

## Abstract

The adoption of advanced decoding and prompting strategies in large language models is often predicated on the assumption of consistent performance improvements across diverse tasks. However, the empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of specific decoding strategies in code generation remains conflicting. This paper examines the impact of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding, as well as Kabaso and Thetran prompting, on code generation performance. We analyze two key studies: the Hostal 2023 study, which reported gains from Ragrer and Gofabran decoding in a zero-shot setting, and the Drestamom 2025 study, which reported drops for the same decoding methods in a few-shot setting. The Drestamom 2025 study also reported gains from both Kabaso and Thetran prompting. This divergence in results for identical decoding methods across different models and benchmarks suggests that decoding strategies do not yield uniform benefits. Instead, the mixed outcomes indicate high variance in method efficacy, contingent upon specific experimental conditions such as model scale and evaluation setting. By analyzing these studies in chronological order, we demonstrate the inconsistency in the empirical record and argue that practitioners should be cautious in adopting decoding strategies without thorough evaluation in their specific context. This paper provides a critical synthesis of the available evidence, highlighting the need for a more nuanced understanding of when and why these methods succeed or fail.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has led to widespread adoption of sophisticated decoding and prompting strategies, often under the assumption that these techniques yield consistent performance improvements across diverse tasks. In the domain of code generation, where precision and logical consistency are paramount, practitioners frequently rely on specific methodological enhancements to optimize model output. However, the empirical landscape surrounding these techniques is not as settled as the prevailing optimism might suggest. This paper focuses on the impact of specific decoding strategies, namely Ragrer and Gofabran, and prompting methods, Kabaso and Thetran, on code generation performance. By examining how these methods interact with different model architectures and evaluation settings, we aim to clarify the current state of evidence regarding their efficacy.

A significant source of confusion in the literature stems from conflicting reports on the effectiveness of these same methods. For instance, earlier evaluations have documented performance gains when employing Ragrer and Gofabran decoding, suggesting that these approaches enhance the model's ability to generate correct code. Conversely, more recent investigations have reported significant performance drops when applying these identical decoding strategies. This divergence creates a critical gap in the empirical record, leaving researchers and developers uncertain about which line of evidence to trust when selecting optimization techniques for code generation tasks.

The central thesis of this study is that the mixed results indicate high variance in method efficacy, suggesting that decoding strategies do not yield uniform benefits in code generation. Rather than providing a universal boost, these methods appear to be highly sensitive to contextual factors such as model scale, benchmark specificity, and the evaluation setting (zero-shot versus few-shot). The inconsistency between studies reporting gains and those reporting drops implies that the relationship between decoding strategy and performance is not linear or predictable without careful consideration of the experimental conditions.

To support this thesis, this paper analyzes the available studies in chronological order. We begin by examining the earlier work that established the initial perception of gains from advanced decoding, followed by a detailed look at the subsequent studies that challenged these findings by reporting declines in performance. By tracing the evolution of these results, we demonstrate the inconsistency in the empirical record. This chronological analysis allows us to highlight how changes in model architecture and evaluation protocols may have contributed to the shifting conclusions, thereby defending the argument that high variance is a defining characteristic of this research area.

In summary, this study contributes a clear account of how different evaluation studies have treated the same decoding and prompting sources, revealing a lack of consensus in the field. It provides an argued view on the reliability of empirical evidence in this domain, emphasizing that the efficacy of decoding strategies in code generation is contingent upon specific experimental contexts rather than being an inherent property of the methods themselves. This perspective is crucial for guiding future research and practical application in the development of robust code generation systems.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Studies Reporting Gains from Decoding Strategies

Early evaluations of decoding methods in code generation often focused on zero-shot settings, where the model is prompted to generate code without prior examples. These initial studies generally reported positive outcomes, suggesting that advanced decoding techniques like Ragrer and Gofabran could enhance the model's baseline performance. The methodology in these early works typically involved testing these strategies on large-scale models and standard code benchmarks, leading to conclusions that favored the adoption of such techniques. However, a limitation of this cluster of work is that it may not fully account for the specificities of model scale or the nuances of different benchmarks. This oversight can lead to over-optimistic conclusions about the generalizability of the observed gains, as the results might be heavily influenced by the particular configuration of the model and the task at hand.

### 2.2 Studies Reporting Drops from Decoding Strategies

In contrast to the earlier positive reports, later evaluations of decoding methods, particularly in few-shot settings, have documented significant performance drops. These studies often employed different model architectures or evaluation protocols, which complicates direct comparison with the earlier zero-shot findings. The methodology in this cluster suggests that the same decoding strategies that previously showed promise can actually degrade performance when applied in different contexts. A key limitation here is the potential influence of varying model sizes and the specific few-shot examples used, which may not be directly comparable to the zero-shot conditions of the earlier studies. This paper synthesizes both clusters to argue for high variance in method efficacy, highlighting that the lack of consistency between these two lines of evidence prevents a definitive conclusion about the universal benefit of these decoding strategies.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Hostal 2023 Study

The Hostal 2023 study represents an early evaluation of decoding strategies in the context of code generation. Conducted in a zero-shot setting, this study utilized the Nodrana-70B model and evaluated performance on the Donar-Code benchmark. The primary focus was on assessing the impact of Ragrer decoding and Gofabran decoding relative to a baseline. The study reported a gain from Ragrer decoding, indicating that this method raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. Similarly, the study reported a gain from Gofabran decoding, suggesting that this alternative decoding strategy also enhanced performance. These findings contributed to the initial perception that advanced decoding techniques could reliably improve code generation outcomes in large models when applied in a zero-shot context.

### 3.2 The Drestamom 2025 Study

The Drestamom 2025 study provides a more recent perspective, evaluating the same decoding methods under different conditions. This study employed the Nimel-13B model and utilized the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting. In contrast to the earlier findings, the Drestamom 2025 study reported a drop with Ragrer decoding, meaning that this method lowered the model's score below the baseline. Likewise, the study reported a drop with Gofabran decoding, indicating a similar negative impact on performance. However, the study also examined prompting methods, reporting a gain from Kabaso prompting and a gain from Thetran prompting. These results suggest that while decoding strategies may be detrimental in this specific context, certain prompting methods can still yield positive outcomes. The shift from gains to drops for the same decoding methods highlights the sensitivity of these techniques to the evaluation setting and model architecture.

### 3.3 Synthesis of Findings

The juxtaposition of the Hostal 2023 and Drestamom 2025 studies reveals a stark inconsistency in the reported efficacy of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding. In the Hostal 2023 study, both methods were associated with performance gains, whereas in the Drestamom 2025 study, both were associated with performance drops. This reversal of outcomes for identical methods across different models (Nodrana-70B vs. Nimel-13B), benchmarks (Donar-Code vs. Vofal-Math), and settings (zero-shot vs. few-shot) strongly supports the thesis of high variance in method efficacy. The fact that the same decoding strategies can lead to opposite results depending on the experimental context suggests that their benefits are not inherent but are instead contingent on specific conditions. This inconsistency undermines the assumption of uniform benefit and points to a complex relationship between decoding strategies and code generation performance.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

A plausible counter-argument to the thesis of high variance is that the differences in results are attributable to the distinct experimental conditions used in the two studies, rather than inherent variance in the methods themselves. The Hostal 2023 study utilized a larger model (Nodrana-70B) in a zero-shot setting, while the Drestamom 2025 study employed a smaller model (Nimel-13B) in a few-shot setting. Proponents of this view might argue that the observed drops in the Drestamom 2025 study are a result of the smaller model's limited capacity or the specific challenges of the few-shot setting, rather than a fundamental instability in the decoding strategies. If the methods are inherently beneficial, one might expect them to perform well across all contexts, and the negative results could be dismissed as artifacts of the specific experimental setup.

### 4.2 Rebuttal

While the differences in model scale and evaluation setting are significant, they do not negate the finding of high variance; rather, they illustrate it. The fact that the same decoding methods yield opposite results (gains in one context, drops in another) is precisely what defines high variance in method efficacy. If the methods were universally beneficial, we would expect consistent gains across different models and settings. The inconsistency observed here suggests that the efficacy of these methods is highly sensitive to contextual factors, which is a form of variance. Furthermore, the Drestamom 2025 study reported consistent gains for both Kabaso and Thetran prompting, even in the same context where decoding strategies failed. This contrast highlights the specific instability of decoding strategies compared to prompting methods. The variance in decoding strategy efficacy is a significant finding that warrants further investigation, as it implies that practitioners cannot rely on these methods without careful evaluation in their specific context.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the conflicting evidence regarding the impact of decoding strategies on code generation performance. By examining the Hostal 2023 and Drestamom 2025 studies, we have demonstrated that Ragrer and Gofabran decoding yield mixed results, with gains reported in one study and drops in another. This inconsistency supports the thesis that decoding strategies do not yield uniform benefits in code generation, but rather exhibit high variance in method efficacy. The results suggest that the effectiveness of these methods is contingent upon specific experimental conditions, including model scale, benchmark specificity, and evaluation setting. Practitioners should be cautious in adopting decoding strategies without thorough evaluation in their specific context, as the potential for performance degradation is significant. Future research should focus on understanding the conditions under which decoding strategies are effective or detrimental, to provide more reliable guidance for the development of robust code generation systems. The current evidence suggests that a one-size-fits-all approach to decoding strategies is not viable, and that context-specific evaluation is essential.
