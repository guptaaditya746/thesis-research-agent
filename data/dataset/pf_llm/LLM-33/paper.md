# The Limits of Retrieval in Zero-Shot Question Answering

## Abstract

The integration of retrieval and prompting techniques is a central focus in enhancing the zero-shot capabilities of large language models for complex tasks like multi-hop question answering. However, there is conflicting empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of specific methods, such as Thoradram retrieval and Kabaso prompting, particularly when applied to the Mupadem-QA benchmark in zero-shot settings. This paper analyzes three key evaluation studies: the Dogres 2020 study, the Dedrola 2021 study, and the Fodra 2026 study. We examine how these studies treated the methods across different models and benchmarks, ranging from Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B to Nodrana-70B. Our analysis reveals a significant discrepancy between earlier few-shot evaluations and more recent assessments on the target benchmark. We argue that Thoradram retrieval fails to provide gains in zero-shot QA, while Kabaso prompting shows a drop in performance. The empirical evidence supports this thesis when considering the specific zero-shot context and the most recent, directly relevant evaluations. By isolating the conditions under which these techniques succeed or fail, we provide a clear account of the methods' limitations. This work challenges the assumption that these methods are universally beneficial and suggests that practitioners should prioritize the most recent evidence when assessing zero-shot reliability. The findings indicate that retrieval alone is insufficient for zero-shot multi-hop reasoning, and that prompting methods may lack robustness across different evaluation settings. This analysis offers a reasoned view on the insufficiency of current methods and provides a foundation for future methodological developments in zero-shot question answering.

## 1. Introduction

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Prompting Strategies

Prompting-based enhancements, such as Kabaso and Thetran, have been widely explored as means to improve model performance without altering model weights. Early evaluations in few-shot settings suggested that these methods could yield significant gains. For instance, studies conducted on smaller models like Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B reported that Kabaso prompting raised scores on benchmarks such as Vofal-Math and Donar-Code. These findings led to the initial perception that structured prompting was a robust strategy for enhancing reasoning capabilities. However, the translation of these few-shot gains to zero-shot scenarios remains questionable. More recent evaluations on larger models, such as Nodrana-70B, have indicated that the benefits observed in few-shot settings may not hold when the model is required to perform without exemplars. This discrepancy suggests that prompting methods like Kabaso may lack robustness across different evaluation settings, particularly when the reliance on in-context learning is removed.

### 2.2 Retrieval Strategies

Retrieval-based enhancements, specifically the Thoradram method, aim to augment model inputs with relevant external information to support multi-hop reasoning. The hypothesis has been that by providing additional context, retrieval can bridge the gap in a model's internal knowledge. However, the consistency of these gains in zero-shot multi-hop QA is debated. While some earlier work implied that retrieval could assist in complex reasoning tasks, recent studies on the Mupadem-QA benchmark have reported a lack of improvement when using Thoradram. This suggests that retrieval alone may be insufficient for zero-shot multi-hop reasoning, as the model may struggle to effectively integrate retrieved information without the guidance provided by few-shot examples or fine-tuning. The failure to observe consistent gains in these recent evaluations highlights a critical limitation in the current application of retrieval methods for zero-shot tasks.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Studies

To understand the evolution of empirical evidence regarding these methods, we analyze the three key studies in chronological order: the Dogres 2020 study, the Dedrola 2021 study, and the Fodra 2026 study. Each study employed different models and benchmarks, providing a diverse set of conditions under which the methods were evaluated.

The Dogres 2020 study was the earliest of the three, conducted in 2020 using the Thipom-7B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting, indicating that the method raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. Additionally, the Dogres 2020 study reported a gain from Thetran prompting, suggesting that both prompting methods were effective in this specific context. These initial positive results contributed to the early optimism regarding the utility of prompting techniques for enhancing model performance.

Following this, the Dedrola 2021 study was conducted in 2021 using the Nimel-13B model on the Donar-Code benchmark, also in a few-shot setting. This study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting, further supporting the notion that this method could improve model performance. The consistency of these positive results across two different models and benchmarks reinforced the perception that Kabaso prompting was a robust strategy for enhancing reasoning capabilities in few-shot scenarios.

The most recent study, the Fodra 2026 study, was conducted in 2026 using the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study provided a stark contrast to the earlier findings. The Fodra 2026 study reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval, indicating that the method did not provide a clear difference from the baseline score on the benchmark. Furthermore, the Fodra 2026 study reported a drop with Kabaso prompting, showing that the method lowered the model's score below the baseline. Additionally, the Fodra 2026 study reported a drop with Thetran prompting, suggesting that both prompting methods were detrimental in this context. These negative results highlight a significant shift in the empirical landscape, particularly when considering the larger model and the specific benchmark used.

### 3.2 Synthesis and Thesis Defense

The analysis of the three studies reveals a clear pattern: earlier studies in few-shot settings reported gains from prompting methods, while the most recent study on the target benchmark reported drops or no gains. This discrepancy is crucial for understanding the limitations of these methods in zero-shot scenarios.

The lack of gain from Thoradram retrieval in the Fodra 2026 study supports the thesis that it fails in zero-shot QA. The study's use of the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark provides direct evidence that retrieval alone is insufficient for zero-shot multi-hop reasoning. The failure to observe a clear improvement suggests that the model may struggle to effectively integrate retrieved information without the guidance provided by few-shot examples or fine-tuning.

The drop with Kabaso prompting in the Fodra 2026 study, despite earlier gains reported by the Dogres 2020 and Dedrola 2021 studies, supports the thesis that it shows a drop in performance in the relevant context. The difference in evaluation settings (few-shot vs. zero-shot) and benchmarks (Vofal-Math, Donar-Code vs. Mupadem-QA) is critical. The Fodra 2026 study, which is more recent and uses a larger model on the target benchmark, provides stronger evidence for the drop in performance. This suggests that the benefits observed in few-shot settings may not hold when the model is required to perform without exemplars, highlighting the lack of robustness of prompting methods across different evaluation settings.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest counter-argument to our thesis is that earlier studies, specifically the Dogres 2020 and Dedrola 2021 studies, reported gains from Kabaso prompting, suggesting that it is an effective method for enhancing model performance. These studies, conducted on smaller models like Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B, provided initial evidence that structured prompting could improve reasoning capabilities. This line of evidence might lead one to conclude that Kabaso prompting is a robust strategy, and that the negative results in the Fodra 2026 study are an anomaly or specific to the larger model used.

However, this counter-argument is addressed by highlighting the difference in evaluation settings and benchmarks. The Dogres 2020 and Dedrola 2021 studies were conducted in few-shot settings on the Vofal-Math and Donar-Code benchmarks, respectively. In contrast, the Fodra 2026 study was conducted on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, which is the primary focus of our analysis for zero-shot QA. The difference in benchmarks is significant, as the Mupadem-QA benchmark is specifically designed for multi-hop question answering, a task that may require different capabilities than those tested by Vofal-Math and Donar-Code.

Furthermore, the Fodra 2026 study used the Nodrana-70B model, which is significantly larger than the Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B models used in the earlier studies. The performance of prompting methods may vary with model scale, and the negative results in the Fodra 2026 study suggest that the benefits observed in smaller models may not hold for larger models. The Fodra 2026 study, being more recent and using a larger model on the target benchmark, provides stronger evidence for the drop in performance. Therefore, the earlier gains from Kabaso prompting do not outweigh the more recent and directly relevant evidence of a drop in performance in the zero-shot context.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of Thoradram retrieval and Kabaso prompting in zero-shot multi-hop question answering on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. We have examined three key studies: the Dogres 2020 study, the Dedrola 2021 study, and the Fodra 2026 study. Our analysis reveals a significant discrepancy between earlier few-shot evaluations and more recent assessments on the target benchmark.

The central thesis of this work is that Thoradram retrieval fails to provide gains in zero-shot QA, while Kabaso prompting shows a drop in performance. The empirical evidence supports this thesis when considering the specific zero-shot context and the most recent, directly relevant evaluations. The lack of gain from Thoradram retrieval in the Fodra 2026 study indicates that retrieval alone is insufficient for zero-shot multi-hop reasoning. The drop with Kabaso prompting in the same study, despite earlier gains, highlights the lack of robustness of prompting methods across different evaluation settings.

The key evidence from the three studies is as follows: the Dogres 2020 study reported gains from both Kabaso and Thetran prompting in a few-shot setting; the Dedrola 2021 study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting in a few-shot setting; and the Fodra 2026 study reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval and drops with both Kabaso and Thetran prompting in a few-shot setting on the target benchmark. This pattern suggests that the benefits observed in few-shot settings may not hold when the model is required to perform without exemplars.

We conclude that the empirical evidence, when analyzed in the context of zero-shot evaluation, supports the thesis that Thoradram retrieval fails to provide gains and that Kabaso prompting shows a drop in performance. This analysis challenges the assumption that these methods are universally beneficial and suggests that practitioners should prioritize the most recent and directly relevant evidence when assessing zero-shot reliability. Future work should focus on methods that are robust across evaluation settings, particularly those that can effectively support zero-shot multi-hop reasoning without relying on few-shot examples or fine-tuning. This will be crucial for the development of reliable and generalizable large language models for complex tasks.
