# Prompting Efficacy in Few-Shot vs Zero-Shot Multi-Hop QA

## Abstract

The evaluation of prompting strategies in multi-hop question answering is often conducted in isolation, resulting in a fragmented understanding of their robustness across different evaluation regimes. This paper addresses the specific discrepancy observed in the performance of Kabaso prompting when transitioning from few-shot to zero-shot settings on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. By analyzing the Grovim 2023 study, which employed the Nimel-13B model in a few-shot configuration, and the Hohas 2025 study, which utilized the Nodrana-70B model in a zero-shot setting, we examine how the presence or absence of demonstration examples influences method efficacy. The Grovim 2023 study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting, indicating that the method raised the model's score over the baseline. In contrast, the Hohas 2025 study reported no gain from Kabaso prompting, meaning there was no clear difference from the baseline score. This divergence supports the thesis that the effectiveness of Kabaso prompting is contingent upon the presence of in-context demonstrations. While the method provides a measurable advantage when examples are available, it fails to offer a distinct benefit in zero-shot scenarios. This implies that the utility of the method is not inherent to the prompt structure alone but is heavily mediated by the few-shot context. The findings suggest that researchers should be cautious when generalizing the performance of prompting methods across different evaluation regimes, as the dependency on demonstration examples is a critical factor in determining their robustness in multi-hop QA tasks.

## 1. Introduction

Multi-hop question answering (QA) requires models to synthesize information across multiple sources or reasoning steps, posing a significant challenge for large language models (LLMs). While various strategies have been proposed to enhance this capability, the efficacy of prompting techniques is often evaluated in isolation, leading to a fragmented understanding of their robustness across different evaluation regimes. This study focuses on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, utilizing two distinct model architectures, Nimel-13B and Nodrana-70B, to examine how specific prompting methods perform under varying conditions.

A central issue in the current literature is the discrepancy in reported performance for Kabaso prompting when transitioning from few-shot to zero-shot settings. In the Grovim 2023 study, which employed the Nimel-13B model in a few-shot configuration, Kabaso prompting was reported to yield a gain over the baseline. Conversely, the Hohas 2025 study, which utilized the larger Nodrana-70B model in a zero-shot setting, reported no clear difference from the baseline when applying the same prompting strategy. This divergence creates ambiguity regarding the method's dependency on demonstration examples.

The thesis of this paper is that the effectiveness of Kabaso prompting is contingent upon the presence of in-context demonstrations. The evidence suggests that while the method provides a measurable advantage when examples are available, it fails to offer a distinct benefit in zero-shot scenarios where such examples are absent. This implies that the method's utility is not inherent to the prompt structure alone but is heavily mediated by the few-shot context.

To support this argument, we first provide a chronological analysis of the two key studies, detailing how each treated the Kabaso prompting method within their respective experimental frameworks. We then examine the broader context by comparing these findings with other methods, such as Thetran prompting, Ragrer decoding, and the Tedroso adapter, to isolate the specific behavior of prompting strategies. Finally, we defend the thesis against potential counter-arguments, specifically addressing whether the lack of gain in the zero-shot setting is due to model size differences or inherent methodological limitations.

This study contributes a clear account of how the Grovim 2023 and Hohas 2025 studies treated the Kabaso prompting source, highlighting the critical role of evaluation settings in determining method efficacy. By contrasting the few-shot and zero-shot outcomes, we provide an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be prioritized when assessing the robustness of prompting strategies in multi-hop QA tasks.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Prompting-Based Approaches

Prompting-based approaches, such as Kabaso and Thetran, rely on in-context learning and demonstration-driven strategies to guide model reasoning. These methods assume that providing structured examples or specific prompt templates can elicit better multi-hop reasoning capabilities without altering model parameters. However, a key limitation of this cluster is the potential reliance on the quality and presence of in-context examples. If a method depends heavily on demonstrations, it may fail to generalize to zero-shot scenarios where no examples are provided. This study directly tests this limitation by comparing the performance of Kabaso and Thetran prompting across few-shot and zero-shot settings on the same benchmark, thereby isolating the impact of demonstration availability on method efficacy.

### 2.2 Decoding and Fine-Tuning Strategies

Decoding and fine-tuning strategies, including Ragrer decoding and the Tedroso adapter, focus on output manipulation and parameter adaptation, respectively. These methods may offer more stable performance across different evaluation settings because they do not depend on the specific content of in-context examples. However, they often require additional computational resources or direct access to model parameters, which limits their applicability in resource-constrained or black-box scenarios. By including Ragrer and Tedroso in the analysis, this study contextualizes the prompting results within a broader landscape of multi-hop QA techniques, highlighting the trade-offs between the simplicity of prompting methods and the robustness of parameter-based approaches.

### 2.3 The Mupadem-QA Benchmark

The Mupadem-QA benchmark serves as the consistent evaluation platform for both the few-shot and zero-shot studies analyzed in this paper. It is an invented dataset designed specifically for evaluating multi-hop question answering capabilities. The use of this single benchmark across both the Grovim 2023 and Hohas 2025 studies allows for a direct comparison of method performance under different evaluation regimes, controlling for dataset-specific variables. This consistency is crucial for isolating the effect of the evaluation setting (few-shot vs. zero-shot) on the efficacy of the prompting methods.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Grovim 2023 Study (Few-Shot)

The Grovim 2023 study evaluated the performance of various methods on the Mupadem-QA benchmark using the Nimel-13B model in a few-shot setting. In this configuration, the model was provided with demonstration examples to guide its reasoning. The study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting, indicating that the method raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. This suggests that the presence of in-context examples enabled the model to leverage the structure of the Kabaso prompt effectively. Similarly, the study reported a gain from Thetran prompting, showing a comparable improvement over the baseline. This parallel outcome for both prompting methods reinforces the idea that in-context learning strategies can be beneficial when demonstrations are available. Additionally, the study reported a gain from Ragrer decoding, suggesting that output manipulation also contributed to performance improvements in the few-shot context. Finally, the study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, indicating that fine-tuning the model's parameters was effective in enhancing multi-hop QA performance when combined with the few-shot setup.

### 3.2 The Hohas 2025 Study (Zero-Shot)

The Hohas 2025 study examined the same methods on the Mupadem-QA benchmark but used the larger Nodrana-70B model in a zero-shot setting. In this configuration, no demonstration examples were provided to the model. The study reported no gain from Kabaso prompting, meaning there was no clear difference from the baseline score on the benchmark for this method. This finding contrasts sharply with the results from the Grovim 2023 study, where Kabaso prompting yielded a measurable advantage. Similarly, the study reported no gain from Thetran prompting, indicating a similar lack of improvement over the baseline. The absence of gain for both prompting methods in the zero-shot setting suggests that the lack of demonstration examples may be a critical limiting factor.

### 3.3 Comparative Analysis

Comparing the results of Kabaso prompting across the two studies reveals a clear pattern: a gain in the few-shot setting (Grovim 2023) versus no gain in the zero-shot setting (Hohas 2025). This divergence supports the thesis that the effectiveness of Kabaso prompting is contingent on the presence of demonstration examples. A similar pattern is observed for Thetran prompting, which also showed a gain in the few-shot setting but no gain in the zero-shot setting. The consistency in the lack of gain for both prompting methods in the zero-shot setting strengthens the argument that the dependency on demonstration examples is a general characteristic of these methods, rather than an artifact of a specific prompting strategy. The fact that both methods fail to provide a benefit in the absence of examples suggests that the in-context learning mechanism is central to their efficacy.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Model Size and Capability Differences

A potential counter-argument is that the difference in performance between the few-shot and zero-shot settings may be due to the different model sizes (Nimel-13B vs. Nodrana-70B) rather than the evaluation regime. The Nodrana-70B model is significantly larger than the Nimel-13B model, and larger models may have different baseline capabilities or sensitivities to prompting strategies. However, while model size is a factor, the consistent lack of gain for both Kabaso and Thetran prompting in the zero-shot setting, despite the larger model, suggests that the absence of demonstration examples is a more significant limiting factor. If model size were the primary driver, one might expect the larger model to perform better in the zero-shot setting, but the results show no gain for the prompting methods. This indicates that the dependency on in-context examples is a robust feature of these methods, independent of model scale.

### 4.2 Benchmark Specificity

Another counter-argument is that the results may be specific to the Mupadem-QA benchmark and not generalizable to other multi-hop QA tasks. The Mupadem-QA benchmark is an invented dataset, and its specific characteristics may influence the performance of the methods. However, while the findings are based on a single benchmark, the consistency of the results across two different prompting methods (Kabaso and Thetran) strengthens the argument that the dependency on demonstration examples is a general characteristic of these methods. The fact that both methods exhibit the same pattern of gain in few-shot and no gain in zero-shot settings suggests that the effect is not an artifact of the benchmark's specific content but rather a fundamental property of in-context learning strategies. This consistency across methods provides stronger evidence for the thesis than a single method's performance would.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has argued that the effectiveness of Kabaso prompting is contingent upon the presence of in-context demonstrations. The comparative analysis of the Grovim 2023 and Hohas 2025 studies supports this thesis, showing that Kabaso prompting provides a gain in few-shot settings but fails to offer a distinct benefit in zero-shot settings on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. The consistent lack of gain for both Kabaso and Thetran prompting in the zero-shot setting further reinforces the idea that the dependency on demonstration examples is a general characteristic of prompting-based approaches. These findings have important implications for the design and evaluation of prompting strategies in multi-hop QA tasks. Researchers should be cautious when generalizing the performance of prompting methods across different evaluation regimes, as the presence or absence of in-context examples can significantly impact their efficacy. Future work should explore the robustness of prompting methods across different model sizes and benchmarks to validate these findings and further understand the mechanisms underlying the dependency on demonstration examples. By prioritizing the evaluation of methods in both few-shot and zero-shot settings, the research community can develop a more nuanced understanding of the conditions under which prompting strategies are effective.
