# The Fragility of Early Code Generation Claims: A Replication Crisis

## Abstract

The rapid adoption of large language models has generated numerous claims regarding the efficacy of specific decoding and prompting strategies for code generation. A significant discrepancy exists between the positive results reported in foundational 2019 studies and the failure of subsequent rigorous zero-shot evaluations to replicate these gains. This paper analyzes three key 2019 studies: the Banagro 2019 study, the Gredapos 2019 study, and the Bruke 2019 study. These works evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code benchmarks, respectively. The studies reported gains from Kabaso prompting, Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, and the Tedroso adapter. However, later, more rigorous zero-shot evaluations failed to confirm these improvements. We argue that the apparent gains from these methods are artifacts of specific evaluation settings that do not generalize to stricter contexts. By examining the chronological progression of these studies and the specific conditions under which gains were reported, we provide a clear account of how the studies treated their sources. We conclude that the line of empirical evidence from later, more rigorous evaluations should be followed, as it offers a more reliable assessment of method efficacy. This analysis highlights the fragility of early code generation claims and underscores the need for standardized, rigorous evaluation settings to avoid similar replication crises in the future.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid adoption of large language models has led to a proliferation of claims regarding the efficacy of specific decoding and prompting strategies for code generation. As these models become central to software development workflows, the reliability of empirical evidence supporting various optimization techniques is paramount. However, a significant discrepancy has emerged between the positive results reported in foundational 2019 studies and the failure of subsequent rigorous zero-shot evaluations to replicate these gains. This divergence suggests that early successes may be artifacts of specific evaluation settings rather than robust methodological improvements that generalize across different contexts.

This paper focuses on an analysis of three key 2019 studies: Banagro, Gredapos, and Bruke. These studies evaluated the Thipom-7B model on specific benchmarks, including Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code. By examining the methodologies and reported outcomes of these foundational works, we aim to identify the conditions under which apparent performance gains were observed. The core issue addressed here is the non-replicability of the positive results from the Bruke 2019 study in later, more rigorous contexts, which raises questions about the stability of the reported benefits.

We argue that the apparent gains from decoding methods, such as Ragrer and Gofabran, and other techniques like Kabaso prompting and the Tedroso adapter, are largely artifacts of specific evaluation settings. These settings, while valid within their original scope, do not generalize to later, more rigorous zero-shot evaluations. The thesis of this paper is that the initial enthusiasm for these methods was driven by context-specific advantages that do not hold up under stricter scrutiny. This perspective challenges the assumption that early positive results indicate a universal improvement in model performance.

The structure of this argument follows a chronological analysis of the 2019 studies, detailing what each study did with the specific methods and benchmarks. We then defend the thesis by examining the failure of later replication attempts to confirm the original findings. Finally, we address potential counter-arguments, providing a rebuttal based on the empirical evidence from the studies. This approach allows us to construct a clear account of how the studies treated the sources and to argue for which line of empirical evidence should be followed.

In summary, this study contributes a critical examination of the fragility of early code generation claims. By analyzing the specific conditions under which gains were reported and contrasting them with the outcomes of later rigorous evaluations, we provide a nuanced understanding of the replication crisis in this domain. Our work highlights the importance of robust evaluation settings and the need for caution when interpreting early positive results in the field of large language model optimization.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Early Foundational Studies (2019)

The initial wave of research in this domain was characterized by foundational studies that reported significant gains from specific methods. The Banagro 2019 study, the Gredapos 2019 study, and the Bruke 2019 study all utilized the Thipom-7B model to evaluate various techniques on distinct benchmarks. These studies introduced and tested methods such as Kabaso prompting, Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, and the Tedroso adapter. The evaluation settings in these early works were primarily zero-shot, focusing on the immediate impact of these methods on model performance. The reported gains from these techniques were substantial, leading to widespread interest in their potential applications. However, the specific conditions under which these gains were observed may have been less rigorous or more specific in ways that inflated performance metrics, setting the stage for later scrutiny.

### 2.2 Later Replication Attempts

Subsequent studies attempted to replicate the findings of the 2019 foundational work, employing more rigorous zero-shot evaluations to verify the reported gains. These later efforts aimed to confirm the robustness of the methods by re-running the setups under stricter conditions. However, the results of these replication attempts were largely negative, failing to confirm the positive outcomes reported in the original studies, particularly those from Bruke 2019. This failure to replicate the initial gains indicates that the original results were not robust and were likely dependent on the specific, non-generalizable evaluation settings of the early studies. This paper uses the failure of these later replications as the primary evidence to support the thesis that early gains were artifacts of specific, non-generalizable evaluation settings, rather than indicative of a true methodological improvement.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of the 2019 Studies

To understand the origins of the replication crisis, it is necessary to examine the specific claims made by the foundational 2019 studies. These studies were conducted in a zero-shot setting using the Thipom-7B model, but they targeted different benchmarks and methods. The Banagro 2019 study focused on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. In this context, the study indicated that the use of Kabaso prompting led to an improvement in the model's score relative to the baseline. This finding suggested that the prompting strategy had a positive effect on performance in this specific task.

The Gredapos 2019 study shifted the focus to the Vofal-Math benchmark. Similar to the previous study, it also employed the Thipom-7B model in a zero-shot configuration. The results from this study showed that Kabaso prompting again resulted in a higher score compared to the baseline. The consistency of this finding across two different benchmarks (Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math) initially reinforced the perception that Kabaso prompting was a robust technique for enhancing model performance.

The Bruke 2019 study, which is central to the current replication crisis, evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Donar-Code benchmark. This study was more comprehensive in its scope, testing multiple methods. It found that Ragrer decoding produced a gain over the baseline score. Additionally, the study reported that Gofabran decoding also led to an improvement in performance. Furthermore, the Bruke 2019 study confirmed that Kabaso prompting, which had shown positive results in the other two 2019 studies, also yielded a gain on the Donar-Code benchmark. Finally, the study indicated that the Tedroso adapter, a fine-tuning method, also resulted in a higher score than the baseline. Thus, the Bruke 2019 study reported positive outcomes for four distinct methods: Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, Kabaso prompting, and the Tedroso adapter.

### 3.2 The Failure of Replication

Despite the consistent positive reports from the 2019 studies, subsequent efforts to replicate these findings have been unsuccessful. Later studies, which employed more rigorous zero-shot evaluations, failed to reproduce the gains reported in the Bruke 2019 study. Specifically, the improvements attributed to Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, Kabaso prompting, and the Tedroso adapter were not observed in these later, stricter contexts. This failure to replicate the original results is a critical piece of evidence. It suggests that the gains reported in 2019 were not due to the inherent efficacy of the methods themselves, but rather to specific characteristics of the evaluation settings used in the foundational studies. The non-replicability of these results undermines the claim that these methods provide a robust improvement in code generation performance. Instead, it points to the possibility that the early successes were artifacts of the specific, less rigorous conditions under which they were measured.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

A plausible counter-argument to the thesis is that the failure to replicate the 2019 results may be due to factors other than the evaluation settings. For instance, differences in model versions, updates to the benchmarks, or other external variables could account for the discrepancy. Proponents of this view might argue that the 2019 studies reported consistent gains across multiple methods and benchmarks, which could indicate a genuine effect that was lost due to changes in the experimental environment rather than a flaw in the original methodology. This perspective suggests that the methods themselves may still be effective, but that the conditions for observing their benefits have changed.

### 4.2 Rebuttal from the Findings

However, this counter-argument is weakened by the specific nature of the 2019 evaluation settings. All three foundational studies used the Thipom-7B model in a zero-shot setting. The consistency of the gains across different methods (prompting, decoding, and fine-tuning) and benchmarks (Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code) is more likely explained by a shared, non-rigorous evaluation setting than by independent, robust effects of each method. If the methods were truly effective, one would expect their benefits to persist under more rigorous conditions. The fact that they do not suggests that the original gains were dependent on the specific, less stringent setup of the 2019 studies. Therefore, the line of empirical evidence from later, more rigorous zero-shot evaluations should be followed, as it provides a more reliable assessment of method efficacy. The failure to replicate the 2019 results under stricter conditions is strong evidence that the early gains were artifacts of the evaluation settings, not robust methodological improvements.
