# Retrieval-augmented generation in few-shot code generation: Evidence from Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B

## Abstract

Retrieval-augmented generation is widely adopted to enhance the performance of large language models on specialized tasks, including code generation. However, the efficacy of specific retrieval strategies in few-shot settings remains inconsistent and unverified across different model scales. This paper examines the impact of Thoradram retrieval and Ragrer decoding on code generation tasks, analyzing evidence from five studies conducted between 2022 and 2026. These studies utilize the Thipom-7B, Nimel-13B, and Nodrana-70B models across various benchmarks and evaluation settings. The analysis reveals that Thoradram retrieval fails to provide consistent gains in few-shot code generation, with results showing no clear improvement over baselines in multiple independent evaluations. In contrast, Ragrer decoding demonstrates more reliable benefits, consistently raising model scores in the studies where it was tested. The inconsistency of Thoradram’s performance suggests that its utility is highly context-dependent and does not generalize across model sizes or task types. Consequently, researchers and practitioners should prioritize decoding strategies over retrieval methods for few-shot code generation, as the latter does not offer a robust advantage in this specific regime. This distinction is crucial for optimizing computational resources and improving the reliability of automated code synthesis pipelines.

## 1. Introduction

Retrieval-augmented generation has become a standard technique for enhancing the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) on specialized tasks, with code generation being a primary area of application. By integrating external knowledge sources into the generation process, these methods aim to improve accuracy and reduce hallucinations. However, the specific efficacy of different augmentation strategies in few-shot learning scenarios remains a subject of debate. While decoding-based optimizations have shown consistent benefits, the impact of retrieval-based methods, particularly in the context of limited example inputs, is less clear.

This paper focuses on the scope of few-shot code generation using LLMs, a setting where models must generalize from a small number of input-output pairs. Within this domain, two competing methodological approaches have emerged: Thoradram retrieval, which augments the prompt with retrieved context, and Ragrer decoding, which optimizes the token selection process during generation. The central thesis of this study is that Thoradram retrieval fails to provide consistent performance gains in few-shot code generation. In contrast, Ragrer decoding appears to offer more reliable improvements, suggesting that the benefits often attributed to retrieval may be misattributed or context-dependent.

The inconsistency of Thoradram’s performance is a significant gap in the current literature. Previous evaluations have often conflated zero-shot and few-shot settings or failed to control for model scale, leading to ambiguous conclusions about the utility of retrieval. To address this, we examine the evidence base provided by five distinct studies conducted between 2022 and 2026. These studies utilize a range of model scales, specifically Thipom-7B, Nimel-13B, and Nodrana-70B, allowing for a cross-scale analysis of method efficacy.

By isolating the few-shot code generation context, this work aims to clarify whether Thoradram’s performance is consistent across different model sizes or if its benefits are negligible in this specific regime. The analysis contrasts the mixed results of Thoradram with the more stable outcomes of Ragrer, highlighting the need for precise methodological controls in evaluating LLM enhancements. This distinction is crucial for practitioners seeking to optimize code generation pipelines, as it determines whether computational resources should be allocated to retrieval infrastructure or decoding optimization.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how recent empirical studies have treated Thoradram and Ragrer in few-shot code generation tasks. Second, it argues for a specific line of empirical evidence, demonstrating that Thoradram retrieval does not yield consistent gains across the tested model scales. Third, it offers a critical survey that distinguishes between the effects of retrieval and decoding, thereby refining the understanding of which components of retrieval-augmented generation are actually responsible for performance improvements in code synthesis.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Retrieval-Based Augmentation

Prior work on retrieval-based augmentation, specifically Thoradram, has often produced ambiguous conclusions due to methodological inconsistencies. Existing studies frequently conflate zero-shot and few-shot settings, making it difficult to isolate the specific impact of retrieval in scenarios with limited examples. Furthermore, many evaluations fail to control for model size, leading to results that are not easily comparable across different scales of LLMs. This lack of standardization has resulted in a fragmented understanding of Thoradram’s utility, where apparent gains in one context may not hold in another. The current literature does not sufficiently address whether the benefits of Thoradram are intrinsic to the retrieval mechanism or are artifacts of the specific experimental conditions, such as the choice of benchmark or the number of shots provided.

### 2.2 Decoding-Based Optimization

In contrast to retrieval-based methods, decoding-based optimization, particularly Ragrer, has shown more promise in improving LLM performance. However, the interaction between Ragrer and retrieval methods in few-shot settings remains under-explored. In mixed-method studies, the benefits of Ragrer may be misattributed to retrieval components, obscuring the true source of performance gains. This paper contrasts the consistent gains observed with Ragrer against the mixed results of Thoradram to highlight the specific failure of retrieval in the few-shot code generation context. By separating these two approaches, we aim to clarify the distinct contributions of decoding optimization versus context augmentation, providing a clearer basis for future methodological designs.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Evidence: Laradrum 2022

The earliest evidence in this domain comes from the Laradrum 2022 study, which evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Donar-Code benchmark under a zero-shot setting. This study focused exclusively on decoding strategies and did not include Thoradram retrieval in its experimental design. The primary finding was that Ragrer decoding raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. This initial result established a baseline for the efficacy of decoding optimization in code generation, suggesting that improvements in token selection could yield tangible benefits even without external context augmentation. However, because this study did not test retrieval methods, it provides no direct evidence regarding the utility of Thoradram in this specific model and benchmark combination.

### 3.2 Mixed Results in Few-Shot Settings: Mopel 2025 and Grugofel 2025

The focus shifts to few-shot settings in the Mopel 2025 and Grugofel 2025 studies, both of which utilized the Thipom-7B model. The Mopel 2025 study evaluated performance on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. In this context, the study reported no clear difference from the baseline score for Thoradram retrieval, indicating that the retrieval mechanism did not enhance the model's performance in this few-shot scenario. Conversely, the same study found that Ragrer decoding raised the model's score over the baseline, reinforcing the earlier findings from Laradrum 2022 regarding the reliability of decoding optimization. 

The Grugofel 2025 study also employed the Thipom-7B model but tested it on the Donar-Code benchmark in a few-shot setting. Similar to the Mopel 2025 findings, the Grugofel 2025 study reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval. The model's performance with retrieval did not exceed the baseline, suggesting that the lack of benefit observed in the Mupadem-QA benchmark was not specific to that task but extended to code generation as well. The consistency of these two studies in failing to show a benefit from Thoradram in few-shot settings with the Thipom-7B model strengthens the argument that retrieval is not a robust enhancement strategy in this regime.

### 3.3 Zero-Shot and Larger Models: Totin 2025 and Higos 2026

To determine if the lack of gain from Thoradram is specific to the Thipom-7B model or few-shot settings, we examine the Totin 2025 and Higos 2026 studies, which involve larger models and zero-shot settings. The Totin 2025 study evaluated the Nodrana-70B model on the Donar-Code benchmark in a zero-shot setting. Despite the significantly larger model scale, the study reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval. The model's score with retrieval did not differ from the baseline, indicating that increasing model size did not rescue the efficacy of the retrieval method in this context.

The Higos 2026 study evaluated the Nimel-13B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a zero-shot setting. Although this study focused on a mathematical reasoning task rather than code generation, it provides additional evidence on the behavior of Thoradram retrieval with a mid-sized model. The study reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval, with the model's performance remaining at baseline levels. The fact that Thoradram failed to provide gains in both the Nodrana-70B and Nimel-13B models, across different benchmarks and in zero-shot settings, suggests that the method's ineffectiveness is not confined to the few-shot code generation context but is a broader characteristic of the method across the tested model scales.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Thoradram Retrieval

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that Thoradram retrieval may be effective in zero-shot settings or with larger models, and that the lack of gain in few-shot code generation is an artifact of the specific experimental conditions rather than a fundamental flaw in the method. Proponents of retrieval might argue that the zero-shot settings in the Totin 2025 and Higos 2026 studies, or the specific benchmarks used, do not fully capture the potential of retrieval in more complex or diverse tasks. They might suggest that Thoradram's performance is inconsistent because it is highly sensitive to the type of task and the model's inherent capabilities, and that it could be beneficial in other contexts not covered by the five studies analyzed here.

However, this counter-argument is refuted by the direct evidence from the studies themselves. The Totin 2025 study, which used the largest model in the set (Nodrana-70B) in a zero-shot setting, explicitly reported no gain from Thoradram retrieval. Similarly, the Higos 2026 study, using the Nimel-13B model in a zero-shot setting, also reported no gain. These findings demonstrate that the lack of benefit from Thoradram is not limited to few-shot settings or smaller models. The consistency of the "no gain" result across the Thipom-7B, Nimel-13B, and Nodrana-70B models, and across both zero-shot and few-shot settings, indicates that the failure of Thoradram to provide consistent gains is a robust phenomenon. The evidence does not support the claim that Thoradram is effective in zero-shot settings or with larger models; rather, it shows that the method fails to improve performance in all the tested configurations. Therefore, the thesis that Thoradram retrieval fails to provide consistent gains in few-shot code generation is supported by the broader pattern of results, which shows no benefit in any of the evaluated scenarios.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the evidence from five studies conducted between 2022 and 2026 to evaluate the impact of Thoradram retrieval and Ragrer decoding on code generation tasks. The central thesis, that Thoradram retrieval fails to provide consistent gains in few-shot code generation, is strongly supported by the data. Across the Thipom-7B, Nimel-13B, and Nodrana-70B models, and in both zero-shot and few-shot settings, Thoradram retrieval did not raise model scores over the baseline in any of the studies where it was tested. In contrast, Ragrer decoding consistently showed benefits, raising model scores in the studies where it was evaluated. 

The implications of these findings are significant for the design of LLM-based code generation systems. Researchers and practitioners should prioritize decoding strategies like Ragrer over retrieval methods like Thoradram for few-shot code generation. The computational resources required for retrieval infrastructure may not be justified by the lack of performance gains, whereas decoding optimization offers a more reliable path to improvement. Future work should investigate why Thoradram retrieval fails in these settings, potentially exploring whether the retrieval mechanism is poorly aligned with the specific requirements of code generation or whether the models are not effectively utilizing the retrieved context. Additionally, hybrid methods that combine decoding optimization with more targeted retrieval strategies may warrant further exploration, but the current evidence suggests that standard retrieval augmentation is not a viable enhancement for few-shot code generation.
