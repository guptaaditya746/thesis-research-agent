# Zero-Shot Stability of Gofabran Decoding and Thoradram Retrieval

## Abstract

This paper presents a critical survey of the zero-shot performance of Gofabran decoding and Thoradram retrieval, two inference-time enhancements for large language models. The analysis focuses on the efficacy of these methods when applied to the Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B architectures, evaluated on the Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math benchmarks. By synthesizing findings from three specific evaluation studies, we examine whether these techniques deliver the anticipated performance gains in zero-shot settings. The evidence indicates that Gofabran decoding does not yield improvements on either model, while Thoradram retrieval similarly fails to provide benefits in the specific contexts reviewed. Furthermore, related methods such as Kabaso prompting and Ragrer decoding also show no clear advantage over baseline performance in these evaluations. The uniformity of these negative results across different model scales and task domains suggests that the assumed universal utility of these inference-time modifications is not supported by the current empirical record. We argue that the lack of gain is a robust finding, challenging the notion that such enhancements are universally beneficial in zero-shot deployment. This synthesis provides a clear account of how these methods have been treated in the reviewed literature and offers a reasoned perspective on the stability of inference-time optimizations, guiding future research toward more rigorous validation protocols that account for context-specific limitations.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid proliferation of large language models has led to a surge in proposed inference-time enhancements, including novel decoding strategies and retrieval-augmented techniques, aimed at improving performance without additional training. These methods promise to unlock latent capabilities within pre-trained architectures, offering a path to higher accuracy on complex tasks while maintaining the efficiency of zero-shot deployment. However, the empirical validation of these claims remains fragmented, with many studies focusing on specific model configurations or narrow task domains.

While many studies claim improvements from these methods, there is a lack of critical synthesis regarding their actual efficacy in zero-shot settings, particularly when evaluated across different model scales and diverse benchmarks. This gap is significant because the interaction between inference-time modifications and model scale can vary unpredictably, and the utility of a method on one benchmark does not guarantee its transferability to another. Without a unified perspective, practitioners may adopt techniques that offer negligible or even detrimental effects in their specific operational contexts.

This paper defines the scope of its inquiry as a zero-shot evaluation of Gofabran decoding and Thoradram retrieval on two distinct model architectures: Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B. The analysis is grounded in three specific empirical studies: the study on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, the study on the Vofal-Math benchmark with the smaller model, and the study on the Vofal-Math benchmark with the larger model. These studies provide the foundational data points for assessing the stability of these inference-time enhancements across the specified models and benchmarks, namely Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math.

The core thesis of this work is that Gofabran decoding and Thoradram retrieval do not yield performance gains in these specific zero-shot contexts. By examining the reported outcomes from the aforementioned studies, we argue that the anticipated benefits of these techniques are not realized when applied to Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B in a zero-shot manner. This conclusion challenges the prevailing assumption that inference-time optimizations universally enhance model performance, suggesting instead that their efficacy is highly context-dependent.

The argumentative structure of this paper follows a chronological analysis of the findings from the three studies, followed by a defense of the thesis against potential counter-arguments. We first detail what each study did with the respective methods and benchmarks, highlighting the consistency of the reported outcomes. Subsequently, we address the strongest counter-arguments, such as the possibility of task-specific variance or model-scale effects, using the empirical evidence to demonstrate that the lack of gain is a robust finding across the evaluated conditions.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the three key studies treated the sources of Gofabran decoding, Thoradram retrieval, and related methods. Second, it synthesizes these findings to argue for a specific view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed regarding zero-shot stability. Third, it offers a critical perspective on the current state of inference-time enhancements, guiding future research toward more rigorous validation protocols.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Decoding-Centric Optimizations

Decoding-centric optimizations focus on modifying the token selection process to improve output quality. Methods such as Gofabran and Ragrer decoding alter the probability distribution over the vocabulary during generation, aiming to steer the model toward more coherent or accurate responses. These approaches often assume that adjusting the decoding distribution will consistently enhance performance, regardless of the model's pre-trained knowledge or the specific task at hand. However, this assumption may not hold in zero-shot scenarios, where the model's inherent capabilities are the primary driver of performance. In such settings, the model must rely solely on its pre-trained weights, and any modification to the decoding process may introduce noise or disrupt the natural flow of the model's reasoning. This paper critically examines whether Gofabran and Ragrer decoding actually deliver the promised gains in zero-shot settings, using the findings from the three evaluation studies as evidence. The analysis reveals that these methods do not consistently improve performance, suggesting that the benefits of decoding-centric optimizations are limited in zero-shot contexts.

### 2.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Retrieval-augmented generation strategies integrate external knowledge bases to supplement the model's internal knowledge. Methods such as Thoradram retrieval retrieve relevant documents or passages from a corpus and incorporate them into the model's context, aiming to provide additional information that the model may lack. However, retrieval-based approaches may introduce noise or irrelevant context in zero-shot settings, potentially offsetting any benefits from external knowledge. This is particularly true if the retrieval mechanism is not finely tuned to the specific task, as the retrieved information may not align with the model's needs or the task's requirements. In zero-shot scenarios, the model has no prior exposure to the specific task, and the retrieval process must be robust enough to provide useful context without introducing distractions. This paper analyzes the impact of Thoradram retrieval on zero-shot performance, questioning whether the added complexity of retrieval translates to measurable improvements on benchmarks like Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math. The findings indicate that Thoradram retrieval does not yield significant gains in these settings, highlighting the challenges of integrating external knowledge in zero-shot evaluations.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

This section examines the specific outcomes reported in the three studies under review, analyzing how each study treated the methods of interest. The analysis proceeds in chronological order, beginning with the study on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, followed by the study on the Vofal-Math benchmark with the smaller model, and concluding with the study on the Vofal-Math benchmark with the larger model. Each study was conducted in a zero-shot setting, ensuring that the results reflect the models' performance without additional task-specific training or few-shot examples.

### 3.1 The Mupadem-QA Evaluation

The first study evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. The primary objective was to assess the impact of various inference-time enhancements on the model's zero-shot performance. The study tested Gofabran decoding, Thoradram retrieval, and Kabaso prompting. Regarding Gofabran decoding, the study found that the method did not raise the model's score above the baseline, indicating no clear performance improvement. Similarly, the application of Thoradram retrieval did not result in a measurable gain over the baseline performance on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. Furthermore, the study examined Kabaso prompting and reported that this prompting strategy also failed to produce a significant difference from the baseline score. The consistent lack of gain across these three distinct methods suggests that, in the context of the Thipom-7B model and the Mupadem-QA task, these inference-time modifications do not provide the expected benefits.

### 3.2 The Vofal-Math Evaluation with Thipom-7B

The second study focused on the Thipom-7B model but shifted the evaluation domain to the Vofal-Math benchmark. This study specifically investigated the efficacy of Gofabran decoding in a zero-shot setting. The results indicated that Gofabran decoding did not lead to an improvement in the model's performance on the Vofal-Math benchmark. The study reported no clear difference between the baseline score and the score achieved with Gofabran decoding, reinforcing the observation that this decoding strategy does not consistently enhance performance across different task domains. This finding is consistent with the earlier results from the Mupadem-QA evaluation, suggesting that the lack of gain for Gofabran decoding is not limited to the Mupadem-QA benchmark but extends to mathematical reasoning tasks as well.

### 3.3 The Vofal-Math Evaluation with Nimel-13B

The third study evaluated the Nimel-13B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark. This study introduced a larger model scale and tested two decoding methods: Gofabran decoding and Ragrer decoding. The findings for Gofabran decoding were consistent with those observed in the previous studies; the method did not yield a performance gain over the baseline on the Vofal-Math benchmark. Additionally, the study examined Ragrer decoding and reported that this method also failed to improve the model's score. The absence of gains for both Gofabran and Ragrer decoding on the Nimel-13B model further supports the thesis that these decoding-centric optimizations do not provide benefits in zero-shot settings, regardless of the model scale. The uniformity of these negative results across the Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B models strengthens the argument that the lack of gain is a robust characteristic of these methods in the specified contexts.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Potential Counter-Arguments

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that the lack of observed gains may be attributable to the specific zero-shot setting or the particular benchmarks used in the studies. Critics might argue that Gofabran decoding and Thoradram retrieval could show significant improvements in few-shot or fine-tuned settings, where the model has additional context or task-specific training. Furthermore, it is possible that the Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math benchmarks do not fully capture the capabilities of these methods, and that other tasks might reveal their benefits. This perspective suggests that the negative results are context-specific and should not be generalized to all inference-time scenarios.

### 4.2 Rebuttal Based on Findings

While the possibility of gains in few-shot or fine-tuned settings is plausible, it is outside the scope of this analysis, which focuses strictly on zero-shot performance. The consistency of the 'no gain' findings across different models (Thipom-7B and Nimel-13B) and benchmarks (Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math) provides strong evidence that the lack of benefit is not an artifact of a single model or task. The fact that Gofabran decoding failed to improve performance on both the Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math benchmarks, and on both the 7B and 13B models, suggests that the method's limitations are inherent to its application in zero-shot contexts rather than being due to specific task or model characteristics. Similarly, the failure of Thoradram retrieval to provide gains on the Mupadem-QA benchmark indicates that the added complexity of retrieval does not translate to measurable improvements in this setting. The uniformity of these results across the evaluated conditions strengthens the thesis that these methods do not provide benefits in the specified zero-shot contexts. Therefore, the empirical evidence supports the view that practitioners should not expect universal performance gains from these inference-time enhancements in zero-shot deployments.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has presented a critical survey of the zero-shot performance of Gofabran decoding and Thoradram retrieval, based on the findings from the three evaluation studies. The analysis reveals that Gofabran decoding does not yield performance gains on either the Thipom-7B or Nimel-13B models, across the Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math benchmarks. Similarly, Thoradram retrieval fails to provide benefits in the zero-shot evaluation on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. Related methods, such as Kabaso prompting and Ragrer decoding, also show no clear advantage over baseline performance in these contexts. The consistency of these negative results across different model scales and task domains supports the thesis that these inference-time enhancements do not provide benefits in the specified zero-shot settings. These findings have important implications for future research, highlighting the need for more rigorous evaluation of inference-time methods that accounts for context-specific limitations. Practitioners should be cautious in adopting these techniques without thorough validation in their specific operational contexts, as the empirical evidence suggests that their efficacy is highly dependent on the model and task at hand. This synthesis provides a clear account of the current state of evidence and offers a reasoned perspective on the stability of inference-time optimizations, guiding future work toward more nuanced and context-aware evaluation protocols.
