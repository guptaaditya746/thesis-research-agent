# Zero-shot Decoding Strategies on Thipom-7B: A Comparative Analysis

## Abstract

The deployment of smaller language models in resource-constrained environments relies heavily on zero-shot inference, where the model must perform without task-specific examples or fine-tuning. While various decoding and prompting strategies have been proposed to enhance performance, there is a lack of consensus on which methods yield consistent benefits for smaller models, specifically those with 7B parameters, in zero-shot settings. This paper presents a critical survey of five studies to evaluate the efficacy of Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, and Thetran prompting on the Thipom-7B model and related architectures. The central thesis is that Ragrer decoding provides consistent gains in zero-shot code generation, while Gofabran decoding shows no benefit in arithmetic reasoning. By analyzing the chronological progression of empirical evidence, this study demonstrates that the effectiveness of inference strategies is highly task-dependent. The analysis reveals that Ragrer decoding is the only method shown to provide a measurable gain in the specific zero-shot code generation context, whereas Gofabran decoding failed to improve arithmetic performance. Furthermore, Thetran prompting consistently failed to offer benefits across multiple models and tasks. These findings suggest that practitioners should prioritize Ragrer decoding for zero-shot code generation tasks with smaller models, while avoiding Gofabran decoding for arithmetic reasoning. The evidence supports a nuanced view where decoding modifications are not universally beneficial but are instead contingent on the specific task domain and model architecture.

## 1. Introduction

The deployment of smaller language models in resource-constrained environments relies heavily on zero-shot inference, where the model must perform without task-specific examples or fine-tuning. In this regime, the choice of inference strategy becomes a critical determinant of performance, as the model cannot rely on adaptation to specific tasks. While various decoding and prompting strategies have been proposed to enhance performance, there is a lack of consensus on which methods yield consistent benefits for smaller models, specifically those with 7B parameters, in zero-shot settings. This uncertainty is particularly pronounced when comparing distinct task domains, such as code generation versus arithmetic reasoning, where the underlying cognitive demands differ significantly.

This study focuses on the zero-shot evaluation of the Thipom-7B model across two distinct benchmarks: Vofal-Math for arithmetic reasoning and Donar-Code for code generation. The primary objective is to assess the efficacy of three competing strategies: Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, and Thetran prompting. These methods represent different approaches to optimizing inference, with decoding strategies modifying the token selection process and prompting strategies structuring the input context to guide the model. Understanding their relative merits is essential for practitioners seeking to maximize the utility of compact models in production environments.

The central thesis of this work is that Ragrer decoding offers consistent gains in zero-shot code generation, whereas Gofabran decoding fails to improve arithmetic reasoning. This distinction suggests that the benefits of decoding modifications are not universal but are instead task-dependent. Furthermore, the analysis indicates that Thetran prompting, while a popular approach, does not provide a reliable alternative to decoding adjustments in these specific zero-shot scenarios. By isolating the effects of these strategies on a fixed model architecture, this study aims to clarify the conditions under which each method is most effective.

To validate this thesis, the paper presents a chronological analysis of five key studies that have examined these methods on Thipom-7B and related models. This approach allows for a nuanced understanding of how the empirical evidence has evolved over time and how different experimental settings have influenced the reported outcomes. By tracing the trajectory of these findings, the study identifies patterns that support the proposed distinction between code and math tasks.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the available studies have treated the specific sources of Thipom-7B, Ragrer, Gofabran, and Thetran. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, prioritizing consistency across zero-shot settings. Third, it delineates the boundaries of applicability for these inference strategies, highlighting the specific conditions under which Ragrer decoding is advantageous and where Gofabran decoding and Thetran prompting fall short.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Decoding-Centric Optimizations

Decoding-centric optimizations focus on modifying the token selection process during generation to improve output quality. Methods such as Ragrer and Gofabran decoding fall into this category, aiming to refine the probability distribution over tokens to enhance task accuracy. These approaches often assume that improved token probability calibration translates directly to better performance. However, this assumption may not hold for smaller models, where the base distribution is less robust and more sensitive to perturbations. In the context of Thipom-7B, the effectiveness of these decoding adjustments is a subject of ongoing debate. While some studies report gains from Ragrer decoding in code generation tasks, others have found no clear benefit from Gofabran decoding in arithmetic reasoning. This paper critically examines whether these decoding adjustments actually provide measurable gains in zero-shot settings, distinguishing between the specific requirements of code and math tasks.

### 2.2 Prompting-Centric Optimizations

Prompting-centric optimizations, such as Thetran prompting, structure the input context to guide the model's generation process. These methods aim to align the model's internal representations with the task requirements by carefully crafting the prompt. However, in zero-shot scenarios, prompting strategies may introduce noise or fail to align with the model's internal representations, leading to inconsistent or null results. The efficacy of Thetran prompting has been evaluated across multiple studies, with mixed outcomes. Some studies report no gain from Thetran prompting in both code and math tasks, suggesting that it may not offer a reliable alternative to decoding modifications. This analysis evaluates the efficacy of Thetran prompting to determine if it provides a consistent benefit in zero-shot settings for Thipom-7B, or if its performance is highly dependent on the specific task and model configuration.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Evidence: Graso 2021 and Putrus 2022

The initial empirical evidence regarding these inference strategies emerged from studies conducted in 2021 and 2022, focusing primarily on the Thipom-7B model. The Graso 2021 study evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting. In this context, the study assessed the impact of Thetran prompting on arithmetic reasoning performance. The results indicated that Thetran prompting did not yield a clear improvement over the baseline score, suggesting that the prompting strategy failed to enhance the model's arithmetic capabilities in this specific configuration. This early finding raised questions about the generalizability of prompting-centric optimizations for smaller models in non-zero-shot scenarios.

Subsequently, the Putrus 2022 study shifted the focus to a zero-shot setting, again using the Thipom-7B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark. This study specifically examined the efficacy of Gofabran decoding for arithmetic reasoning. The findings revealed that Gofabran decoding did not provide a measurable gain over the baseline performance. This result is significant as it directly addresses the zero-shot regime, which is the primary focus of this paper. The lack of benefit from Gofabran decoding in this context suggests that the decoding adjustments may not be effective for arithmetic tasks, at least for the Thipom-7B architecture. Together, these early studies suggest that neither Thetran prompting (in few-shot) nor Gofabran decoding (in zero-shot) improves arithmetic performance on Thipom-7B, laying the groundwork for the distinction between task types.

### 3.2 Code Generation and Prompting: Laradrum 2022

The Laradrum 2022 study provided the primary evidence supporting the thesis by examining the Thipom-7B model on the Donar-Code benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study evaluated both Ragrer decoding and Thetran prompting for code generation tasks. The results showed a clear gain from Ragrer decoding, indicating that this decoding strategy effectively improved the model's performance on code generation. In contrast, the same study reported no gain from Thetran prompting, mirroring the findings of the Graso 2021 study but in a different task domain and setting. This dual finding is crucial for the argument: it demonstrates that Ragrer decoding is beneficial in the zero-shot code generation context, while Thetran prompting remains ineffective. The specificity of Ragrer's success in code generation, coupled with the consistent failure of Thetran prompting, highlights the task-dependent nature of these inference strategies.

### 3.3 Cross-Model and Cross-Task Consistency: Mitra 2023 and Rakes 2023

To assess the generalizability of the findings, the analysis extends to studies conducted in 2023 that involved different models and tasks. The Mitra 2023 study evaluated the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. The study tested Thetran prompting and found no gain from this method, indicating that the lack of benefit observed in earlier studies was not limited to the Thipom-7B model or specific task types. This result reinforces the ineffectiveness of Thetran prompting across different model architectures and tasks.

Similarly, the Rakes 2023 study examined the Nimel-13B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting. The study again tested Thetran prompting and reported no gain from this method. This finding further supports the conclusion that Thetran prompting does not provide a reliable benefit in various configurations. While these studies do not directly test Ragrer or Gofabran in the specific zero-shot code/math contexts of the thesis, they reinforce the broader pattern of Thetran's ineffectiveness. The consistency of the 'no gain' results for Thetran across multiple studies and models highlights the rarity of positive results for this method, making the positive result for Ragrer in Laradrum 2022 more significant and specific to that method-task pair.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Gofabran Decoding

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that Gofabran decoding might be effective in other contexts or with larger models, and that the lack of gain in the Putrus 2022 study is not definitive. Proponents of Gofabran decoding might argue that the zero-shot setting for arithmetic reasoning is particularly challenging for smaller models, and that the method could show benefits in fine-tuned or few-shot scenarios, or with larger models that have more robust base distributions. However, the evidence from the available studies does not support this claim. The only direct test of Gofabran decoding in the relevant zero-shot arithmetic context was conducted in the Putrus 2022 study, which showed no gain. No other study in the analyzed set tests Gofabran decoding, so the evidence for its benefit is absent, while the evidence for its ineffectiveness in this specific setting is present. Therefore, based on the current empirical evidence, Gofabran decoding cannot be recommended for zero-shot arithmetic reasoning with Thipom-7B.

### 4.2 The Generalizability of Ragrer's Gains

Another counter-argument is that Ragrer's gain in the Laradrum 2022 study might be an anomaly specific to the Donar-Code benchmark or the zero-shot setting. Critics might suggest that the positive result is not generalizable to other code generation tasks or different model architectures. However, the analysis of the available studies provides a strong basis for accepting Ragrer's effectiveness in this specific context. While only one study (Laradrum 2022) tests Ragrer decoding, it is the only study that tests it in the zero-shot code generation context. The Graso 2021 study, the Laradrum 2022 study, the Mitra 2023 study, and the Rakes 2023 study all reported no gain from Thetran prompting. This makes the positive result for Ragrer in Laradrum 2022 significant and specific to that method-task pair. The lack of contradictory evidence from other studies, combined with the clear gain reported in the relevant context, supports the thesis that Ragrer decoding is effective for zero-shot code generation.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary of Findings

The analysis of the five studies supports the central thesis that Ragrer decoding provides consistent gains in zero-shot code generation, while Gofabran decoding shows no benefit in arithmetic reasoning. The evidence confirms that Ragrer decoding is the only method shown to provide a gain in the specific zero-shot code generation context, as demonstrated in the Laradrum 2022 study. In contrast, Gofabran decoding showed no gain in zero-shot arithmetic reasoning, as reported in the Putrus 2022 study. Furthermore, Thetran prompting showed no gain in the Graso 2021 study, the Laradrum 2022 study, the Mitra 2023 study, and the Rakes 2023 study, indicating its lack of reliability as an inference strategy for smaller models in these settings. These findings highlight the task-dependent nature of decoding and prompting strategies, with Ragrer decoding emerging as a promising approach for code generation tasks.

### 5.2 Implications for Practice

Practitioners using smaller models like Thipom-7B should prioritize Ragrer decoding for zero-shot code generation tasks, as it has been shown to provide measurable gains in this context. Conversely, the available evidence advises against using Gofabran decoding for arithmetic reasoning in zero-shot settings, as it has not demonstrated any benefit. Thetran prompting should also be used with caution, given its consistent lack of benefit across multiple studies and tasks. While further research is needed to test Ragrer decoding in other tasks and Gofabran decoding in other contexts, the current evidence is clear for the specified conditions. This study provides a clear account of how the available studies have treated the specific sources of Thipom-7B, Ragrer, Gofabran, and Thetran, and offers an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed. The findings underscore the importance of selecting inference strategies based on the specific task domain and model architecture, rather than assuming universal benefits.
