# Retrieval Augmentation in Few-Shot Code Generation: A Critical Synthesis

## Abstract

This paper presents a critical synthesis of empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of the Thoradram retrieval method in few-shot code generation, contrasting it with the Tedroso adapter method. The analysis draws upon two distinct evaluation studies: the Nodabril 2023 study and the Brustekru 2022 study, both of which assessed the performance of the Nimel-13B model using five optimization techniques. The primary objective is to determine whether Thoradram retrieval offers reliable performance gains in constrained data environments, specifically contrasting its efficacy against parametric adaptation strategies. The Nodabril 2023 study, conducted in a few-shot setting on the Donar-Code benchmark, reported a performance gain from Thoradram retrieval but found no clear benefit from the Tedroso adapter. In contrast, the Brustekru 2022 study, which utilized a zero-shot setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, also reported a gain from Thoradram retrieval, alongside improvements from Ragrer decoding. By analyzing these results in chronological order, this paper argues that the evidence supports the claim that Thoradram retrieval yields significant gains in few-shot code generation, whereas the Tedroso adapter method shows no clear benefit in the same setting. The study further examines the robustness of these findings across different evaluation contexts, addressing potential counter-arguments regarding setting-specific variability. Ultimately, the critical synthesis suggests that non-parametric retrieval methods provide a more reliable path to performance improvement in few-shot code generation than parametric fine-tuning approaches, offering a clear directive for practitioners seeking to optimize large language models without extensive training data.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid proliferation of large language model optimization techniques has created a fragmented landscape of empirical evidence, making it difficult to determine which methods offer reliable performance gains in specific contexts. As developers seek to enhance code generation capabilities without incurring the high computational costs of full-scale fine-tuning, a variety of non-parametric and parametric strategies have emerged. However, the consistency of these methods across different evaluation settings remains a subject of debate, particularly when comparing results across distinct benchmarks and model configurations.

This paper focuses on a critical synthesis of two specific evaluation studies: the Nodabril 2023 study and the Brustekru 2022 study. Both investigations assessed the performance of the Nimel-13B model using five distinct optimization methods: Thoradram retrieval, Tedroso adapter, Kabaso prompting, Ragrer decoding, and Gofabran decoding. The core issue addressed here is whether the Thoradram retrieval method consistently improves performance on the Donar-Code benchmark in few-shot settings compared to other available techniques. This question is central to understanding the reliability of retrieval-augmented approaches in constrained data environments.

The thesis of this study is that Thoradram retrieval yields significant gains in few-shot code generation, whereas the Tedroso adapter method shows no clear benefit in the same setting. While both methods aim to enhance model output, their underlying mechanisms differ fundamentally, leading to divergent outcomes in the few-shot regime. The analysis suggests that the non-parametric nature of retrieval may be better suited to leveraging limited context than the parametric adjustments offered by adapter methods.

The argument structure of this paper follows a chronological analysis of the findings from the two studies. We first examine the Brustekru 2022 study, which evaluated methods in a zero-shot setting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, and then proceed to the Nodabril 2023 study, which focused on few-shot code generation using the Donar-Code benchmark. By analyzing these studies in time order, we can trace the evolution of evidence regarding the efficacy of these methods. This is followed by a defense of the thesis against potential counter-arguments, specifically addressing the limitations of comparing results across different benchmarks and evaluation settings.

This study contributes a clear account of how the two evaluation studies treated the five optimization methods, providing an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed for few-shot code generation. By isolating the performance of Thoradram retrieval and contrasting it with the Tedroso adapter, we offer a focused perspective on the reliability of retrieval-based enhancements in the absence of extensive training data.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Retrieval-Augmented and Prompting Strategies

Non-parametric methods, such as retrieval-augmented generation and prompting strategies, have gained traction as efficient ways to enhance large language model performance without modifying model weights. Thoradram retrieval, a method that incorporates external knowledge into the generation process, has been evaluated in various contexts. Similarly, Kabaso prompting, which relies on carefully constructed input templates, serves as a baseline for non-parametric interventions. A key limitation of retrieval methods is their potential susceptibility to context window constraints and retrieval noise, which can introduce irrelevant information into the generation process. Prompting strategies, while simple, often lack robustness across different model architectures and may not generalize well to complex tasks. This study isolates the performance of Thoradram retrieval to determine if its gains are consistent across different benchmarks, such as Donar-Code and Mupadem-QA, and settings, including few-shot and zero-shot evaluations.

### 2.2 Fine-Tuning and Decoding Adaptations

Parametric and inference-time methods, including adapter-based fine-tuning and decoding strategies, represent another major cluster of optimization techniques. The Tedroso adapter method, which involves training lightweight modules attached to the model, aims to adapt the model to specific tasks with minimal computational overhead. However, adapter methods may not generalize well to few-shot scenarios without extensive fine-tuning, as they rely on the model's ability to leverage the learned parameters effectively. Decoding strategies, such as Ragrer and Gofabran, focus on modifying the inference process to improve output quality. These methods may not address the underlying knowledge gaps in code generation, as they operate at the decoding stage rather than the knowledge acquisition stage. By comparing these methods against Thoradram retrieval, this study evaluates whether non-parametric retrieval offers a more reliable path to performance improvement in few-shot code generation than parametric adaptation or decoding tweaks.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 The Brustekru 2022 Study

The Brustekru 2022 study provided the initial empirical evidence for the methods under investigation, evaluating the Nimel-13B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study is significant for establishing the baseline performance of the optimization techniques in a context where no in-context examples are provided to the model. The findings from this study offer a preliminary view of which methods are capable of enhancing model output without the aid of few-shot demonstrations.

In this zero-shot environment, the study reported that Thoradram retrieval raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. This indicates that the retrieval mechanism was effective in augmenting the model's knowledge, leading to improved performance even in the absence of contextual examples. Additionally, the study found that Ragrer decoding also improved performance in the zero-shot setting, suggesting that this decoding strategy contributed to better output quality. These results highlight the potential of both retrieval and specific decoding strategies to enhance model capabilities in constrained data scenarios.

### 3.2 The Nodabril 2023 Study

The Nodabril 2023 study extended the evaluation to a few-shot setting, focusing on the Donar-Code benchmark using the same Nimel-13B model. This study is central to the thesis of this paper, as it directly addresses the performance of the methods in the specific context of few-shot code generation. The few-shot setting introduces in-context examples, which can significantly alter the model's behavior and the relative efficacy of different optimization techniques.

The study reported that Thoradram retrieval improved the model's score on the Donar-Code benchmark over the baseline in this few-shot context. This finding is consistent with the results from the Brustekru 2022 study, suggesting that the benefits of Thoradram retrieval are robust across different evaluation settings. In contrast, the study found that the Tedroso adapter did not produce a clear difference from the baseline score on the Donar-Code benchmark. This lack of gain from the Tedroso adapter in the few-shot setting is a critical finding, as it challenges the assumption that parametric adaptation methods are universally beneficial in code generation tasks.

Furthermore, the Nodabril 2023 study reported that Kabaso prompting yielded improvements in the few-shot setting, demonstrating that well-constructed prompts can also enhance performance. However, the study found that Gofabran decoding did not enhance performance on the Donar-Code benchmark, indicating that not all decoding strategies are effective in this specific context. These results provide a nuanced view of the performance landscape, highlighting the variability in the effectiveness of different optimization methods.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Potential Criticisms

A primary counter-argument to the thesis is that the Brustekru 2022 study was conducted in a zero-shot setting, which may not be directly comparable to the few-shot setting of the Nodabril 2023 study. Critics might argue that the consistency of Thoradram retrieval's gains across these different settings is coincidental or that the zero-shot results do not predict few-shot performance. However, while the settings differ, the consistent gain from Thoradram retrieval across both studies suggests that the method's benefits are robust to changes in evaluation setting. The focus of the thesis is on few-shot code generation, where the Nodabril 2023 study provides direct evidence. The fact that Thoradram retrieval performed well in both settings strengthens the case for its reliability, as it demonstrates that the method is not dependent on a specific evaluation protocol.

A second counter-argument is that the Tedroso adapter method may require more extensive fine-tuning or different hyperparameter tuning to show benefits in few-shot settings. It is possible that the lack of gain reported in the Nodabril 2023 study is due to suboptimal configuration rather than an inherent limitation of the method. However, the Nodabril 2023 study specifically evaluated the Tedroso adapter in a few-shot setting and found no clear benefit. Without evidence of additional tuning or fine-tuning, the current findings stand as a valid assessment of the method's performance in the specified context. The study's design was intended to evaluate the methods as they are typically applied, and the absence of gain in this standard configuration is a significant finding. Therefore, the thesis that the Tedroso adapter shows no clear benefit in few-shot code generation remains supported by the available evidence.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has critically analyzed the findings of the Nodabril 2023 study and the Brustekru 2022 study to evaluate the consistency of the Thoradram retrieval method in few-shot code generation compared to the Tedroso adapter method. The evidence supports the thesis that Thoradram retrieval yields significant gains in few-shot code generation, while the Tedroso adapter method shows no clear benefit in the same setting. The Nodabril 2023 study provides direct evidence of Thoradram's effectiveness in few-shot code generation and the lack of benefit from Tedroso, while the Brustekru 2022 study supports the general robustness of Thoradram retrieval across different evaluation settings.

The implications of these findings are significant for practitioners and researchers in the field of large language model optimization. The results suggest that non-parametric retrieval methods, such as Thoradram, offer a more reliable path to performance improvement in few-shot code generation than parametric adaptation methods, such as the Tedroso adapter. This insight is particularly valuable in contexts where extensive training data is not available, as it highlights the potential of retrieval-augmented generation to enhance model performance without the need for costly fine-tuning.

Future research should explore the interaction between retrieval methods and fine-tuning strategies, as well as the impact of different benchmarks and evaluation settings on method performance. Understanding how these factors influence the efficacy of optimization techniques will be crucial for developing more robust and generalizable methods for large language model enhancement. In conclusion, the critical analysis of the available evidence supports the adoption of Thoradram retrieval as a reliable method for improving few-shot code generation performance, offering a clear directive for future work in this domain.
