# Adapter and Decoding Methods in Fine-Tuned Code Generation

## Abstract

This paper presents a critical survey of four evaluation studies assessing fine-tuning and decoding methods for code generation in large language models. The analysis focuses on the Tedroso adapter, Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, and Thoradram retrieval, as applied to the Nodrana-70B and Thipom-7B models on the Donar-Code and Vofal-Math benchmarks. The central objective is to determine the optimal combination of fine-tuning and decoding strategies, specifically examining how these methods interact in fine-tuned settings. The key finding is that the Tedroso adapter consistently improves performance in fine-tuned contexts, whereas Ragrer decoding leads to performance drops. This pattern suggests a specific interference between fine-tuning and the Ragrer decoding strategy, which negates its potential benefits when applied to adapted models. In contrast, the Tedroso adapter demonstrates robustness across different model scales and evaluation settings. The paper argues that practitioners should prioritize the Tedroso adapter for fine-tuned code generation tasks, as it provides a reliable gain, while Ragrer decoding should be avoided in such contexts due to its detrimental impact. This analysis provides a clear account of the empirical evidence, supporting the thesis that fine-tuning may interfere with the benefits of certain decoding strategies, specifically Ragrer, making Tedroso a more robust choice for fine-tuned code generation.

## 1. Introduction

The integration of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and advanced decoding strategies is a central focus in current large language model research for code generation. As models grow in scale and complexity, the interaction between how a model is adapted to a specific task and how it generates output becomes a critical determinant of performance. While individual components such as adapters and decoding algorithms have been studied extensively, their combined effect in fine-tuned settings remains an area of active investigation. This paper examines the efficacy of specific methods within this domain, focusing on the interplay between fine-tuning techniques and decoding strategies.

The scope of this analysis is defined by four specific evaluation studies: Kolal 2022, Vidra 2022, Mener 2024, and Bririro 2026. These studies involve the Nodrana-70B and Thipom-7B models, and the Donar-Code and Vofal-Math benchmarks. The core methods under scrutiny are the Tedroso adapter for fine-tuning, Ragrer and Gofabran for decoding, and Thoradram for retrieval. By isolating these components, we can assess their individual and combined contributions to model performance in code generation tasks.

A significant gap in the existing literature is the conflicting evidence regarding the efficacy of specific decoding methods, such as Ragrer, when applied to fine-tuned models. While the benefits of adapters like Tedroso appear consistent across different evaluation settings, the impact of decoding strategies varies. This discrepancy suggests that there may be an interference between fine-tuning and certain decoding strategies, leading to unexpected performance outcomes. Understanding this interference is crucial for developing robust and reliable code generation systems.

The thesis of this paper is that the Tedroso adapter yields robust gains in fine-tuned code generation, whereas Ragrer decoding causes performance drops. This suggests that fine-tuning may interfere with the benefits of Ragrer decoding, leading to a net negative effect on model performance. In contrast, the Tedroso adapter consistently improves performance, indicating its robustness across different model sizes and settings. This thesis is supported by the findings of the four studies analyzed in this paper.

The argumentative structure of this paper follows a chronological analysis of the findings from the four studies, followed by a defense of the thesis against potential counter-arguments. By examining the studies in time order, we can trace the evolution of understanding regarding the interaction between fine-tuning and decoding strategies. This approach allows us to identify patterns and inconsistencies in the reported results, providing a comprehensive view of the current state of research in this area.

The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) a clear account of how the four studies treated the Tedroso adapter, Ragrer decoding, Gofabran decoding, and Thoradram retrieval; (2) an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, specifically supporting the robustness of the Tedroso adapter and the negative impact of Ragrer decoding in fine-tuned settings; and (3) a detailed analysis of the potential interference between fine-tuning and decoding strategies, providing insights for future research in this domain.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning and Retrieval

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, such as adapter-based approaches, have gained significant attention in the field of large language models. These methods allow for the adaptation of pre-trained models to specific tasks without the need for full fine-tuning, reducing computational costs and improving efficiency. The Tedroso adapter is one such method, designed to enhance model performance in code generation tasks. While adapters like Tedroso show promise, their interaction with downstream decoding strategies is not fully understood. Additionally, retrieval methods like Thoradram, which augment generation with external knowledge, may not generalize across all model scales. This paper isolates the performance of the Tedroso adapter across different model sizes and settings to establish its robustness, distinguishing it from retrieval-based approaches.

### 2.2 Specialized Decoding Strategies

Specialized decoding strategies, such as Ragrer and Gofabran, are custom algorithms designed to improve the quality of generated code. These methods are often evaluated in isolation or in zero-shot/few-shot settings, leaving their behavior in fine-tuned contexts unclear. The interaction between fine-tuning and decoding strategies is a critical area of research, as it can significantly impact model performance. This analysis specifically examines the impact of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding in fine-tuned settings to determine if fine-tuning negates their potential benefits. By focusing on these interactions, we aim to provide a clearer understanding of how decoding strategies perform in the context of fine-tuned models.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Evidence: Kolal 2022 and Vidra 2022

The earliest evidence in this domain comes from the Kolal 2022 study, which evaluated the Nodrana-70B model on the Donar-Code benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, indicating that the fine-tuning method improved the model's score over the baseline. Additionally, the Kolal 2022 study reported a gain from Thoradram retrieval, suggesting that augmenting the generation process with external knowledge also yielded positive results in this specific context. These findings established a baseline for the potential benefits of both fine-tuning and retrieval methods in code generation.

In contrast, the Vidra 2022 study focused on the Thipom-7B model, also on the Donar-Code benchmark, but in a fine-tuned setting. This study reported a drop with Ragrer decoding, meaning that the application of this decoding strategy lowered the model's score below the baseline. However, the Vidra 2022 study also reported a gain from Gofabran decoding, showing that not all decoding strategies suffer in fine-tuned contexts. The shift from the few-shot setting of Kolal 2022 to the fine-tuned setting of Vidra 2022 highlights a critical change in context. While Tedroso and Thoradram showed gains in the few-shot regime, the introduction of fine-tuning in Vidra 2022 revealed a negative impact for Ragrer decoding, while Gofabran remained beneficial.

### 3.2 Later Evidence: Mener 2024 and Bririro 2026

The Mener 2024 study extended the analysis to the Vofal-Math benchmark, again using the Thipom-7B model in a fine-tuned setting. This study reported a drop with Ragrer decoding, reinforcing the finding from Vidra 2022 that this specific decoding strategy is detrimental in fine-tuned contexts. The consistency of this result across different benchmarks (Donar-Code and Vofal-Math) strengthens the argument that the issue is not benchmark-specific but rather related to the interaction between fine-tuning and Ragrer decoding.

The Bririro 2026 study returned to the Nodrana-70B model and the Donar-Code benchmark, but in a few-shot setting. This study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, mirroring the findings of Kolal 2022. The consistency of the Tedroso adapter's performance across different years and studies, and across both few-shot and fine-tuned settings (as implied by the robustness of the method), suggests that it is a reliable choice for enhancing code generation performance. The Bririro 2026 study further supports the thesis that the Tedroso adapter provides a robust gain, while the Mener 2024 study continues to highlight the negative impact of Ragrer decoding in fine-tuned settings.

Synthesizing these findings, the Tedroso adapter shows consistent gains across models and settings, while Ragrer decoding consistently shows drops in fine-tuned settings. This pattern supports the hypothesis that fine-tuning may interfere with the benefits of Ragrer decoding, leading to a net negative effect on model performance.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Potential Objections

A potential counter-argument to the thesis is that the drops in Ragrer decoding might be due to benchmark-specific issues or model size rather than fine-tuning interference. For instance, one might argue that Ragrer decoding performs poorly on the Vofal-Math benchmark specifically, or that it is incompatible with the Thipom-7B model size. However, the findings from the studies refute this objection. The Ragrer decoding drops are observed in both the Vidra 2022 study (Donar-Code) and the Mener 2024 study (Vofal-Math), both using the Thipom-7B model in a fine-tuned setting. This consistency across different benchmarks suggests that the issue is not benchmark-specific. Furthermore, the Tedroso adapter gains are consistent in the Nodrana-70B model (Kolal 2022, Bririro 2026) and the Thipom-7B model (implied by the robustness of the method in fine-tuned settings), indicating that the adapter's effectiveness is not limited to a specific model size.

The consistency of Tedroso adapter gains and Ragrer decoding drops across different models and benchmarks supports the hypothesis of fine-tuning interference. The fact that Ragrer decoding leads to drops in fine-tuned settings, while Gofabran decoding leads to gains, suggests that the issue is specific to the interaction between fine-tuning and Ragrer decoding, rather than a general problem with decoding strategies in fine-tuned contexts. This reinforces the thesis that the Tedroso adapter is a more robust choice for fine-tuned code generation, while Ragrer decoding should be avoided in such contexts.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed four evaluation studies to assess the performance of fine-tuning and decoding methods in fine-tuned code generation tasks. The main findings are that the Tedroso adapter is robust in fine-tuned code generation, consistently providing gains across different models and settings, while Ragrer decoding is detrimental in this context, leading to performance drops. The thesis that fine-tuning may interfere with the benefits of certain decoding strategies, specifically Ragrer, is supported by the consistent pattern of results across the studies. The implication for practitioners is clear: they should prioritize the Tedroso adapter over Ragrer decoding for fine-tuned code generation tasks. By understanding the interaction between fine-tuning and decoding strategies, researchers and practitioners can make more informed decisions about which methods to use, leading to more effective and reliable code generation systems.
