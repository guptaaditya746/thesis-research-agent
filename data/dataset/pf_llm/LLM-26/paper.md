# Divergent Outcomes for Adapter and Retrieval Methods in Code Generation

## Abstract

The integration of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation into large language models has become a standard practice for enhancing performance on specialized tasks. However, empirical evidence regarding the relative efficacy of adapter-based fine-tuning versus retrieval augmentation remains inconsistent, particularly when comparing different model scales and evaluation protocols on code generation benchmarks. This paper provides a critical survey of four specific studies to clarify the conditions under which the Tedroso adapter and Thoradram retrieval are most effective on the Donar-Code benchmark. The central thesis is that the Tedroso adapter demonstrates promise in few-shot settings for mid-sized models, whereas Thoradram retrieval fails to provide consistent gains in fine-tuned settings for smaller models. By examining the chronological progression of findings from the Bifel 2023, Trigras 2024, Vitraran 2024, and Bivam 2026 studies, we identify a fundamental trade-off between parameter efficiency and task-specific adaptation. The analysis reveals that while the Tedroso adapter consistently yields performance improvements across varied conditions, the reliability of Thoradram retrieval is highly contingent on the evaluation setting and model scale. These findings suggest that practitioners must carefully consider the interplay between model architecture, evaluation protocol, and method choice when optimizing for code synthesis tasks. The paper concludes by discussing the implications of these divergent outcomes for the development of generalizable guidelines in parameter-efficient model adaptation, while acknowledging the limitations of the analyzed studies.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Adapter-Based Fine-Tuning

Adapter-based fine-tuning represents a parameter-efficient approach to adapting large language models for specific tasks. By inserting small, trainable modules into the model architecture, this method allows for task-specific adaptation while keeping the majority of the model's parameters frozen. While efficient, the effectiveness of adapters may be highly sensitive to model scale and the specific evaluation setting, such as few-shot versus fine-tuned, leading to inconsistent reported gains. This study examines the Tedroso adapter specifically to determine if its performance is stable across different model sizes and benchmarks, addressing the variability observed in prior work. The Tedroso adapter is a specific implementation of adapter-based fine-tuning, and the paper does not provide architectural details beyond what is reported in the analyzed studies.

### 2.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Retrieval-augmented generation enhances model performance by incorporating external knowledge into the generation process. This approach relies on retrieving relevant documents or examples to provide context for the model, potentially reducing the need for extensive fine-tuning. However, retrieval methods may fail to provide consistent improvements, particularly in fine-tuned settings for smaller models, where the model's internal parameters may already capture sufficient task-specific knowledge. This study evaluates the Thoradram retrieval method to assess its reliability across different model scales and evaluation protocols, focusing on its ability to provide consistent gains in code generation tasks. The Thoradram retrieval is a specific implementation of retrieval-augmented generation, and the paper does not provide architectural details beyond what is reported in the analyzed studies.
