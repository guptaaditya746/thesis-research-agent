# Adapter Fine-Tuning vs. Prompting in Multi-Hop QA

## Abstract

The field of large language models remains divided on the most effective strategy for enhancing performance in complex reasoning tasks, specifically multi-hop question answering. This paper presents a critical survey of six specific evaluation studies comparing two distinct approaches: Thetran prompting and Tedroso adapter fine-tuning. By analyzing these studies involving the Nodrana-70B and Thipom-7B models across the Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code benchmarks, we assess the reliability of these methods in a controlled comparative context. The analysis reveals a significant discrepancy between the initial optimism surrounding prompting techniques and the more stable outcomes observed with fine-tuning. While early zero-shot prompting studies suggested substantial gains, subsequent few-shot attempts frequently failed to replicate these results or showed no clear improvement over baseline performance. In contrast, Tedroso adapter fine-tuning demonstrated consistent performance gains across multiple studies and benchmarks. The evidence indicates that Thetran prompting is an unreliable foundation for complex QA, often yielding null results in replication settings. Conversely, the adapter-based approach provides a robust path to performance improvement, maintaining its advantages regardless of model scale or task domain. This paper argues that the empirical weight of consistent gains from Tedroso outweighs the theoretical simplicity and lower computational cost of prompting. We conclude that for practical applications requiring consistent performance in multi-hop QA, adapter-based fine-tuning is the superior and more reliable strategy, provided the resource overhead is manageable. The findings underscore the importance of replication and consistency over isolated positive reports in evaluating intervention strategies for large language models.

## 1. Introduction

The field of large language models is currently divided on the most effective strategy for enhancing performance in complex reasoning tasks, specifically multi-hop question answering. While early reports suggested that sophisticated prompting techniques could unlock significant capabilities in large models, subsequent attempts to replicate these gains have often yielded inconsistent or null results. In contrast, parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, particularly those utilizing adapter architectures, have demonstrated more stable improvements across various evaluation settings. This discrepancy between the initial optimism surrounding prompting and the more reliable outcomes observed with fine-tuning creates a critical gap in the empirical literature, as a systematic comparison of these specific lines of evidence is lacking.

This study focuses on the comparative effectiveness of two distinct approaches: Thetran prompting and Tedroso adapter fine-tuning. These methods were evaluated using two specific model architectures, Nodrana-70B and Thipom-7B, across three distinct benchmarks: Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code. The selection of these six specific studies allows for a controlled examination of how different intervention strategies perform across varying model scales and task domains. By isolating these variables within this bounded set of investigations, the analysis aims to clarify whether the perceived superiority of prompting is an artifact of specific experimental conditions or a robust phenomenon. The inclusion of these specific studies is driven by their direct comparison of the two methods under similar but distinct conditions, providing a clear trajectory for evaluating reliability.

The central thesis of this paper is that Tedroso adapter fine-tuning offers superior and more reliable performance gains than Thetran prompting. The evidence suggests that while prompting may yield positive results in isolated zero-shot scenarios, it frequently fails to maintain these advantages in few-shot settings or when replicated in subsequent studies. Conversely, the adapter-based approach has shown a consistent capacity to enhance model performance, regardless of the specific benchmark or model size employed. This reliability is crucial for practical applications where consistent performance is required.

To support this thesis, the argument proceeds through a chronological analysis of the six specific studies. These studies are examined in the order they were conducted, allowing for a clear trajectory of how the field’s understanding of these methods has evolved. Each study is assessed based on its specific experimental setup, the models and benchmarks utilized, and the reported outcomes relative to baseline performance. This temporal approach highlights the progression from initial exploratory findings to more rigorous replication attempts.

The analysis reveals a pattern where prompting-based methods, particularly in few-shot configurations, struggle to replicate the positive outcomes reported in earlier zero-shot studies. In contrast, the fine-tuning-based methods demonstrate a more stable profile of improvement. This paper argues that the instability of prompting results makes it a less reliable foundation for complex QA compared to parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The following sections detail the background of these methods and benchmarks, analyze the reported results in depth, and address potential counter-arguments to this conclusion.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the reviewed studies treated the specific sources and methods involved. Second, it offers an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed for future research in multi-hop QA. Third, it establishes a framework for evaluating the reliability of different intervention strategies in large language models, emphasizing the importance of replication and consistency over isolated gains.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Prompting-Based Approaches

Prompting-based methods, such as Thetran, rely on zero-shot and few-shot strategies to elicit complex reasoning from large language models without modifying the underlying model parameters. These approaches have been widely explored due to their simplicity and low computational overhead. However, the empirical evidence for their effectiveness in multi-hop question answering is mixed. Early studies reported significant gains in zero-shot settings, suggesting that carefully crafted prompts could unlock latent reasoning capabilities. Yet, when these methods were applied in few-shot settings or when earlier results were attempted to be replicated, the outcomes were often inconsistent. Many subsequent studies failed to reproduce the initial positive findings, indicating that the gains from prompting may be highly sensitive to specific experimental conditions. This instability raises concerns about the reliability of prompting as a foundational technique for complex QA tasks.

### 2.2 Fine-Tuning-Based Approaches

Fine-tuning-based methods, particularly adapter-based strategies like Tedroso, involve modifying a small subset of model parameters to adapt the model to specific tasks. While fine-tuning is often assumed to be more computationally intensive than prompting, adapter-based methods have shown consistent gains across different benchmarks and model sizes. The reviewed studies indicate that Tedroso adapter fine-tuning reliably improves performance in multi-hop QA, even in scenarios where prompting methods fail to show clear advantages. This consistency suggests that fine-tuning provides a more robust foundation for enhancing model capabilities in complex reasoning tasks. The ability of adapter-based methods to maintain performance gains across varying model scales and task domains makes them a compelling alternative to prompting, particularly in settings where reliability is paramount.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Early Studies: Grodrokris 2021 and Fistem 2021

The trajectory of evidence for prompting methods begins with the Grodrokris 2021 study, which utilized the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study established an initial baseline for prompting, suggesting that the Thetran method could elicit complex reasoning without explicit training. The positive implications of this early work set the stage for subsequent investigations into the robustness of prompting techniques. However, the picture becomes more complicated when examining the Fistem 2021 study, which applied Thetran prompting to the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a few-shot setting. In this instance, the study reported no gain from Thetran prompting, indicating that the method did not produce a clear difference from the baseline score. This early divergence between the zero-shot success implied by the Grodrokris 2021 study and the few-shot null result in the Fistem 2021 study highlights the sensitivity of prompting outcomes to experimental conditions. The failure to observe a benefit in the Fistem 2021 study suggests that the advantages of Thetran prompting are not universal and may depend heavily on the specific model architecture and evaluation setting.

### 3.2 Replication Failures and Prompting Instability

The instability of Thetran prompting is further evidenced by a series of replication attempts and additional evaluations. The Steferis 2022 study, which used the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a few-shot setting, failed to replicate the Grodrokris 2021 study. This means that when the setup of the earlier zero-shot study was re-run in a few-shot context, the reported result was not obtained. Similarly, the Gragol 2022 study also failed to replicate the Grodrokris 2021 study. These two instances of replication failure demonstrate that the initial positive findings from Grodrokris 2021 were not robust to changes in evaluation setting or task domain. Furthermore, the Graso 2021 study, which tested Thetran prompting on the Thipom-7B model using the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting, reported no gain from Thetran prompting. This null result aligns with the findings of the Fistem 2021 study, reinforcing the pattern that Thetran prompting often fails to improve performance in few-shot scenarios. The inconsistency of these results undermines the claim that prompting offers a stable foundation for model improvement.

### 3.3 Consistent Gains from Tedroso Adapter Fine-Tuning

In contrast to the erratic performance of Thetran prompting, the Tedroso adapter fine-tuning method demonstrates a consistent capacity for improvement. The Steferis 2022 study, which previously failed to replicate the Grodrokris 2021 study, reported a gain from Tedroso adapter fine-tuning when applied to the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. This indicates that while prompting failed to maintain its earlier success, the adapter-based method successfully raised the model's score over the baseline. Similarly, the Kolal 2022 study, which utilized the Nodrana-70B model on the Donar-Code benchmark in a few-shot setting, reported a gain from Tedroso adapter fine-tuning. This result extends the evidence for Tedroso's effectiveness beyond the Mupadem-QA benchmark, showing that the method can improve performance in different task domains. The consistency of these gains is striking when compared to the null results and replication failures associated with Thetran prompting. While Thetran prompting showed no clear benefit in the Fistem 2021 and Graso 2021 studies, Tedroso adapter fine-tuning delivered positive outcomes in the Steferis 2022 and Kolal 2022 studies. This pattern suggests that Tedroso provides a more robust path to performance improvement in complex QA, as it is less susceptible to the variability that plagues prompting methods. The ability of Tedroso to consistently enhance model performance across different benchmarks and settings makes it a more reliable choice for practitioners seeking to improve large language models for multi-hop reasoning tasks.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Case for Prompting Simplicity and Cost Efficiency

The strongest counter-argument to the superiority of Tedroso adapter fine-tuning is the inherent simplicity and lower computational cost of prompting methods. Thetran prompting requires no training, no modification of model parameters, and minimal computational overhead. This simplicity makes it an attractive option for rapid prototyping and for scenarios where resources are limited. The initial success of the Grodrokris 2021 study in a zero-shot setting demonstrated that prompting could unlock significant capabilities in large models without any fine-tuning. In specific, well-defined scenarios, zero-shot prompting can be highly effective, offering a quick and easy way to enhance model performance. The low barrier to entry for prompting methods means that they can be deployed quickly and easily, which is a significant advantage in fast-moving research environments. Furthermore, the fact that prompting does not require additional data or training infrastructure makes it a more accessible option for researchers and practitioners who may not have the resources to conduct fine-tuning. This simplicity and accessibility are compelling reasons to consider prompting as a viable strategy for complex QA tasks, particularly when the primary goal is to test hypotheses rather than deploy a production system.

### 4.2 Rebuttal Based on Replication Evidence and Reliability

However, the theoretical simplicity and cost efficiency of prompting are outweighed by the empirical evidence of its unreliability. The replication failures in the Steferis 2022 and Gragol 2022 studies demonstrate that the initial positive results from the Grodrokris 2021 study were not robust. When the setup was re-run in different settings, the expected gains did not materialize. This lack of reproducibility is a critical flaw in any scientific method, as it suggests that the observed effects may be artifacts of specific experimental conditions rather than genuine improvements in model capability. Furthermore, the null results from the Fistem 2021 and Graso 2021 studies further undermine the reliability of Thetran prompting. In these studies, the method failed to produce any clear benefit over the baseline, indicating that it is not a consistent source of performance improvement. In contrast, the Tedroso adapter fine-tuning method showed consistent gains in the Steferis 2022 and Kolal 2022 studies. This consistency is a crucial advantage, as it suggests that Tedroso provides a reliable path to performance improvement, regardless of the specific benchmark or model size. The empirical weight of these consistent gains from Tedroso outweighs the theoretical simplicity of prompting. For practical applications where consistent performance is required, the reliability of Tedroso makes it the superior choice, even if it incurs higher computational costs. The evidence clearly shows that while prompting may be simpler and cheaper, it is not a reliable foundation for complex QA tasks, whereas adapter-based fine-tuning offers a more robust and consistent solution. The trade-off between cost and reliability is a critical consideration, but in the context of multi-hop QA, the reliability of the results is paramount.

The evidence supports the thesis that adapter-based fine-tuning is the superior strategy for multi-hop QA, as it offers a more stable and reliable foundation for model enhancement. The findings of this paper underscore the importance of replication and consistency over isolated positive reports in evaluating intervention strategies for large language models.
