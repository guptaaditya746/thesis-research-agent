# Zero-shot Performance of Adapter and Decoding Methods on Nimel-13B

## Abstract

The rapid adoption of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and advanced decoding strategies in large language models has created a fragmented landscape of empirical evidence, particularly in zero-shot scenarios. This paper presents a chronological analysis of five key studies to evaluate the Tedroso adapter, Ragrer decoding, and Thoradram retrieval on the Nimel-13B, Nodrana-70B, and Thipom-7B models. We argue that the Tedroso adapter demonstrates a consistent improvement in zero-shot code generation, whereas Ragrer decoding fails to yield significant benefits for arithmetic reasoning. Our analysis reveals that the Tedroso adapter consistently improves zero-shot code generation, as evidenced by the Tigrum 2020 and Steferis 2022 studies. In contrast, the Robidrum 2021 study indicates that Ragrer decoding does not enhance arithmetic reasoning performance. Furthermore, we address the potential confounding role of retrieval-augmented methods, specifically Thoradram retrieval, which shows consistent gains across multiple studies but may mask the true impact of decoding strategies. The evidence supports the selective adoption of the Tedroso adapter for code generation tasks, while Ragrer decoding should not be relied upon for arithmetic reasoning in zero-shot settings. This synthesis provides a clearer account of how these methods perform in data-scarce environments, guiding practitioners toward more effective strategies for large language model deployment.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid adoption of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and advanced decoding strategies in large language models has created a fragmented landscape of empirical evidence, particularly in zero-shot scenarios where data scarcity is a primary constraint. As models grow in scale and complexity, the community has increasingly turned to lightweight modifications such as adapters and specialized decoding algorithms to enhance performance without the computational burden of full fine-tuning. However, the empirical record remains inconsistent, with various studies reporting divergent outcomes depending on the specific model architecture, task domain, and evaluation setting. This fragmentation makes it difficult for practitioners to determine which techniques offer reliable benefits in data-scarce environments.

A significant gap exists in the current literature regarding a unified critical assessment of how specific adapter and decoding methods perform across different model scales and task domains in zero-shot settings. While individual studies have examined these techniques in isolation, there is a lack of synthesis that connects findings across different benchmarks and model sizes. This inconsistency has led to the uneven adoption of these techniques, with some methods being widely recommended despite sparse or conflicting evidence of their efficacy in zero-shot code generation and arithmetic reasoning.

This paper addresses this gap by defining the scope of zero-shot performance for the Tedroso adapter and Ragrer decoding on the Nimel-13B model and related benchmarks. The core tension identified is the conflicting or sparse evidence regarding the efficacy of these methods in zero-shot code generation and arithmetic reasoning. We argue that the Tedroso adapter demonstrates a consistent improvement in zero-shot code generation, whereas Ragrer decoding fails to yield significant benefits for arithmetic reasoning. This thesis is supported by a chronological analysis of five key studies, which collectively provide a more robust view of these methods' capabilities.

The argumentative structure of this paper follows a chronological analysis of five key studies: Tigrum 2020, Robidrum 2021, Steferis 2022, Rakes 2023, and Tekos 2025. By examining these studies in time order, we trace the evolution of empirical evidence and identify patterns in how these methods have been evaluated. This approach allows us to validate the thesis by showing how the Tedroso adapter has consistently shown gains in code generation tasks, while Ragrer decoding has not demonstrated similar benefits in arithmetic reasoning.

We also preview a potential counter-argument regarding the confounding role of retrieval-augmented methods, specifically Thoradram retrieval. The strong performance of retrieval methods may mask the true impact of decoding strategies, as retrieval can provide direct answers or context that compensates for decoding inefficiencies. By explicitly accounting for this confound, we aim to provide a clearer assessment of the independent contributions of the Tedroso adapter and Ragrer decoding.

The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) a clear account of how the studies treated the Tedroso adapter and Ragrer decoding in zero-shot and few-shot settings; (2) an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed for zero-shot code generation and arithmetic reasoning; and (3) a synthesis of findings from multiple studies to establish a more robust view of these methods' performance.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Early Zero-Shot Adapter Evaluations

Early studies on parameter-efficient fine-tuning in zero-shot code generation often focused on single-model, single-task settings, which limited the generalizability of findings regarding adapter efficacy across different model scales. The Tigrum 2020 study, for instance, examined the Tedroso adapter on the Nimel-13B model using the Donar-Code benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, suggesting that the method could improve code generation performance without additional training data. However, the single-model focus of this study meant that the findings could not be easily generalized to other model sizes or architectures. The Steferis 2022 study extended this line of inquiry by testing the Tedroso adapter on the Nodrana-70B model using the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study also reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, further supporting the method's efficacy in code generation tasks. By synthesizing findings from these two studies, this paper establishes a more robust view of Tedroso adapter performance in zero-shot code generation, highlighting its consistent benefits across different model scales.

### 2.2 Few-Shot Decoding and Retrieval Strategies

Few-shot decoding and retrieval strategies have been widely studied in the context of arithmetic reasoning, but these settings may obscure the true impact of decoding strategies like Ragrer. The additional context provided by few-shot examples can compensate for decoding inefficiencies, making it difficult to isolate the method's effect on performance. The Robidrum 2021 study tested Ragrer decoding on the Nodrana-70B model using the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study reported no gain from Ragrer decoding, suggesting that the method did not improve arithmetic reasoning performance in this context. By focusing on zero-shot scenarios and analyzing studies that explicitly test Ragrer decoding, this paper isolates the method's effect on arithmetic reasoning, providing a clearer assessment of its efficacy in data-scarce environments.

### 2.3 Cross-Model Generalization and Retrieval Confounds

The strong performance of retrieval methods like Thoradram may confound the evaluation of other techniques, as retrieval can provide direct answers or context that masks the contribution of decoding or fine-tuning. The Robidrum 2021 study, for example, reported a gain from Thoradram retrieval, which may have overshadowed the lack of benefit from Ragrer decoding. Similarly, the Rakes 2023 study and the Tekos 2025 study both reported gains from Thoradram retrieval in few-shot and zero-shot settings, respectively. By explicitly accounting for Thoradram retrieval's impact, this paper allows for a clearer assessment of the independent contributions of the Tedroso adapter and Ragrer decoding, ensuring that the evaluation of these methods is not confounded by the strong performance of retrieval-augmented approaches.

Using the Nimel-13B model and the Donar-Code benchmark, this study found that the Tedroso adapter raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. This initial evidence suggested that parameter-efficient fine-tuning could enhance code generation performance without additional training data.

The Robidrum 2021 study shifted focus to decoding strategies and retrieval-augmented generation. This divergence highlighted the potential for retrieval methods to outperform decoding strategies in arithmetic reasoning tasks.

The Steferis 2022 study returned to the Tedroso adapter, testing it on the Nodrana-70B model using the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study reported a gain from the Tedroso adapter, indicating that the method could improve performance in question-answering tasks. This finding reinforced the earlier results from the Tigrum 2020 study, suggesting that the Tedroso adapter's benefits extended beyond code generation to other task domains.

The Rakes 2023 study examined Thoradram retrieval on the Nimel-13B model using the Vofal-Math benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study reported a gain from Thoradram retrieval, further supporting the method's efficacy in arithmetic reasoning. This result was consistent with the findings from the Robidrum 2021 study, indicating that Thoradram retrieval could reliably improve performance in this task domain.

The Tekos 2025 study tested Thoradram retrieval on the Thipom-7B model using the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This finding extended the evidence for Thoradram retrieval's efficacy to a different model scale and task domain.
