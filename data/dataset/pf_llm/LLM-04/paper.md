# Conflicting Evidence on Retrieval-Augmented Decoding: A Methodological Analysis

## Abstract

The integration of retrieval and decoding strategies into large language models is a central focus of current research, yet the empirical evidence regarding their efficacy is inconsistent. This paper presents a critical analysis of five 2025 studies—Tekos, Rokrar, Vomobral, Mopel, and Grugofel—that evaluate the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code benchmarks. A direct conflict exists in the literature regarding the impact of the Thoradram retrieval method: the Tekos 2025 study reported a gain in a zero-shot setting, while the Mopel 2025 study reported no gain in a few-shot setting for the same method on the same benchmark. We argue that the stability of method impacts is compromised by evaluation setting differences. Specifically, we contend that zero-shot evidence provides a more reliable basis for assessing the intrinsic efficacy of these methods, as zero-shot settings isolate the method's contribution from the confounding variables of in-context learning. Our analysis reveals that while retrieval results are inconsistent across settings, decoding results for Ragrer decoding show a consistent negative impact in zero-shot evaluations. We conclude that the conflict in retrieval findings arises from setting differences, and that the zero-shot line of empirical evidence offers a clearer lens for evaluating the fundamental contribution of these methods.

## 1. Introduction

The integration of retrieval and decoding strategies into large language models is a central focus of current research, yet the empirical evidence regarding their efficacy is inconsistent. While these methods are widely adopted to enhance model performance, the stability of their impact across different evaluation settings remains a subject of debate. This paper examines the reliability of findings in this domain by analyzing a specific set of recent studies that evaluate the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code benchmarks.

A direct conflict exists in the literature regarding the impact of retrieval methods, specifically concerning the Thoradram retrieval technique. One study reports a gain from this method on the Mupadem-QA benchmark, while another reports no gain for the same method on the same benchmark. This discrepancy undermines the reliability of these findings and raises questions about the conditions under which such methods are effective. The core issue is not merely the presence or absence of a gain, but the inconsistency in how these gains are reported across different experimental setups.

The scope of this analysis is limited to five specific 2025 studies: Tekos, Rokrar, Vomobral, Mopel, and Grugofel. These studies evaluate the Thipom-7B model on the aforementioned benchmarks, using either zero-shot or few-shot evaluation settings. The methods under investigation are Thoradram retrieval and Ragrer decoding. By focusing on these specific studies, we can isolate the variables that contribute to the conflicting results and assess the validity of the reported findings.

The thesis of this paper is that the stability of retrieval and decoding method impacts is compromised by evaluation setting differences. We argue that the evidence from zero-shot settings should be prioritized over few-shot settings for assessing the intrinsic efficacy of these methods. Zero-shot settings provide a cleaner measure of the method's contribution, free from the confounding factors introduced by in-context learning. In contrast, few-shot settings can mask or amplify the effect of the method, leading to inconsistent results that do not reflect the method's true potential.

The argument structure of this paper follows a chronological analysis of the studies, examining the specific treatment of sources and defending the thesis against counter-arguments. We begin by analyzing the studies in time order, detailing what each did with each source. We then present the strongest counter-argument to our thesis and answer it from the findings. This approach allows us to provide a clear account of how the studies treated these sources and to argue for a more reliable line of empirical evidence.

The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) a clear account of how the five 2025 studies treated the Thoradram retrieval and Ragrer decoding methods; (2) an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed, specifically prioritizing zero-shot results over few-shot results for intrinsic efficacy; and (3) a defense of the thesis that evaluation setting differences compromise the stability of method impacts.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Zero-shot Evaluation Approaches

Zero-shot evaluation approaches assess the impact of retrieval and decoding methods without providing the model with in-context examples. This setting is used to measure the intrinsic contribution of the method, free from the confounding factors introduced by prompt engineering. Studies such as Tekos 2025, Rokrar 2025, and Vomobral 2025 employ this approach to evaluate the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA, Vofal-Math, and Donar-Code benchmarks, respectively. The advantage of zero-shot settings is that they provide a cleaner measure of the method's effect, as they isolate the method's contribution from the model's few-shot capabilities. However, a limitation of this approach is that it may not capture the full potential of methods that rely on in-context learning. Despite this, zero-shot results are more reliable for assessing the fundamental impact of retrieval and decoding methods, as they are not influenced by the model's ability to leverage in-context examples.

### 2.2 Few-shot Evaluation Approaches

Few-shot evaluation approaches assess the impact of retrieval and decoding methods by providing the model with a small number of in-context examples. This setting is used to evaluate the method's performance in a more realistic scenario, where the model is expected to leverage in-context learning. Studies such as Mopel 2025 and Grugofel 2025 employ this approach to evaluate the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA and Donar-Code benchmarks, respectively. The advantage of few-shot settings is that they provide a more realistic measure of the method's performance, as they account for the model's ability to leverage in-context examples. However, a limitation of this approach is that it introduces confounding variables, as the model's ability to leverage in-context examples can mask or amplify the effect of the retrieval/decoding method. This can lead to inconsistent results that do not reflect the method's true potential. Therefore, few-shot results are less reliable for assessing the intrinsic efficacy of methods, as they are influenced by the model's few-shot learning capabilities, which can vary across benchmarks and settings.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of the Studies

To understand the source of the conflicting evidence, we analyze the five 2025 studies in chronological order, examining how each treated the Thoradram retrieval and Ragrer decoding methods. The first study, the Tekos 2025 study, evaluated the Thipom-7B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. The second study, the Rokrar 2025 study, focused on the Vofal-Math benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study evaluated the Ragrer decoding method and reported that it lowered the model's score on the Vofal-Math benchmark below the baseline. The Rokrar 2025 study did not evaluate the Thoradram retrieval method.

The third study, the Vomobral 2025 study, examined the Donar-Code benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study found that the Thoradram retrieval method raised the model's score on the Donar-Code benchmark over the baseline. The Vomobral 2025 study did not evaluate the Ragrer decoding method.

The fourth study, the Mopel 2025 study, returned to the Mupadem-QA benchmark but employed a few-shot setting. This study evaluated the Thoradram retrieval method and reported no clear difference from the baseline score on the Mupadem-QA benchmark for the method.

The fifth study, the Grugofel 2025 study, examined the Donar-Code benchmark in a few-shot setting. This study evaluated the Thoradram retrieval method and reported no clear difference from the baseline score on the Donar-Code benchmark for the method. The Grugofel 2025 study did not evaluate the Ragrer decoding method.

### 3.2 The Core Conflict: Thoradram Retrieval on Mupadem-QA

The most significant conflict in the literature arises from the differing results reported for the Thoradram retrieval method on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. The Tekos 2025 study, conducted in a zero-shot setting, reported that the Thoradram retrieval method raised the model's score on the Mupadem-QA benchmark over the baseline. In contrast, the Mopel 2025 study, conducted in a few-shot setting, reported no clear difference from the baseline score on the Mupadem-QA benchmark for the same method. This direct conflict—where one study reports a gain and another reports no gain for the same method on the same benchmark—undermines the reliability of the findings and raises questions about the conditions under which the method is effective.

The difference in evaluation settings is the most plausible explanation for this conflict. In the zero-shot setting, the model's performance is driven solely by the method's intrinsic contribution, as there are no in-context examples to influence the model's behavior. In the few-shot setting, the model's ability to leverage in-context examples can mask or amplify the effect of the retrieval method. If the model is already performing well on the Mupadem-QA benchmark due to its few-shot capabilities, the additional benefit provided by the Thoradram retrieval method may be negligible, leading to a report of no gain. Conversely, in the zero-shot setting, the method's contribution is more apparent, as it is the primary factor influencing the model's performance.

We argue that the zero-shot result from the Tekos 2025 study is more reliable for assessing the intrinsic efficacy of the Thoradram retrieval method. The zero-shot setting isolates the method's effect from the model's few-shot capabilities, providing a cleaner measure of the method's true potential. The few-shot result from the Mopel 2025 study, while valid in its own right, is confounded by the model's ability to leverage in-context examples, making it less suitable for assessing the method's intrinsic contribution.

### 3.3 Consistency in Decoding Method Impacts

In contrast to the conflicting results for the Thoradram retrieval method, the results for the Ragrer decoding method show a consistent pattern. Both the Tekos 2025 study (Mupadem-QA, zero-shot) and the Rokrar 2025 study (Vofal-Math, zero-shot) reported that the Ragrer decoding method lowered the model's score on their respective benchmarks below the baseline. This consistency across different benchmarks and zero-shot settings suggests that the negative impact of the Ragrer decoding method is a more reliable finding compared to the conflicting retrieval results.

The consistent negative impact of the Ragrer decoding method in zero-shot settings indicates that the method may have inherent limitations that are not dependent on the specific benchmark or the model's few-shot capabilities. This finding supports our thesis that zero-shot settings provide a more reliable basis for assessing method efficacy, as they reveal the method's intrinsic contribution without the confounding factors introduced by in-context learning. The fact that the Ragrer decoding method consistently lowers performance in zero-shot settings suggests that it may not be a suitable method for enhancing model performance in this context.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 The Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest counter-argument to our thesis is that few-shot settings are more representative of real-world usage, and therefore the Mopel 2025 result (no gain from Thoradram retrieval) should be prioritized over the Tekos 2025 result (gain from Thoradram retrieval). This argument holds that in practical applications, large language models are often used in few-shot settings, where they are provided with in-context examples to guide their behavior. Therefore, the performance of a method in a few-shot setting is more relevant for assessing its practical utility. From this perspective, the Mopel 2025 study's finding that the Thoradram retrieval method provided no gain in a few-shot setting is more indicative of the method's real-world effectiveness than the Tekos 2025 study's finding of a gain in a zero-shot setting.

We acknowledge the validity of this counter-argument. Few-shot settings do reflect a common evaluation paradigm and may be more relevant for practical applications. If the goal is to assess the method's performance in a realistic deployment scenario, then few-shot results are indeed more appropriate. However, this paper is concerned with assessing the intrinsic efficacy of the method, not its practical utility in a specific deployment scenario. The intrinsic efficacy of a method is best measured in a setting that isolates the method's contribution from other factors, such as the model's few-shot capabilities.

### 4.2 Rebuttal from the Findings

We rebut the counter-argument by pointing to the confounding variables introduced by the few-shot setting. The few-shot setting introduces confounding variables, as the model's ability to leverage in-context examples can mask or amplify the effect of the retrieval method. This means that the few-shot result is not a pure measure of the method's intrinsic contribution, but rather a measure of the method's contribution in combination with the model's few-shot capabilities. If the model is already performing well on the benchmark due to its few-shot capabilities, the additional benefit provided by the retrieval method may be negligible, leading to a report of no gain. This does not necessarily mean that the method is ineffective, but rather that its effect is masked by the model's few-shot capabilities.

The findings from the Grugofel 2025 study support this rebuttal. The Grugofel 2025 study, conducted in a few-shot setting on the Donar-Code benchmark, also reported no clear difference from the baseline score for the Thoradram retrieval method. This suggests that the few-shot setting may systematically understate the method's impact, as the model's few-shot capabilities may be masking the method's contribution. In contrast, the zero-shot studies (Tekos 2025 and Vomobral 2025) reported gains from the Thoradram retrieval method, indicating that the method does have an intrinsic contribution that is visible when the model's few-shot capabilities are not a factor.

We conclude that the zero-shot evidence provides a cleaner measure of the method's intrinsic contribution and should be prioritized for assessing method efficacy. The few-shot evidence, while relevant for practical applications, is confounded by the model's few-shot capabilities and is therefore less suitable for assessing the method's intrinsic efficacy. The conflict in the literature regarding the Thoradram retrieval method arises from the difference in evaluation settings, and the zero-shot evidence provides a more reliable basis for assessing the method's true potential.
