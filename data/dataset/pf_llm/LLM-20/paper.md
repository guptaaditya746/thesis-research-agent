# The Replication Crisis in Large Model Evaluation: A Case Study of Nodrana-70B

## Abstract

The reliability of empirical claims in large language model research is currently undermined by inconsistent replication outcomes across independent evaluation studies. This paper investigates a specific instance of this crisis involving the Nodrana-70B model, focusing on the failure of subsequent studies to replicate the initial findings of the Grodrokris 2021 study. The Grodrokris 2021 study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. Furthermore, the Stutohas 2023 study reported no gain from Thetran prompting on the Vofal-Math benchmark, contributing to a pattern of inconsistent results. By analyzing these studies in chronological order and examining how each treated the underlying sources, this paper argues that the weight of evidence does not support the generalizability of the initial positive findings. The analysis reveals that the initial gains were likely context-dependent or subject to uncontrolled variables rather than indicative of a robust improvement in model performance. This critical survey provides a clear account of the empirical landscape and advocates for a more cautious interpretation of prompting strategies, emphasizing the need for rigorous replication practices to ensure the reliability of future evaluations in the field.

## 1. Introduction

The reliability of empirical claims in large language model research is currently undermined by inconsistent replication outcomes across independent evaluation studies. While initial reports often suggest significant performance improvements from specific prompting strategies, subsequent investigations frequently fail to reproduce these results, creating ambiguity about the true efficacy of such methods. This discrepancy poses a significant challenge for the field, as it complicates the development of robust evaluation protocols and the adoption of new techniques in practical applications.

This paper focuses on a specific instance of this replication crisis involving the Nodrana-70B model. The core issue is the failure of subsequent studies to replicate the initial findings of the Grodrokris 2021 study, which reported a gain from Kabaso prompting on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. The scope of this analysis is limited to four specific studies: Grodrokris 2021, Brastom 2021, Steferis 2022, Stutohas 2023, Mitra 2023, and Rokrar 2025. These studies examine two prompting methods, Kabaso and Thetran, across two benchmarks, Mupadem-QA and Vofal-Math. By narrowing the focus to these specific interactions, we can isolate the variables contributing to the inconsistent outcomes.

The central thesis of this paper is that the findings of the Grodrokris 2021 study were not replicated by the Steferis 2022 study. Furthermore, the Stutohas 2023 study reported no gain from Thetran prompting on the Vofal-Math benchmark, adding to the pattern of inconsistent results. This lack of replication suggests that the initial positive findings may be context-dependent or subject to uncontrolled variables rather than indicative of a generalizable improvement in model performance.

To support this thesis, the paper will analyze the studies in chronological order, examining how each treated the sources and methods involved. We will first review the initial exploratory evaluations, followed by the subsequent replication attempts. This structure allows us to trace the evolution of the evidence and identify where discrepancies arise. By comparing the methodologies and results of these studies, we aim to provide a clear account of the empirical landscape and argue for a more cautious interpretation of the initial findings.

This study contributes a critical survey of the Nodrana-70B evaluation literature, offering a clear account of how the studies treated the sources and an argued view on which line of empirical evidence should be followed. By synthesizing the available data, we highlight the need for more rigorous replication practices in large language model research to ensure the reliability of empirical claims.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Initial Exploratory Evaluations

Prior work in this domain can be clustered into initial exploratory evaluations, which typically involve single-study assessments of prompting strategies on specific model-benchmark pairs. These studies often report isolated gains without accounting for variability or providing robust replication evidence. For instance, the Grodrokris 2021 study assessed Kabaso prompting on the Nodrana-70B model using the Mupadem-QA benchmark in a zero-shot setting. Such initial evaluations are crucial for identifying potential improvements but can lead to potentially misleading conclusions about method efficacy if not supported by subsequent verification. This paper addresses this limitation by conducting a critical survey that compares multiple independent studies to identify consistent patterns and discrepancies in reported results.

### 2.2 Subsequent Replication Attempts

The second cluster of prior work consists of subsequent replication attempts, which are independent re-runs or related evaluations aimed at verifying initial findings. These studies are essential for establishing the robustness of the initial claims. However, replication attempts have yielded mixed results, with some studies failing to reproduce initial gains. This suggests that the original findings may be context-dependent or subject to uncontrolled variables. This paper synthesizes these replication attempts to argue that the weight of evidence does not support the generalizability of the initial positive findings, emphasizing the need for more rigorous evaluation protocols in future research.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Studies

The analysis begins with the Grodrokris 2021 study, which established the initial baseline for this investigation. In a zero-shot setting, this study evaluated the Nodrana-70B model on the Mupadem-QA benchmark. The results indicated that the model's score was raised over the baseline when using Kabaso prompting, suggesting a potential efficacy for this method. In contrast, the same study found no clear difference from the baseline score when applying Thetran prompting to the same model and benchmark. This initial divergence between the two prompting methods set the stage for subsequent investigations.

Following the initial report, the Brastom 2021 study attempted to verify these findings. However, the Brastom 2021 study failed to replicate the results of the Grodrokris 2021 study, indicating that the initial gains were not consistently observed in this independent re-run. Similarly, the Steferis 2022 study also failed to replicate the findings of the Grodrokris 2021 study. The consistent failure of both the Brastom 2021 and Steferis 2022 studies to reproduce the initial positive results strongly suggests that the original findings were not robust or generalizable.

The investigation then shifted to the Vofal-Math benchmark. The Stutohas 2023 study evaluated the Nodrana-70B model in a zero-shot setting on this benchmark. The results showed that the model did not exhibit a clear difference from the baseline score when using Thetran prompting. Furthermore, the Stutohas 2023 study also reported no gain from Kabaso prompting on the Vofal-Math benchmark, indicating that neither prompting method yielded a significant improvement in this context.

Returning to the Mupadem-QA benchmark, the Mitra 2023 study provided additional data points. In a zero-shot setting with the Nodrana-70B model, the Mitra 2023 study reported no gain from Thetran prompting. This finding aligns with the earlier results from the Grodrokris 2021 and Stutohas 2023 studies, reinforcing the observation that Thetran prompting does not consistently improve performance on this model.

Finally, the Rokrar 2025 study introduced a different model, the Thipom-7B, into the analysis. In a zero-shot setting on the Vofal-Math benchmark, the Rokrar 2025 study reported a gain from Kabaso prompting. This result suggests that Kabaso prompting may be effective under different conditions, specifically when applied to the Thipom-7B model rather than the Nodrana-70B model.

### 3.2 Synthesis of Findings

Synthesizing these findings reveals a clear pattern that supports the central thesis. The initial positive findings of the Grodrokris 2021 study were not replicated by the Steferis 2022 and Brastom 2021 studies, which is a critical indicator of the lack of robustness in the original results. Additionally, the Stutohas 2023 study reported no gain from Thetran prompting on the Vofal-Math benchmark, further contributing to the evidence against the generalizability of the initial claims. The consistent failure to replicate the initial gains, combined with the lack of improvement from Thetran prompting in subsequent studies, suggests that the initial findings were likely context-dependent or subject to uncontrolled variables. The mixed results across different models and benchmarks highlight the complexity of evaluating prompting strategies and underscore the need for careful consideration of model and benchmark specificity.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis is provided by the Rokrar 2025 study, which reported a gain from Kabaso prompting on the Vofal-Math benchmark with the Thipom-7B model. This finding suggests that Kabaso prompting may still be effective under different conditions, potentially challenging the conclusion that the initial findings were not generalizable. However, this counter-argument is limited by the fact that the Rokrar 2025 study used a different model (Thipom-7B) and benchmark (Vofal-Math) than the Grodrokris 2021 study. Therefore, its findings do not directly replicate the initial results, which were specific to the Nodrana-70B model and the Mupadem-QA benchmark. The failure of the Steferis 2022 and Brastom 2021 studies to replicate the Grodrokris 2021 study's findings on the same model and benchmark provides stronger evidence against the generalizability of the initial results. The Rokrar 2025 study's findings, while interesting, do not undermine the core argument that the initial positive findings were not robust or generalizable across the specific conditions tested in the original study.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has analyzed the replication crisis in large language model evaluation through a case study of the Nodrana-70B model. The main findings indicate that the Grodrokris 2021 study's findings were not replicated by the Steferis 2022 and Brastom 2021 studies, and the Stutohas 2023 study reported no gain from Thetran prompting on the Vofal-Math benchmark. These results support the thesis that the initial positive findings were not generalizable and were likely context-dependent or subject to uncontrolled variables. The line of empirical evidence should follow the studies that failed to replicate the initial findings, as they provide more consistent and reliable data. The implications for future research are significant, highlighting the need for more robust replication studies and careful consideration of model and benchmark specificity in evaluating prompting strategies. By adopting a more cautious interpretation of initial findings and emphasizing rigorous replication practices, the field can improve the reliability of empirical claims and advance the development of effective prompting strategies.
