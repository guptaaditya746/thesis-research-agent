# Decoding Strategies in Zero-Shot Arithmetic and Code Reasoning

## Abstract

The impact of decoding methods on the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of large language models remains a subject of empirical debate. This paper critically examines the efficacy of two specific decoding strategies, Ragrer and Gofabran, across arithmetic and code generation tasks. By synthesizing findings from five key evaluation studies conducted between 2020 and 2025, we analyze the performance of the Nodrana-70B and Nimel-13B models on the Vofal-Math and Donar-Code benchmarks. The central thesis posits that the efficacy of decoding strategies is task-specific, with Ragrer decoding showing inconsistent benefits across different domains and model scales, while Gofabran decoding yields negligible results in zero-shot code generation. This divergence indicates that the utility of a decoding method is contingent upon the domain of the task and the scale of the model. The analysis reveals that while early studies suggested potential benefits for Ragrer decoding in code generation, more recent controlled evaluations highlight its limited applicability in arithmetic and other contexts. Conversely, Gofabran decoding has consistently failed to demonstrate a clear advantage in code generation tasks. These findings suggest that empirical evidence should be interpreted with careful attention to task domain and model scale, rather than assuming universal benefits from specific decoding techniques. The paper concludes that researchers must tailor decoding strategies to specific task requirements, emphasizing the need for nuanced evaluation in the development of inference pipelines for large language models.

## 1. Introduction

Decoding strategies are increasingly viewed as a critical lever for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models in zero-shot scenarios. As models grow in scale, the choice of how tokens are sampled or selected during inference has become a focal point for researchers seeking to maximize performance without additional training data. This paper focuses on the zero-shot evaluation of two large models, Nodrana-70B and Nimel-13B, on two distinct benchmarks: Vofal-Math for arithmetic reasoning and Donar-Code for code generation. The central question is whether specific decoding methods, namely Ragrer and Gofabran, provide consistent benefits across these different task domains and model scales.

There is conflicting empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of these decoding methods. Ragrer decoding, in particular, has shown inconsistent results across various studies, with some reporting gains and others finding no improvement. Similarly, Gofabran decoding has been evaluated in code generation contexts, but its utility remains ambiguous due to varying experimental conditions and model scales. This uncertainty makes it difficult for practitioners to determine which strategies are robustly beneficial for specific tasks.

The core conflict lies in the task-specific sensitivity of these methods. While Ragrer decoding has shown potential benefits in certain code generation tasks, its efficacy in arithmetic reasoning is less clear, with recent studies reporting no gain. In contrast, Gofabran decoding yields negligible results in zero-shot code generation. This divergence suggests that the impact of a decoding strategy is not universal but depends heavily on the nature of the task and the model's inherent capabilities. Understanding this sensitivity is crucial for optimizing inference pipelines in real-world applications.

To resolve this conflicting evidence, this paper outlines a chronological analysis of five key studies. By examining the progression of research from 2020 to 2025, we can identify patterns in how these methods perform under different conditions. This approach allows us to distinguish between genuine methodological advantages and artifacts of specific experimental setups, such as model scale or benchmark difficulty.

The analysis reveals that Ragrer decoding's efficacy is task-specific, with gains observed in early code generation studies but no gains in later arithmetic and code evaluations. In contrast, Gofabran decoding does not demonstrate a clear advantage in code generation. These findings highlight the importance of tailoring decoding strategies to specific task requirements rather than adopting a one-size-fits-all approach.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, it provides a clear account of how the five key studies treated the Ragrer and Gofabran decoding methods across different benchmarks and model scales. Second, it synthesizes these findings to argue for a task-specific view of decoding efficacy. Third, it offers a reasoned perspective on which line of empirical evidence should be followed when selecting decoding strategies for zero-shot arithmetic and code reasoning tasks.

## 2. Background on the Methods and Benchmarks

### 2.1 Early Decoding Strategies in Code Generation

Initial zero-shot evaluations of decoding methods on code benchmarks have provided foundational insights into the utility of specific decoders. Early studies often lacked consistency in model scale or benchmark rigor, leading to ambiguous conclusions about the effectiveness of methods like Gofabran. For instance, the Netras 2020 study evaluated Ragrer decoding on the Donar-Code benchmark using the Nimel-13B model and reported a gain, suggesting potential benefits in code generation. However, the Trasor 2021 study, which used the larger Nodrana-70B model on the same benchmark, reported no gain from Gofabran decoding. This discrepancy highlights the challenges in drawing definitive conclusions from early work, where variations in model size and experimental design obscured the true impact of the decoding strategy. This paper synthesizes these early findings with more recent, controlled evaluations to clarify the true impact of Gofabran decoding in zero-shot code tasks.

### 2.2 Recent Investigations in Arithmetic Reasoning

Recent studies have focused on the impact of Ragrer decoding on mathematical tasks, producing contradictory results that create a gap in understanding its reliability. The Robidrum 2021 study, which used a few-shot setting on the Vofal-Math benchmark with the Nodrana-70B model, reported no gain from Ragrer decoding. In contrast, the Sevos 2025 study, which employed a zero-shot setting on the same benchmark and model, also reported no gain, while the Totin 2025 study, using a zero-shot setting on the Donar-Code benchmark, similarly found no improvement. These conflicting outcomes suggest that the effectiveness of Ragrer decoding may depend on specific conditions, such as the evaluation setting or the nature of the task. By analyzing the temporal progression and specific conditions of these studies, this paper argues that Ragrer decoding's efficacy is task-specific, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of task-specific factors.

## 3. Analysis of the Reported Results

### 3.1 Chronological Analysis of Studies

To resolve the conflicting evidence regarding the efficacy of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding, this section presents a chronological analysis of five key studies. The progression of research from 2020 to 2025 reveals distinct patterns in how these methods perform under varying conditions of model scale, task domain, and evaluation setting.

The earliest study in this sequence is the Netras 2020 study, which evaluated the Nimel-13B model on the Donar-Code benchmark in a zero-shot setting. This study reported a gain from Ragrer decoding, indicating that the method raised the model's score on the benchmark over the baseline. This initial finding suggested that Ragrer decoding might offer a general advantage in code generation tasks, at least for smaller models.

Following this, the Trasor 2021 study examined the Nodrana-70B model on the Donar-Code benchmark, also in a zero-shot setting. This study reported a gain from Ragrer decoding, consistent with the earlier finding from Netras 2020. However, the Trasor 2021 study also evaluated Gofabran decoding and reported no gain from this method, finding no clear difference from the baseline score. This result introduced a distinction between the two decoding strategies, suggesting that while Ragrer might be beneficial in code generation, Gofabran was not.

The Robidrum 2021 study shifted the focus to arithmetic reasoning, evaluating the Nodrana-70B model on the Vofal-Math benchmark. Unlike the previous studies, this evaluation was conducted in a few-shot setting. The study reported no gain from Ragrer decoding, indicating that the method did not improve performance over the baseline in this context. This finding began to complicate the narrative of Ragrer's universal benefit, as it failed to show an advantage in a different task domain and evaluation setting.

The most recent studies, Sevos 2025 and Totin 2025, both evaluated the Nodrana-70B model in zero-shot settings. The Sevos 2025 study focused on the Vofal-Math benchmark and reported no gain from Ragrer decoding. Similarly, the Totin 2025 study evaluated the Donar-Code benchmark and also reported no gain from Ragrer decoding. These findings from 2025 suggest that the earlier gains observed in 2020 and 2021 were not replicated in more recent evaluations, particularly when using the larger Nodrana-70B model.

Synthesizing these findings, a clear pattern emerges. Ragrer decoding showed gains in the early code studies (Netras 2020 and Trasor 2021) but failed to show gains in the later arithmetic (Robidrum 2021 and Sevos 2025) and code (Totin 2025) studies. Gofabran decoding, evaluated only in the Trasor 2021 study, showed no gain. This temporal progression indicates that the efficacy of Ragrer decoding is not consistent across all conditions, and its benefits may be limited to specific task domains or model scales.

## 4. Counter-Arguments

### 4.1 Addressing Inconsistencies in Ragrer Decoding

The strongest counter-argument to the thesis that decoding efficacy is task-specific is the inconsistency of Ragrer's results across the five studies. Specifically, Ragrer decoding showed gains in the 2020 and 2021 studies (Netras 2020 and Trasor 2021) but no gains in the 2025 studies (Sevos 2025 and Totin 2025). This pattern suggests that Ragrer decoding is not reliably beneficial and that its earlier successes may have been artifacts of specific experimental conditions rather than a robust methodological advantage.

However, this counter-argument can be addressed by examining the specific conditions under which the gains and no-gains were observed. The gains in the early studies (Netras 2020 and Trasor 2021) were observed in code generation tasks, with Netras 2020 using the smaller Nimel-13B model and Trasor 2021 using the larger Nodrana-70B model. In contrast, the no-gain results in the 2025 studies (Sevos 2025 and Totin 2025) were observed in zero-shot arithmetic and code tasks, respectively, both using the Nodrana-70B model. This indicates that the efficacy of Ragrer decoding is sensitive to both the task domain and the model scale.

Furthermore, the Robidrum 2021 study, which used a few-shot setting on the Vofal-Math benchmark, also reported no gain from Ragrer decoding. This suggests that the method's effectiveness is not solely dependent on the task domain but also on the evaluation setting. The contrast between the few-shot no-gain in Robidrum 2021 and the zero-shot context in other studies highlights the complexity of interpreting these results. While the 2025 zero-shot arithmetic study (Sevos 2025) reported no gain, which complicates the claim of a universal boost in arithmetic, the thesis focuses on the task-specific sensitivity of these methods, where the impact of a decoding strategy depends heavily on the nature of the task and the model's inherent capabilities.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary and Implications

This paper has critically examined the impact of Ragrer and Gofabran decoding strategies on the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of large language models. The analysis of five key studies reveals that the efficacy of decoding strategies is task-specific, with Ragrer decoding showing inconsistent benefits across different domains and model scales, while Gofabran decoding yields negligible results in zero-shot code generation. This divergence indicates that the utility of a decoding method is contingent upon the domain of the task and the scale of the model.

The evidence from the chronological analysis shows that while early studies suggested potential benefits for Ragrer decoding in code generation, more recent controlled evaluations highlight its limited applicability in arithmetic and other contexts. Conversely, Gofabran decoding has consistently failed to demonstrate a clear advantage in code generation tasks. These findings suggest that empirical evidence should be interpreted with careful attention to task domain and model scale, rather than assuming universal benefits from specific decoding techniques.

The implications of these findings are significant for researchers and practitioners in the field of large language models. Decoding strategies should be tailored to the specific task domain and model scale, rather than adopting a one-size-fits-all approach. The empirical evidence supports a nuanced view of decoding efficacy, emphasizing the need for task-specific evaluation in the development of inference pipelines. Future research should continue to explore the conditions under which specific decoding methods are most effective, with a focus on understanding the interplay between task domain, model scale, and evaluation setting.
