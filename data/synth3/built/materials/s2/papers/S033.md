# Holmquist et al. (2016): How Zerathane Process changes hardness

## Abstract

We compare Zerathane Process with Untreated alloy on hardness. Overall, Zerathane Process lowers hardness compared with Untreated alloy. Overall, Zerathane Process increases cracked samples compared with Untreated alloy.

## Setup

Untreated alloy is the alloy as cast, with no extra treatment. Zerathane Process is a slow heat cycle that relaxes stress inside the metal. The study was run at the Harbor Street Materials Group. It covers these subsets:

- Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory

## Results

| Subset | Arm | Measure | Count | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Untreated alloy | Cracked samples | 159 | 700 |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Zerathane Process | Cracked samples | 188 | 700 |

| Subset | Arm | Measure | Mean | SD | n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Untreated alloy | Hardness | 202 | 30 | 700 |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Zerathane Process | Hardness | 168 | 25 | 700 |

## Conclusion

Overall, Zerathane Process lowers hardness compared with Untreated alloy. Overall, Zerathane Process increases cracked samples compared with Untreated alloy. Further work should repeat the comparison in other settings.
