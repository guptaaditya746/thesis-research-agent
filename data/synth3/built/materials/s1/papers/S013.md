# Nygaard et al. (2023): A comparison of Zerathane Process and Untreated alloy on hardness

## Abstract

We report a controlled comparison of Zerathane Process and Untreated alloy. Overall, Zerathane Process and Untreated alloy reach about the same hardness. Overall, Zerathane Process reduces cracked samples compared with Untreated alloy.

## Setup

Untreated alloy is the alloy as cast, with no extra treatment. Zerathane Process is a slow heat cycle that relaxes stress inside the metal. The study was run at the Oakfield Metallurgy Unit. It covers these subsets:

- Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory

## Results

| Subset | Arm | Measure | Count | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Untreated alloy | Cracked samples | 122 | 400 |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Zerathane Process | Cracked samples | 109 | 400 |

| Subset | Arm | Measure | Mean | SD | n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Untreated alloy | Hardness | 245 | 37 | 400 |
| Raised temperature, Thick samples, University laboratory | Zerathane Process | Hardness | 246 | 37 | 400 |

## Conclusion

Overall, Zerathane Process and Untreated alloy reach about the same hardness. Overall, Zerathane Process reduces cracked samples compared with Untreated alloy. Further work should repeat the comparison in other settings.
