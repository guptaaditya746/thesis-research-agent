# Aldridge et al. (2024): Measuring cracked samples under Zerathane Process

## Abstract

We compare Zerathane Process with Untreated alloy on cracked samples. Overall, Zerathane Process increases cracked samples compared with Untreated alloy. Overall, Zerathane Process lowers hardness compared with Untreated alloy.

## Setup

Untreated alloy is the alloy as cast, with no extra treatment. Zerathane Process is a slow heat cycle that relaxes stress inside the metal. The study was run at the Willow Lane Materials Centre. It covers these subsets:

- Room temperature, Thin samples, University laboratory

## Results

| Subset | Arm | Measure | Count | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Room temperature, Thin samples, University laboratory | Untreated alloy | Cracked samples | 169 | 600 |
| Room temperature, Thin samples, University laboratory | Zerathane Process | Cracked samples | 216 | 600 |

| Subset | Arm | Measure | Mean | SD | n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Room temperature, Thin samples, University laboratory | Untreated alloy | Hardness | 290 | 44 | 600 |
| Room temperature, Thin samples, University laboratory | Zerathane Process | Hardness | 244 | 37 | 600 |

## Conclusion

Overall, Zerathane Process increases cracked samples compared with Untreated alloy. Overall, Zerathane Process lowers hardness compared with Untreated alloy. All numbers are reported so that others can add them to their own.
