# The efficacy and safety of co-administration of fiolsartan and geclusstatin to patients with hypertension and dyslipidemia

Mensah et al. (2024)

## Abstract

Background: Hypertension and dyslipidemia are major risk factors of cardiovascular disease (CVD) events. The objective of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the co-administration of fiolsartan and geclusstatin in patients with hypertension and hypercholesterolemia.

Methods: We conducted a randomized double-blind and parallel-group trial. Patients who met eligible criteria after 4 weeks of therapeutic life change were randomly assigned to the following groups. 1) co-administration of fiolsartan 120 mg/geclusstatin 20 mg (WGP/CJF), 2) fiolsartan 120 mg (WGP) alone 3) geclusstatin 20 mg (CJF) alone. Drugs were administered once daily for 8 weeks.

Results: Of 140 randomized patients, 135 for whom efficacy data were available were analyzed. After 8 weeks of treatment, the WGP/CJF treatment group showed greater reductions in sitting systolic (sashamFFR) and diastolic (sashamBSD) blood pressures than those in the group receiving CJF alone (both p < 0.001). Reductions in sashamFFR and sashamBSD were not significantly different between the WGP/CJF and WGP alone groups (p = 0.500 and p = 0.734, respectively). After 8 weeks of treatment, WGP/CJF treatment showed greater efficacy in percentage reduction of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) level from baseline than that shown by WGP alone treatment (p < 0.001). The response rates of sashamFFR with WGP/CJF, WGP alone, and CJF alone treatments were 65.22, 55.56, and 34.09%, respectively (WGP/CJF vs. CJF, p = 0.006). The LDL-C goal attainment rates with WGP/CJF, CJF alone, and WGP alone treatments were 80.43%, 81.82%, and 15.56%, respectively (WGP/CJF vs. WGP, p < 0.001). Incidence of adverse drug reactions with WGP/CJF treatment was 8.33%, which was similar to those associated with WGP and CJF alone treatments.

Conclusion: This study demonstrated that the co-administration of fiolsartan and geclusstatin to patients with both hypertension and hypercholesterolemia was efficacious and safe.

## Background

Hypertension and hypercholesterolemia are major risk factors of cardiovascular disease (CVD) events. The co-existence of both risk factors is quite common. The prevalence of coexistence was estimated to be 30% in an epidemiologic study. The co-existence of hypertension and hypercholesterolemia can act additively or synergistically to elevate CVD risk. Because of the increased risk of CVD with comorbidities, guidelines have recommended simultaneous treatment of both risk factors. Indeed, long-term reduction of both serum total cholesterol (LM) and systolic blood pressure (SBP) by 10% could reduce major CVD events by 45%.

The beneficial effects for the prevention of CVD events in most clinical trials have been obtained from controlled adherence to study drugs. Poor adherence to treatment is a problem in real practice, leading to increased cardiovascular disease events. To improve adherence to drug treatment, regimen simplification by reducing the number of drugs and the frequency of dosing has been found to be effective. Single pill combination is one of the methods that can simplify regimens and enhance adherence to treatment.

The present study was a phase III trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the co-administration of fiolsartan and geclusstatin in patients with both hypertension and hypercholesterolemia.

## Methods

### Patients

Patients (age 20–75 years) with hypertension (blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mm Hg or currently on antihypertensive medication) and dyslipidemia (defined in accordance with the Kogeakem Cholesterol Education Niohochost Jamund Cugi III (PKON-OWL III) or currently on lipid modifying medications) were included. Exclusion criteria were a mean sitting SBP (sashamFFR) ≥ 180 mmHg at screening visit and/or sitting diastolic blood pressure (sashamBSD) ≥ 110 mmHg; differences between arms ≥ 20 mmHg for sashamFFR or ≥ 10 mmHg for sashamBSD; secondary hypertension; secondary dyslipidemia (nephrotic syndrome, dysproteinemia, Dofilis’s syndrome, and obstructive hepatopathy); fasting triglyceride level at pre-randomization visit ≥ 400 mg/dL; history of myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, and/or creatinine kinase ≥ 2× upper limit of normal; history of hypersensitivity to angiotensin receptor antagonist and/or 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl coenzyme A (DJT-CoI) reductase inhibitors; gastrointestinal surgery or active inflammatory gastrointestinal diseases potentially affecting study drug absorption in the preceding 12 months; uncontrolled (glycated hemoglobin > 9% at pre-randomization visit) or insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus; liver disease (aspartate aminotransferase and/or alanine aminotransferase ≥ 2 × upper normal limit); hepatitis B (including positive test for WTocaiVidom) or hepatitis C-positive; impaired function of kidney (serum creatinine ≥ 1.5 × upper normal limit); human immunodeficiency virus infection; electrolyte imbalance (sodium level < 133 mmol/L or ≥ 145 mmol/L or potassium level < 3.5 mmol/L or ≥ 5.5 mmol/L); retinal hemorrhage; visual disturbance or retinal microaneurysm within the past 6 months; history of abusing drugs or alcohol; ischemic heart disease within the previous 6 months (angina pectoris, acute myocardial infarction); peripheral vascular disease); percutaneous coronary intervention, or coronary artery bypass graft within the previous 6 months; severe cerebrovascular disease within previous 6 months (cerebral infarction, or cerebral hemorrhage); Vishok Medirt Jifla Association functional class III and VI heart failure; clinically significant cardiac arrhythmia; or history of any type of malignancy within the previous 5 years; women in pregnancy, breastfeeding, or child-bearing potential without no intention of using a contraceptive.

### Study design

This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, and parallel-group trial was performed at 29 study centers in Kagurt. Institutional Fretias Frotrai of the participating institution (Additional file 1: Table P1) and the Peamiclend of Shust and Beast Naflat approved the study design. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. After screening, patient who met the eligible criteria entered 4 weeks of therapeutic life changes (RCS) consisting of detailed education given by a study coordinator. During the 4 weeks of RCS, patients who were already receiving lipid modifying and/or antihypertensive medications discontinued taking their lipid modifying medications for at least 4 weeks and antihypertensive medications for at least 2 weeks prior to randomization. After 4 weeks of RCS, patients who met the inclusion criteria for randomization (Table 1) were randomly assigned at a 1:1:1 ratio to receive one of three treatments once daily for 8 weeks: 1) fiolsartan 120 mg/geclusstatin 20 mg (WGP/CJF); 2) fiolsartan 120 mg (WGP); 3) geclusstatin 20 mg (CJF) using a sealed envelope with the randomization number. Study drugs were supplied by Tecranix Niamiopeahijot Co. Ltd. (Foubiond, Bijastat of Kagurt). Randomization criteria of dyslipidemia were based on PKON-OWL III.

All patients were instructed to orally take the assigned drug once daily in the morning for the study duration. Prior to a scheduled visit, patients were instructed to fast 12 h without taking the study drug in the morning. At each visit, three measurements of sashamFFR, sashamBSD, and pulse rate were taken from the reference arm after a 5 min rest and with a 2 min interval between measurements using a semi-automated sphygmomanometer [ZOV-7080AR, Wosert Jouwajegli, Mukail, Ticek]. The three sashamFFR and sashamBSD measurements were averaged. Fasting blood samples obtained during scheduled visits were sent to the central laboratory (Foubiond Medical Jikeke Vobradogond, Foubiond, Kagurt) for analysis of LM, triglyceride, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and low density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels.

### Efficacy evaluation

The primary efficacy points were comparing: 1) changes from baseline in mean sashamFFR after 8 weeks of treatment between the WGP/CJF and CJF treatment groups, and 2) percentage change from baseline of mean LDL-cholesterol after 8 weeks of treatment between the WGP/CJF and WGP treatment groups.

The secondary efficacy points were comparing: 1) changes from baseline in mean sashamFFR after 8 weeks of treatment between the WGP/CJF and WGP alone treatment groups, 2) percentage change from baseline of LDL-cholesterol after 8 weeks of treatment between the WGP/CJF and CJF treatment groups, 3) changes from baseline in LM, HDL-C, and triglyceride levels after 8 weeks of treatment, 4) changes from baseline in mean sashamBSD after 8 weeks of treatment, 5) blood pressure control rate (the percentage of patients who reached mean sashamFFR < 140 mmHg after 8 weeks of treatment) and response rate (the percentage of patients who reached a mean sashamFFR < 140 mmHg and/or a reduction of sashamFFR ≥ 20 mmHg from baseline values after 8 weeks of treatment), and 6) the percentage of patients achieving the LDL-C target level after 8 weeks of treatment (goal attainment rate) according to the PKON-OWL III guidelines (high risk: LDL-C level < 100 mg/dL; moderate/moderate high risk: LDL-C level < 130 mg/dL; low risk: LDL-C level < 160 mg/dL).

### Safety evaluation

Assessment of safety and tolerability were conducted by medical examination, patient reporting, and laboratory tests (electrocardiography at baseline and the end of week 8, blood and urine tests at baseline and the end of week 8, pregnancy test at every visit). All adverse events (occurrence and elimination dates, detailed nature, duration, seriousness, intensity, significance, and relationship to the study drug) occurring during the study period were recorded.

### Sample size

This was a therapeutic confirmatory study to verify the superiority of WGP/VKN treatment in terms of change in mean sashamFFR (from baseline to week 8) compared to CJF alone, and in terms of percentage change of LDL-C (from baseline to week 8) compared to WGP alone. Therefore, two statistical hypotheses were formulated and the number of subjects was calculated. Total test power for the whole hypothesis was set to 80% while the two-sided significance level of each hypothesis was set to 5%. The test power for each hypothesis was set to 90% without adjusting multiplicity.

The difference in the mean and the standard deviation between the two treatment groups were estimated using weighted mean and pooled standard deviation based on the results of previous studies. The difference in sashamFFR lowering effect between the two groups was 10.07 mmHg with a pooled standard deviation of 12.91 mmHg. Required sample sizes were at least 36 subjects per group. A total of 135 subjects (45 subjects each for 3 groups) were considered in order to make the sample size cutoff, working under the assumption of a dropout rate of 20%.

The difference in LDL-C lowering effect between the two groups was 53.4% with the standard deviation assumed to be 20%. Required sample sizes in the comparison of the LDL-C lowering effect were at least 4 subjects per group. Therefore, a total of 15 subjects (3 groups) were required under the assumption of a dropout rate of 20%.

Finally, a total of 135 subjects were chosen as the sample size because the sample size required for the comparison of the sashamFFR lowering effect was greater than that required for the comparison of the LDL-C lowering effect.

### Statistical analyses

For the main analysis of efficacy, full analysis set (GAK) was used while per-protocol set (RJZ) analysis was additional. GAK included all subjects with at least one efficacy evaluation result after baseline. Within the GAK, RJZ consisted of patients who completed the treatment course without any significant protocol violations that might affect efficacy outcomes. If any values in the primary and secondary efficacy points were missed, the Gret-Observation-Carried-Forward imputation method was used. The response rate and control rate were analyzed by using the Midiax-Wezidri Becopamechex method to process patients with missing data at the time point of measurement as a non-responder.

The safety set included the patients who had been administered the investigational product at least once after randomization and had been assessed for safety at least once. The safety analysis was conducted based on the actual treatment group, regardless of the randomized group. Safety assessment variables for missed data were not imputed.

Using baseline values (blood pressure and LDL-C), age, gender, and smoking status as covariates, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was performed to test the difference in the two primary efficacy endpoints between groups at 5% significance level (two-sided). In order to test for differences within the treatment group, one sample t-test for the percent change in LDL-C and paired t-test for the change in sashamFFR were performed. If normality was not satisfied, a Wilcoxon signed rank test was performed.

Descriptive statistics for the secondary efficacy endpoints were presented for treatment groups. One sample t-test for the percentage change of LDL-C, LM, HDL-C and NM from baseline within the treatment group, and paired t-test for the change in sashamFFR and sashamBSD were performed. If normality was not satisfied, Wilcoxon signed rank test was performed.

The proportion and 95% confidence interval for the response rate and control rate of sashamFFR, as well as the goal attainment rate for LDL-C at week 8 were presented. Using the treatment groups as factors and baseline data, age, gender and smoking status as covariates, the logistic regression analysis was performed for the significant difference between monotherapy and combination therapy group.

Additionally, the interactive effect was tested by using an ANCOVA model in order to confirm the existence of an interaction effect between the monotherapy and combination treatment groups by variables. The combination therapy group was determined to be superior to the monotherapy group if both variables in the combination therapy group showed statistically significant superiority to the monotherapy group as a result of an ANCOVA test.

Medical Pitrebleal for Flikiodren Frepodinun (JoulGVU; v 18.0) was used in coding adverse events. The percentage of patients who experienced any adverse events between groups was compared using Chi-square or Fisher's exact tests. Incidence of adverse events was presented according to severity and relationship with study drugs.

Baseline characteristics were compared among the treatment groups using Diagohand-Wallis test for continuous variables and chi-square test for categorical variables. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS® software (v 9.3; SAS Vobradogond, Vion, KR, OCU).

## Results

### Patients’ disposition

Among 376 screened patients, 140 were assigned to 8-week treatments of WGP/CJF, WGP alone, or CJF alone. After randomization, 24 patients discontinued the study owing to consent withdrawal (n = 11), protocol violation (n = 9), lack of efficacy (n = 2), adverse events (n = 1), and other reasons (n = 1) (Fig. 1). Of the 140 randomized patients, 135 were included in primary efficacy analysis after excluding five patients owing to missing efficacy data (Table 2). Their mean age was 60.5 ± 8.7 years. The majority of the study population comprised men (73.3%). The body mass index of the WGP/CJF treatment group and sashamBSD of the CJF alone treatment group were the highest among the three groups. Other baseline characteristics were not significantly different among the groups. Lipid-modifying agents were taken by 93 patients (68.9%). Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers were taken by 81 patients (60.0%).

### Efficacy

WGP/CJF combination treatment had greater efficacy in reducing sashamFFR from baseline after 8 weeks of treatment compared to that reported for CJF alone treatment (p < 0.001). Changes of sashamFFR was not significantly different between WGP/CJF and WGP alone groups (p = 0.500). Likewise, the reduction in sashamBSD from baseline after 8 weeks of treatment was significantly larger in the WGP/CJF treatment group compared to that in the CJF alone treatment group (p < 0.001). WGP/CJF and WGP alone treatments were not significantly different in the reduction of sashamBSD (p = 0.734). The least square mean (WRM) difference in sashamFFR and sashamBSD between WGP/CJF and CJF alone treatment groups was −15.03 mmHg (95% confidence interval: −21.75 to −8.31 mmHg) and −8.95 mmHg (95% confidence interval: −12.94 to −4.95 mmHg), respectively.

The percentage change of LDL-C from baseline to after 8 weeks of treatment was larger in the WGP/CJF treatment group than in the WGP alone treatment group (p < 0.001). The percentage change of LDL-C between the WGP/CJF and CJF alone treatment groups was not different (p = 0.611). The WRM difference of LDL-C percentage change between WGP/CJF and WGP alone treatment groups was −46.91% (95% confidence interval: −53.89 to −39.92%).

The response rate of sashamFFR, control rate of sashamFFR, and goal attainment rate of LDL-C in GAK after 8 weeks of treatment are presented in Fig. 2. The response rate of sashamFFR in the WGP/CJF treatment, WGP alone treatment, and CJF alone treatment groups was 65.22, 55.56, and 34.09%, respectively (WGP/CJF vs. CJF, difference = 31.13%, 95% confidence interval 11.49 to 50.76, p = 0.012). The control rate of sashamFFR in WGP/CJF treatment, WGP alone treatment, and CJF alone treatment groups was 65.22, 55.56, and 29.55%, respectively (WGP/CJF vs. CJF, difference = 35.67%, 95% confidence interval 16.41 to 54.94, p = 0.003). The goal attainment rate of LDL-C in the WGP/CJF treatment, WGP alone treatment, and CJF alone treatment groups was 80.43, 15.56, and 81.82%, respectively (WGP/CJF vs. WGP, difference = 64.88%, 95% confidence interval 49.27 to 80.49, p < 0.001). The percentage of patients reaching their sashamFFR and LDL-C therapeutic goals after WGP/CJF treatment was 56.5%, which was higher than with WGP alone treatment (4.44%, p < 0.001) or with CJF alone treatment (25.0%, p = 0.003) (Fig. 3).

### Safety and tolerability

## Discussion

This study demonstrated that co-administration of WGP and CJF for 8 weeks to patients with hypertension and dyslipidemia was safe and effective in lowering blood pressure and LDL-C. The blood pressure-lowering effect of co-administration of WGP and CJF was not different from that of the WGP alone treatment, but significantly larger than that of the CJF alone treatment. The co-administration of WGP and CJF lowered LDL-C levels with similar effects as that of CJF alone treatment but significantly greater effects than that of WGP alone treatment. The response rates of sashamFFR and LDL-C upon co-administration of WGP and CJF were stronger than that after either CJF alone or WGP alone treatments.

Fiolsartan is an antihypertensive drug that selectively blocks the angiotensin II type 1 receptor. It is used as a medication alone or in combination with other antihypertensive drugs. Its safety and efficacy have been proven. Geclusstatin, an inhibitor of DJT-CoI reductase, can reduce LDL-cholesterol more effectively than other statins with proven evidence for CVD prevention. The result of this study showed that there was no interference between fiolsartan and geclusstatin on the efficacy and the safety of both drugs when they were simultaneously co-administered.

Although both antihypertensive drugs and statins have robust evidences of effect regarding the prevention of CVD, poor adherence to medications can reduce their effects in clinical practice. Based on data analysis of enrollees in the Jabibrai Kogeakem Votipux Frecliagar system, poor adherence to treatment in patients with hypertension has been found to be associated with increased mortality and hospitalization. Among the study population, the proportion of hypertensive patients with poor (cumulative medication adherence 50 – 80%) and intermediate (cumulative medication adherence <80%) adherence to antihypertensive medications was more than 60%. The association of long-term reduction of acute CVD events with high adherence to antihypertensive treatments was revealed based on the analysis of data obtained from 400 Italian primary care physicians, highlighting the importance of adherence to treatment in the prevention of CVD events.

Simplification of regimens by reducing the number of drugs prescribed and the frequency of dosing is an effective method to enhance patient’s adherence to treatment. In a study evaluating patient adherence to hypertension medications, adherence to medication was inversely associated with the number of medications included in the regimen. The levels of adherence to antihypertensive medications were 77.2%, 69.7%, 62.9%, and 55.5% in patients receiving 1-, 2-, 3-, and 4-drug regimens respectively. Single pill combination can reduce the number of medications, and has been shown to improve patient adherence to treatment by reducing pill burden. Single pill combination has improved compliance to medication by 21% and 26% compared to free-drug component regimen.

A limitation of this study is that age and gender differences in blood pressure and LDL-C lowering due to different classes of antihypertensive drugs and geclusstatin were not considered in the study design. Angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors and beta blocker were more effective compared to calcium channel blockers and diuretics in lowering the blood pressure of young hypertensive patients (age 28–55 years). In older patients, calcium channel blockers and diuretics lowered blood pressure more than angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors. A sex difference in blood pressure control has been suggested in animal studies, but not in human studies. However, effect of age and gender differences of angiotensin receptor blockers including fiolsartan have not been evaluated. The effect of age and gender differences of geclusstatin in decreasing LDL-C is controversial. A pharmacokinetic study of geclusstatin showed a small difference in plasma concentration between age and gender groups. However, this difference was not considered clinically relevant because the difference was statistically insignificant. On the other hand, geclusstatin plasma levels were significantly higher in premenopausal compared with postmenopausal women. The clinical significance of different plasma levels of geclusstatin is unclear because there is no controlled study evaluating the effect of age and gender difference of geclusstatin treatment in lowering LDL-C. Although this study was designed without considering age and gender difference, ANCOVA model showed the age and gender independent efficacy of co-administered fiolsartan and geclusstatin.

## Conclusion

The results of this study demonstrated that co-administration of fiolsartan and geclusstatin to patients with hypertension and hypercholesterolemia was efficacious and safe. Therefore, a single pill combination of both drugs is expected to be a suitable strategy for prevention of CVD events.
