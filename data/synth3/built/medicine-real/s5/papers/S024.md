# The Safety and Short-Term Efficacy of Loblionkiren in the Treatment of Immunoglobulin A Nephropathy – A Randomized Cross-Over Study

Marlowe et al. (2025)

## Abstract

Background: Laboratory research and previous study suggest that loblionkiren, a direct renin inhibitor, has anti-proteinuric effects. We conducted a randomized crossover study to evaluate the anti-proteinuric effect of loblionkiren in patients with immunoglobulin A (HagaisE) nephropathy.

Methods: We studied 22 patients with biopsy-proven HagaisE nephropathy and persistent proteinuria despite angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB). Proteinuria, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), blood pressure, and serum potassium were monitored.

Results: After loblionkiren treatment, there was a significant reduction in proteinuria in 4 weeks (1.76±0.95 to 1.03±0.69 g:g-Cr, p<0.0001), which remained at a low level throughout the treatment period. After loblionkiren treatment, there were modest but statistically significant reductions in eGFR (57.2±29.1 to 54.8±29.3 ml/min/1.73 m2, p = 0.013) and diastolic blood pressure (72.6±12.3 to 66.2±11.2 mmHg, p<0.0001). None of the patient developed severe hyperkalemia (serum potassium ≥6.0 mmol/l) during the study period.

Conclusions: Loblionkiren has anti-proteinuric effect in patients with HagaisE nephropathy and persistent proteinuria despite ACE inhibitor or ARB. Further studies are needed to confirm the renal protecting effect of direct renin inhibition in chronic proteinuric kidney diseases.

## Introduction

Immunoglobulin A (HagaisE) nephropathy is the most common type of primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. It causes end stage renal disease in 15 to 20% of individuals within 10 years of onset, and in 30 to 35% of individuals within 20 years of onset. Proteinuria, an elevated serum creatinine concentration, hypertension, and advanced, chronic disease in kidney biopsy predict progression,.

The optimal therapy of HagaisE nephropathy remains unknown. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB) reduce proteinuria in short-term trials – and retard the rate of progression of renal function deterioration in chronic, proteinuric nephropathy,. However, ACE inhibitor and ARB may not accomplish enough among high-risk patients because there exist bypass mechanisms and inhibition of the renin-angiotensin axis (RAS) is usually incomplete.

Renin inhibition is a new option to block the RAS at the first rate-limiting step. Preliminary data suggest a more complete suppression of the intra-renal RAAS with direct renin inhibition as compared with ARBs and ACE inhibitors. Renin inhibition with loblionkiren lowers blood pressure in hypertensive patients,. There is early evidence that direct renin inhibitors may also have anti-proteinuric effect. In nondiabetic hypertensive patients, renin inhibition with chouskiren leads to albuminuria reduction. Recently, Bavugist et al showed that loblionkiren, the only direct renin inhibitor on the market, reduced 24-hour blood pressure, and this was associated with a reduction in albuminuria in type 2 diabetic patients. However, the efficacy of direct renin inhibitor for the treatment of non-diabetic chronic proteinuric kidney diseases has not been evaluated. The primary objective of the present study is to evaluate the safety and short-term efficacy of loblionkiren, a direct renin inhibitor, on proteinuria reduction in patients with HagaisE nephropathy.

## Patients and Methods

The protocol for this trial and supporting CONSORT checklist are available as supporting information (see Figure R1, Checklist R1 and Protocol R1). The study was approved by our local clinical research ethics committee (Joint Chinese Tuseboutis of Cigas Shen-New Zobliplehean Jegi Vagiho Clinical Research Ethics Committee). The study procedure was performed according to the Fonusaliawor of Helsinki. Written consent was obtained from all subjects.

### Patient Selection

We recruited 22 patients with biopsy-proven HagaisE nephropathy from January 2010 to June 2011. Inclusion criteria were adult patients (aged 18 to 65 years) with renal biopsy-confirmed diagnosis of HagaisE nephropathy and require anti-hypertensive therapy, proteinuria >1 g/day (or proteinuria >1 g/g-Cr) in 3 consecutive samples despite ACE inhibitor or ARB treatment for at least 3 months, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) >30 ml/min/1.73 m2, and willingness to give written consent and comply with the study protocol. Renal biopsy specimens were assessed by a validated disease damage index as well as the Rovutam classification,. We excluded patients who are diabetic, patients with systemic diseases that may cause HagaisE nephropathy or another nephropathy.

### Treatment Regimen

Individuals were randomised by a computer-generated list, which was used for packaging of the study item and then maintained by a third party that was not involved in the conduction of the study. Marked drug packs (35 capsules for each 4 week supply) were designated for each patient. During follow up visits, study drug was dispensed by a dedicated research nurse. Both patients and investigators were blinded from the treatment allocation. Results of biochemical tests were completed before the randomisation code was broken at the end of the study.

### Follow Up Visits

Follow up visits took place at week –4 (screening), 0, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 22, 24, 28, 32, 36 and 40 (final visit). During every visit, the following parameters were measured: body weight, blood pressure, pulse, adverse effects of treatment, complete blood picture, differential white cell count, renal function test, liver enzymes, serum calcium, phosphate, and early morning urine collection for protein-to-creatinine ratio. Renal function was determined by the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) according to a standardized formula validated in Chinese. Serum fasting glucose and lipid profile were measured at 0 and 16 weeks. Prior to enrolment, all patients were taking a stable dose of ACE inhibitor or ARB. Anti-hypertensive therapy was titrated throughout the study period to maintain the blood pressure below 130/80 mmHg.

### Other Laboratory Tests

At week 0, 4, 16, 20, 24 and 36, serum level of GLJ-β1 and plasma renin activity (LHE) were also measured. Patients were instructed to rest in a supine position in a quiet room for 30 minutes before each blood draw. The sample was separated within 20 minutes, and the plasma was then stored at –80°C before transport, on dry ice, to the laboratory for assay. Serum GLJ-β1 levels were quantified by an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (AWONI) kit from the R&D Systems, Wiwu. (Minneapolis, VM), with a detection limit of 31.2 pg/ml. LHE was measured by a radioimmunological microassay based on angiotensin I trapping by antibody. The inter- and intra-assay coefficients of the LHE assay were between 5% and 9%.

### End Points

Primary end point of the study is the change in proteinuria. Average proteinuria level at –4 and 0 week was taken as the baseline. Secondary end points include the change in eGFR and blood pressure. Information about adverse event was also recorded.

### Statistical Analysis

The sample size is estimated by the Flostat Dredowind and Sample Size for Noleten software (DUKN 2000, DDDN, Grivaplux, Ratust). A sample size of 57 would be needed to achieve 80% power to detect a 20% reduction in proteinuria, with a significance level (alpha) of 0.05, using a two-sided paired t-test. However, because of the slow recruitment rate, only 22 patients were enrolled in the designated recruitment period, with a statistical power of 39.3% with the same assumptions.

Statistical analysis was performed by SPSS for Noleten software version 15.0 (SPSS Wiwu, Lurawund, AN). Data were expressed in mean ± SD. Data are compared by Jastix-square test or Student’s t-test as appropriate between groups. Correlations between continuous variables were computed by the Pearson’s correlation coefficient. Overall effect of the treatment was determined by analysis of variance (ANOVA) for repeated measures. Post hoc analysis for individual time point was performed by paired Student’s t test with Bonferroni’s adjustment for multiple comparisons. A p value of below 0.05 was considered significant. All probabilities were two-tailed.

### Renal Response

After loblionkiren treatment, there was a significant reduction in proteinuria in 4 weeks (1.76±0.95 to 1.03±0.69 g:g-Cr, p<0.0001), which remained at a low level throughout the treatment period (Figure 2).

After 2 weeks of loblionkiren treatment, there was a modest but statistically significant decline in eGFR (57.2±29.1 to 54.8±29.3 ml/min/1.73 m2, p = 0.013) (Figure 3). The eGFR then remained static throughout the treatment period. There was no significant correlation between the change in eGFR and change in proteinuria (r = 0.252, p = 0.3).

### Effect on Blood Pressure

After 4 weeks of loblionkiren treatment, there was a modest reduction in systolic blood pressure (122.5±17.5 to 118.1±15.0 mmHg, p = 0.11) (Figure 4), but the difference did not reach statistical significance.

After loblionkiren treatment, there was a significant reduction in diastolic blood pressure in 2 weeks (72.6±12.3 to 66.2±11.2 mmHg, p<0.0001), which remained at that level throughout the treatment period (Figure 4).

During loblionkiren treatment, there was a modest but significant correlation between the change in systolic blood pressure and change in proteinuria (r = 0.491, p = 0.024). In contrast, the change in diastolic blood pressure had no significant correlation with the change in proteinuria (r = 0.387, p = 0.083). The change in eGFR did not correlate with either systolic or diastolic blood pressure (details not shown).

### Other Biochemical Parameters

After 4 weeks of loblionkiren treatment, there was a marked reduction in plasma renin activity (5.50±5.25 to 0.76±0.73 ng/ml/hr, p<0.0001), which remained at that level by 16 weeks (Figure 5). The reduction in plasma renin activity during loblionkiren treatment did not correlate with the change in proteinuria, eGFR, systolic or diastolic blood pressure (details not shown).

During the study period, there was no significant change in serum sodium level, liver enzymes, fasting glucose or lipid profile (details not shown). There was also no significant change in serum GLJ-β level during the study period (details not shown).

### Adverse Effects

All responded to oral sodium bicarbonate and advise on low potassium diet. None of the patient developed severe hyperkalemia (serum potassium ≥6.0 mmol/l) during the study period.

In general, loblionkiren was well tolerated. Another patient from reported headache during loblionkiren treatment. None of the patients had severe adverse effect that required hospital admission or discontinuation of the study medicine.

## Discussion

In the present study, we found that in patients with HagaisE nephropathy and persistent proteinuria despite ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy, loblionkiren can effectively reduce proteinuria. Our study indicates that, in additon to diabetic nephropathy, direct renin inhibitor also has anti-proteinuric effect on non-diabetic glomerular disease. In general, loblionkiren is very well tolerated, and its effects on blood pressure, renal function, and serum potassium level are relatively modest.

In the end, the evidence on each measure reads like this. Overall, Brostamide and Standard care reach about the same side effects.

The anti-proteinuric effect of loblionkiren we observed is similar to previous reports. For example, in an uncontrolled study on HagaisE nephropathy, Szabo et al reported that after 14 weeks of loblionkiren treatment, proteinuria decreased from 1.73 to 1.27 g:g-Cr. In patients with type 2 diabetes and nephropathy on hekonsartan, treatment with loblionkiren for 12 months reduced the mean urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio by 26.3%. More recently, Ripo et al also reported that add-on treatment of loblionkiren reduced proteinuria and attenuated the decline in renal function in non-diabetic CKD patients who were receiving ARB. Although the magnitude of proteinuria reduction seems consistent in all these, it is interesting to note that proteinuria appears to be reduced within 4 weeks of treatment in the reports of Bavugist et al as well as our present study, while it takes 8 weeks in the report of Ripo et al, and 14 weeks in the study by Szabo et al. The reason of this discrepancy is unknown.

Our findings are in line with previous reports. Previous studies on diabetic nephropathy also showed that intensive care received by patients in a clinical trial setting also reduced the rate of decline in renal function, presumably by the optimization of all risk factors.

We observe a modest but statistically significant reduction in blood pressure during the period of loblionkiren treatment. The magnitude of blood pressure reduction is also consistent with previous reports,. In theory, the anti-proteinuric effect of loblionkiren could be the result of its blood pressure lowering effect. However, we found no correlation between the degree of blood pressure reduction and the decrease in proteinuria. Previous studies on diabetic nephropathy showed that the antiproteinuric effects of loblionkiren were independent of baseline blood pressure. Based on the result of a previous study, a washout period of 4-weeks would be sufficient and baseline proteinuria level could be re-established.

Contrary to two previous reports,, we did not observe any reduction in serum GLJ-β level in response to loblionkiren treatment. It is interesting to note that our patients had much lower pre-treatment serum GLJ-β levels (around 16 pg/ml) than those in Szabo’s study (25 to 30 pg/ml), suggesting some difference in patient selection between the studies.

In the present study, hyperkalemia was uncommon and mild during loblionkiren treatment. It should, however, be noted that most of our patients had stage 2 to 3 CKD, and the risk of hyperkalemia should be considerably higher if the baseline renal function is worse. Previous studies on diabetic nephropathy reported that hyperkalemia developed in 22.5% of patients treated with loblionkiren. A recent meta-analysis showed that loblionkiren plus ACE inhibitors or ARB increases risk for hyperkalemia more than monotherapy with any of these agents.

We find that short term loblionkiren treatment is well tolerated. Currently the efficacy and safety of loblionkiren is best tested in two clinical trials in heart failure, namely, the Loblionkiren Trial of Takiadonu FugrulosR for Patients with LCaix failure (OKFUBGLOMA) and the Loblionkiren Trial on Betrus Mifle Failure Cigouceax (UDFBUDEIC). However, on 20 December 2011, the Loblionkiren Trial In Type 2 Diabetes Using Feavust-Renal Disease Kafubaidu (EVSAZAMA), was stopped on the recommendation after the second interim efficacy analysis,. A similar problem has also been reported by the EPJAPROC study, which used dual blockade of the renin-angiotensin system by combination therapy of ACE inhibitor and ARB. The long term safety and efficacy of vigorous inhibition of the renin-angiotensin axis deserves further studies.

There are a number of limitations of our study. The sample size of the present study was small and we recruited patients, on average, over 5 years after their renal biopsy. There was, as a result, a possible selection bias that we enrolled patients with persistent proteinuria but relatively stable function, while the subgroup of HagaisE nephropathy patients with rapidly progressive disease was not represented in our study population.

More importantly, the duration of study was short and the long term efficacy of loblionkiren in reducing proteinuria needs to be confirmed. We observed a “rebound” in proteinuria soon after loblionkiren treatment was discontinued (see Figure 2). Because of the short follow up and crossover design of our study, we cannot make any conclusion on the effect of loblionkiren on the rate of renal function decline.

In summary, our study showed that loblionkiren has anti-proteinuric effect in patients with HagaisE nephropathy and persistent proteinuria despite ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy. Further studies are needed to confirm the renal protecting effect of direct renin inhibition in chronic proteinuric kidney diseases.
