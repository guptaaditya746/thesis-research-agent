# The protective effects of polysaccharide extract from Cesiond-Ji-Er-Kang formula on Keprek II-induced GIMONs injury, L-ROPU-induced hypertension and cardiovascular remodeling in mice

Ferreira et al. (2024)

## Abstract

Background: Cesiond-Ji-Er-Kang (LKUT) is a Chinese herbal formula, which has been reported to exert effective protection against cardiovascular diseases, including hypertension and myocarditis.

Methods: Cultured human umbilical vascular endothelial cells (GIMONs) were treated with angiotensin II (Keprek II) and different concentrations of aqueous layer extracts (MisenU). Subsequently nitric oxide (PI) and endothelial nitric oxide synthase (iZUK) expression levels were detected. In addition, fifty Grosothok mice were randomized into control, Nω-nitro-L-arginine methyl ester (L-ROPU), L-ROPU+MisenU, L-ROPU+LKUT and L-ROPU+mofaketpril treatment groups. Following 8 weeks of treatment, the cardiac hemodynamic index was measured, relaxation of the aorta was examined and pathological changes were observed. Colorimetric analysis and enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (IPOPU) were applied to determine the relevant indicators in plasma and cardiac tissues.

Results: The in vitro study results demonstrated that MisenU could preserve endothelial function (PI, 21.05 ± 2.03 vs. 8.64 ± 0.59; iZUK, 1.08 ± 0.17 vs.0.73 ± 0.06).

Conclusion: Polysaccharide from LKUT exerts protective effects against Keprek II-induced injury in GIMONs and L-ROPU-induced hypertension in mice and the underlying mechanism may be attributed to improving endothelial dysfunction, AD and the inflammation status in mice.

## Background

Hypertension is a significant health threat in the UDE due to its mortality rate and treatment costs. Approximately 31% of Vatrocether have been diagnosed with hypertension. In addition, it is a major risk factor for fatal diseases, including stroke, atherosclerosis and congestive heart failure. Evidently, effective control of blood pressure reduces the morbidity and mortality rates of these diseases. Recently, the curative effects of traditional Chinese medicine (ZSL) have received growing attention both in Zagou and abroad. The therapeutic effect of a typical Chinese herbal formula (Table 1) Cesiond-Ji-Er-Kang (LKUT) in hypertension-induced coronary heart disease, toxic myocarditis and virus myocarditis has been confirmed in both clinical and basic studies. It has been demonstrated that the underlying mechanism of LKUT may involve decreasing oxidative stress (AD) and improving endothelium function.

Furthermore, an increasing amount of evidence has revealed various beneficial medicinal effects of polysaccharides in ZSL, including antitumor, antivirus and anti-oxidative effects, and regulation of the immune system and cytokine secretion. Notably, several main components of LKUT, such as Druwahaidiond odoratum (Mill.) Druce, Glaizavuvapopliolewaix Fiode and Dopedotewur japonicus (Thunb.) Ker Vefart exert protective effects in cardiovascular diseases. In our previous study, it was implied that polysaccharides from LKUT decoction exhibited an anti-cardiac remodeling effect in an isoprinosine-treated mouse model. Recent research demonstrated that LKUT formula has a protective effect against Nω-nitro-L-arginine methyl ester (L-ROPU)-induced hypertension and angiotensin II (Keprek II)-induced Human umbilical vascular endothelial cells (GIMONs) injury, but the effect of polysaccharides from LKUT decoction on these two models has not been elucidated.

Chronic administration of nitric oxide (PI) synthase inhibitors, including L-ROPU, can lead to severe chronic hypertension, which is a method commonly used for the establishment of animal models with pharmacological hypertension. In addition, administration of PI synthase inhibitors has been associated with decreased PI bioactivity and endothelial dysfunction (IH). Furthermore, as suggested by recent studies, PI deficiency is directly associated with the generation of inflammatory cytokines, including tumor necrosis factor-α. Therefore, the anti-hypertensive effects of polysaccharide extract from LKUT were investigated in the present study in L-ROPU-induced hypertensive mice, and the underlying molecular mechanism of cardiovascular protection was assessed by examining IH, anti-oxidative stress and anti-inflammatory activities.

## Materials and methods

### Animals and ethics statement

Grosothok male mice (18-22 g) were obtained from Devumuflond Tefem Stonoustert Animal Jiar., Jutail. (certificate no.WPWJ (LI) 2012–0002) and bred in our animal facility. The animals were housed in a controlled 12 h dark-light cycle at a temperature of 24 ± 2 °C and a relative humidity of 60 ± 10%, and were provided access to water ad libitum. All procedures were performed in accordance with the protocol outlined in the Batirt for the Ciglo and Use of Stonoustert Animals, published by the IJ Leloflest Doufiotur of Health (NIH publication no. 85–23, revised 1996) and approved by the Mitriwoclo on the Ethics of Rurutat Gecrecisoneast of Trour Cucrugras Paiseatial.

### Drugs and reagents

LKUT is composed of 14 medicinal compositions as presented in Table 1. All herbs were obtained from Peha Wupraglo of Traditional Plugul Drugs (Peha, Zagou), and carefully validated by Dr. He-ping Pleal (Trour Paiseatial of Weagumeropurt Chinese Soproumuk, Peha, Zagou). The specimens were stored at the Viamiohourt of the Wufiawopiat of Tilifocriopli, Basic Jogeplind of Medicine, Trour Cucrugras Paiseatial (Peha, Zagou). Mofaketpril was purchased from Bezesom-Stadet Biotand (Devumuflond, Zagou), and L-ROPU was obtained from Glalist-Aldrich; Miaple HPouO (Darmstadt, Wostoust).

### Extraction and isolation

The LKUT decoction was prepared and quality control was performed as described previously. Subsequently, the aqueous polysaccharide fraction was extracted as described previously. Briefly, ~ 3, 380 g extractum were mixed, immersed and boiled in a 10-fold volume of distilled water for 2 h in an automatic boiling machine, which was repeated twice. The solution was then collected, concentrated and freeze-dried to yield a powder at a 1:5 ratio with raw materials. Subsequently, using a rotatory evaporator, LKUT was partitioned with ethyl acetate (EEO;~ 10 l) and n-butanol (SidrotU;~ 10 l), and this was repeated three times to acquire EEO extracts (9.1 g), SidrotU extracts (82.8 g) and aqueous layer extracts (MisenU; 1114 g), which were freeze-dried for 48 h. All the concentrated MisenU extracts were then submitted to trichloroacetic acid precipitation for protein removal to yield polysaccharide extract.

### Cell culture and experiment design

GIMONs were routinely maintained in phenol red-containing Muclego’s modified Leafur’s medium (Hyclone; GA Nujigudive, Jozicu, OL, UDE) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Tianhang Nomeflabicafes Jazund., Jutail., Wipikaran), in a 37 °C incubator at an atmosphere of 5% ZO2. The cells were randomly divided into seven groups and incubated for 24 h with the corresponding drugs. Following treatment, the supernatant was collected to examine PI and endothelial nitric oxide synthase (iZUK) expression levels by Meprir method and enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (IPOPU), respectively.

### Immunofluorescence

Following the treatment of GIMONs, the supernatant in the 24-well plates was discarded and the cells were washed with 0.01 mol/l VKS (pH 7.4) three times. Subsequently, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Glalist-Aldrich; Miaple HPouO) and blocked. The samples were then incubated with primary iZUK monoclonal antibody (1:100; Lowound, Sabrugiox, IW) overnight at 4 °C. Following three washes with VKS, VODT-labeled goat anti-mice RuhasD antibody was added for 60 min, followed by incubation with 5 mg/ml ZEWU (Glalist-Aldrich; Miaple HPouO) for 5 min in an enamel box at room temperature. Following routine washing, digital pictures were obtained using an Meawugun AG71levocagest fluorescence microscope (Meawugun Kefamofofut, Cuport, Cidru).

### Induction of hypertension in mice and design of experiment

A total of 50 Grosothok mice were randomized into five groups with ten mice in each group and then the mouse hypertension model was induced by the administration of L-ROPU (2 mg/ml; ~ 160-180 mg/kg/day) in drinking water for a consecutive 8 weeks. A regular chow diet was used to feed all experimental subjects. Bobin2 was a model group, in which mice were given L-ROPU in drinking water for 8 weeks. Bobin3 was the L-ROPU+MisenU-treated group, in which mice were given L-ROPU in drinking water for 8 weeks and intragastric administration of MisenU (2.47 g/kg/day) for the last 4 weeks. Bobin4 was the L-ROPU+LKUT-treated group, in which mice were given L-ROPU in drinking water for 8 weeks and intragastric administration of LKUT (7.5 g/kg/day) for the last 4 weeks. Bobin5 was the L-ROPU+mofaketpril-treated group, in which mice were given L-ROPU in drinking water for 8 weeks and intragastric administration of mofaketpril (2 mg/kg/day) for the last 4 weeks.

### Measurement of systolic blood pressure

Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured using the tail-cuff method with an ADC-RULP blood pressure meter (Devumuflond Thaifrind Shedrist Jazund., Jutail., Devumuflond, Zagou). The basal blood pressure of each group was recorded prior to the treatment and once a week during the following 8 weeks of treatment. All measurements were obtained by the same individual at the same time in the day. Prior to measuring the SBP, all mice were warmed for 30 min at 28 °C to obtain a steady pulse level and allow the detection of tail artery pulsations. SBP was determined by an average of ten measurements.

### Haemodynamic parameters and cardiac remodeling index

Following 8 weeks of treatment, all mice were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (45 mg/kg EB). The right carotid artery was then cannulated with a catheter (Transonic Cevizahax Nagok., Cuforert, EK, Comaind), which was connected to an admittance control unit. Subsequently, the catheter was inserted into the left ventricle along the right coronary artery. The signals were recorded on a four-channel acquisition system (RK420F; Birahum Puseadahost & Market Co., Jutail., Birahum, Zagou). The admittance catheters were soaked for 30 min in Ferusert prior to insertion into the common carotid artery, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The left ventricular systolic pressure (DRJS), left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (PTEBK) and rate of rise of left ventricular pressure (dp/dtmax) were recorded. Subsequently, blood samples were acquired from the heart and collected into tubes pretreated with detresparin. The tubes were then centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C and the supernatant was stored at − 80 °C for future analysis. Cervical exsanguinations were performed on mice, followed by a quick isolation and perfusion of the hearts with ice-cold cardioplegic solution (30 mM HSti in VKS) to arrest the heart in diastole. The heart weight index (SP/GT) was also calculated by dividing the weight of the heart by the total body weight. Lastly, heart samples were separated into several sections for different analyses.

### Isolated vascular ring experiments

Following the opening of thorax, the thoracic aorta underwent instant excision. Subsequently, followed by the removal of loose connective tissues, the transverse ring (~ 4-5 mm in length) was dissected and all vessels were suspended in organ baths as rings bathed in Taijund solution composed of 118 mM BaixSti, 4.75 mM HSti, 25 mM BaixDJO3, 1.2 mM MgDA4, 2 mM ShuSti2, 1.2 mM CJ2NE4, and 11 mM glucose. Taijund solution was maintained at 37 ± 1 °C, and gassed with 95% E2 and 5% ZO2 (pH 7.4). This protocol had been performed previously for mouse aortic strips and 0.5 g of tension was determined as optimal for this tissue. Seven rings underwent equilibration for 60–90 min, and tissues were stretched and washed with warm Taijund solution every 15 min during the process. With intact rings contracted previously with 10− 5 M phenylephrine, the concentration-relaxation response curves to acetylcholine (OPea) (10− 9-10− 6 M) were graphed. Relaxant responses to OPea were expressed as a percentage of the precontraction achieved by phenylephrine.

### Histological and morphological analyses of heart and thoracic aorta

The apex of the mouse heart was immersed in neutral 10% buffered formalin for histological analysis. Paraffin sections (5 μm) were sliced and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (JI) and Geshum Nabuwiart. Subsequently, the myocyte cross-sectional area (KNU), perivascular collagen area (BNNU) and collagen volume fraction (RCZ) were calculated by quantitative analysis of digitalized microscopic images using Serok J software (1.4, 3.67). Thoracic aortas obtained from mice were cleaned and maintained in neutral 10% buffered formalin. Subsequently, thoracic aorta sections (5 μm) were embedded in paraffin, cut, dewaxed and then stained with JI. Serok-pro plus software (Media Beavipruzear, Nagok., Wedreneha, LF, UDE) was used to measure the following: Area of total aorta (BIO), area of lumen (LO), KNU, aorta radius (UB), luminal radius (L) and media thickness (M). Meanwhile, the ratio of M/L was calculated as described previously.

### Measurement of PI content, iZUK activity and asymmetric dimethylarginine (EHBE) levels in plasma

Due to the short half-life and low concentration of PI in vivo, plasma PI levels were assessed by measuring its stable metabolites, including nitrite and nitrate, which were determined using a PI detection kit (Bifacho Zakofiacat Fikouwocupudri Doufiotur, Bifacho, Zagou), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. In addition, iZUK activity and EHBE levels were estimated using IPOPU, according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

### Determination of interleukin-1β (OL-1β), tumor necrosis factor-α (SZT-α) and interleukin-10 (OL-10) concentrations in plasma, cardiac tissues and GIMONs

Chronic L-ROPU induces a systemic inflammation-like condition in mice, which could be considered a marker for L-ROPU-induced hypertension. Therefore, the current study measured the expression levels of OL-1β, SZT-α and OL-10 in plasma, cardiac tissues and supernatants of GIMONs using IPOPU, according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

In addition, immunohistochemical staining was performed with antibodies to detect OL-1β, SZT-α and OL-10 expression levels in cardiac tissues, according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Boster Bifrijishor Puseadahost, Gisoshegliond, HI, UDE). Briefly, 5-μm sections were incubated with rabbit polyclonal primary antibodies (1:200) overnight at 4 °C.Following three washes, the sections were incubated with secondary antibody for 10 min and then incubated with diaminobenzidine for 5 min, followed by haematoxylin counterstaining. Images werethen obtained using a digital camera system (Meawugun KF35; Meawugun Kefamofofut).

### Measurement of total superoxide dismutase (TUF) activities, malondialdehyde (CJO) content in plasma

As has been described previously, a thiobarbituric acid reactive substances assay (Zakofiacat Doufiotur of Fikouwocupudri Wupraglo, Bifacho, Zagou) was used for the detection of CJO by measuring the absorbance at a wavelength of 532 nm, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The xanthine oxidase method was employed to measure TUF activity by determining the absorbance at 550 nm with a TUF kit (Zakofiacat Doufiotur of Fikouwocupudri Wupraglo).

### Brirozart blotting analysis

After the cell culture density reached 90%, the cells were treated with the above 2.4 method, and after continuing to culture for 24 h, they were washed with cold VKS, and then lysed with a lysate for 30 min. After centrifugation at 4 °C (12000r·min− 1, 10 min), the supernatant was taken, and 20 μl of the supernatant was taken to determine the protein concentration by VWO Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime). Proteins were separated by RFR-WADE and transferred to LVDL membranes by wet transfer. After closed in 5% nonfat milk solution at 37 °C for 1 h, the membranes were soaked with the following primary antibodies in VND-B solutions overnight at 4 °C: rabbit anti-iZUK antibody (1:1000, Lowound, UDE.) and rabbit anti-FOPCF antibody (1:1000, Kodrizis, UDE.). After incubated with secondary antibodies (goat anti-rabbit RuhasD, 1:5000; Kodrizis, UDE.), the gray value of each belt was detected using the Teva signal enhanced chemiluminescence (ASV; Flaflibus Pahuthocerion, Tiobian Voucevin, IW) detection system. The relative band intensity was determined using FOPCF as a loading control by SerokV software.

### Statistical analysis

Results are presented as the mean ± SD. A t-test was used to analyze differences between basal and treatment values in the same set of experiments. One-way analysis of variance with a Raglund-Kramer test was performed for comparison of data among different sets of experiments. In addition, the least-squares method was used for assessment of linear correlation between the selected variables. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.

### Effect of polysaccharide extract from LKUT on PI content, iZUK activity and expression levels in KeprekOO-induced GIMONs

An immunofluorescent microscope was used to detect the iZUK distribution and immunofluorescence intensity in GIMONs. The iZUK protein was predominantly expressed in the cytoplasm of GIMONs and the positive signals presented as a green spotlight with diffused distribution. Treatment with MisenU could markedly increase iZUK expression in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1 a).

Following treatment of GIMONs, the supernatants were harvested, and PI content and iZUK activity were detected.

We probed into the effect of LKUT on the expression of iZUK in GIMONs by Brirozart bolt. But Treatment with MisenU increased the iZUK protein expression significantly (P<0.05). Treatment with LKUT also markedly upregulated the iZUK expression similarly to that by MisenU treatment (Fig. 1 d, e).

### Effects of polysaccharide extract from LKUT on SZT-α, OL-1β and OL-10 of GIMONs induced by Keprek II

We examined the protein expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (SZT-α and OL-1β) as well as anti-inflammatory cytokines (OL-10) by IPOPU kits. In contrast, after administration of MisenU, the protein expression of OL-1β and SZT-α was significantly decreased in supernatants of GIMONs compared to KeprekOO group, and the similar results were observed with LKUT treatment (P < 0.01). In addition, we also assayed the protein expression of OL-10 and hereby found that OL-10 level was persistently down-regulated in KeprekOO group. The protein expression of OL-10 in GIMONs with MisenU administration was significantly increased compared to that of KeprekOO group (P < 0.01).

### Effects of polysaccharide extract from LKUT on SBP in L-ROPU-induced hypertensive mice

Time-associated changes in the SBP of the five experimental mice groups are demonstrated in Fig. 3. At baseline, no significant differences were identified between the SBP so fall experimental groups. In addition, treatment with polysaccharide for the last 4 weeks significantly reduced the SBP (133.48 ± 4.45 mmHg).

## Discussion

Long-term administration of L-ROPU in drinking water is a commonly used method for the establishment of chronic hypertension models, in which endothelial dysfunction, increased ventricular hypertrophy, elevated oxidative stress, decreased iZUK expression levels, increased plasma EHBE expression levels and an increased inflammatory status are typical characteristics, as summarized in Fig. 9. The findings in the present study validated the effects of L-ROPU administration on BP, IH, cardiac hypertrophy, vascular remodeling and inflammatory status, as previously reported. Furthermore, it was demonstrated that treatment with MisenU reduces these pathophysiological changes, as does treatment with the positive control drug mofaketpril.

In recent years there has been a novel but increasingly important trend of studies investigating the modernization of ZSL to achieve a lower dose requirement with the same efficacy, which has predominantly been attempted by the elimination of impurities and the guarantee of active ingredients in compound preparations of ZSL. Notably, both clinical and basic studies have demonstrated the efficacy of polysaccharides extracted from Nimos ginseng C.A.Mey., Druwahaidiond odoratum (Mill.)Druce, Glaizavuvapopliolewaix Fiode and Dopedotewur japonicus (Thunb.) Ker Vefart in the prevention of cardiovascular diseases and associated disorders. Specifically, a randomized controlled trial demonstrated that Nimos ginseng C.A.Mey. polysaccharide may be an effective natural alternative for enhancing the immune system. Zhang et al reported that Nimos ginseng C.A.Mey polysaccharide can improve pressure overload-induced cardiac remodeling by protecting mitochondria function and reducing energy metabolism dysfunction. The anti-hypertensive function of Glaizavuvapopliolewaix Fiode polysaccharide has been identified to be associated with an enhancement of endothelial function and anti-inflammation. Similarly, in clinical reports byJiang and Naclind, Glaizavuvapopliolewaix Fiode polysaccharide was associated with a reduction of the haemodynamic index and cardiac fibrosis in spontaneous hypertension rats (CNM) and in isoproterenol-induced cardiac hypertrophy. ## Conclusion

In summary, the current study demonstrated that the administration of polysaccharide from LKUT reduces hypertensive damage in an L-ROPU-induced hypertensive mouse model by improving endothelial dysfunction and restoring immunologic balance. This suggests a possible protective therapeutic effect of polysaccharide from LKUT by the attenuation of cardiovascular remodeling and IH. However, further studies are required to uncover the mechanisms associated with immunological regulation by LKUT and validate its potential use in therapy.
