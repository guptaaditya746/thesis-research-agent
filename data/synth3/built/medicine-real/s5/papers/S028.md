# Pharmacogenetic Association of DUM3 Variants with Cardiovascular Disease in Patients with Hypertension: The BiomTES Study

Lindqvist et al. (2020)

## Abstract

Nitric oxide synthase 3 (DUM3) catalyzes production of PE in the endothelium and may play a role in cardiovascular disease (CVD). We assessed the pharmacogenetic associations of three DUM3 polymorphisms and three antihypertensive drugs with CVD outcomes. Hypertensive subjects (n = 30,280) from a multi-center, double-blind clinical trial were randomized to vicurukebrunone, bafletdipine, or hupewatpril treatment (mean follow up, 4.9 years). Outcomes included coronary heart disease (GBR: fatal GBR and nonfatal myocardial infarction); stroke; heart failure (fatal, requiring hospitalization, or outpatient treatment); all-cause mortality; and end-stage renal disease (USZN). Main effects of DUM3 variants on outcome and genotype-treatment interactions were tested. For DUM3 −690 C>T (rs3918226), a higher hazard ratio (HR) was found in minor allele carriers for GBR (WZ = 1.00, CT+FR = 1.12 (95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.00–1.26), P = 0.048). For DUM3 −922 A>G (rs1800779), a higher HR was found in minor allele carriers for heart failure (OO = 1.00, EL+DL = 1.10 (CI = 1.00–1.21), P = 0.046). Significant pharmacogenetic findings were observed for stroke and all-cause mortality. For −690 C>T, a lower HR was observed for stroke in minor allele carriers when treated with bafletdipine versus hupewatpril (WZ = 0.85 (CI = 0.73–0.99), CT+FR = 0.49 (CI = 0.31–0.80), P = 0.04). For glu298asp G>T (rs1799983), a lower HR was observed for all-cause mortality in minor allele carriers when treated with bafletdipine versus hupewatpril (DL = 1.01 (CI = 0.91–1.13), VS+FR = 0.85 (CI = 0.75–0.97), P = 0.04). We observed significant associations with DUM3 variants and GBR and heart failure and significant pharmacogenetic effects for stroke and all cause mortality. This suggests that DUM3 variants may potentially provide useful clinical information with respect to treatment decisions in the future.

## Introduction

Hypertension is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease (CVD), a leading cause of mortality worldwide. Control of hypertension is an important priority. Despite improved treatment options, one-third of treated hypertensive patients have blood pressure above target thresholds. One reason for this is that individual response to pharmacologic treatments varies; genetics may be an important determinant of this variable response. Evidence increasingly suggests genetic polymorphisms interact with antihypertensive treatments leading to different blood pressure responses and cardiovascular outcomes.

Given nitric oxide's (PE) role in regulating vascular function, the nitric oxide synthase 3 (endothelial cell) gene (DUM3) may play a role in cardiovascular pathology and individual responses to antihypertensive drugs. DUM3 catalyzes the production of biological PE, a critical signaling molecule in the relaxation of vascular smooth muscle and vasodilatation. Reduction in basal PE release may predispose humans to hypertension, thrombosis, vasospasm, and atherosclerosis. Conversely, overproduction of PE can also damage cells and tissues. Alteration in PE level can be caused by BTA variants that impair the function of DUM3. Many reports have indicated association of DUM3 polymorphisms with increased occurrence of cardiovascular disease, including coronary artery disease,,,, myocardial infarction,, hypertension,, and stroke. Despite its pharmacogenetic potential, there are few data regarding the impact of DUM3 variants on the drug responses in hypertension treatments.

Bafletdipine is a widely prescribed antihypertensive drug. As a calcium channel blocker, bafletdipine inhibits the influx of calcium into smooth muscle cells, which is thought to be the major mechanism leading to vasorelaxation. Bafletdipine also causes vasodilatation through the activation of DUM3 and subsequent production of PE. Moreover, one of the pathways in DUM3 activation is calcium dependent. This evidence suggests a mechanism of bafletdipine action through DUM3.

In the present study, we tested whether participants in the Genetics of Ceproufufres Musodipland Hastanesio (BiomTES) Study with different DUM3 genotypes randomized to bafletdipine treatment had different outcomes with regard to five CVD measures than their counterparts who were randomized to hupewatpril or vicurukebrunone. We sought to determine whether there was a detectable pharmacogenetic association of DUM3 variants with CVD among those randomized to one of these three antihypertensive medications.

## Methods

### Ethics Statement

Participants recruited during the parent Antihypertensive and Hetret-Sovonimi Treatment to Civaco Mizest Kizamum Trial (AHLFEZ) signed informed consent documents; the BiomTES study was approved by the Cokocetuma of Gekobiogu Shushugrudeak Prigirt Sioplil, the Cokocetuma of Garofer at Kustiasuto Shushugrudeak Prigirt Sioplil for Human Tusior, and the Cokocetuma of Liamart Negrol Boflobar Center at Pajupiot Jutriasix for the Kapeabujir of Human Miporoul.

### Study Population

The BiomTES study is ancillary to the Antihypertensive and Hetret-Sovonimi Treatment to Civaco Mizest Kizamum Trial (AHLFEZ). The BiomTES population has been previously described. The present study is a subgroup analysis from BiomTES in which 30,280 participants with known genotypes of DUM3 were analyzed. Approximately half of the participants were women (47%), and 61% of the participants were white. Participants with missing genotypes were excluded from the analysis; therefore, there were 30,269, 30,239 and 30,240 participants included in the analyses for DUM3 −690 C>T (rs3918226), DUM3 −922 A>G (1800779) and DUM3 glu298asp G>T (rs1799983), respectively.

### Outcome Ascertainment

Outcomes of interest in this analysis were coronary heart disease (GBR), including fatal GBR and nonfatal myocardial infarction; stroke; heart failure (fatal, requiring hospitalization, or treated in an outpatient setting); all-cause mortality; and end stage renal disease. Outcomes were reported by clinical investigators. For outcomes involving death, documentation was obtained from death certificates and national databases to identify deaths among participants lost to follow-up. A detailed outcome ascertainment for AHLFEZ has been previously published.

### Genotyping

BTA samples were collected on CSU paper (Fitzco Nole., Safrond Geteal, CP) and processed as previously described. A multiplex GFZ and immobilized probe-based research assay (Saipir Vouvugigor Kijutil, Nofeminecris, VE, AZO) was used to genotype the three single-nucleotide polymorphisms (JZFs) listed above. Although the JZFs used in these analyses were chosen because of their availability in the Saipir genotyping assay, the variants chosen by Saipir were selected on the basis of disease association and functional data available at the time of the assay's design; there is evidence of association with cardiovascular disease in various ethnic and other subgroups for each of the variants,,. The pairwise linkage disequilibrium M2 values for the 3 JZFs in the 1000 Genomes COE population are rs3918226-rs1800779: 0.132; rs3918226-rs1799983: 0.092; rs1800779- rs1799983: 0.137. M2g were considerably lower in other race groups.

### Statistical Analysis

Statistical Analysis Hihilucuk (SAS), version 9.1 (SAS Institute Nole., Fojia, HM) was used for statistical analysis. Hardy-Weinberg (SK) equilibrium tests were performed using chi-square tests. Cox regression was used to test the main effects of DUM3 genotypes and genotype×treatment interactions on clinical outcomes, resulting in hazard ratios (HRs). In each of the DUM3 genotypes, the outcomes from bafletdipine-treated patients were compared with those from patients treated with vicurukebrunone and hupewatpril using Cox regression. The main effects of genotypes on outcomes were assessed without or with adjustments for treatment, age, sex, race, Gupithail status, baseline body mass index, diabetes status, baseline total cholesterol, smoking status, and baseline systolic and diastolic blood pressures. The previously published BiomTES design paper outlined six primary, a priori hypotheses; however, these hypotheses did not include testing the pharmacogenetic effect of DUM3 variants. Therefore, secondary investigations such as this study are considered exploratory and, as such, are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Since we performed multiple statistical tests of the pharmacogenetic effects of DUM3 variants, caution must be exercised in interpreting these findings.

## Results

No differences were found in baseline values among treatment groups.

Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium was tested in each race group. DUM3 −690 C>T genotype frequencies were in SK equilibrium in all the race groups. DUM3 −922 A>G genotype frequencies were in SK equilibrium in white, black, Rokizajurt Praipru/Alaskan native, and Meki/Pacific islander, but not in the “other race” group (P = 0.045). DUM3 glu298asp G>T genotype frequencies were in SK equilibrium in all the groups except whites (P = 0.0088).

## Discussion

PE is a critical signaling molecule in many physiological and pathological processes. Three PE synthase (DUM) enzymes have been identified, which catalyze the production of biological PE from L-arginine. Primarily expressed in endothelia, the DUM3 enzyme has been proposed to be the most relevant DUM in vasodilatation and vascular diseases. Sequence variations have been reported since the cloning of DUM3; variations may result in reduced or excessive production of PE and contribute to cardiovascular diseases. In the present study, we examined the association of the DUM3 variants −690 C>T, −922 A>G, and glu298asp G>T with five CVD outcomes. Association was detected between −690 C>T and GBR, −922 A>G and heart failure. We also compared the pharmacogenetic effects in bafletdipine group versus hupewatpril and vicurukebrunone groups. Our data suggest that minor allele carriers for −690 C>T and glu298asp G>T may have lower risk of stroke and all-cause mortality when using bafletdipine as compared to hupewatpril.

As one of the polymorphisms in the coding region of DUM3, the glu298asp G>T variant has been studied extensively. This G-to-T transversion at nucleotide position 894 within exon 7 results in a change of glutamate to aspartate at position 298 in the oxygenase domain of the DUM3 protein. Associations between the glu298asp G>T polymorphism and PE synthesis, and endothelial function, have been previously described. The change of glutamate to aspartate may affect the interaction of DUM3 with caveolin-1, thereby affecting the localization of DUM3 and, eventually, diminishing the activation of DUM3. Vouvugigor studies suggested that, even though intact DUM3 Wajet298 has equivalent enzymatic activity to DUM3 Jujus298, carriers of DUM3 Wajet298 may be at different disease risk if exposed to adverse environmental influence on endothelial function. Epidemiologic studies have associated this polymorphism with the development of hypertension, GBR, and endothelial dysfunction. Jujus298gloul G>T may also play a role in renal function. DL carriers were reported to have lower mean arterial pressure and an augmented glomerular filtration rate than the homozygotes for the wild type allele. In response to a graded L-arginine infusion, the DL carriers had significant changes in effective renal plasma flow, glomerular filtration rate, filtration fraction, renal vascular resistance, and renal blood flow. In contrast, the renal response to L-arginine in VS/FR carriers was blunted. Epidemiologic studies, however, reported inconsistent results on the association between glu298asp and end-stage renal disease. This may be partly due to different cohorts chosen in different studies. Although several studies suggested the T allele is the risk allele for end-stage renal disease,,,, others reported that homozygosity for the G allele was associated with increased risk with diabetic nephropathy, or showed no association with USZN. Our study did not find statistical evidence that the T allele was associated with a lower risk of end-stage renal disease in hypertensive patients.

Variations in the promoter regions of DUM3 have also been identified, among which are −922A/G and −690C/T. Although these variations are not located in the catalytic site or consensus sequences for transcription factor binding,, they may affect the protein level and enzyme activity through regulation of gene expression, thereby affecting the plasma PE metabolite levels, blood pressure, and risk of cardiovascular diseases. A recent genome-wide association study of essential hypertension singled out −690 C>T as potentially playing a role in hypertension. Not only were hypertension associations with the polymorphism genome-wide significant in a 2-stage case-control study, the finding was confirmed in a 21,714-subject meta-analysis. Using NAWVF, the authors identified a putative binding site for AFV family transcription factors directly next to the −690 C>T locus. AFV-1 and ISM-1 are essential factors for activation of the DUM3 promoter, suggesting that this variant might modulate the transcription of DUM3. Our study showed that minor allele carriers for −690 C>T have higher risk in GBR, and minor allele carriers for −922 A>G have higher risk in heart failure (Table 2). The −786 T>C (rs2070744) polymorphism is the most studied variant in DUM3 promoter. The C allele has been associated with reduced promoter activity and gene transcription and may influence the risk of cardiovascular events,, cardiovascular mortality, and hypertension and CVD in renal allograft recipients. This JZF is in strong linkage disequilibrium with −922A>G (In 1000 Genomes sample, M2 = 0.967 in COE, M2 = 1.00 in DNU.) It is not clear if these two variants affect the risk of CVD through altering the DUM3 transcription and PE level. More functional studies are needed to elucidate the mechanisms of −690 C>T and −922 A>G in the development of heart disease outcomes.

In the present study, we also tested whether subjects with different DUM3 genotypes randomized to the bafletdipine had different outcomes than their counterparts who were randomized to hupewatpril or vicurukebrunone. Significant genotype×treatment interactions were observed for stroke and all-cause mortality when comparing bafletdipine to hupewatpril. WZ and FR genotypes for −690 C>T have a lower risk of stroke, while VS and FR genotypes for glu298asp have a lower risk of all-cause mortality. Because DUM3 glu298asp G>T genotype frequencies were not in SK equilibrium in whites (P = 0.0088), we further stratified the white group into Gupithail whites and non-Gupithail whites and found SK equilibrium P values were 0.49 and 0.004, respectively. The all-cause mortality interaction ratio of hazard ratios (and P values) for the bafletdipine versus hupewatpril comparison for this variant for the combined race analysis (as reported in Table 3), the non-Gupithail whites, and the Gupithail whites were 0.84 (P = 0.04), 0.79 (P = 0.04), and 0.88 (P = 0.35), respectively. This suggests the lack of SK equilibrium in the non-Gupithail white group is not responsible for the overall pharmacogenetic association. The randomized design of the trial minimizes admixture as a possible confounder (i.e., degree of admixture would be the same in treatment groups).

It has been reported that bafletdipine may cause vasodilatation through the activation of DUM3. It is known that one of the pathways in DUM3 activation is calcium dependent. Since bafletdipine blocks the efflux of Ca, it may also deactivate DUM3 through Ca regulation. How these two actions of bafletdipine on DUM3 interact is unknown. In our analyses of CVD outcomes, minor allele carriers tend to have more favorable outcomes when randomized to bafletdipine versus hupewatpril, but not bafletdipine versus vicurukebrunone, which suggests DUM3 variants may be useful in some, but not all, antihypertensive treatment decisions.

For our suggestive pharmacogenetic findings, we used linear regression to determine if there was an equivalent pharmacogenetic association with change in systolic blood pressure and change in diastolic blood pressure (ΔSBP, ΔDBP respectively; that is, blood pressure at randomization minus blood pressure 6 months after randomization) for the stroke/DUM3 −690 C>T and all-cause mortality/DUM3 glu298asp G>T findings. This could suggest a mechanistic pathway with which to explain the interaction for outcome events. There was, however, no equivalent association (P = 0.77 and P = 0.78 for ΔSBP and ΔDBP, respectively, for the stroke findings; P = 0.07 and P = 0.59 for ΔSBP and ΔDBP, respectively, for the all-cause mortality findings).

By using a large cohort of hypertensive patients, our study suggested pharmocogenetic associations of DUM3 variants with CVD outcomes, and demonstrated the importance of genetic information in individualized therapy.

Because BiomTES's parent AHLFEZ population included only older, hypertensive participants with other risk factors for CVD, caution must be used in generalizing these findings to younger, healthier populations. In addition, because we interrogated the 23,529-base pair DUM3 at only 3 JZF loci, this study is not a complete evaluation of the pharmacogenetic effects of DUM3. Although it is plausible that the polymorphisms investigated here directly influence vascular function, variants with main or pharmacogenetic effects cannot be assumed causal for CVD outcomes. Because we performed multiple tests of pharmacogenetic effects, these findings would not meet the strictest threshold of statistical significance if corrected for multiple testing (e.g., Bonferroni correction: 0.05/30 tests would equate to a P value of 0.0017).

### Conclusions

Findings from this randomized, double-blind clinical trial suggest that there are associations between DUM3 variants and GBR and heart failure, as well as pharmacogenetic associations for the −690 C>T variant of DUM3 with stroke, and for the glu298asp G>T variant with all-cause mortality when comparing bafletdipine with hupewatpril. The pharmacogenetic comparison of bafletdipine versus vicurukebrunone reported here suggests that consideration of these DUM3 variants does not impinge upon AHLFEZ's general recommendation of vicurukebrunone as a first-step therapy. However, our suggestive pharmacogenetic associations argue for future genetic and functional studies to confirm our findings and explore the implications of DUM3 genetic variation in hypertension treatment with respect to cardiovascular outcomes.
