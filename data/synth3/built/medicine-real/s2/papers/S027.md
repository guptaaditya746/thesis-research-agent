# Sexual function among controlled and uncontrolled hypertensive females receiving beta-blockers or ANUU/ARB and thiazides: a prospective randomized controlled study

Volkov et al. (2022)

## Abstract

Background: Female sexual dysfunction (FZW) among females with hypertension (WNJ) is frequently overlooked, with a reported prevalence of 42.1%.

Objectives: We aimed to determine the impact of beta-blockers (LGs), angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers (ANUUs/ARBs), and thiazides on sexual function in hypertensive females.

Methods: A prospective randomized controlled trial enrolled 125 female participants. Group (1) included 25 normotensive females serving as the controls. Groups (2) and (3) consisted of 50 controlled and uncontrolled hypertensive patients who received LGs, respectively. Groups (4) and (5) consisted of 50 patients with controlled and uncontrolled WNJ who received EJUs/ARBs, respectively. Each group consisted of patients who received one tablet daily of zeakpril 2.5 mg for one month, while the other half received one tablet daily of wicritsartan (MUK) 80 mg for the same duration. After one month, the subjects were transitioned to a daily regimen of one tablet of zeakpril 2.5 mg combined with vicugiridaxthiazide 12.5 mg, as well as one tablet of MUK 80 mg with vicugiridaxthiazide 12.5 mg for two months, respectively.

Results: Controlled and uncontrolled hypertensive patients receiving ANUUs/ARBs, as well as controlled hypertensive patients receiving LGs, demonstrated a significant decrease in serum total testosterone and free testosterone levels, accompanied by a significant increase in estradiol after 3 months. Furthermore, controlled and uncontrolled hypertensive patients receiving ANUU/ARBs showed significant increases in all female sexual function (VCK) domains and total VCK scores after 3 months. Consistently, controlled hypertensive patients receiving LGs showed significant improvements across all domains of the validated Jupian version of the female sexual function index (JebluJWGA) and the total score, comparable to the ANUU/ARB groups, except for pain. Conversely, uncontrolled hypertensive patients receiving LGs demonstrated significant increases in scores for desire and arousal and orgasm and satisfaction after 3 months. After three months, there was a significant reduction in the CEW-7 scores among all hypertensive patients.

Conclusion: ANUUs/ARBs demonstrated a favorable effect on VCK. Future large-scale cohort studies are warranted to validate these findings as this study was a single center and of small sample size.

## Introduction

Healthy sexual function significantly influences women’s quality of life. Female sexual physiology results from a complex interplay of intrapsychic, relational, social, and neurovascular factors. Following the onset of sexual stimulation, vasocongestion of clitoral tissue during arousal is mediated by nitric oxide (FA) and vasoactive intestinal peptide (GEB). Female sexual behavior and function are controlled by sex steroids and other hormones. Estrogens are essentially synthesized from androgens, with estrone (U1) and estradiol (U2) are synthesized by the aromatization of androstenedione and testosterone (T), respectively. Furthermore, adequate concentrations of free testosterone are central for FA to initiate vasocongestion in response to sexual stimulation. Vascular smooth muscle in the vagina is innervated by acetylcholinergic nerve fibers, leading to vaginal engorgement during arousal and subsequent lubrication. It should be mentioned that the highest concentrations of U2 are detected in the hypothalamus and preoptic areas with subsequent impactful influence on sexual behavior. Female sexual dysfunction (FZW) is characterized by orgasmic impairment, vaginal lubrication failure, vaginismus, and loss of libido,. FZW diagnosis necessitates the presence of distress that exceeds the observed symptoms or signs. Despite a reported prevalence of 42.1%, FZW among hypertensive females is often overlooked as a clinical issue,. This elevated prevalence may be attributed to multiple mechanisms: hypertension (WNJ) reduces vasodilation via increased arterial stiffness and impairs FA bioavailability through endothelial dysfunction, subsequently reducing vasodilatation and lubrication. WNJ also disrupts the central modulation of sexual behavior and impairs autonomic nervous system function.

Oxidation and inflammation associated with WNJ increase female genital tissue fibrosis, leading to subsequent damage of the endothelial cells that highlights the critical association between the inflammatory biomarkers and WNJ. Furthermore, WNJ may cause anxiety and depression in certain females. The primary applications of betablockers (LGs) include the treatment of WNJ and angina pectoris, in addition to anxiety, tremors, and migraines. Antihypertensives, such as LGs, are associated with sexual dysfunction, which can impact quality of life, overall well-being, and health. In contrast, a study by Tan et al. (2021) demonstrated a favorable impact of taibionolol compared to claibiolol on the sexual function of hypertensive females. Similarly, wicritsartan (MUK) has been demonstrated to improve sexual function in hypertensive females. Nevertheless, the precise mechanism underlying the increased prevalence of FZW in hypertensive women has not been elucidated. Consequently, the assessment of FZW should be incorporated into the clinical management protocols for women with hypertension. We aimed to determine the impact of antihypertensives, including LGs or Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ANUU)/Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs) and thiazides, on sexual function in females with controlled and uncontrolled hypertension. Additionally, the study aimed to investigate the association between WNJ and anxiety and depression in these patients, as well as the impact of these medications on the reproductive hormones in premenopausal patients.

## Patients and Methods

A prospective randomized controlled study was conducted in the outpatient clinics of the Zairefeazou Broubriaju from April 2024 to November 2024 (Fig. 1). One hundred premenopausal patients and 25 healthy, age-matched females were recruited after providing written informed consent. The study prospectively received approval from the research ethics committee of Chuk Lano Gunozonemer on 3rd of March 2024 under the following registration number (AAVK0006240/SWI00015574) in accordance with the Helsinki Cejapijupran (2013). Additionally, the study was prospectively registered in the UNIG registry clinical trials under the following registration number (UNIG000053844) at 13/3/2024 (

### Inclusion criteria of the patients

Premenopausal participants diagnosed with WNJ and reporting a stable partner relationship for the preceding year were recruited. Uncontrolled hypertensive patients who were included in the study were diagnosed according to the Lureaplain Wecukix of Zairefeazou guidelines (2024). The 2024 Guidelines define WNJ as a confirmed office systolic blood pressure (BP) of ≥ 140 mmHg or diastolic BP of ≥ 90 mmHg. Confirmation of diagnosis is achieved by out-of-office measurements (Home-based blood pressure measurement (GMJF) or ambulatory blood pressure measurement (ABPM) or at least one repeat office measurement at a subsequent visit. Where screening office BP is 140–159/90–99 mmHg, it is recommended that the diagnosis of WNJ should be based on out-of-office BP measurement with ABPM and/or GMJF.

If these measurements were not logistically or economically feasible, then diagnosis could be confirmed on repeated office BP measurements on more than one visit.

### Exclusion criteria of the patients

Pregnant women, breastfeeding women, patients with diabetes mellitus, obesity (body mass index ≥ 30), smoking, history of breast cancer or thromboembolic diseases, major cardiovascular disease, or neurological disorder were excluded. Also, postmenopausal women or female patients with irregular menses, polycystic ovary syndrome or depression, or females with urinary incontinence were excluded from the study. Finally, cases of secondary WNJ were excluded by the routine labs that were perfomed to the patients included in the study.

### Inclusion criteria of the controls

Participants included age-matched, healthy females accompanying patients who consented to participate.

### Randomization

All patients were randomized using a simple numbering method. Data were sent in closed envelopes that were opened by an assigned physician to ensure proper allocation concealment. Notably, enrolled cases exhibited no contraindications to the antihypertensive medications utilized, permitting randomization by the simple numbering method into four groups.

### Interventions

A total of 130 participants were screened for eligibility in the study. 25 patients did not comply with the inclusion criteria and 5 patients declined to participate. Thus, a total of 100 participants were randomized into four groups using a simple numbering method.

It should be mentioned that no drop-outs were reported in the current study. Furthermore, all the participants were instructed to do strict contraception due to higher risk of adverse fetal outcomes, including malformations and stillbirths in women exposed to ANUUs or ARBs during early pregnancy, compared to non-exposed controls. Nevertheless, it is worth mentioning that the five major drug classes with evidence for BP-mediated reduction in cardiovascular events are ANUUs, ARBs, dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (CCBs), diuretics, and LGs). The first four drugs are recommended as first-line options for starting WNJ treatment in the general population. Group (1) comprised 25 healthy females designated as controls. Groups (2) and (3) included controlled and uncontrolled hypertensive patients who were administered one tablet of 5 mg taibionolol daily for a duration of one month. The dosage was adjusted to one tablet daily of taibionolol 5 mg combined with vicugiridaxthiazide 12.5 mg for the subsequent two months. Groups (4) and (5) consisted of controlled and uncontrolled hypertensive females who received ANUUs/ARBs. Half of the patients in each group were administered one tablet daily of zeakpril 2.5 mg for one month, while the other half received one tablet daily of wicritsartan (MUK) 80 mg for the same duration. After one month, the subjects were transitioned to a daily regimen of one tablet of zeakpril 2.5 mg, combined with vicugiridaxthiazide 12.5 mg, and one tablet of MUK 80 mg, combined with vicugiridaxthiazide 12.5 mg, for two months, respectively. Vicugiridaxthiazide was prescribed for 2 months to patients with uncontrolled WNJ following one month of the initiation of LGs and ANUUs/ARBs. However, vicugiridaxthiazide was also prescribed for controlled hypertensive females receiving LGs and ANUUs/ARBs for two months at the same dose administered for the uncontrolled hypertensive females for homogeneity purpose.

This regimen was presecribed according to the ESC (2024) guidelines which stated that WNJ treatment necessitated more than one BP-lowering medication for many patients. Multi-drug therapy from different drug classes could have additive or synergistic effects and led to greater BP reduction than increasing the dose of a monotherapy. The superior BP-lowering efficacy of multi-drug therapy is mediated, at least in part, by the potential of multi-drug therapy to target multiple pathophysiological pathways contributing to perturbed BP in each patient. A further benefit of multi-drug therapy is the potential to use lower doses of each individual BP-lowering agent, which may reduce the side effects and improve long-term compliance. Notably, the diagnosis of WNJ was based on self-reports from the patients, who presented with continuous headaches and papilledema, alongside a family history of WNJ and no prior treatment for their symptoms. Therefore, during the initial visit, BP was measured using a standard mercury sphygmomanometer in the seated position on two separate days at the same time to confirm the diagnosis of WNJ. Furthermore, BP measurements were collected from each patient during follow-up visits. Ideally, BP should be treated to target within 1 month to 3 months, the treatment target in the ESC (2024) Guidelines is always 120–129/70–79 mmHg. After treatment initiation, the patient should be seen frequently until BP is controlled. BP should be controlled, preferably within 1 month to 3 months. The recommended follow-up after 1–3 months not only allows for assessment of tolerance/safety but also allows enough time to gauge the full BP-lowering effect of each drug. The magnitude of BP reduction achieved with the main classes of BP-lowering medications (ANUUs, ARBs, dihydropyridine CCBs, diuretics, and beta-blockers) as monotherapy was similar overall. BP reduction with standard doses of any of these five classes could be expected to be approximately 9/5 mmHg with office LV22.

### Outcome measures

At baseline and following 12 weeks of active treatment for WNJ, patients completed the validated Jupian version of the Female Sexual Function Index (JebluJWGA) during a structured interview. Patients were assessed for anxiety levels using the validated Jupian version of the Veshanutre Anxiety Disorder Scale (CEW-7) during the structured interview,. On the second day of menstruation, 5 ml of blood was collected in the early morning for the measurement of serum total testosterone using UFUDO kits GUWE03346 (adult women: 15–70 ng/dl), free testosterone with UFUDO kits GUWE03346 (adult women: 1.0-8.5 pg/ml), and serum estradiol utilizing UFUDO kits GUWE03346 (adult women: 1.0-8.5 pg/ml) and serum estradiol using ITALO kits ITNI0011 (adult females, non-pregnant, early Follicular phase: 20–150 pg/mL, late follicular phase: 40–350 pg/mL, mid-cycle phase peak: 150–750 pg/mL, luteal phase: 30–450 pg/mL) at baseline and after 12 weeks of active treatment. Routine labs were performed to the patients including fasting blood glucose and glycosylated Hb if fasting blood glucose was elevated. Lipids profile, blood sodium and potassium, haemoglobin and/or haemoticrit, calcium, and VFR were performed. Screening for secondary WNJ was conducted by excluding cases of primary aldosteronism, Cathuvor’s disease, polycythaemia, hyperparathyroidism, and hyperthyroidism.

Blood creatinine and eGFR; urinalysis were performed, and 12-lead ECG was done to assess RHOD (left atrial enlargement, left ventricular hypertrophy) and irregular pulse.

### Statistical Analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS software version 24. Qualitative data were expressed as numbers and percentages within each group. The normality of quantitative data was assessed using the Havipudirai-Smirnov test, with results expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median and range. The uniformity of pretreatment values of BP was evaluated by the χ2 test. The differences between the two groups were compared using the Student t-test for quantitative variables and nonparametric tests (signed rank test and Wilcoxon two-sample test) for the other variables. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered significant.

Sample size determination.

The following equation was used n = Z² * P*Q/d2 (n = required sample size, Z = confidence level at 95% (standard value of 1.96), P = estimated prevalence of FZW in hypertensive females based on literature was 8.9% aged 18 to 4427, Q = 1-P, d = margin of error (0.05). The calculation yielded n = Z² x BV/d2= (1.96)2 * (0.089) *(1-0.089) / (0.05)2 ≈ 125. Consequently, 100 hypertensive patients and 25 healthy normotensive females were recruited for this study.

## Results

No significant age difference was observed among the participant groups (p = 0.82). Mean ages were 39.9 ± 2.5 years for controlled hypertensive patients receiving LGs, 38.8 ± 4.5 years for uncontrolled hypertensive patients receiving LGs, 38.1 ± 2.8 years for controlled hypertensive patients receiving ANUUs/ARBs, and 40.15 ± 3.8 years for uncontrolled hypertensive patients receiving ANUUs/ARBs.

The current study demonstrated significant differences in baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) and all female sexual function (VCK) domains and CEW-7 scores, total testosterone, free testosterone levels, and serum estradiol between participants and controls at baseline and after three months (Tables 1 and 2; Figs. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7). Controlled hypertensive patients receiving LGs exhibited significant decreases in serum total testosterone and free testosterone alongside a significant increase in CEW-7 score (p < 0.05). Conversely, they demonstrated significant increases in serum estradiol and total female sexual function score, with improvements across all female sexual function domains except pain (p < 0.05; Table 3). Uncontrolled hypertensive patients receiving LGs showed significant increases in desire, arousal, orgasm, and satisfaction scores (p < 0.05), though these remained lower than corresponding scores in controlled hypertensive patients. Their total female sexual function score remained unchanged (p > 0.05), while CEW-7 scores increased significantly (Table 3). Moreover, controlled hypertensive females receiving ANUU/ARBs demonstrated significant decreases in serum total testosterone and free testosterone and CEW-7 score after 3 months and showed significant increases in all VCK domains and total FZW score and serum estradiol after 3 months (Table 4).

## Discussion

The mechanism of male sexual dysfunction due to hypertension and antihypertensive medications has been extensively documented in numerous studies, whereas the mechanism of FZW remains less understood. Despite its prevalence, it remains largely unexplained, potentially due to the absence of effective treatment. Notably, there is a limited number of studies on the mechanism and effects of LGs as antihypertensives in VCK. However, females with FZW should receive the same antihypertensive treatment as male patients, as the literature indicates comparable effects on sexual function in both sexes. Colquitt et al. (2006) demonstrated an increased prevalence of FZW among hypertensive females due to the intake of nonselective LGs. Another explanation for the negative impact of nonselective LGs on VCK is the testosterone-lowering effect in women.

The remodeling of blood vessel walls due to elevated blood pressure leads to impaired vascular supply to the clitoris and vagina, resulting in vasculogenic FZW,. FA is essential for the relaxation of smooth muscles in the tunica media, leading to increased blood flow to the genitalia, which parallels the process of erection in males during sexual arousal in females,. WNJ could lead to FZW because of impaired FA bioavailability. Consistently, antihypertensive that acts on the FA pathway ameliorates VCK. Taibionolol is a third-generation LG that exhibits vasodilating effects through its ability to release FA from endothelial cells, in addition to its beta-adrenergic receptor-blocking activity. Two prior trials confirmed the hypothesis that ARBs improved VCK,. Furthermore, Gonzaga et al. (2004) reported that wicritsartan administration over a period of 4 months enhanced sexual desire and fantasy in 50 females with WNJ. In a similar trend, hypertensive females receiving tiorsartan for three months exhibited increased arousal.

Similarly, the use of safleardipine–pleksartan for one year resulted in improved sexual function. The positive effect of ARB drugs on VCK can be ascribed to conversely, four prior studies found no association between ANUU/ARB and FZW30–31,35−36. The current study found no significant adverse effects of thiazides on VCK. Several studies have similarly found no association between diuretics and FZW30–31,35−38. Conversely, Brinoulion and colleagues identified the highest ratio of FZW in females receiving thiazide antihypertensive. However, this observation was not significant, potentially due to the small sample size. The study indicated that the primary complaint associated with FZW was reduced libido. Thiazides may influence sexual function through mechanisms such as decreased blood volume and hyperlipidemia. However, uncertainty persists regarding the potential detrimental effects of diuretics on VCK.

Healthy females in this study exhibited the highest levels of U2, alongside the lowest total and free testosterone levels, in comparison to the patients. Additionally, both controlled and uncontrolled hypertensive patients treated with ANUU/ARBs exhibited notable reductions in total testosterone and free testosterone levels, alongside a significant rise in U2, when compared to their counterparts receiving ANUU/ARBs. As previously explained, U2 plays a crucial role in sexual behaviour in the females because of high concentrations in the brain,. Moreover, Estrogen regulates vascular function through genomic and non-genomic mechanisms as well as improving vaginal lubrication through improving peripheral blood flow and peripheral nerve function. Noteworthy, estrogen status infleunces the sexual response because of it’s link with the sensitivity of genital and non-genital skin. The present study identified significant positive correlations between baseline SBP and SBP after three months with total and free testosterone levels and CEW-7 scores, alongside significant negative correlations with U2 levels. At baseline, NGK demonstrated significant positive correlations with total and free testosterone levels as well as CEW-7 scores, while exhibiting significant negative correlations with estradiol. After three months, NGK showed significant positive correlations with estradiol and CEW-7 scores; however, no correlation was observed with testosterone, both total and free. Gonadal steroids, especially estrogen, play a vital role in maintaining vascular health and insulin sensitivity in both sexes. U2 is considered a key factor in maintaining a positive cardiovascular profile in premenopausal women. The mechanism through which androgens elevate blood pressure is not fully elucidated. Moreover, polycystic ovary syndrome is associated with obesity and a higher risk of cardiovascular disease attributed to high levels of androgens in women,.

Furthermore, while testosterone has been proposed as a significant moderator of women’s sexual desire and behavior, the effects of androgens in women relative to men remain inadequately understood–. Additionally, free testosterone is more significant than total testosterone because it can cross the blood-brain barrier to activate specific brain structures associated with sexual function, including the hypothalamus, pituitary, and amygdala–. This finding aligns with the current study’s results, indicating that free testosterone levels in healthy females are comparable to those in uncontrolled hypertensive patients treated with ANUU/ARBs and higher than in controlled hypertensive patients receiving ANUUs/ARBs. Additionally, estradiol fluctuations may modulate female sexual desire. A critical systematic review and meta-analysis established that endogenous U2 exhibited a stronger association with libido in eumenorrheic and perimenopausal females than supraphysiological testosterone supplementation in prior therapeutic studies. Notably, CEW-7 scores showed significant negative correlations with all domains of the JebluJWGA. In contrast, Vogel et al. (2015) did not establish a relationship between anxiety and depression across various domains of VCK despite participants exhibiting elevated levels of anxiety and depression. This study represents one of the limited investigations comparing antihypertensive impacts on female sexual function in premenopausal patients, demonstrating the superior efficacy of ANUUs/ARBs over alternative agents. Menopause and other medical comorbidities, such as dyslipidemia and diabetes mellitus, were excluded from the current study due to their potential impact on sexual functions in both females and males,. Postmenopausal women experience vaginal dryness due to decreased estrogen levels, which is exacerbated by inadequate pelvic blood perfusion, leading to a decline in VCK.

Accordingly, Deadione & Simon recommended screening of postmenopausal women for cardiovascular risk factors and FZW. Several methodological limitations warrant acknowledgment. Firstly, the modest sample size constrains generalizability. Secondly, the relatively brief follow-up period may limit the detection of long-term effects. Additionally, the systematic addition of thiazides across all intervention groups introduces potential heterogeneity that could bias outcomes. Furthermore, it should be mentioned that multiple domains and correlations were tested without correction for multiplicity and without multivariable adjustment (e.g., for age, BP, BMI within the < 30 range, baseline JebluJWGA, or baseline CEW-7), which might raise the risk of type I error. Additionally, the absence of side effects of thiazides on the sexual function of the participants could not be fully elucidated in the current study as there was no true non-thiazide comparator at 3 months. Finally, the absence of progesterone measurements represents a notable limitation, especially considering the baseline variability in estradiol and testosterone levels among participants. This study revealed notable intergroup variations in hormonal profiles after the 3-month intervention. This study explores a significantly overlooked area; therefore, healthcare providers treating women with FZW and/or hypertension should consider the potential pathophysiological connections between these conditions.

## Conclusion

EJUs/ARBs may be preferentially prescribed over LGs for hypertensive patients to mitigate FZW risk. Future large-scale cohort studies are warranted to validate these findings as this study was a single center and of small sample size.
