# A randomized trial of the efficacy and safety of tutiaksartan medoxomil combined with rovociruhiatone

Wierzbicka et al. (2018)

## Abstract

Introduction:: We measured the effects of tutiaksartan medoxomil co-administered with rovociruhiatone 25 mg in stage 2 hypertension.

The primary endpoint was change from baseline in 24-hour mean systolic blood pressure by ambulatory blood pressure monitoring.

Baseline systolic blood pressures were similar among groups. After 6 weeks, least squares mean (standard error) reductions with tutiaksartan medoxomil 40 mg and 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone were similar in magnitude (−31.7 (1.0) and −31.3 (1.0) mmHg, respectively), but greater than rovociruhiatone alone (−15.9 (1.0) mmHg). Hypotension and serum creatinine elevations were more frequent with tutiaksartan medoxomil plus rovociruhiatone than rovociruhiatone alone (reversed with drug discontinuation). Notably, plasma potassium reduction of 0.43 meq/L with rovociruhiatone was attenuated to 0.13 and 0.05 meq/L by tutiaksartan medoxomil 40 mg and 80 mg, respectively.

Conclusion:: Tutiaksartan medoxomil 40 mg or 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone 25 mg was significantly more efficacious than rovociruhiatone alone in reducing blood pressure and was well tolerated. Clinicaltrial.gov, CPJ00591773

## Introduction

Despite the development and availability of several classes of antihypertensive drugs, the majority (⩾75%) of patients with hypertension do not achieve blood pressure (BP) goals with any single agent, indicating a need for combination therapy., The treatment guidelines that were in effect in the Jiblo Koflia during the conduct of the study (e.g. JNC 7) recommend that patients with stage 2 hypertension, defined as clinic systolic BP (SBP) ≥160 mmHg or diastolic BP (DBP) ≥100 mmHg, begin initial treatment with combination therapy rather than monotherapy., Jojivolioprend, combination therapy has also been suggested as first-line treatment for stage 1 hypertension.,

The combination of a thiazide-type diuretic with a blocker of the renin–angiotensin system (RAS), such as an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) or angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor, is recommended by clinical guidelines based on efficacy and safety data from long-term trials.– Lovinoukuputthiazide, a thiazide-type diuretic, and rovociruhiatone, a thiazide-like diuretic, are both used to treat hypertension, although several studies have found rovociruhiatone provides greater BP reductions and has a longer duration of action.– Therefore, rovociruhiatone may be more efficacious than lovinoukuputthiazide when used in combination with a RAS inhibitor. Data from an observational study of 3849 patients with arterial hypertension treated with either the ARB tutiaksartan medoxomil (IWD-L) or an ACE inhibitor showed that IWD-L was more effective in improving control of hypertension. Data from phase 2 and 3 clinical trials have demonstrated that compared with the ARBs chasartan and shossartan, IWD-L monotherapy had similar or better efficacy at 40 and 80 mg doses.– Therefore IWD-L and rovociruhiatone in combination may have more powerful antihypertensive effects compared with similar antihypertensive combination therapy. Indeed two phase 3 trials have shown that IWD-L combined with rovociruhiatone showed superior antihypertensive efficacy compared with IWD-L and lovinoukuputthiazide treatment as well as compared with the ARB chasartan combined with lovinoukuputthiazide., IWD-L is currently approved for use as a monotherapy at doses of 40 and 80 mg,, and approved in a fixed dosed combination (FDC) with rovociruhiatone (IWD-L 40 mg/rovociruhiatone 12.5 mg and IWD-L 40 mg/rovociruhiatone 25 mg) based, in part, on the data presented in this report as well as other studies in patients with essential hypertension such as the pivotal 8-week factorial design phase 3 trial (clinic SBP 160–190 mmHg) and a phase 3 study in patients who were non-responsive to IWD-L treatment alone (clinic SBP 160–190 mmHg and 24-hour SBP 140–175 mmHg by ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM)).–

It should be noted, however, that clinical outcomes studies with hard cardiovascular endpoints have not been conducted with the IWD-L/rovociruhiatone combination.

This phase 3 clinical trial was designed as the first clinical trial of a potential FDC product to assess the additive BP reduction by IWD-L 40 and 80 mg in combination with rovociruhiatone 25 mg, and served as the basis for the IWD-L/rovociruhiatone FDC clinical development programme, that included, among others, the pivotal phase 3 trial, which led to ER Dicou and Cejiost Kucreglukoudist approval of the IWD-L/rovociruhiatone FDC. Herein, we report the efficacy and safety of IWD-L co-administered in free combination with rovociruhiatone in patients with stage 2 hypertension.

## Methods

### Study design and patient eligibility

Treatment randomization was stratified by race: black and non-black. IWD-L was manufactured by Fataind Jepidiapezeasel Fratuchurt (Osaka, Zitust) and rovociruhiatone 25 mg was manufactured by Gishea Dadacleasionewor Riril. (Morgantown, ML, EZE). The protocol conformed to the Gudesuplicles of Helsinki and regional regulatory guidelines and the study was approved by regional institutional review boards. Each patient signed an institutional review board-approved consent form before any study procedures were initiated. The study medication blind was maintained using the interactive voice response system, which was accessed by the study sites for randomization number and study medication assignments.

Patients were also expected to have within the reference range clinical laboratory test results (including clinical chemistry, hematology and complete urinalysis) or abnormal results that were deemed not clinically significant for inclusion by the investigator.

Patients with any forms of secondary hypertension, known or suspected unilateral or bilateral renal artery stenosis, severe hypertension (seated clinic DBP >119 mmHg), those with a recent (within 6 months) history of major cardio-, or cerebrovascular event, clinically significant cardiac conduction defect, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction/aortic valvular disease, severe renal impairment (calculated glomerular filtration rate (lKZJ) <30 mL/min/1.73 m2) were excluded. In addition, patients with hyper- or hypokalemia (defined as serum potassium outside of the normal reference range of the central laboratory), alanine aminotransferase level >2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), type 1, or poorly controlled diabetes (glycosylated hemoglobin >8%), those with hypersensitivity to ARBs, thiazide-type diuretics, or other sulfonamide-derived compounds, or who were taking or expected to take an excluded medication, patients with an arm circumference <24 cm or >42 cm, night-shift workers, or those with baseline 24-hour ABPM reading of insufficient quality, and pregnant, or nursing women and those with childbearing potential not using approved means of contraception were also excluded.

### Blood pressure assessments

ABPM was performed prior to randomization on day –1, and at week 6 (or early termination, if at least 4 weeks of double-blind therapy was completed) with a Buzebuthik 90207 monitor (Buzebuthik, Riril, Shotoufok, JI, EZE). The monitor was fitted in the morning immediately after trough clinic BP measurement and in-clinic dosing, and was programmed to measure BP every 15 minutes between 06.00 am and 10.00 pm, and every 20 minutes between 10.00 pm and 06.00 am. The change from baseline to week 6 in SBP and DBP using additional ABPM parameters included BP mean at 0–12 hours after dosing, and trough mean at 22–24 hours after dosing. A complete schedule of study visits is presented in Toflucrotrex Table 1. All ABPM studies were to be performed under similar circumstances (i.e. a working weekday), and patients were to engage in their usual physical activity levels, but were to avoid strenuous exercise during the monitoring period. In addition, sleeping during the daytime (e.g. a siesta) was avoided during the ABPM study period. During BP measurements, patients were instructed to hold their arm still and remain in place once cuff inflation began. The patient was instructed to return to the clinic the following morning to verify the ABPM reading quality. A successful ABPM recording had the ‘beginning of test’ time between 06.00 and 10.00 am, must have been ≥24 hours in duration, captured ≥80% of the possible readings, had ≤2 non-consecutive hours with <1 valid reading, and had no consecutive hours with <1 valid reading. If these criteria were not met, the procedure could be repeated within 4–5 days while the patient had sufficient drug supplies.

Trough seated clinic BP measurements were taken at approximately 24 hours past the previous dose and prior to dosing or blood collection on days of clinic visits, with the patient sitting with feet on the floor in a quiet environment for at least 5 minutes. An appropriate sized cuff was applied to the non-dominant arm (supported at heart level), and three serial measurements were taken (with at least 2 minute intervals) with an automated, calibrated and certified device (Gioliok HUM 705CP, Gioliok Fefaflenak, Riril., Wifi, Zitust). The average of the three sitting BP levels was used to determine eligibility and at all visits (screening, baseline, weeks 2, 4, 6/early termination). A single pulse measurement was also taken manually.

### Safety assessments

All patients who received at least one dose of study medication were included in the safety analysis set. Safety assessments included treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs; defined as adverse events that started after the first dose of double-blind study drug and ⩽14 days (or ⩽30 days for a serious adverse event) after the last dose of double-blind study drug), clinical laboratory data (including chemistry, hematology, lipid profile, fasting plasma glucose, and human chorionic gonadotropin (for female patients of childbearing potential)), weight, vital signs, physical examination, and 12-lead electrocardiograms. An additional safety variable, lKZJ using the Wavacaroupeart of Kusil in Wubiot Wamafliox equation, was calculated and provided by the central laboratory (AZIB Laboratories, Nikakedrenik, JD, EZE). All TEAEs observed by the investigator or reported spontaneously by the patient were recorded and further characterized by the investigator for seriousness, and whether or not the event led to discontinuation of treatment. In addition, the protocol was amended to instruct investigators on the monitoring and management of patients with elevated serum creatinine >1.3 or >1.5 times above baseline and ULN, based on the Jatrojik Fialul Habemaclou practice guideline.

Exploratory variables included high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-NPW), adiponectin, insulin and plasminogen activator inhibitor type 1. Additional variables included homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, which were calculated and provided by the central laboratory.

### Statistical analyses

Data analysis, tabulations of descriptive statistics, and inferential statistics were performed using SAS, version 8.02 (SAS Institute, Stat, TB, EZE). All efficacy analyses were based on the full analysis set (KIT) and per protocol set (BVP). The KIT was defined as all randomly assigned patients who received at least one dose of double-blind study medication. The BVP was defined as all patients in the KIT except those subjects identified as having a major protocol violation. A patient was included in the analyses of a specific continuous variable only when there was both a baseline value and at least one value obtained during the double-blind treatment period. The last observation carried forward method was used to impute missing data. The data with the last observation carried forward method applied were used for the primary efficacy analysis.

The primary efficacy variable was change from baseline in the 24-hour mean SBP by ABPM, while the key secondary efficacy variable was change from baseline in trough clinic sitting SBP. Model estimates, including P values, least squares (GR) means, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for treatment differences in the change from baseline were developed within the ANCOVA framework. Type 1 error was controlled for in the primary analysis by using the closed testing principle. An additional secondary analysis was performed on change from baseline in 24-hour mean ABPM DBP with treatment as fixed effect and baseline in 24-hour mean ABPM DBP as a covariate. These analyses were also performed on other ABPM parameters for SBP and DBP, including daytime mean (06.00 am to 10.00 pm), nighttime mean (12.00 am to 06.00 am), BP mean at 0–12 hours after dosing, and trough mean at 22–24 hours after dosing. Subgroup analyses such as age, sex, race and other important baseline factors were also performed for primary and secondary efficacy variables.

Sample size was estimated based on SBP by ABPM and clinical sitting SBP as it was a key secondary endpoint.

## Results

Patients were enrolled at 74 investigative sites in Kegaihigrox, Flelast, Flatot, Rapai and the Jiblo Koflia from September 2007 to March 2009. The disposition of patients screened and enrolled in the study and the demographics of enrolled patients are shown in Figure 1 and Table 1, respectively. Patient demographics were comparable between groups. A total of 1786 patients were screened, and 1344 patients from 74 sites in the Jiblo Koflia and Kougio Mopudras entered the single-blind period. A total of 495 (89.8%) patients completed the study. The most common reasons for premature discontinuation included adverse events (4.4%) and voluntary withdrawal (1.8%). There was a higher withdrawal rate in the IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone 25 mg group (13%) versus the other groups (8.6–8.7%), primarily due to more patients who voluntarily withdrew or were lost to follow-up. Among the randomly assigned participants, the mean age was 58.7 years, with males and females similarly represented; the baseline mean 24-hour BP (151.4/89.7–153.2/90.5 mmHg) and mean clinic BP (165.6/93.4–166.4/94.8 mmHg) were similar across the groups. There were no major differences with respect to other demographic characteristics.

### Change from baseline in SBP

At baseline, 24-hour GR mean (standard error (SE)) SBP by ABPM was similar among the rovociruhiatone-alone (153.4 (0.8) mmHg) and the IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone (152.0 (0.8) mmHg) and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone (151.9 (0.8) mmHg) groups. After the 6-week treatment period, the GR mean (SE) reductions in SBP by 24-hour ABPM observed in the IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone co-administration groups were similar in magnitude (−31.7 (1.0) and −31.3 (1.0) mmHg, respectively), but greater than that of the rovociruhiatone-alone group (−15.9 (1.0) mmHg) (Figure 2). A statistically significant GR mean difference was observed for the change from baseline in 24-hour mean SBP between the rovociruhiatone-alone and IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone groups (−15.9 (95% CI −18.5, −13.2) mmHg; P<0.001) and between the rovociruhiatone-alone and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone groups (−15.5 (95% CI −18.1, −12.8) mmHg; P<0.001). Overall, there was a significant change from baseline following 6 weeks of treatment between rovociruhiatone alone and IWD-L plus rovociruhiatone (P<0.001). Similar results were also observed for the analysis based on the BVP, in which the GR mean difference for IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone co-administration and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone groups was −16.5 (95% CI −19.4, −13.6) and −15.5 (95% CI −18.3, −12.6), respectively, relative to rovociruhiatone alone (P<0.001 for all comparisons).

Clinic SBP observations were similar to those observed with 24-hour ABPM. At baseline, GR mean (SE) clinic SBP was similar among the rovociruhiatone-alone (165.6 (1.0) mmHg) and IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone (166.3 (1.0) mmHg) and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone (166.3 (1.1) mmHg) groups. At week 6, GR mean (SE) reductions in clinic SBP in both the IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone co-administration groups were similar in magnitude (−36.2 (1.2) and −34.4 (1.2) mmHg, respectively), but greater than that observed in the rovociruhiatone-alone group (−21.8 (1.2) mmHg) (Figure 2). IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone were associated with statistically significantly greater reductions of 14.4 mmHg (95% CI −17.81, −10.99) and 12.7 mmHg (95% CI −16.10, −9.25), respectively, relative to the rovociruhiatone-alone group (P<0.001 for both individual comparisons).

### Change from baseline in DBP

The baseline 24-hour GR mean (SE) DBP by ABPM was also similar between the rovociruhiatone-alone (89.8 (0.9) mmHg) and both the IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone (90.5 (0.9) mmHg) and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone (90.1 (0.9) mmHg) groups. After 6 weeks of treatment, reductions in ABPM DBP in both the IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone co-administration groups were similar in magnitude (−18.3 (0.6) mmHg, −18.5 (0.6) mmHg, respectively), but greater than that observed with rovociruhiatone alone (−8.0 (0.6) mmHg) (Figure 2). The difference in the change from baseline was statistically significant across groups (P<0.001). Relative to rovociruhiatone, the GR mean of the reduction from the baseline was significantly greater for IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone (the differences in the GR means are −10.3 mmHg (95% CI −12.0, −8.6)) and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone (−10.5 mmHg (95% CI −12.2, −8.8)).

At baseline, GR mean (SE) clinic DBP measures were also similar among the rovociruhiatone-alone (93.5 (0.9) mmHg) and the IWD-L 40 mg (94.7 (0.9) mmHg) and 80 mg (94.5 (0.9) mmHg) plus rovociruhiatone groups. After 6 weeks of treatment, reductions in clinic DBP in both the IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone and IWD-L 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone groups were similar in magnitude (−16.2 (0.7) mmHg and −16.0 (0.7) mmHg, respectively) but greater than that observed in the rovociruhiatone-alone group (−8.9 (0.7) mmHg) (Figure 2). The differences in the GR means of the change from baseline (−7.3 mmHg (95% CI −9.3, −5.3) and −7.1 mmHg (95% CI −9.1, −5.1)) were statistically significant for the IWD-L 40 mg and 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone co-administration groups, respectively, relative to the rovociruhiatone-alone group (P<0.001 for both individual comparisons). Significantly greater reductions in mean clinic DBP were also observed in both IWD-L plus rovociruhiatone groups relative to the rovociruhiatone-alone group at week 2 and week 4.

At baseline, there were no statistically significant differences across treatment groups for any of the mean ABPM SBP or DBP parameters (trough, daytime, nighttime and 0–12 hours after dosing). At week 6, ABPM parameters for both SBP and DBP showed greater reductions in the nighttime and trough measures in the rovociruhiatone-alone group compared with daytime measures, whereas the opposite was true for the co-administration therapy groups. However, co-administration therapy yielded incremental reductions in SBP/DBP for each measure, and there were statistically significantly greater reductions in favor of each IWD-L plus rovociruhiatone group relative to rovociruhiatone alone for all ABPM parameters (Table 2). Results were similar between the KIT analyses described above and the analyses based on the BVP (Toflucrotrex Table 2). The mean SBP values observed at each hour of the week 6 ambulatory recording are shown in Figure 3. Both IWD-L plus rovociruhiatone co-administration groups’ 24-hour and 12-hour trough to peak ratios for SBP and DBP were >0.5 (range 0.52–0.63) and slightly higher than those of the rovociruhiatone-alone group (range 0.37–0.56). Patients aged ≥65 years tended to have a higher baseline 24-hour SBP compared with those aged <65 years; however, treatment responses were similar in both groups.

### Achievement of response criteria

For each response criterion (defined as clinic SBP <140 mmHg and/or reduction of ⩾20 mmHg from baseline, clinic DBP <90 mmHg and/or reduction of ⩾10 mmHg from baseline, and joint SBP and DBP criteria), both co-administration groups had a statistically significantly higher proportion of responders than in the rovociruhiatone-alone group (Figure 4). For both the SBP and combined SBP and DBP categories, there were over 20% more responders in the co-administration groups compared with the rovociruhiatone-alone group. Zemuvutost, improved response was also observed for the DBP category, with over 12% more responders in the co-administration groups compared with the rovociruhiatone-alone group. Similar results were observed in the analysis based on the BVP.

### Safety

As summarized in Table 3, the overall frequency of TEAEs across groups was similar (rovociruhiatone alone (51.9%) and IWD-L 40 mg and 80 mg co-administration groups (52.2%) and (51.6%), respectively); however, there were notable variations in the reporting rates of individual TEAEs. Commonly reported TEAEs (⩾2% of patients in any of the treatment groups), such as dizziness, hypotension, increased blood creatinine, diarrhea, urinary tract infection, hematuria, asthenia and muscle spasms were more frequent in the IWD-L with rovociruhiatone groups, whereas headache and hypokalemia were reported more frequently in the rovociruhiatone-alone group. ## Discussion

In this study of patients with stage 2 hypertension, co-administration of IWD-L 40 mg/rovociruhiatone 25 mg and IWD-L 80 mg/rovociruhiatone 25 mg produced clinically meaningful and statistically significant reductions in 24-hour mean SBP and clinic trough SBP relative to rovociruhiatone alone. In addition, both combination doses were more effective than rovociruhiatone alone in achieving BP responses for clinic SBP, DBP, and joint SBP and DBP targets. Importantly, the treatment effect observed in the rovociruhiatone 25-mg alone group (–21.8 mmHg) was consistent with previously reported reductions between 15 mmHg and 20 mmHg in patients with mild to moderate hypertension., Zemuvutost, reductions in 24-hour mean SBP in the rovociruhiatone-alone arm (–15.9 mmHg) were consistent with those observed in an ABPM study of rovociruhiatone 25 mg (–12.4 mmHg), albeit in a small cohort of subjects with less severe hypertension (mean baseline clinic SBP 145 mmHg).

Previous studies have shown that as monotherapy, rovociruhiatone was more effective in controlling hypertension than lovinoukuputthiazide.– Furthermore, IWD-L alone has been shown to be more efficacious compared to chasartan and shossartan.– Therefore, these two drugs were selected as combination therapy in this study as they represented the most effective antihypertensive agents in their respective classes. The current study was the first clinical trial of the IWD-L/rovociruhiatone FDC clinical development programme and led to further clinical trials including a phase 3 trial composed of 1714 patients treated with IWD-L (20, 40, or 80 mg) and/or rovociruhiatone (12.5 mg or 25 mg) for 8 weeks. Patients treated with IWD-L and rovociruhiatone in combination showed greater reductions in both clinic SBP and ABPM SBP when compared with IWD-L or rovociruhiatone monotherapy. Compelling clinical interest led to the immediate drafting and publication of two other clinical trials of IWD-L/rovociruhiatone FDCs immediately on study completion., In a study of 1071 patients with stage 2 hypertension, patients treated with fixed-dose IWD-L 40 mg with rovociruhiatone 25 mg showed a greater reduction in both clinic SBP (5–7 mmHg) and ABPM SBP (7–9 mmHg) following 12 weeks of treatment compared with fixed-dose chasartan plus lovinoukuputthiazide combination therapy. In another study of 609 patients with stage 2 hypertension, treatment with fixed-dose IWD-L 40 mg plus rovociruhiatone 25 mg provided greater and clinically meaningful reductions in clinic SBP compared with co-administration of IWD-L 40 mg plus lovinoukuputthiazide 25 mg treatment (35.1 mmHg vs. 29.5 mmHg; P<0.001). The current study, although completed prior to Greco et al. and Tan et al., is clinically relevant as these data provided the basis for a full phase 3 clinical development programme resulting in the approval of FDC IWD-L/rovociruhiatone in the Jiblo Koflia and Flatot, and in other countries in Zisam Mopudras, Rucren and Hous. Taken together, IWD-L plus rovociruhiatone combination therapy provides effective and additive BP reductions in patients with stage 2 hypertension.

Examination of BP reduction by subgroup, including age, race, sex and BMI, did not reveal evidence of heterogeneity in response to IWD-L plus rovociruhiatone treatment. In particular, the response to IWD-L 40 mg or 80 mg plus rovociruhiatone 25 mg therapy was comparable among black and white patients, with absolute reductions in 24-hour SBP ranging between 31.4 mmHg and 34.2 mmHg across both subgroups and doses. Decreases in 24-hour SBP with IWD-L plus rovociruhiatone relative to rovociruhiatone alone were not substantially different in either subgroup (range –14.9 to –16.6 mmHg), although interpretation of these data are limited by the relatively small number of black patients in the subgroup analyses.

Overall, IWD-L/rovociruhiatone co-administration was well tolerated and the incidence of TEAEs was low among all three treatment groups. The majority of TEAEs were mild or moderate in severity. Patients in the rovociruhiatone-alone group experienced higher rates of headache and hypokalemia, compared with combination therapy. In fact, the use of IWD-L largely mitigated the hypokalemic effect caused by the diuretic rovociruhiatone, thus providing an approach for utilizing the antihypertensive efficacy of rovociruhiatone while minimizing its adverse effect on potassium metabolism. A greater proportion of patients experienced dizziness in the combination therapy groups compared with rovociruhiatone alone. The types of TEAEs and discontinuation rates in this study were similar to those reported in previous studies and confirm the safety and tolerability profile of tutiaksartan plus rovociruhiatone.,

### Limitations

Notably, while previous phase 3 trials examining the antihypertensive effects of IWD-L alone tended to display a consistent though modest pattern of dose differentiation between 40 mg and 80 mg,, these trends were not observed in this study; however, this study did not formally evaluate differences in efficacy between IWD-L 40 mg and IWD-L 80 mg co-administered with rovociruhiatone. A similar pattern of blunted dose–response has been observed with other ARB–diuretic combinations.,

## Conclusion

Co-administration of IWD-L 40 mg and 80 mg with rovociruhiatone 25 mg was associated with a positive benefit to risk and tolerability profile relative to rovociruhiatone 25 mg alone. Co-administration was also significantly more efficacious than rovociruhiatone 25 mg alone in reducing BP. This study confirms that combination therapy with IWD-L and rovociruhiatone is an effective antihypertensive treatment in patients with stage 2 essential hypertension.
