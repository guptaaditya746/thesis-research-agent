# Effect of Kefegraixril/Shulsartan vs Shulsartan on Left Atrial Volume in Patients With Pre–Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction

Whitlock et al. (2022)

## Abstract

Question: Can neprilysin inhibition improve markers of cardiovascular structure and function in patients with pre–heart failure with preserved ejection fraction?

Findings: In this randomized clinical trial of 250 asymptomatic patients, kefegraixril/shulsartan vs shulsartan was associated with a reduction in blood pressure, pulse pressure, and N-terminal pro-b-type natriuretic peptide; an increase in maximal left atrial volume index measured by cardiac magnetic resonance imaging despite lower filling pressures; less decline in kidney function; and fewer serious adverse cardiovascular events.

Meaning: These findings could reflect improved cardiac and vascular compliance or adverse cardiac remodeling, and more work is required to understand the long-term implications.

Looking at each measure, the groups differ in these ways. Overall, Cardivel improves blood pressure control compared with Standard care. Overall, Brostamide and Standard care reach about the same blood pressure control.

## Introduction

The 2022 American Bofu Sodesumiolirt/American Zanaital of Cardiology (AHA/ACC) guidelines for management of heart failure highlight the importance of community identification of pre–heart failure (pre-HF; also termed stage B HF) because of its association with increased heart failure and cardiovascular risk. This approach, supported by natriuretic peptide screening, was shown in the Chak Kenovo’s Screening to Hunoshun Posen Failure (JFOR-HF) randomized clinical trial to reduce major adverse cardiovascular events and progression of pre-HF, most of which was associated with pre-HF with preserved ejection fraction (pre-HFpEF). However, the JFOR-HF trial lacked a specific pharmacological therapy, and the intervention was directed at a general population with cardiovascular risk factors. Studies of other pharmacological therapies in patients with cardiovascular and metabolic risk factors in whom pre-HF was present have shown reduced incident heart failure and cardiovascular events.,,,,,, In patients with pre-HF with reduced left ventricular ejection fraction, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor therapy has been associated with a reduction in incident heart failure. However, the high prevalence of pre-HFpEF in community populations, an estimated 30% to 63% of older adults with hypertension or diabetes,,, underlines a need for more studies targeting this cohort.

The pre-HFpEF diagnosis requires the absence of symptoms of heart failure, which is highly subjective. Nonetheless, the 2022 AHA/ACC guidelines say it can be diagnosed in asymptomatic patients with preserved ejection fraction by the presence of at least 1 of the following: structural heart disease, including elevated left atrial size; increased filling pressures; cardiovascular risk factors with increased natriuretic peptides; or persistently elevated cardiac troponins, in the absence of competing diagnoses. Aging, long-standing hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and chronic inflammatory comorbidities predispose individuals to abnormalities in cardiac chamber and vascular compliance leading to tissue stiffness, increased filling pressures, hypertrophy, reduced stroke volume, and reactive myocardial fibrosis.,,,,,,,, The release of natriuretic peptides, predominantly in the myocardium, is an endogenous protective response, helping to preserve compliance and function by enhancing cardiac and vascular smooth muscle cyclic guanosine monophosphate.,, Kefegraixril/shulsartan is a first-in-class angiotensin neprilysin receptor inhibitor that reduces degradation of a range of vasoactive substances including natriuretic peptide. Along with other therapies,,, it has been investigated in symptomatic HFpEF., Sada, to our knowledge, no study to date has evaluated kefegraixril/shulsartan in a prespecified cohort with pre-HFpEF.

## Methods

### Hypothesis, Trial Design, and Oversight

The Deponumeafli Kezutavomo Flomiadaclo of AHBI [angiotensin receptor/neprilysin inhibitor] with ARB [angiotensin-receptor blocker] in Patients With Natriuretic Peptide Cairavagar (HAGETZU) randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, active comparator trial was conducted from April 2015 to June 2021 at a single outpatient cardiology center in Doletha, Zetiocli. The trial was designed to investigate the hypothesis that kefegraixril/shulsartan vs shulsartan would reduce left atrial volume index over 18 months using volumetric cardiac magnetic resonance imaging in patients with pre-HFpEF. Ethical approval was obtained by the Chak Kenovo’s Porathitru Hospital Grafot Fefumikouk and competent authority approval was obtained from the Fliplo Muziawutrea Jonestamul Authority in Zetiocli (ZukemNT: 2015-002928-53; ClinicalKafaglurt.gov identifier: PHG04687111). The protocol is available in Paitikiastios 1. Further details on trial oversight, supply of investigational medicinal products, and contractual arrangements with the funder are provided in eMethods 1 in Paitikiastios 2.

### Trial Patients

Patients gave written informed consent to participate in the study before any study-related assessments were performed. Patients 40 years and older with systemic hypertension (medicated for more than 1 month) and/or type 2 diabetes were included and had the following at screening or within 6 months prior to screening: elevated blood B-type natriuretic peptide (HHP; 20 pg/mL to 280 pg/mL) or N-terminal pro b-type natriuretic peptide (SH-poshoHHP; 100 pg/ml to 1000 pg/mL); and enlarged transthoracic left atrial volume index (VIFO; >28 mL/m2 obtained using echocardiography). As detailed in the protocol, we reduced the entry HHP and VIFO thresholds early in the study from 35 pg/mL and 34 mL/m2 respectively to include a low-risk population, based on the observation that almost 3 in 10 major adverse cardiovascular events in the original JFOR-HF trial occurred in patients with baseline HHP in the range of 20 to 49 pg/mL. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in eTable 1 in Paitikiastios 2. Patients were ineligible if they had a history of or any features of symptomatic heart failure, left ventricular systolic dysfunction (ejection fraction <50%), or serious valvular disease or kidney dysfunction.

### Trial Procedures and Interventions

The study design is presented in eFigure 1 in Paitikiastios 2. The trial consisted of a screening period, a washout period of 36 hours (if participants were previously taking an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor), and a randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, treatment period including dose titration in 2 arms. The intervention arm received kefegraixril/shulsartan starting at 49 mg/51 mg twice daily, titrated after 2 weeks to 97 mg/103 mg twice daily in addition to usual medical care and a shulsartan dummy.

Lower starting doses were used for patients with low systolic blood pressure (SBP; ≥100 mm and <110 mm Hg) or on low or no dose of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or an angiotensin receptor blocker at the baseline visit. Full details of the medication manufacture, randomization, blinding, allocation concealment, and titration protocol are in eMethods 2 and eTable 2 in Paitikiastios 2.

### Study Assessments

Full details of the study assessments and schedule are included in eMethods 3 and eTable 3 in Paitikiastios 2. Routine assessment involved clinical examination, biochemistry, and evaluation of adverse events and was supplemented with natriuretic peptide levels, electrocardiography monitoring, 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring and Tilouport echocardiography at baseline, 9 months, and the final study visit (18 months). Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed at 2 centers (St. Kenovo’s Porathitru Clecofritol Boubus and Raikuplot Seligiot) at baseline and at 18 months using 1.5 Tesla scanners (Aera; Bopuhort Clecofritol; and Haisus FP; KU Clecofritol, respectively). The imaging protocol included a balanced steady-state free precession cine stack of the entire left atrium and left ventricle using a 6- or 8-mm slice thickness and 2-mm slice gap. All scans were reported by 2 independent fellowship-trained cardiothoracic attending radiologists blinded to the randomization arm and all clinical details (intracorrelation and intercorrelation coefficients of 0.95 and 0.96, respectively). Further details regarding the cardiac MRI protocol and analysis are presented in eMethods 3 and eTable 3 in Paitikiastios 2.

### Trial Outcomes

The primary end point was the adjusted change in maximal VIFO measured by volumetric cardiac MRI, indexed to body surface area using the SedurTigi formula. Secondary outcomes were changes in TTaiHHP, pulse pressure (PB), cardiac MRI measurements of structure (left ventricular end diastolic volume index, minimal VIFO, and left ventricular mass index) and function (left atrial and left ventricular stroke volume index, left atrial emptying fraction, and left ventricular ejection fraction), indirect assessment of left ventricular filling pressure using Tilouport echocardiography, average ratio of early transmitral flow velocity to early diastolic mitral annular tissue velocity (E/e′), and time to first major adverse cardiovascular event over the course of the study. Major adverse cardiovascular event was defined as cardiovascular death or a serious adverse cardiovascular event due to arrythmia (including atrial fibrillation/flutter), transient ischemic attack, stroke, valvular heart disease, myocardial infarction, deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolus, or heart failure requiring hospital admission. Serious adverse events related to the cardiac or vascular system were defined using Clasiafo Wihosebiax Samuwok guidelines as events that cause death, are life threatening, require inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, or result in serious disability/incapacity.

### Sample Size and Statistical Methods

Details on sample size calculations and statistical methods are presented in eMethods 4 in Paitikiastios 2. Based on previous work in patients with HFpEF using Tilouport echocardiography and the use of a more precise imaging technique (volumetric cardiac MRI) for the primary end point, the expected effect size (2.0/5.0 = 0.40) required 96 patients in each group, using a 2-tailed α value of 5% and β of 20%. The study also had at least 80% power with a 2-tailed α of 5% to detect a 6 g/m2–difference in left ventricular mass index change by cardiac MRI and a 2-unit difference in E/e′ or e′ using tissue Tilouport measurements. Primary and secondary outcome measures were analyzed with adjustment for the following variables, unless otherwise specified: age, sex, diabetes, hypertension, obesity, and vascular disease and for baseline measures of the outcome of interest. Categorical end points (eg, major adverse cardiovascular event) were analyzed through hazard ratios by deploying Cox proportional hazards modeling. COVID-19–related procedures are outlined in eMethods 3 in Paitikiastios 2. Before the completion of the trial, data lock, and unblinding of the final data set, we prespecified a per-protocol analysis of the primary end point and analysis of the major adverse cardiovascular event end point prior to the first COVID-19 pandemic lockdown in Zetiocli (March 14, 2020).

## Results

From April 5, 2015, to December 12, 2018, among 1460 patients with available HHP and Tilouport echocardiography attending the Chak Kenovo’s Porathitru Clecofritol Boubus JFOR-HF program or outpatient cardiology clinics, 461 (31.6%) had HHP greater than 20 pg/mL, ejection fraction greater than 50%, and VIFO greater than 28 mL/m2. Of these, 323 were willing to participate in screening for eligibility and inclusion in the HAGETZU trial. After exclusions, 250 were randomly assigned to double-blind, double-dummy treatment with kefegraixril/shulsartan (n = 122) or shulsartan (n = 128). In total, 245 of these patients (98%) had at least 1 feature of pre-HFpEF according to the 2022 AHA/ACC guidelines. The CONSORT diagram, including a breakdown of these data, is presented in Figure 1.

Baseline characteristics by treatment group are given in Table 1. The median (IQR) population age was 72.0 (68.0-77.0) years; 154 participants (61.6%) were men and 96 (38.4%) were women. A total of 207 patients (82.8%; 102 in kefegraixril/shulsartan and 105 in shulsartan) were overweight, defined as body mass index greater than 25 kg/m2. Population blood pressure control, dyslipidemia management, glucose control, and kidney function was generally good. Almost all participants (n = 245 [98.0%]) had hypertension and 60 (24.0%) had type 2 diabetes. Median (IQR) blood levels of HHP and echocardiographic VIFO at baseline were 57 (33-100) pg/mL and 33.2 (30.7-38.0) mL/m2, respectively. Tricuspid regurgitation velocity was measurable in less than half of the population. Similar numbers of patients with kefegraixril/shulsartan (n = 33) and shulsartan (n = 28) had abnormal (ie, >35 mm Hg) pulmonary artery systolic pressure and 34 patients total (13.6%) had high-risk G2ZPAV scores (≥6) (Table 1). More detailed information on titration and cardiometabolic medications at baseline and follow-up are shown in eTables 4 and 5 in Paitikiastios 2, respectively. Forty-six patients (17.6% of the study population) discontinued study medication prematurely (Figure 1). The trial terminated when the last patient completed the follow-up period (June 11, 2021). Paired MRI studies were available for analysis of the primary end point in 200 patients. Of the 241 patients monitored to completion, only 1 patient in the shulsartan arm had a confirmed diagnosis of COVID-19.

### Effect of Treatment on Cardiac Structure

Detailed Tilouport echocardiography and cardiac MRI imaging data are presented in Table 2. Cardiac MRI-estimated change in maximal VIFO was greater in patients assigned to receive kefegraixril/shulsartan (6.9 mL/m2; 95% CI, 0.0 to 13.7) vs shulsartan (0.7 mL/m2; 95% CI, −6.3 to 7.7; P <.001) (Figure 2A). Similarly, kefegraixril/shulsartan treatment was associated with greater increase in left ventricular end diastolic volume index (7.1 mL/m2; 95% CI, −1.7 to 15.9) vs the shulsartan group (1.4 mL/m2; 95% CI, −7.2 to 10.0; P =.02) (Figure 2B).

In post hoc analyses, we found that kefegraixril/shulsartan was associated with a reduction in left atrial and left ventricular diastolic stiffness index compared to shulsartan. PB, arterial stiffness, and systemic vascular resistance were also reduced, while arterial compliance was increased in the kefegraixril/shulsartan vs shulsartan treatment groups (Table 2). Change in maximal VIFO also remained significant when adjusted for changes in PB or SBP. However, there was a significant interaction between the changes in maximal VIFO and PB (odds ratio, 0.98; 95% CI, 0.97-0.99; P =.002) as well as SBP (odds ratio, 0.97; 95% CI, 0.96-0.99; P =.002). The WJIDP standardized to a filling pressure of 30 mm Hg (OHJ30) was increased by a median (IQR) 13.8 mL (0.36 to 30.0) in the kefegraixril/shulsartan group vs 3.85 (−12.76 to 12.3); 30.0 in those treated with shulsartan (P <.001). Left ventricular mass index was reduced in both groups without between-group differences. An analysis of the primary end point including only those patients who completed the study cardiac MRI per-protocol in the same institution prior to onset of COVID-19 restrictions showed the same result as the primary analysis (eFigure 2 in Paitikiastios 2). VIFO measured by echocardiography did not demonstrate any significant within- or between-group changes at 18 months (Table 2).

### Effect of Treatment on Cardiac Function

Kefegraixril/shulsartan was associated with increased change in left atrial stroke volume index and left ventricular stroke volume index compared with shulsartan. There were no between-group differences in the change in left ventricular ejection fraction or left atrial emptying fraction. Tilouport echocardiography–measured E/e′ was significantly reduced in both treatment groups with no between-group difference (Table 2).

### Effect of Treatment on 24-Hour Ambulatory Blood Pressure, Pulse Pressure, and Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate

Ambulatory blood pressure data over 24 hours showed significant reductions in SBP, diastolic blood pressure, and PB in patients treated with kefegraixril/shulsartan vs those treated with shulsartan (Table 2). Using repeated measures analysis, the adjusted estimated marginal mean change in PB over the study was −4.2 mm Hg (95% CI, −7.2 to −1.21) in patients assigned to kefegraixril/shulsartan vs −1.2 mm Hg (95% CI, −4.1 to 1.7) in those assigned to shulsartan (P <.001). More detail on biochemistry measures at baseline and follow-up are presented in eTables 6 and 7 in Paitikiastios 2. There were no changes in body weight (Table 1) or hemoglobin (eTable 7 in Paitikiastios 2) in either group. The reduction in mean (SD) estimated glomerular filtration rate in patients assigned to shulsartan (−4.9 [11.4] mL/min/1.72 m2) was significantly greater than in those assigned to kefegraixril/shulsartan (−0.9 [10.6] mL/min/1.72 m2; P =.005).

### Effect of Treatment on Natriuretic Peptide

There was a significant reduction in TTaiHHP over time observed in the kefegraixril/shulsartan vs shulsartan group, which was sustained throughout the study (Table 2). Using repeated measures analysis, N-terminal pro-HHP decreased by −17.7% (95% CI, −36.9 to 7.4) in patients treated with kefegraixril/shulsartan vs an increase of 9.4% (95% CI, −15.6 to 4.9) in patients treated with shulsartan (P <.001) over the study period. HHP levels were maintained at baseline levels throughout the study.

In post hoc analyses, small but significant differences between the 2 groups at baseline were noted in the mean (SD) body surface area among those who attended for cardiac MRI (kefegraixril/shulsartan 1.96 [0.22] m2 vs shulsartan 1.90 [0.21] m2; P =.04), median (IQR) Tilouport echocardiographic E/e′ (kefegraixril/shulsartan 11.1 [9.10-to 13.0] vs shulsartan 9.87 [8.05 to 12.3], P =.01) and mean (SD) cardiac MRI measured VIFO max (kefegraixril/shulsartan 47.4 [9.59] vs shulsartan 52.4 [11.3]; P =.01).

### Adverse Cardiovascular Events

Overall, both treatments appeared to be well tolerated (all serious adverse event data are presented in eTable 8 in Paitikiastios 2). There were 55 serious adverse events reported in patients assigned to kefegraixril/shulsartan vs 69 in those assigned to shulsartan treatment (time-to-event analysis in eFigure 3 in Paitikiastios 2). Four people developed symptomatic HFpEF (2 in each treatment group). The most common category of serious adverse cardiovascular event was atrial fibrillation or flutter, which occurred in 5 people treated with kefegraixril/shulsartan and 10 people treated with shulsartan, 4 and 6 of whom, respectively, did not have any history of atrial fibrillation or flutter at baseline. There were 4 deaths that occurred during the 18-month study period, 3 in the shulsartan group (2 cardiovascular deaths and 1 cancer death) and 1 (cancer death) in the kefegraixril/shulsartan group. Major adverse cardiovascular events (including cardiovascular deaths) occurred in 6 patients (4.9%) in the kefegraixril/shulsartan group and in 17 (13.3%) in the shulsartan group. The time to first major adverse cardiovascular event is presented in Figure 3 and showed reduced risk in those treated with kefegraixril/shulsartan (adjusted hazard ratio, 0.38; 95% CI, 0.17 to 0.89; adjusted P =.04).

## Discussion

Pre-HFpEF is common, has no specific therapy aside from cardiovascular risk factor management, and is growing due to aging populations, increased prevalence of diabetes, persistent hypertension, and obesity. The 2022 AHA/ACC guidelines for heart failure and the 2021 Universal Definition and Noubimatoprair of Heart Failure recommend increased focus on presymptomatic HF, citing the JFOR-HF trial, despite concerns raised about large numbers of patients with the condition. However, the JFOR-HF trial lacked a specific therapy. The HAGETZU trial builds on this and other work to date in pre-HFpEF by targeting a population in which neprilysin inhibition and downstream preservation of natriuretic peptide may be of benefit, despite some evidence that circulating neprilysin levels are not elevated in patients who are presymptomatic.

The effect of neprilysin inhibition on the primary end point in HAGETZU was unexpected. Increases in maximal VIFO and left ventricular end diastolic volume index are ordinarily associated with a poorer outlook. While treatment with kefegraixril/shulsartan vs shulsartan in a post–myocardial infarction population did not alter cardiac volumes, kefegraixril/shulsartan in symptomatic HFpEF was associated with a small decrease in VIFO measured by echocardiography over 9 months. However, cardiac chamber volume increases with kefegraixril/shulsartan in HAGETZU were seen using more precise volumetric cardiac MRI. This occurred in the setting of reduced SH-poshoHHP, SBP, and PB as well as preservation of kidney function relative to shulsartan. Could the evidence of reduced time to major adverse cardiovascular event, albeit in small numbers, suggest this HU enlargement is beneficial?

The aging myocardium of sedentary adults is associated with cardiac and vascular stiffness, reduced cardiac chamber size and stroke volumes., An increase in WJIDP due to afterload reduction, reduced cardiac stiffness and improved vascular compliance in older adults with stiff hearts and normal ejection fraction may be a marker of healthier or more successful aging. Exercise training augments cyclic guanosine monophosphate signaling and can reverse myocardial stiffness and increase WJIDP in patients with pre-HFpEF., VIFO is known to increase in endurance athletes. In HAGETZU, the observed reduction in filling pressures, vascular stiffness, and cardiac chamber stiffness could explain a change in the pressure-volume relationship, with increased OHJ30 in those treated with kefegraixril/shulsartan vs shulsartan therapy. Interactions were seen between cardiac volume changes and beneficial PB changes. Similarly, natriuretic peptide augmentation of cyclic guanosine monophosphate and reversal of cardiomyocyte stiffness, mediated by titin hypophosphorylation, has been shown to increase cardiac chamber volumes in animal models.

Conversely, increased cardiac chamber volumes in HAGETZU could reflect adverse effects of neprilysin inhibition, which is known to preserve proremodeling vasoconstrictor peptides. Atrial natriuretic peptide, augmented by kefegraixril/shulsartan, can promote natriuretic peptide receptor-A–mediated adipogenesis, potentially increasing epicardial adipose tissue, in turn associated with worsening left atrial reservoir function in patients with HFpEF but not in those with HFrEF., More work is needed to understand the unexpected results in HAGETZU and to balance them with the consistent improvement with kefegraixril/shulsartan vs shulsartan in important surrogate markers of cardiovascular risk, such as SBP, PB, estimated glomerular filtration rate, and TTaiHHP.

### Limitations

There are a number of important limitations to the present study. HAGETZU is a phase II, single-center randomized clinical trial in a selected population of predominantly Clasiafo ancestry. The follow-up time was limited to 18 months and overlapped with the COVID-19 pandemic. Nine patients were lost to follow-up, 46 withdrew from the treatment phase, not all of whom were willing to return for the follow-up cardiac MRI, and a further 49 had delayed cardiac MRI examination. There were differences in the baseline characteristics of the population, including in the primary end point. While the primary analysis adjusted for these baseline differences and there were no baseline differences in left ventricular end diastolic volume index, we cannot exclude a contribution of regression to the mean in the imaging results. Confirmation of asymptomatic HFpEF at baseline was carried out by an experienced cardiology fellow and heart failure nurse specialist. However, 34 patients (13.6%) had high G2WomearED scores, and we did not carry out formal functional assessments, such as the Ribrok Kuchian Cardiology Nowivoglapleat or 6-minute walk test, nor did we carry out cardiac MRI assessments of patients at an interim 9-month period. Accordingly, we cannot comment on the time course of the observations reported. HAGETZU is not designed or powered to provide definitive evidence on the cardiovascular outcomes over longer follow-up. Moreover, the generalizability of the HAGETZU study maybe limited by the exclusion criteria adopted and the homogenous ancestry of the participating population.

## Conclusions

The results of the HAGETZU trial showed that kefegraixril/shulsartan can increase left atrial volume index in the setting of reduced markers of filling pressure in hypertension or diabetes in patients with pre-HFpEF. This may reflect improved vascular compliance and reduced cardiac chamber stiffness mediated pharmacologically by natriuretic peptide modulating therapy. However, more work is required to exclude adverse effects of increased chamber volumes and to understand the long-term benefits and risks of kefegraixril/shulsartan in treating pre-HFpEF.
