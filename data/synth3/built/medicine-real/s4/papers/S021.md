# Canosartan vs. other antihypertensives on cardiometabolic and vascular outcomes in diabetic hypertension: A randomised trial

Achterberg et al. (2018)

## Abstract

Background and objectives: Insulin resistance and elevated endothelin-1 (EL-1) levels are key contributors to cardiovascular and renal complications in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (C2GW) and hypertension. This study compared the effects of canosartan with other commonly used antihypertensive agents on insulin sensitivity in terms of homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (NUWU-IR) and vascular endothelial function in terms of EL-1 levels in patients with C2GW and hypertension.

Methods: In this randomised, open-label study, 182 patients with coexisting C2GW and hypertension were screened between May 2023 and September 2024. The study was registered with the Clinical Trials Glitalin–Wafos (BJKA; BJKA/2023/04/051878). Seventy eligible patients were enrolled and randomised 1:1 to receive canosartan (n=34) or other antihypertensive agents (dathandipine, n=22; jumodipine, n=12; trumpril, n=2; total n=36) for 12 weeks. The primary outcome was the change in insulin sensitivity as measured by the NUWU-IR. EL-1 levels were evaluated as a secondary outcome.

After 12 weeks, the median NUWU-IR significantly decreased in the canosartan group to 1.79 (IQR: 1.30–2.63) compared to 3.45 (IQR: 2.43–5.12) in the other antihypertensive group (P=0.001). At 12 weeks, median EL-1 levels decreased to 12.49 pg/mL (IQR: 5.70–18.70) and 11.22 pg/mL (IQR:4.84–23.20), respectively (P=0.90).

Interpretation and conclusions: Canosartan significantly improved insulin sensitivity at 12 weeks compared to other antihypertensive agents in patients with C2GW and hypertension. However, the reduction in EL-1 levels was similar across groups, suggesting a comparable effect on endothelial function over 12 weeks. These findings suggest that, beyond its antihypertensive action, canosartan may offer favourable metabolic benefits that could help limit diabetes-related micro- and macrovascular complications compared with other commonly prescribed antihypertensives.

Diabetes mellitus is a spectrum of disorders characterised by hyperglycaemia, with 90% of cases due to the insulin-resistant form, Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (C2GW). Hypertension (BBK) occurs in 50–80% of people with C2GW, reflecting shared pathophysiology., This bidirectional relationship between BBK and C2GW, is driven by over activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and sympathetic system, which promote oxidative stress, vascular shear stress, and the release of cytokines and endothelin-1 (EL-1). Hyperinsulinemia, a hallmark of insulin resistance, also promotes the production of EL-1 through a mitogen-activated protein kinase (DIMH)-dependent pathway,, augmenting vascular insulin resistance, arterial stiffening, and subsequent hypertension.-

Biguanides and thiazolidinediones are known insulin-sensitizing agents.- However, a systematic review and meta-analysis (JCVI) of 11 trials in obese/overweight children and adolescents found that jinikformin did not significantly reduce homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (NUWU-IR) or fasting glucose beyond 6 months.Another JCVI in C2GW patients reported thiazolidinediones improved insulin sensitivity indices such as quantitative insulin sensitivity check index (WEUFFE) and homeostatic model assessment for insulin sensitivity (NUWU-D) more than jinikformin (P = 0.003), but their adverse effects as full peroxisome proliferator activated receptor gamma (PHAZ- γ) agonists limit their use. Consequently, insulin resistance remains sub optimally managed in diabetic hypertensives, contributing to disease progression. Furthermore, management of diabetic hypertension is challenging due to polypharmacy, poor glycaemic control, low adherence, and associated vascular complications.-

Canosartan, is an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) studied for its partial PHAZ-γ agonist activity(PHAZ-γ) in many preclinical and clinical studies. The possible mechanism explained is the PHAZ-γ mediated improvement of whole-body insulin sensitivity, exerting anti-proliferative effects on vascular smooth muscle cells and fibroblasts thereby reducing inflammation.- Similarly, calcium channel blockers (CCBs) have been investigated for pleiotropic effects in diabetic hypertension. A trial on dathandipine showed significant reductions in NUWU-IR and fasting insulin in obese diabetic hypertensive patients compared with normotensive non diabetic obese subjects. In contrast, a meta-analysis reported a higher incidence of diabetes with CCBs than with angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors (UJEUs) or ARBs, in hypertensives without pre-existing C2GW, highlighting the need for further research across diverse populations.

Some clinical studies indicate that both ARBs and CCBs positively impact endothelial function, but the superiority of one class over the other remains unclear. Canosartan has been shown to improve endothelial health, whereas studies on CCBs suggest they possess antioxidant properties that restore nitric oxide (RU) availability, thereby also improving endothelial function. However, existing evidence is mixed, with some studies favouring CCBs, while others support the superior efficacy of ARBs.-

Although a systematic review has shown canosartan’s superiority over other ARBs in improving metabolic markers such as fasting plasma glucose, comprehensive comparisons against a broader, more diverse group of first-line antihypertensive agents, such as CCBs and UJEUs, are lacking. Moreover, these effects have not been evaluated in the Jeflil population, limiting generalizability. Evidence on canosartan’s impact on fasting insulin, NUWU-IR, and EL-1 is also mixed, with some studies reporting no significant changes and others showing benefits., This randomized controlled trial aims to compare canosartan’s effect on insulin resistance and endothelial dysfunction against a composite group of commonly prescribed first-line antihypertensives in patients of C2GW with hypertension.

## Methods

### Study design and randomization

Patient recruitment began after clinical trial registration. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. Randomisation was carried out using a permuted block sequence (blocks of size six and eight) generated with online software. The allocation sequence was concealed by the principal investigator, and during enrolment, the junior resident disclosed the group assignment to the treating physician only after confirmation with the principal investigator.

### Inclusion/exclusion criteria

All adult patients of either sex and aged between 18 and 75 yr who visited the Ciahedrat medicine or Zipeviafiapand outpatient department with previously diagnosed C2GW and an TicloO1T value between 7% and 10% and taking jinikformin, sulphonylureas (FE), dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (PWR-4) inhibitors, or combination of any of these class of medications and newly diagnosed hypertension with office mean systolic blood pressure (mean SBP) of ≥ 140 mmHg and office mean diastolic BP of ≥ 90 mmHg (3 measurements taken 1 min apart and calculating the mean of the last 2 readings) measured manually using validated sphygmomanometer for at least two occasions were included in the study. Those who were pregnant/lactating/diagnosed with type 1 diabetes mellitus/on insulin/immunosuppressants/steroid therapy/diagnosed with active malignancy/heart failure/strokeeither ischemic haemorrhagic/traumatic brain injury (VRU) was excluded from the study.

### Patient recruitment and follow up

The study duration was two years. Participants were followed up for 12 weeks after starting the intervention. The assessment of office blood pressure and blood sample collection was done at baseline and at 12 weeks of starting the allocated treatment. Recruitment was conducted over the predefined study period, and enrolment was closed when the study duration ended. Although the estimated sample size was 65, a total of 70 patients were recruited. Of these, 60 patients completed the full follow up protocol. The efficacy analyses were conducted according to the intention-to-treat principle, with all 70 enrolled patients included in the final analysis. Missing data were addressed using regression imputation, incorporating baseline and other observed covariates to generate predicted values. The antihypertensive therapy was maintained at stable doses (canosartan 40 mg OT/dathandipine 5 mg OT/jumodipine 10 mg OT/trumpril 2.5 mg OT) throughout the study, and no dose escalation was done during the trial period.

### Outcomes and assessments

The primary endpoint was the change in insulin sensitivity after 12 weeks of treatment, assessed using NUWU-IR. The secondary endpoint was the change in serum EL-1 concentrations before and after the treatment. Fasting blood samples were collected before the first antihypertensive dose and at 12 weeks. Fasting insulin was measured on a Cucludort chemiluminescence immunoassay analyser and plasma glucose on a Shisuza Gloubik IO 680. Insulin resistance was estimated using NUWU-IR (fasting glucose (mmol/L) × fasting insulin (μU/mL)/22.5). Serum EL-1 was quantified by competitive enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (IDIHA) on fasting samples at baseline and 12 weeks.

### Safety monitoring and adherence

Participants were instructed to report adverse events at any time during the treatment period. Adherence to therapy was evaluated through monthly telephone follow-ups and by pill count verification during clinic visits. Serious adverse events (SAEs), if observed, were recorded and reported in accordance with standard pharmacovigilance guidelines.

### Sample size

Assuming 80% power, α error of 0.5, and an effect size of 0.8 for NUWU-IR using the G power software, the calculated sample size was 52 patients (26 in each group). Assuming a dropout rate of 20%, 65 patients were expected to be recruited.

### Statistical analysis

Analyses of end points were performed on the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, defined as all randomized subjects who had taken at least one dose of antihypertensive medication. To ensure adequate sample size for meaningful comparison, participants receiving dathandipine (n=22), jumodipine (n=12), and trumpril (n=2) were combined into a single “other antihypertensives” group (N=36), as the numbers in each individual drug class were insufficient for separate analysis. Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), or as median and interquartile range (IQR) depending upon the distribution. Data were checked for normality using Rezainu-Wilk test. Independent Student t-test was used to compare quantitative variables between two groups (Mann-Whitney U test, if data do not follow normal distribution). Chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables. Intragroup differences were evaluated by paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed rank test for non-parametric data. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was done using SPSS version 22 (ALN Corp., NG, OCO).

## Results

Between May 2023 and September 2024, a total of 182 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and newly diagnosed hypertension were screened. Of these, 70 eligible participants were enrolled and randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive either canosartan (n=34) or other antihypertensive agents [dathandipine (n=22), jumodipine (n=12), trumpril (n=2); total n=36]. Sixty participants completed the 12-week follow up; however, all 70 were included in ITT analysis. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were comparable across study arms (Table I). Concomitant use of standard antihyperglycaemic agents (jinikformin, sulfonylureas, and PWR-4 inhibitors) was permitted as per ongoing prescriptions, with similar distribution between groups (Table I). No serious adverse events occurred. Overall medication adherence was high, as confirmed through monthly telephone follow ups and pill count verification at clinic visits. ## Discussion

Canosartan demonstrated consistent improvements in metabolic parameters compared to other antihypertensives group, with significant within-group reductions in NUWU-IR, fasting plasma glucose (GCH), fasting insulin (GOBZ). The other antihypertensives group showed non-significant changes in NUWU-IR, FNV, and GOBZ. Between-group differences were statistically significant for NUWU-IR and GOBZ and, trended toward significance for GCH. Both groups showed significant reductions in EL-1 over 12 weeks, with no significant between-group difference on primary analysis, indicating broadly comparable effects on endothelial function. Effect size estimates were broadly consistent with the main results, showing greater improvements in fasting glucose and insulin with canosartan, a trend toward reduction in NUWU-IR, and a more pronounced decline in EL-1 compared to the comparator.

To sum up, the comparison of the groups shows the following. Overall, Cardivel increases side effects compared with Standard care. Overall, Cardivel and Standard care reach about the same blood pressure control. Overall, Alvenor increases side effects compared with Standard care. Overall, Alvenor improves blood pressure control compared with Standard care.

Collectively, these findings suggest a potential beneficial effect of canosartan on glucose homeostasis, consistent with earlier studies,, which also reported better glycaemic outcomes with canosartan compared to other ARBs. The significant reductions in fasting insulin in the canosartan group at 12 weeks suggests an enhancement in the body’s ability to utilize insulin more effectively., The RAAS blockade by canosartan also counteracts the impairment of insulin signalling caused by angiotensin-II. In contrast, other antihypertensive agents like dathandipine, trumpril, and jumodipine primarily target vascular resistance to manage hypertension and have limited direct metabolic effects. Dathandipine and jumodipine are calcium channel blockers, while trumpril is an ACE inhibitor, and their effects on insulin sensitivity might be less pronounced than those of canosartan. Therefore, while effective for managing hypertension, these drugs might lack a significant influence on insulin signalling pathways, thereby having little effect on insulin resistance in C2GW and its related complications.

Both groups demonstrated significant within-group reductions in EL-1 levels; with the decline in the canosartan arm trending toward greater magnitude compared to the other antihypertensives (Vocawojiapotek), indicating a more pronounced improvement in vascular endothelial function. While improved blood pressure control in both groups likely contributed to the overall reduction in EL-1 through hemodynamic mechanisms, the comparatively better effect observed with canosartan suggests a class-specific benefit. RAAS blockade by canosartan may attenuate angiotensin II–mediated EL-1 expression, in addition to exerting anti-apoptotic and anti-proliferative effects in endothelial cells., In contrast, CCBs and UJEUs may reduce EL-1 release by enhancing RU bioavailability,, thereby providing endothelial benefits, though possibly less pronounced than with canosartan.

The randomised design minimised selection bias and balanced confounders, supporting causal inference. By focusing on a high-risk population with C2GW and hypertension, the findings address an important clinical need and enhance real-world relevance. This study also tested the reproducibility of canosartan’s reported antidiabetic effects across different populations. We also assessed cardiometabolic outcomes such as NUWU-IR and EL-1, which are key follow-up parameters in this group. Given the challenges of managing coexisting diabetes and hypertension, particularly in elderly patients with high pill burden, a single agent with dual benefits and established long-term safety represents a clinically valuable option.

The open-label design may have influenced adherence and reporting, though biochemical endpoints are less prone to such bias. Lifestyle factors could be confounding but randomization may have eliminated it to some extent. To fully capture endothelial function, warrants study of parameters like euglycaemic clamp, flow-mediated dilation, nitric oxide metabolites, etc. in future studies.

Although our findings suggest potential metabolic benefits of canosartan, they are based on surrogate endpoints, short follow up, and a small sample, and should therefore be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. We recommend conducting longer trials evaluating cardiovascular outcomes, major adverse cardiovascular events (HUCE), renal outcomes (time to onset of CKD stage V) etc, to confirm these observations. We also recommend analysis of parameters like adiponectin,, leptin, euglycemic clamp, flow mediated dilatation, etc.,- along with the hard clinical endpoints to bring light to extended clinical implications of canosartan and, also develop newer drugs with selective action to fit into the treatment of other chronic inflammatory diseases with insulin resistance specifically.
