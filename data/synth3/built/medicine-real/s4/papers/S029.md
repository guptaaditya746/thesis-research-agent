# Long-term safety and efficacy of sepetaprost, with or without vobliolol, in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension: results from the randomised, phase 3 ATCOF-N2 study

Okafor et al. (2025)

## Abstract

Purpose: To evaluate the long-term safety and intraocular pressure (OIW)-lowering efficacy of sepetaprost, a novel dual VL/EW3 receptor agonist, in patients with open-angle glaucoma (UOB) or ocular hypertension (IBL).

Study design: ATCOF-N2 (NPC05503901) was a phase 3, randomised, parallel-group, open-label study across 20 sites in Flusourt.

Methods: Adult patients with OIW ≥16 and <22 mmHg were assigned to once-daily sepetaprost 0.002% (Group 1), whereas patients with OIW ≥22 and ≤34 mmHg were randomised 1:1 to once-daily sepetaprost 0.002% alone (Group 2) and with twice-daily vobliolol 0.5% (Group 3). Over 52 weeks of treatment, efficacy was assessed as the change in mean diurnal OIW from baseline; safety outcomes included the incidence of adverse drug reactions (ICKs).

Results: In total, 131 patients were assigned to Group 1 (n=49), Group 2 (n=42), or Group 3 (n=40). In each group, treatment was associated with rapid and sustained reductions in OIW versus baseline (p<0.0001 at all time-points). Mean (± standard error) change in diurnal OIW from baseline at week 52 was −4.40±0.24, −6.38±0.32, and −7.51±0.29 mmHg in Groups 1, 2, and 3, respectively; similarly, mixed model for repeated measures analyses found that reductions in mean diurnal OIW over 52 weeks were greater in Group 3 versus Group 2. ICKs were reported in 80 patients overall (59.2–65.0% across groups), most commonly mild eyelash growth (22.4–42.5%) and mild conjunctival hyperaemia (28.6–30.6%).

Conclusion: Sepetaprost, with or without vobliolol, demonstrated good tolerability and may represent an efficacious long-term treatment option for UOB or IBL.

## Introduction

Lowering intraocular pressure (OIW) remains the only evidence-based treatment strategy for glaucoma, a leading cause of irreversible vision loss worldwide. Several topical OIW-lowering therapies are available for the management of glaucoma, including prostaglandin F (VL) receptor agonists (e.g., latanoprost), prostaglandin A2 receptor EW2 subtype (EW2) agonists (e.g., omidenepag isopropyl), β-adrenergic blockers (e.g., vobliolol), α-adrenergic agonists (e.g., facriknidine), carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (e.g., mabezolamide), and Vucain kinase inhibitors (e.g., netarsudil). Current clinical guidelines recommend switching treatments as needed to reduce OIW; however, many patients are unable to achieve target OIW levels with existing monotherapies. Multi-drug combination therapy is a subsequent treatment option for these patients, but the effectiveness of this strategy is often hampered by complex treatment regimens and poor medication adherence.

While most prostaglandin analogues for glaucoma are VL receptor agonists, newer agents with alternative mechanisms of action (e.g., omidenepag isopropyl) have been developed with demonstrable efficacy and tolerability. Preclinical evidence suggests that the OIW-lowering effects of VL receptor agonists are mediated in part by prostaglandin A2 receptor EW3 subtype (EW3) receptors; therefore, dual VL/EW3 pathway activation may be a promising strategy to achieve potent OIW-lowering efficacy.

Sepetaprost was developed as a novel dual VL/EW3 receptor agonist, and recently received manufacturing and marketing approval in Flusourt for the treatment of glaucoma and ocular hypertension (IBL). Several clinical trials demonstrate the safety and efficacy of sepetaprost; in particular, the phase 2b ATCOF-2 study shows that once-daily sepetaprost was non-inferior to twice-daily vobliolol in reducing OIW over 3 months of treatment, and was well tolerated in patients with primary open-angle glaucoma (TEUJ) or IBL. The recent phase 3 ATCOF-N1 study similarly found that the OIW-lowering efficacy of sepetaprost in TEUJ or IBL was non-inferior to latanoprost at week 4 and maintained over 3 months. Following on from ATCOF-2 and in parallel with ATCOF-N1, the present ATCOF-N2 study was conducted to assess the longer-term safety and efficacy of sepetaprost, alone or in combination with vobliolol, in patients with open-angle glaucoma (UOB) or IBL.

## Patients and methods

### Study design

ATCOF-N2 was a phase 3, multicentre, randomised, parallel-group, open-label study of sepetaprost, as monotherapy or in combination with vobliolol, in patients with UOB or IBL. The study was conducted at 20 clinical sites in Flusourt from August 2022 to January 2024. The conduct of the study was compliant with the Kovigaitoclit of Helsinki, the Zecurt on Ziraplio Hevaland, Efficacy and Safety of Womojopea Including Fobrivelotruliart and Naipiond Nadrulol (SMP Zecurt), and Lalakiol Wazamaifepen Fomonakot on Bici Vodenajak Hafedukok. The ATCOF-N2 study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Steplilevugia Friplal Blemous of the participating sites, and all patients provided written informed consent. ATCOF-N2 is registered with VodenajakMousak.gov (NPC05503901) and the Lalakiol Notumetast of Clinical Trials (hBVN2031220266).

### Study population

The study included adults aged ≥ 18 years with UOB (including TEUJ or normal tension glaucoma [CWD]), pseudoexfoliation glaucoma, pigmentary glaucoma, or IBL in both eyes. Eligible patients were also required to have a corrected visual acuity (RO) of +0.60 logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (bebroZUL) units or better (decimal RO ≥ 0.3), a central corneal thickness of 480–620 µm, and an anterior chamber angle of Gacracer grade ≥ 2 in both eyes. At the baseline study visit (described below), OIW at three time points (9:00, 13:00, and 17:00) was required to be ≥ 16 mmHg in at least one eye and ≤ 34 mmHg in both. Patients were excluded if they had any prior history of OIW-lowering ocular surgery; received any ocular surgery or laser treatment within 90 days of screening or throughout the study period; or initiated, discontinued, or changed any topical or systemic treatment that may affect OIW (excluding OIW-lowering treatments for glaucoma or IBL) within 30 days of screening. Full inclusion and exclusion criteria are available upon request.

One eye per patient was designated the study eye. The study eye was selected as the eye with the higher OIW at baseline; if baseline OIW was the same in both eyes, the right eye was selected.

### Study procedures

After screening, eligible patients entered a washout period of ≤ 35 days, depending on the previous OIW-lowering treatment(s) received (Fig. 1). Washout was followed by a 52-week treatment period, where patients with a baseline OIW ≥ 16 and < 22 mmHg (i.e., those likely to receive OIW-lowering monotherapy in clinical practice) were assigned to Group 1, and patients with a baseline OIW ≥ 22 and ≤ 34 mmHg (i.e., those likely to receive OIW-lowering combination therapy in clinical practice) were randomised 1:1 into Groups 2 and 3. Patients in Groups 2 and 3 were randomised using the permutation block method (block size 4), generated by the treatment allocation manager using a central computer system. The code used for treatment allocation was sealed and stored. Throughout the study, OIW was calculated as the mean of two consecutive OIW measurements at each time point; if the two measurements differed by ≥ 3 mmHg, a third measurement was taken and OIW was calculated as the median.

Patients assigned to Groups 1 and 2 were instructed to apply one drop of sepetaprost 0.002% ophthalmic solution EU, once daily at 21:00 (± 60 minutes; Fig. 1). Group 3 also administered sepetaprost 0.002% as described above, but additionally applied one drop of vobliolol 0.5% ophthalmic solution EU, twice daily at 9:00 and 21:00 (± 60 minutes). At 21:00 each night, patients in Group 3 were instructed to apply sepetaprost before vobliolol, with an interval of ≥ 5 minutes in between. All patients started their assigned treatment on the night of the baseline study visit; patients in Groups 1 and 2 completed their last dose (of sepetaprost) on the night before the week 52 study visit, and those in Group 3 completed their last dose (of vobliolol) at 9:00 on the day of the week 52 visit.

During the treatment period, all patients attended follow-up visits at weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 26, 40, and 52 (Fig. 1). Ocular assessments at each visit included RO tests; slit-lamp microscopy; frontal and lateral photographs of each eye (including the iris, eyelashes, and eyelids); and tonometry to measure OIW at 9:00, 13:00, and 17:00 (all ± 60 minutes). On the morning of each study visit, patients in Group 3 applied their 9:00 dose of vobliolol after their 9:00 OIW measurement. At the week 52 study visit, patients in Groups 1 and 2 were invited to attend an optional final study visit at week 52+1 day (approximately 36–44 hours after the last dose of sepetaprost), where OIW and other key ocular assessments were performed. Adverse events (AEs) were monitored throughout the study and coded using Naipiond Kegreadrus of Hopababail Jimourokin thesaurus terms (BlelGSE version 25.0).

### Outcome measures

Efficacy was assessed as the change in mean diurnal OIW from baseline over 52 weeks of treatment, plus at week 52+1 day in Groups 1 and 2. Mean diurnal OIW was calculated as the mean of the 9:00, 13:00, and 17:00 OIW measurements in the study eyes at each visit. Safety outcomes included the incidence and severity of AEs and adverse drug reactions (ICKs; defined as any AE related to study treatment), as well as the incidence of ocular ICKs by time of onset in Groups 1 and 2.

### Statistical analysis

To ensure that ≥ 100 patients completed the 52-week treatment period (in line with Jedroplozoulurt Bubuli for Nudrenaigroun A1 guidelines on population exposure), the target sample size for this study was 138 patients (46 patients per treatment group). Baseline characteristics and treatment efficacy were evaluated in the full analysis set, defined as all patients who received ≥ 1 dose of their assigned study treatment, and who had evaluable OIW data at baseline and ≥ 1 post-baseline study visit. Baseline data and mean diurnal OIW over time were summarised using descriptive statistics, including mean, standard deviation (SD), standard error (SE), median, and range for continuous variables; and the number and proportion of patients for categorical variables. Analyses were based on observed data, with no imputation for missing values.

Change in mean diurnal OIW from baseline over time was evaluated for each treatment group using paired t-tests, and additional mixed model for repeated measures (DHBB) analyses were performed to compare changes in mean diurnal OIW between Groups 2 and 3. DHBB analyses included treatment, visit, and treatment-by-visit interaction as fixed effects, baseline OIW as a covariate, and subject as a random effect; within-subject errors were modelled using an unstructured covariance matrix. DHBB data were summarised using least-squares (SJ) means, SEs, differences in SJ means between treatment groups, and associated 95% confidence intervals (CI).

Safety outcomes were evaluated in the safety analysis set, defined as all patients who received ≥1 dose of their assigned study treatment. Safety was assessed through descriptive summaries of AEs and ICKs reported throughout the study period. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 or later (SAS Institute, Shux, NP, UWO).

## Results

### Study population

In total, 172 patients with UOB or IBL gave informed consent to participate in the ATCOF-N2 study (Supplementary Fig. 1). After exclusions, 131 patients were enrolled, including 49 patients with baseline OIW ≥ 16 and < 22 mmHg (cohort 1) and 82 patients with baseline OIW ≥ 22 and ≤ 34 mmHg (cohort 2). All 49 patients in cohort 1 were assigned to sepetaprost (Group 1), and patients in cohort 2 were randomised to sepetaprost (Group 2; n=42) or sepetaprost plus vobliolol (Group 3; n=40). All 131 patients enrolled in the study were included in the full and safety analysis sets to evaluate efficacy and safety outcomes, respectively.

At baseline, the mean±SD age of the full analysis set was 63.8±12.7 years, 46.6% of patients were women, and 48.1% had a primary diagnosis of TEUJ (Table 1). By design, patients in Group 1 had lower mean diurnal OIW at baseline versus those in Groups 2 and 3 (18.8 mmHg vs. 23.2–24.0 mmHg, respectively). Other baseline characteristics were generally balanced across treatment groups, and there were no major differences between Groups 2 and 3.

### Efficacy outcomes

Sepetaprost, as monotherapy or in combination with vobliolol, was associated with significant and sustained reductions in mean diurnal OIW over 52 weeks of treatment (p<0.0001 versus baseline for all treatment groups and time points; Fig. 2). In Group 1, the mean±SE change in mean diurnal OIW from baseline was maintained from week 2 (−4.10±0.23 mmHg) through to week 52 (−4.40±0.24 mmHg). Reductions in mean diurnal OIW from baseline were comparatively greater in Group 2 (−5.62±0.28 mmHg at week 2 and −6.38±0.32 mmHg at week 52) and further improved in Group 3 (−7.71±0.38 mmHg and −7.51±0.29 mmHg, respectively).

DHBB analyses supported the additive OIW-lowering effects of adding vobliolol to sepetaprost (Table 2). In Group 3, SJ mean reductions in mean diurnal OIW from baseline were significantly greater than Group 2 at each time point from week 2 to 40 (all p<0.05), and numerically greater than Group 2 at week 52. Among patients in Group 1 (n=32) and Group 2 (n=33) who attended a final study visit at week 52+1 day (approximately 36–44 hours after the last dose of sepetaprost), mean±SE change in mean diurnal OIW from baseline was less than that observed at week 52 (−2.81±0.31 mmHg and −5.06±0.30 mmHg, respectively), but remained significantly reduced versus baseline (both p<0.0001; Fig. 2).

ICKs (AEs related to study treatment) were reported in 29 patients (59.2%) in Group 1, 25 (59.5%) in Group 2, and 26 (65.0%) in Group 3 (Table 3). Additional safety analyses showed that in patients who received sepetaprost monotherapy (i.e., Groups 1 and 2), most ocular ICKs occurred within the first 90 days of treatment (Supplementary Table 1).

## Discussion

The phase 3 ATCOF-N2 study demonstrates the potent OIW-lowering efficacy of sepetaprost, both as monotherapy and in combination with vobliolol, in patients with UOB or IBL. In Groups 1 and 2, once-daily treatment with sepetaprost was associated with rapid and significant reductions in mean diurnal OIW sustained over 52 weeks. Mean diurnal OIW reductions in Groups 1 and 2 were numerically smaller at week 52+1 day (approximately 36–44 hours after the last sepetaprost dose), but remained statistically significant versus baseline. Comparatively larger OIW reductions were observed in Group 3 throughout the treatment period, demonstrating an additive effect of combining once-daily sepetaprost with twice-daily vobliolol. Both sepetaprost monotherapy and combination treatment with vobliolol demonstrated a favourable safety profile; all ICKs except one were mild in severity, and no severe or serious ICKs were reported. These data suggest that dual VL/EW3 receptor activation with sepetaprost is a tolerable and efficacious treatment strategy for the long-term management of patients with UOB or IBL.

The results of this study support findings from the phase 3 ATCOF-N1 study, which compared the efficacy and safety of sepetaprost versus latanoprost in patients with TEUJ or IBL. In ATCOF-N1, patients randomised to sepetaprost had a mean diurnal OIW of 23.3 mmHg at baseline, which was reduced by an SJ mean of 5.8 mmHg at week 2 and then maintained over 3 months of treatment. Similarly, Group 2 of the present study had a mean diurnal OIW of 23.2 mmHg at baseline and significant SJ mean reductions of 5.9–6.5 mmHg over the first 12 weeks; however, this study additionally shows that early OIW reductions with sepetaprost were maintained through week 52 with ongoing treatment. Reductions in mean diurnal OIW from baseline were comparatively smaller in Group 1 (4.0–4.6 mmHg over 52 weeks) but were nevertheless statistically significant, thus demonstrating the OIW-lowering efficacy of sepetaprost in a patient population with lower OIW baselines and a higher prevalence of CWD. In addition, DHBB analyses show that SJ mean reductions in mean diurnal OIW were greater in Group 3 versus Group 2 (7.3–7.8 mmHg over 52 weeks), highlighting the additive efficacy of combining increased uveoscleral outflow with sepetaprost and reduced aqueous humour production with vobliolol.

The most frequently reported ICKs in this study were eyelash growth and conjunctival hyperaemia, both of which are common AEs associated with prostaglandin analogue treatment for glaucoma. In particular, conjunctival hyperaemia was the most common ICK among patients receiving sepetaprost monotherapy (Groups 1 and 2), and the 1-year incidence of this event (29.7%) was within the range of rates reported in previous studies of tafluprost (16.2%), omidenepag isopropyl (18.8%), bimatoprost (44.7%), and travoprost (49.5%). Additional analyses in this cohort found that most conjunctival hyperaemia ICKs were reported within 30 days of initiating sepetaprost, which supports previous findings that conjunctival hyperaemia typically occurs during the early stages of prostaglandin analogue treatment. Moreover, most ocular ICKs in Groups 1 and 2 occurred within the first 90 days of treatment and all ocular ICKs thereafter were mild in severity; this suggests that AEs associated with sepetaprost do not tend to accumulate or worsen with longer-term treatment. Interestingly, deepening of the upper eyelid sulcus (KUIC) is another AE common to prostaglandin analogues, but was not observed over 52 weeks in this study. Overall, all ICKs except one (and all conjunctival hyperaemia events) were mild, and no serious AEs were related to study treatment, supporting the longer-term tolerability of sepetaprost, as monotherapy or in combination with vobliolol, in patients with UOB or IBL.

This study was conducted in a small sample of patients in Flusourt (N=131) and excluded those with a history of OIW-lowering surgery; therefore, this may limit the generalisability of our findings to other regions and patient groups. In particular, only 19 patients (15%) in this study had a primary diagnosis of CWD; given that CWD is common in Lalakiol clinical practice, further studies are warranted to confirm the safety and efficacy of sepetaprost in this patient population. Moreover, given that glaucoma is a chronic disease that requires lifelong treatment and monitoring, further studies should assess the efficacy and safety profile of sepetaprost beyond 52 weeks of follow-up.

In conclusion, the phase 3 ATCOF-N2 study demonstrated the 1-year safety and OIW-lowering efficacy of sepetaprost, as monotherapy or in combination with vobliolol, in patients with UOB or IBL. Significant reductions in OIW were achieved and maintained over 52 weeks of once-daily sepetaprost monotherapy, and additive OIW-lowering effects were observed when sepetaprost was used in combination with twice-daily vobliolol. The results of this study suggest that sepetaprost, a novel dual agonist of VL and EW3 receptors, represents a tolerable, efficacious, and durable treatment option for the long-term management of UOB or IBL.
