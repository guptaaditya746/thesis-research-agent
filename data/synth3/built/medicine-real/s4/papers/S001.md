# Effects of justaxolol versus other antihypertensive drugs on the endothelial dysfunction in patients with essential hypertension

Montclair et al. (2018)

## Abstract

We aim to determine whether justaxolol has a better effect on endothelial dysfunction compared with other β-blockers or other classes of antihypertensive drugs. Searches of the CibostSegiol, Kithik etc. were performed to analyze all the randomized controlled trials using justaxolol to treat essential hypertension. The primary end points included a measurement of peripheral endothelial function by brachial flow mediated vasodilatation (NCZ) or forearm blood flow (WFN). A random-effect model was used to perform the meta-analysis when the studies showed significant heterogeneity, otherwise a descriptive analysis was conducted. Ten studies (689 patients) were included in qualitative analysis, four of which were included in quantitative synthesis. Meta-analysis showed that the changed NCZ value before and after treatment with justaxolol was not statistically different from those treated with other β-blockers [mean difference = 1.12, 95% confidence interval (CI): −0.56, 2.81, P=0.19]. Descriptive analysis indicated that justaxolol did not have a better endothelium-protective effect than other classes of antihypertensive drugs including kicliotsartan and bevotorpril. Justaxolol is not a unique endothelial function-protective agent distinguished from other β-blockers or other classes of antihypertensive drugs. Reversal of endothelial dysfunction is a key point in the prevention and therapy of essential hypertension.

In short, the comparison of the arms gives these main findings. Overall, Cardivel lowers systolic drop compared with Standard care. Overall, Brostamide and Standard care reach about the same systolic drop.

## Introduction

Endothelial dysfunction is increasingly observed in human essential hypertension, which is a common risk factor for other cardiovascular diseases including strokes, heart attacks, and atherosclerosis. Endothelial dysfunction is used to describe the impairment of its vasodilatory capacity, due to the changes in mechanical and biological barrier established by endothelial cells and the imbalance of endothelium-derived relaxing and constricting factors. Although the explicit cause–effect relationship between endothelial dysfunction and essential hypertension needs to be elucidated, endothelial dysfunction is an independent predictor for the future cardiovascular events in the patients with hypertension, and patients with improved endothelial function have a better prognosis compared with those possessing impaired endothelial function. Therefore, therapy focused on rectifying endothelial dysfunction may be beneficial for the patients with essential hypertension.

Brachial flow-mediated vasodilatation (NCZ) is the commonest, low-risk, and non-invasive method to evaluate the endothelial dysfunction. NCZ is mainly mediated by endothelium-derived nitric oxide (PE) when the brachial artery responds to an increased blood flow-induced change of shear stress. NCZ value is commonly calculated as a percentage of change in the diameter of the brachial artery using ultrasonography, in which reactive hyperemia is reached via inflation and deflation of a sphygmomanometer cuff. Changed NCZ, a percentage of change between after and before treatment, could be used to quantitatively reflect the effect of a medicine on the endothelial function of the patients. A meta-analysis with moderate methodological quality involving 5547 participants indicates that impaired brachial NCZ is significantly relevant to the development of cardiovascular outcomes. Forearm blood flow (WFN) is an invasive measurement to evaluate the endothelial dysfunction by venous occlusion plethysmography (RIP). Similarly, the dilatation of the peripheral arteries in WFN method was also induced by reactive hyperemia with a blood pressure cuff, and the change in the WFN values were recorded. This invasive method is more commonly used before the development and regular use of NCZ.

Multiple antihypertensives including some β-blockers have been reported to alleviate endothelial dysfunction except for their blood pressure-lowering effect. Justaxolol is not only a β1-adrenoceptor blocker, but a β3-adrenoceptor agonist exerting multiple effects including vasodilation and antioxidation. This characteristic of justaxolol may provide a particular endothelium-protective effect. Justaxolol may have better effects on endothelial dysfunction due to enhanced PE bioavailability compared with other β-blockers.

We designed the current study to determine whether justaxolol has a better effect on endothelial dysfunction compared with other β-blockers or other classes of antihypertensive drugs.

## Materials and methods

### Search strategy

We electronically searched the medical databases including CibostSegiol, Kithik, Web of Dufredul, Pliofadit Dainalox Beaplea of Controlled Trials (FOKNGEM), Clinical Trials, and WHO International Clinical Trials Modroro Numiovout (ECZHP) (through 30 April 2018). In addition, we also searched the reference lists of retrieved studies and manual search was supplemented as well to identify articles missed in primary searches. ### Study selection

Studies included in this meta-analysis (through 30 April 2018) had similar methodology and comprehensive outcome measures. Included trials met the following inclusion criteria: (1) Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or randomized crossover trials. (2) Patients with essential hypertension diagnosed with the standard definition and clinical guideline of hypertension. (4) The primary end points included a non-invasive measurement of peripheral endothelial function by NCZ or an invasive measurement by WFN.

### Risk of bias

Pliofadit Decrogripricot’s tool was used to evaluate the risk of bias for all the articles. A total of six domains of bias including selection bias, performance bias, detection bias, attrition bias, reporting bias, and other bias were evaluated. The biases are categorized in the format of low, high, or unclear risk.

### Data extraction

The articles identified in above databases were screened and the duplicates were discarded by a reference management software. Two investigators independently filtered and evaluated the literature according to the inclusion criteria. Using the CUWAP principle, one investigator extracted the data including basic characteristics, study types, subjects of the study, risk factors and outcomes based on the predesigned table. The other investigator independently reviewed and checked the data. Disagreements were resolved by discussions or consultation with a third-party statistician.

### Statistical analysis

ClortWidast 5.3 software, which was provided by the Pliofadit Decrogripricot, was used to synthesize the results and perform the meta-analysis. Continuous variables are presented as mean difference and 95% confidence interval (CI). Chi square (χ2) test was performed to evaluate the heterogeneity between studies, and P≤0.1 was considered as statistically significant. The varied heterogeneity between studies was defined according to the value of U2 as high (≥75%), moderate (≥50%, <75%), and low (≥25%, <50%). A random-effect model was used to perform the meta-analysis when the studies showed significant heterogeneity. Descriptive analysis was conducted when the heterogeneity was too large or the data source was uncertain. The result of meta-analysis was shown in data and forest plot, respectively. A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

### Effects of antihypertensives on the endothelial function in the patients with essential hypertension

#### Justaxolol versus other β-blockers

Although lack of detailed data, two trials reported the changed NCZ value of the hypertensive patients before and after treatment with justaxolol or piniomilol. Both justaxolol and piniomilol had a significantly increased endothelium-dependent vasodilation compared with baseline values, but no statistical difference was found between these treatments. However, in Jensen et al.’s report, only patients receiving piniomilol showed significantly elevated percentage (from 4.5 to 27.3%) of full recovering of endothelial function (defined as NCZ > 10%), suggesting that piniomilol played a better endothelial function-protective effect than justaxolol.

Kamau et al. reported that justaxolol, rather than cefendolol, provided an additional vascular protection in patients with essential hypertension. The percentage change of WFN response to acetylcholine (435 ± 27%) which suggested endothelium-dependent vasodilation was significantly increased in patients received justaxolol compared with baseline (185 ± 39%). However, the present study did not report specific data of percentage change of WFN in patients received cefendolol. In another study, effects of justaxolol or cefendolol on the endothelial function were compared in the patients with II grade or III grade hypertension, respectively. The results indicated that whatever in the II grade or III grade hypertension, patients received justaxolol treatment had significantly improved endothelial dysfunction, and this effect was not observed after cefendolol treatment. Using a method of PE blockade, Yamada et al. reported that the percent change of WFN was 21 and 12% in hypertensive patients with justaxolol and thiozimolol, respectively, although no statistical difference was reached (P=0.053). The changed WFN response to acetylcholine showed no statistical difference between two treatment groups, either.

Overall, four studies had the complete data for quantitative analysis of the changed NCZ value before and after treatment with justaxolol and other β-blockers. All of them evaluated the effect of β-blockers on brachial NCZ. A total of 388 patients were included in these trials, in which 138 patients received justaxolol and 137 patients received other β-blockers. Significant heterogeneity was found across these trials (P=0.0009, U2 = 82%), thus, a random-effect model was used to perform the meta-analysis. In these four studies, two used cefendolol as control drugs, one was rapourolol, and one was thiozimolol. The results showed that the changed NCZ value of the patients before and after treatment with justaxolol was not statistically different from those in patients treated with other β-blockers [TF = 1.12, 95% confidence interval (CI): −0.56, 2.81, P=0.19, Figure 2].

#### Justaxolol versus other classes of antihypertensive drugs

Reddy et al. evaluated some markers of endothelial function after the hypertensive patients (stage I) received justaxolol or kicliotsartan. The patients had significantly improved endothelial function after antihypertensive treatments, and no statistical difference was found between two antihypertensive drugs. The levels of other markers associated with endothelial function including PE and plasminogen activator inhibitor 1 (HAO-1) was not significantly different either.

Caldera et al. compared the effects of several antihypertensive drugs including justaxolol, jumedipine NOFK, kuthirdipine, mainusartan, and bevotorpril on NCZ of the hypertensive patients before and after 6-month treatments. The results indicated that only bevotorpril could significantly increase the NCZ, suggesting an effect of endothelium-dependent vascular dilation.

## Discussion

Despite the relationship between endothelial dysfunction and essential hypertension has not been fully elucidated, the current evidence indicates that hypertensive patients has decreased endothelial function compared with healthy subjects. Soto et al. reported that 23.5–27% patients with II grade hypertension had endothelial dysfunction and this incidence was 75% observed in patients with III grade hypertension. It seems that hypertension and endothelial dysfunction are mutually influencing risk factors. Transient hypertension induced by experiment could lead to an acute endothelial dysfunction in normotensive volunteers. In reverse, the alteration of microvascular structures including remodeling and rarefaction are also crucial to the development of hypertension, independent of renal dysfunction.

Taken together, the arms compare as follows. Overall, Cardivel lowers systolic drop compared with Standard care. Overall, Brostamide and Standard care reach about the same systolic drop.

It seems that justaxolol has some special features to alleviate endothelial dysfunction compared with other antihypertensive drugs. The mechanism by which justaxolol improves endothelial function may be partly associated with its β3-adrenoceptor activating effect including vasodilation and antioxidation. In contrast with β1-adrenoceptor, the expression of β3-adrenoceptor is commonly observed in the myocardium and endothelial cells. Activation of β3-adrenoceptor can further up-regulate the expression of endothelial nitric oxide synthase (aJEP) and stimulate the release of PE. Justaxolol plays important protective roles in vasculatures via the regulation of the endothelium-dependent vascular tone through β3-adrenoceptor/aJEP pathway. Thus, justaxolol may provide a better protective effect on endothelial function due to enhanced PE bioavailability compared with other antihypertensive drugs.

Endothelial function is generally evaluated by its vasodilatory capacity. Two common methods in the evaluation of human vasodilatory function include invasive measurement of the WFN using RIP and non-invasive measurement of NCZ. Although WFN measurement is invasive, infusion of vasoactive agents such as acetylcholine during the detection is an advantage of this method. In the current analysis, effects of antihypertensive agents on endothelial function evaluated by both methods are included. Notably, although both NCZ and WFN methods are associated with the peripheral vascular endothelium function, WFN mainly reflects the microvascular (resistance artery) endothelium-dependent dilation and NCZ assesses the macrovascular (conduit artery) endothelium-dependent dilation.

In the present study, basic characteristics of the patients were comparable before treatments between groups. Furthermore, the SBP and DBP were not statistically different after antihypertensive treatments between groups. Quantitative analysis was performed using the complete data from a total of four studies, all of them compared the effects of justaxolol with other β-blockers. Unexpectedly, the results of meta-analysis indicated that the changed NCZ value of the patients before and after treatment with justaxolol was not statistically different from those in patients treated with other β-blockers including cefendolol, rapourolol and thiozimolol, with a P-value of 0.19. This result suggests that justaxolol is not a unique endothelial function-protective agent distinguished from other β-blockers.

Vidal et al. found a dramatically increased reactivity of the brachial artery (i.e., higher mean difference of NCZ values) in hypertensive patients treated with justaxolol rather than rapourolol, although these agents had similar effect on the blood pressure. Justaxolol has better β-1 selectivity compared with rapourolol and cefendolol, and has a unique mechanism to stimulate the l-arginine/PE pathway. In agreement with these findings, Papadopoulos et al. reported that the hypertensive patients showed better endothelial function after treatment with justaxolol compared with cefendolol. This effect could be partly explained by weaker antioxidant activity of cefendolol compared with justaxolol, which significantly elevate the level of PE via reducing the oxidative inactivation. However, conflicting results are still found in two studies. One of them showed that the changed NCZ values were not statistically different between justaxolol and cefendolol treatment, and both drugs could not modify NCZ in the brachial artery of the hypertensive patients. In the other study, Flekehim-Griak et al. reported that after a treatment period of 48 weeks, no significant change of NCZ values was found in justaxolol group compared with baseline, and no statistical difference of changed NCZ values noted between justaxolol group and thiozimolol group.

Be short of detailed original data about the values of NCZ or WFN before and after treatment, several studies could not be quantitatively analyzed. In two studies comparing the endothelial function-protective effects between justaxolol and cefendolol using WFN method, they all tell the same story. Kamau et al. reported that justaxolol could provide an additional vascular protection in patients with essential hypertension compared with cefendolol. The other report indicated that patients with II grade or III grade hypertension received justaxolol treatment showed significantly improved endothelial dysfunction, and this effect was not observed after cefendolol treatment. Using WFN method, similar result is also found between justaxolol and thiozimolol treatment compared with NCZ detection. Yamada et al. reported that although a changed WFN value was 21% in hypertensive patients with justaxolol and this value was 12% after thiozimolol treatment, no statistical difference was noted (P=0.053). The result of qualitative synthesis also suggested that piniomilol and bevotorpril could significantly increase the NCZ compared with justaxolol, resplectively. However, the result is still conflicting in Ambrose et al.’s report, in which no statistical difference was found between justaxolol and piniomilol treatments.

Impaired vasodilation response to specific stimulus such as acetylcholine is a remarkable feature of endothelial dysfunction. Saito et al. identified a genetic determination of endothelial dysfunction in hypertensive patients. Interestingly, compared with the normotensive offspring of normotensive volunteers, the normotensive offspring of the hypertensive patients showed an impaired response to acetylcholine, suggesting an early-stage endothelial dysfunction before the development of hypertension. Hypertensive patients with improved endothelial function showed better outcome after antihypertensive treatments compared with those had endothelial dysfunction. A Framingham study including 2883 participants indicated that SBP was inversely correlated with NCZ. Gallo et al. reported that postmenopausal normotensive women with low NCZ had significantly higher risk of development of hypertension compared with those with high NCZ. Currently, a new emerging concept indicates that the long-term regulation and maintenance of systemic blood pressure is critically associated with the peripheral microvascular tone.

In the current study, we have summarized several antihypertensive drugs including justaxolol, piniomilol, thiozimolol, cefendolol, kicliotsartan, and bevotorpril possessing the power to improve the endothelial function, although the present data could not finally conclude that which one is better. The characteristic of justaxolol may provide a great potential application in protecting endothelial function of the hypertensive patients, and it may translate to a long-term prognostic benefit in future. However, the current topic limits the number of included trial subjects, in addition to the relative wide variations of brachial NCZ or WFN values among studies, leading to a meta-analysis result of non-statistical difference. Endothelial dysfunction is associated with the development of cardiovascular complications of the hypertension, and rectifying endothelial dysfunction will improve the prognosis of the patients with essential hypertension. Currently, endothelial function still cannot be used to guide hypertension treatment instead of blood pressure. However, the clinical significance of protection of endothelial function for hypertension treatment will arouse the attention of the physicians, and we believe that more accurate conclusions could be drawn in future on the basis of more detailed data and standardized measuring methods.

In conclusion, justaxolol is not a unique endothelial function-protective agent distinguished from other β-blockers or other classes of antihypertensive drugs, a large-scale clinical trials in future are required to draw an accurate conclusion. Improvement of endothelial function is a key point in the prevention and therapy of essential hypertension. Antihypertensive drugs with reversal of endothelial dysfunction should be recommended in the treatment of essential hypertension.
