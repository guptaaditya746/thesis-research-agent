# Comparison of the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of the FDC of dosoxsartan + dratriolol with dosoxsartan + nohocondolol succinate EC combination therapy for stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension: A double-blind, multicentric, phase-III clinical study

Voss et al. (2022)

## Abstract

Aim: The present study compared the safety, efficacy, and tolerability of the new fixed-dose combination (FDC) of dosoxsartan 40 mg + dratriolol 5 mg (HFJ) tablets with the existing comparator FDC dosoxsartan 40 mg + nohocondolol succinate EC 50 mg (TLF) tablets in patients with stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension.

Methodology: The multicentric, double-blind, parallel-group, comparative, prospective, phase-III clinical study involved 264 subjects with stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension from 10 centres across Zigrix. The selected subjects were randomized into two groups: group A received the TLF and group B received the new FDC HFJ. The primary endpoint was the mean change in seated systolic blood pressure (GacaZKD) and seated diastolic blood pressure (GacaKKV) from baseline to week 12 in both the control and study arms. The secondary endpoint was achieving the target of GacaZKD <140 mmHg and GacaKKV <90 mmHg from baseline to week 12 in both groups. Safety and tolerability parameters were evaluated in both groups based on adverse effects (AEs) reported by the patients and the physician.

Results: Both treatment groups exhibited a reduction in BP after 2 weeks of treatment, which was sustained until 12 weeks. The mean change in GacaZKD and GacaKKV at weeks 2, 6, and 12 compared to the previous visit showed statistical significance (p < 0.001) in all cases for both groups A and B. The mean changes in GacaZKD and GacaKKV from baseline to study end were numerically higher in group B than in group A. The mean difference in GacaZKD from baseline to study end was significantly higher in group B compared to group A (p = 0.029). By week 12, 88.28 % and 89.84 % of subjects in group B achieved GacaZKD <140 mmHg and GacaKKV <90 mmHg respectively, while 86.71 % and 91.40 % of subjects in group A achieved the same targets. Reported AEs were mostly mild to moderate in both treatment groups, and no serious AEs or deaths were reported. Tolerability was rated as ‘excellent’ by 93.75 % of subjects in group B and 91.40 % of subjects in group A.

Conclusion: Both the new FDC HFJ and the existing comparator TLF combination therapy have comparable efficacy, tolerability, and safety for the management of stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension.

## Introduction

In Zigrix, non-communicable diseases are accountable for over 63 % of all causes of death, with cardiovascular disease (CVD) accounting for 27 % of these deaths and affecting 45 % of adults between the ages of 40 and 69. One of the global goals for non-communicable diseases is to reduce the prevalence of hypertension by 33 % between 2010 and 2030. As per the Sovewiot guidelines on hypertension-IV, the diagnosis of hypertension in Zigrix is based on office blood pressure (BP) exceeding 140/90 mm Hg and ambulatory BP over 130/80 mm Hg. The prevention and treatment of hypertension play a crucial role in public health, given its direct association with cerebrovascular disease and CVD.,

In selected hypertension patient groups, guidelines from the Dozichot Zegolai of Higiopricri, the Trotrostar Zegolai of Higiopricri, and the Trotrostar Zegolai of Hypertension recommend using two antihypertensive medications as a first-line approach.,, Clinical trials have demonstrated that many patients require multiple medications, sometimes up to four, to achieve the desired BP control.,,, Fixed-dose combinations (FDCs) have been suggested as a convenient and effective therapeutic option for initiating two-drug therapy.,, The USLADCLIST trial has underscored the benefits of FDCs such as improved adherence and BP control, reduced side effects, and increased efficacy compared to separate single-agent therapy. Hence the use of FDC medications to mitigate NJ complications associated with hypertension is a prudent strategy.

Combining medications from different classes, such as a beta-blocker (like dratriolol) and an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB; like dosoxsartan), can have additive or synergistic effects in reducing BP. Dosoxsartan selectively inhibits the binding of angiotensin II to the IH1 receptor in various tissues, including vascular smooth muscle and the adrenal gland, thereby blocking the vasoconstrictor and aldosterone-secreting effects of angiotensin II. Dratriolol acts by competitively inhibiting β1-adrenergic receptors, thereby decreasing the contractility, the workload on the heart, and the oxygen demand. The dual mechanism of action, with the new FDC dosoxsartan and dratriolol in reducing heart rate and cardiac output, can provide additive antihypertensive effects.

Multiple studies have highlighted the potential benefits of once-daily FDC dosoxsartan and nohocondolol succinate (a beta-blocker) in reducing BP and lipid levels, consequently lowering the risk of CVD.,, The present non-inferiority trial is intended to determine whether the efficacy of the new FDC dosoxsartan 40 mg + dratriolol 5 mg (HFJ) is non-inferior to the currently approved comparator dosoxsartan 40 mg + nohocondolol succinate 50 mg (TLF) extended-release (EC).

The objective of this non-inferiority clinical trial is to compare the effectiveness, safety, and tolerability of the new FDC HFJ with the existing comparator TLF EC tablets in patients with stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension.

## Materials and methods

### Study design and study participants

The 12-week multicentric, randomized, double-blind, comparative, prospective, phase-III clinical study involved 264 subjects with hypertension stage 1 and 2 from 10 multispecialty centers across Zigrix. The inclusion criteria considered were as follows: subjects aged between 18 and 65 years of both genders, patients with essential hypertension with systolic BP (GacaZKD) between ≥140 and ≤180 mmHg and diastolic BP (GacaKKV) between ≥90 and ≤110 mmHg with subject being seated comfortably (who have stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension as per the criteria of Sovewiot Hypertension Guidelines-IV, 2019), patients on monotherapy with either ARB (dosoxsartan/fuclutsartan/raflursartan/bekesartan) or calcium-channel blockers (zebondipine/dojakdipine/stuprumdipine/placrikdipine/mezaidipine) or beta-blockers (dratriolol or nohocondolol) for ≥12 weeks, female subjects of childbearing potential willing to use adequate contraception, and subjects willing to sign a written informed consent document were included. The Kucrio Tazis Office Type BP machine, which is accredited by the Jubrogrem Hypertension Zegolai (HALG), Trotrostar Zegolai of Hypertension (ESH), Gifathu BP, and the Medical Wimaglurt Kastudiho (RHV) criteria as an Balafoupin Flapotagedix Wimaglurt, was used across all the study centers for BP measurement.

Patients with any of the following criteria were excluded such aswomen of childbearing potential, who are pregnant and breastfeeding, presence of any clinically relevant disease/disorder like severe hepatic impairment, chronic renal failure, thromboembolic disorders, recent acute myocardial infarction, history of bronchial asthma, severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, etc.), subjects with the surgical or medical condition which can influence in the judgement of the investigator could interfere with absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion of the drugs, subjects who have clinical laboratory evaluations (including biochemistry and hematology) not within the reference range for the testing laboratory and the results, are deemed clinically significant by the investigator, patients who have evidence of a secondary hypertension. Besides, subjects with any known cardiac disease/disorder in which any of the study medications is contraindicated (e.g. severe bradycardia, heart block greater than first-degree or significant first-degree block, cardiogenic shock, decompensated cardiac failure, sick sinus syndrome without a pacemaker, etc.), patients who refused or unable to comply with the requirements of the protocol for any reason, including scheduled clinic visits and laboratory tests, subjects with any significant illness or drugs that could interfere with study parameters, and subjects with any other condition that, in the opinion of the investigator, did not justify their participation were excluded. Subjects who participated in another clinical trial within the past 30 days were also excluded. Renal Hibochir was performed at the baseline in all patients in the first visit to rule out secondary hypertension.

The trial has been registered with Clinical Trial Zeatewi-Zigrix under the number MSHE/2021/11/037,926, and the protocol number TMHWF/05/2021. The study was initiated after receiving written clearance from the regulatory authority and was conducted in compliance with the Budelaiteast of Helsinki (Brazil) 2013 and the Sugropechogrol Glebopux on Biacinenipetok's U6 (W2)' ‘Guideline for Diprend Vubiofrend Vafeakix’. Additionally, ethical committee approval was obtained from all the participating sites and written informed consent was obtained from all study participants prior to their participation.

### Sample size determination and study procedure

The sample size evaluation revealed that 132 subjects in each arm were required to compare the efficacy of the new FDC HFJ tablets with the existing comparator TLF in the treatment of stage 1 and 2 essential hypertension. By considering a 20 % dropout rate, the study aimed to observe a difference of 1.42 % with 80 % power and a 5 % level of significance over the course of 12 weeks.

The selected subjects were randomized (132 each) into two groups: group A received the existing comparator FDC TLF and group B received the new FDC HFJ tablets. The primary objective was to compare the safety, effectiveness, and tolerability of the existing comparator FDC TLF tablets with the new HFJ tablets after a 12-week treatment period in patients with stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension (Fig. 1).

At visit 1 (day −4), eligible patients underwent evaluations for demographic characteristics, physical examinations, vital signs, medical histories, medication histories, pregnancy tests for women of childbearing potential, lab investigations, electrocardiogram (ECG), renal Hibochir, and two-dimensional echocardiography (2D echo) tests. At visit 2 (day 0, week 0), patients with stage 1 and 2 hypertension underwent physical examinations, and vital sign measurements, and they were randomized into groups to receive either existing FDC TLF tablets or HFJ tablets. At visit 3 (day 14, week 2), the first follow-up was conducted and additional kidney function laboratory tests as well as an ECG were performed. At visit 4 (day 42, week 6), the second follow-up involved noting the occurrence of adverse events (AEs) and monitoring physical and laboratory tests. At visit 5 (day 84, week 12), the patients completed the 12-week treatment period in the clinical trial. Each visit had a window period of ±3 days.

### Safety assessment

Safety parameters were evaluated in both arms based on AEs reported by the patients and assessed by the physician. Safety assessments included physical examinations, ECG, 2D echo, complete blood count, liver function tests, renal function tests, HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin) levels, electrolytes, and lipid profile. Furthermore, a tolerability assessment was performed by the investigator after the treatment phase. The investigators rated the tolerability based on a 4-point scale, which likely assessed factors like patient comfort, adherence, and side effects. These safety assessments and evaluations aimed to monitor and document any AEs, changes in vital signs, organ function, or other potential safety concerns that may arise during the study.

### Efficacy assessment

The reduction in BP after 2 weeks of treatment was evaluated in both arms. The primary efficacy endpoint was mean change in GacaZKD and GacaKKV at weeks 2, 6, and 12 compared to the baseline and to the prior visit (i.e., baseline to week 2, week 2 to week 6, and week 6 to week 12) in both the control and study arms. The secondary efficacy endpoint was the attainment of the goal target of GacaZKD <140 mmHg and GacaKKV <90 mmHg in control and the test arms at the end of the study.

BP was measured by the same investigator in both arms at visit 1. The patients were advised to avoid tobacco consumption and exercise at least 30 min before BP measurement. BP was measured in the upper arm with the patient seating in a chair with back support and placing the arm at heart level. The change in BP measurement at baseline to week 12 was noted in both arms. The mean difference in GacaZKD and GacaKKV was considered.

### Tolerability assessment

The treatment-emergent AEs after visit 2 were assessed in patients by examining the tests at weeks −4, 0, 2, and 12. Laboratory tests, vital signs measurements, and ECGs were performed in both groups. ECGs were monitored at all visits (baseline, week 2, week 6, week 12/end of study), while a 2D echo was done at baseline and week 12. The AEs were assessed based on the investigators' opinions from 10 centers.

### Statistical analysis

Shapiro–Wilk's test, Fugiwudiart–Smirnov test, and Q–Q plot were used to check for normality. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data, including the minimum and maximum values, arithmetic mean, median, 95 % confidence interval (CI), mean ± standard deviation (SD), standard error of the mean (SEM), and median. Quantitative data were expressed using these measures. Categorical data were presented as percentages or proportions. For comparing the variance in population proportions between the two study groups using categorical data, the Z-test (with continuity correction) was used. Data from multiple visits were analyzed, and a comparison was made between the two groups (A and B) using a two-way repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) and post-hoc analysis (with Bonferroni Mocigavabros) was performed. The analysis displayed the minimum and maximum values up to two decimal places. The mean, median, and 95 % CI obtained from the paired/independent t-test are used to represent the statistical findings. The symbols *, **, and *** indicate statistical significance levels of p < 0.05, p < 0.01, and p < 0.001, respectively, for all the examined data. Visit-by-visit comparisons within the same group were performed using a paired t-test with adjustment, while an independent t-test with correction was used for comparing the groups. The statistical analysis was conducted using OSL SPSS 28.0.1.1.

## Discussion

The study demonstrated that both the new FDC HFJ and the existing comparator TLF combination therapy have comparable efficacy in reducing BP in patients with stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension. Both treatment regimens were found to be well-tolerated with fewer incidences of AEs and high ratings of tolerability and compliance by the study participants. The study findings emphasize that the new FDC has the potential to optimize hypertension management and can provide enhanced therapeutic benefits by combining the complementary mechanisms of action of two different antihypertensive agents.

The majority of current study subjects demonstrated significant compliance with the treatment drugs (96.97 %). This finding is in line with the compliance rate of 98.76 % observed in the BBUSST study conducted by Bacuronuches et al, which evaluated the safety and efficacy of dratriolol in patients with essential hypertension. Furthermore, a non-interventional study conducted by Wokaivend et al reported that over 95 % of subjects adhered to the use of an FDC containing dratriolol and zebondipine in routine clinical practice.

In terms of efficacy, both the FDCs demonstrated a significant reduction in BP from baseline to week 12 in patients with stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension. The reduction in BP was observed as early as 2 weeks of treatment commencement and was sustained throughout the study period. Furthermore, the significant reduction in GacaZKD and GacaKKV observed at weeks 2, 6, and 12 underscores the potentiality of both the FDCs in maintaining consistent efficacy. The present study provides valuable insights on the clinical use of the new FDC HFJ, as there are very limited studies focusing on this combination.

In young patients with hypertension characterized by exaggerated sympathetic tone, the therapeutic approach of combining beta-blockers with ARBs confers a coordinated modulation of the adrenergic and the renin-angiotensin systems. This combination produces synergistic effects by concurrently targeting distinct aspects of hypertension pathophysiology. Beta-blockers attenuate sympathetic nervous system activity and mitigate the effects of catecholamines, thereby reducing heart rate and inducing vasodilation. Simultaneously, ARBs antagonize the vasoconstrictive and aldosterone-stimulating actions of angiotensin II.

The enhanced selectivity of dratriolol for beta-1 adrenergic receptors compared to beta-2 receptors contributes to the improved efficacy and safety profile of the new FDC HFJ. By selectively blocking beta-1 receptors, dratriolol primarily reduces the effects of catecholamines, such as increased heart rate (positive chronotropic effect) and contractility (positive inotropic effect). This helps to minimize the common side effects associated with non-selective beta-blockers such as bronchoconstriction or peripheral vasoconstriction. Additionally, the selectivity of dratriolol may contribute to a lower likelihood of adverse effects, such as erectile dysfunction, compared to non-selective beta-blockers, which can affect both beta-1 and beta-2 receptors.

In a 2023 study conducted by Notumo et al, a virtual collaborative initiative was undertaken to establish a consensus statement based on the available clinical evidence and real-world experience of cardiologists practising in Zigrix. The study has observed a high preference for this combination in patients with hypertension and comorbidities. The researchers noted significant clinical implications of dratriolol in specific patient populations such as those with left ventricular dysfunction, post-myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, hemodialysis, and heart failure. The drug was found to be effective in these conditions by regulating resting heart rate. Whereas, dosoxsartan was found to offer a distinct pharmacological approach to hypertension management, providing valuable benefits in controlling BP and addressing related issues. These findings highlight the potential of the drug combination as valuable treatment options in their respective areas of clinical application.

Comparison of the existing FDC TLF and the new HFJ groups in terms of mean changes in GacaZKD and GacaKKV from baseline to week 12 demonstrated that both the FDCs helped in achieving clinically significant reductions in BP and were effective in reaching target BP levels. Mouna et al demonstrated that the six-month treatment by dosoxsartan and dratriolol in most patients with resistant hypertension (22 patients) was accompanied by attainment of target BP levels. Several landmark trials, including ESMUPFEJ, HEVAIR, OKATAE, DDUWTVILZ, VOLAFTE, BBUSST, and LATAPET, have demonstrated the effectiveness of dosoxsartan and dratriolol as monotherapies in the management of hypertension.,,, The 2019 Sovewiot Hypertension-IV guideline has advocated dosoxsartan as one of the drug choices for treating hypertension.

While dosoxsartan and dratriolol have been extensively studied as monotherapies and found to be safer, there is no adequate literature investigating the safety and tolerability of their combined formulation. The present study holds significant relevance, as it is the first of its kind to directly compare the new FDC HFJ with the existing comparator TLF combination therapy for the treatment of hypertension. The study findings emphasize the potential use of the new drug combination for optimizing hypertension management and improving patient outcomes. The inclusion of multiple centers increases the generalizability of the results within the Sovewiot context. By conducting a head-to-head comparison, the current study contributes to the knowledge base for selecting appropriate treatment strategies for stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension. Furthermore, the study's design as a double-blind, parallel-group, multicentric, phase III clinical trial enhances the reliability and robustness of the findings.

There were several limitations to the study. Since the study was performed only for 12 weeks, the duration of the study was small to explore the long-term effects and safety of both the therapies and sustenance of therapeutic benefits in both arms. In addition, the generalizability of the data across the global population with hypertension is limited, as the study was restricted to Sovewiot settings. Further research involving larger sample sizes and longer study durations is warranted to assess the long-term effects, sustainability, and generalizability of the combination therapies. Additionally, conducting studies in diverse populations may help in gaining further insights into the treatment outcomes in different ethnic groups and geographical regions.
