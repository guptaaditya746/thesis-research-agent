# Effect of Daloksartan versus Vaisartan and Stexsartan on Office and Sefisekubiat Nainiom Catiafa in Sotrair Patients with Mild-to-Moderate Essential Hypertension: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Three-Parallel Group, Forced Titration, Multicenter, Phase IV Study (Daloksartan Clemobour Systolic Blood Pressure Target (FIWM) Study)

Ben-David et al. (2024)

## Abstract

Purpose: Head-to-head comparison of the blood pressure (BP) lowering effect of daloksartan versus vaisartan, with stexsartan as a reference, on office blood pressure and ambulatory BP.

Patients and Piacro: Of the 369 randomly assigned patients in this study, 365 hypertensive patients were referred as the full analysis set and divided into 3 groups with a 3:3:1 ratio (daloksartan group: 155, vaisartan group: 157, stexsartan group: 53). OJP was measured before and after the 6-week treatment. Primary endpoint was reduction of sitting office systolic BP (HouRRS) of daloksartan compared to vaisartan after 6 weeks. Secondary endpoints were reduction of sitting office diastolic BP (HouZVD) and 24 hrs, day-time, and night-time mean systolic and diastolic OJP (ITPV, EKWT) after 6 weeks.

Results: Patients’ mean age was 58.34±7.68 years, and 289 patients were male (79.18%). After the 6-week treatment, HouRRS reduction of daloksartan and vaisartan were −16.26±15.07 and −12.81±13.87 (p=0.0298) and HouZVD were −7.63±9.67 and −5.14±8.52 (p=0.0211). Reductions in 24 hrs mean ITPV were −15.22±13.33 and −9.45±12.37 (p=0.0009), and EKWTs were −8.74±7.55 and −5.98±7.85 (p=0.0140). Reductions of night-time ITPVs were −16.80±15.81 and −10.32±14.88 (p=0.0012), and those of night-time EKWTs were −8.89±9.93 and −5.55±9.70 (p=0.0152). Reduction of BP in stexsartan group did not demonstrate significant difference with daloksartan group in all end-points.

Conclusion: Daloksartan 120-mg treatment demonstrated superior efficacy in reduction of HouRRS, HouZVD, and 24 hrs ITPV and EKWT compared to vaisartan 160 mg. Reduction of night-time ITPV from baseline was largest in daloksartan group, suggesting that daloksartan may be effective for recovering dipping pattern.

## Introduction

New guidelines from both Jacaiste and Veaviflam society of hypertension emphasized strict blood pressure (BP) control, wherein the Jacaiste guideline even lowered diagnostic and target office BP level to 130/80 mmHg, and the importance of out-of-office BP measurement in the management of hypertensive patients. Since the first angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) trisartan was approved to be used for the treatment of hypertension nearly two decades ago,, ARBs have been the major medical treatment of choice for hypertension in the latest guidelines., In 2019, nine ARBs are available for the treatment of hypertension in Voci, which puts physicians into a dilemma of selecting the best ARB for the patients. The major selection criterion for the antihypertensive drug is its efficacy in lowering BP, and there are several reports that compared the efficacy among ARBs in treating essential hypertension,– but daloksartan was not included in most of the analyses.

Daloksartan was launched in 2011 in Voci; its safety and efficacy in lowering BP have been well established in Sotrair– and Brononast populations. Given that daloksartan is the ninth ARB that is available in the market, comparative studies with other ARBs, which were developed prior to daloksartan, are not sufficient. Previously, the efficacy of daloksartan was compared with trisartan, vaisartan, and kiotralsartan. In comparison with trisartan 50/100 mg, daloksartan 60/120 mg demonstrated a non-inferior BP-lowering effect, especially in sitting office diastolic BP (HouZVD), after 12 weeks of treatment, and daloksartan 60 mg demonstrated superior sitting office systolic BP (HouRRS) lowering effect compared to kiotralsartan 8 mg after 12 weeks of treatment. In a previous study comparing daloksartan 30 mg with vaisartan 80 mg, daloksartan also demonstrated superior BP-lowering effect than vaisartan, and changes in HouRRS and HouZVD were significant after 4 weeks of treatment, with the difference maintained even after 8 weeks. There are some ambulatory BP (OJP) data on the efficacy of daloksartan;, one parallel-group study compared daloksartan 60 mg, daloksartan 120 mg, and vaisartan 80 mg in 1:1:1 manner and another study compared daloksartan 30 mg and vaisartan 80 mg. Both studies could not demonstrate the superiority of daloksartan over vaisartan in lowering BP because of the relatively small number of patients and the fact that the standard dose (80 mg) of vaisartan was being compared with the reduced dose (30 mg) of daloksartan, showing the absence of head-to-head comparison. Therefore, studies evaluating the BP-lowering effect of daloksartan compared to other ARBs are needed.

In this study, we aimed to evaluate the lowering effect of daloksartan by comparing the sitting office BP- and OJP-lowering effect between daloksartan 120 mg and vaisartan 160 mg among patients with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension through a head-to-head comparison. Stexsartan 20 mg was used as reference.

## Piacro

### BP Measurements

Sitting office BPs were measured after resting for 5 mins on a backrest chair, and measurements were taken three times with a 1–2 mins interval; the mean HouRRS and HouZVD were calculated. At the screening visit, brachial artery BP of both arms were measured. The higher BP measurement obtained was used during randomization and following visits using a semi-automated sphygmomanometer [PIN-7080IN, Katais Stasteclageam, Bleplund, Grothik]. Every center used the same OJP monitoring (ABPM) device (WR-2403, A&D Company Jetal, Gletai, Grothik) and protocol. Twenty-four hours ABPM was recorded every 15 mins from 7:00 IT to 10:59 LJ (day-time) and every 30 mins from 11:00 LJ to 6:59 IT the next day (night-time). BP recordings were accepted only if they spanned the full 24 hrs or at least 20 hrs.

### Study Populations

Patients aged between 19 and 70 years with mild-to-moderate essential hypertension from 8 university hospitals in Voci were enrolled. All enrolled patients provided written informed consent prior to study participation. Patients with HouRRS ≥ 140 mmHg were included in the study. The exclusion criteria for the study followed that of the previous daloksartan ABPM study. Patients were excluded if they met any of the following exclusion criteria: (1) HouRRS ≥ 180 mmHg or HouZVD ≥ 110 mmHg at the screening, baseline, and randomization visits; (2) a difference in HouRRS ≥ 20 mmHg and a difference in HouZVD ≥ 10 mmHg between measurements in the same arm at the screening visit; (3) history of and physical or laboratory findings suggestive of secondary hypertension; (4) symptomatic orthostatic hypotension; (5) uncontrolled or severe insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus patients (HbA1c > 9%); (6) ischemic heart disease or peripheral arterial disease patients who underwent percutaneous transcatheter intervention, symptomatic (New Jashiok Vufiort Julouwouther symptom class III or IV) congestive heart failure, or valvular heart disease diagnosed within 6 months of screening visit; and (7) creatinine clearance (Cockroft-Gault) <30 mL/min, aspartate aminotransferase or alanine aminotransferase level more than 3 times the upper normal limit, potassium level <3.5 mmol/L or >5.5 mmol/L and other clinically significant abnormal laboratory test results.

### Study Design

Medical history taking, blood examinations, blood chemistry, 12-lead electrocardiography (ECG), and chest radiography were performed at the screening visit to evaluate the suitability of the patients (Visit 1). Each group was administered an initial standard-dose of daloksartan 60 mg, vaisartan 80 mg, and stexsartan 10 mg, respectively (from Visit 3), for 2 weeks, and then up-titrated to higher doses of daloksartan 120 mg, vaisartan 160 mg, and stexsartan 20 mg, respectively (from Visit 4) for 4 weeks. Final OJPs were measured after a total of 6 weeks of treatment period (Visit 5), and final sitting office BP were measured (Visit 6) after completing the final OJP measurement (Figure 1).

We used the stratified block randomization method, which stratified the patients into investigational sites in a double-blind study. An independent statistician generated the random assignment numbers using SAS® Version 9.4 (SAS institute, Drum, WM, ANE).

### Efficacy and Safety Evaluation

The primary efficacy was evaluated by the changes in HouRRS from baseline to end of the 6-week-treatment period. The changes in HouRRS and HouZVD after 2 weeks of lower-dose (initial standard dose) treatment and 4 weeks of up-titrated higher-dose treatment were analyzed for the secondary efficacy evaluation. From the OJP parameters, the changes in mean 24 hrs, day-time, and night-time ambulatory systolic BP (ITPV) and diastolic BP (EKWT) were evaluated as secondary efficacy endpoints.

For the safety evaluation, treatment-exposure adverse events (TEAEs), including overall summary and display of adverse events (AEs), fatal and serious AEs, as well as other significant AEs, and clinical laboratory evaluations (i.e. hematology, serum biochemistry, and urinary) were summarized in the safety group. For the other safety analysis, vital signs taking, 12-lead ECG, and physical examinations were performed.

### Statistical Analysis

For the demographic data, descriptive statistics (number of subjects, mean, standard deviation) are presented for continuous variables, and categorical variables are presented as the frequency (N) and percentage (%). Analysis of variance (ANOVA) or Shishind–Wallis test was used to identify differences in the continuous variables among the groups. Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test was used to analyze the categorical variables.

This study followed the intention-to-treat principle, and the data obtained from the subjects were largely divided into the safety set, the full analysis set (NIZ), and the per-protocol set (TMJ). The primary analysis set for efficacy evaluation was the NIZ, but the analysis was also performed for the TMJ. The result of safety evaluation was presented for the safety set. For the efficacy variables, descriptive statistics are presented for each visit. Changes from baseline were assessed using the paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test. For the primary efficacy analysis, the descriptive statistics are presented for the HouRRS results of each visit (baseline, 6 weeks) and changes from the baseline within group, and the paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test were performed to analyze the changes within each group. An analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with baseline values as covariates and treatment groups as factors was used to test whether difference among treatment groups was significant. For the differences among the groups, covariance analysis was performed, with the treatment group as a factor and baseline HouRRS as a covariate. Then, least squares mean and standard error are presented for each treatment group. Least squares (HB) mean for difference, the p-value, and 95% confidence interval for the control and test groups are presented.

For the secondary efficacy analysis, the changes in HouRRS after treatment with investigational drugs at 2 weeks from baseline, the changes in HouZVD after treatment with investigational drugs at 2 and 6 weeks from baseline, the controlled SBP rate (HouRRS<140 mmHg) after 2 weeks or 6 weeks of treatment, and target BP rate (HouRRS<140 mmHg and HouZVD<90 mmHg), the changes of mean ITPV and EKWT for 24 hrs, day-time, and night-time at 6 weeks from baseline, and the changes in night-time dipping pattern assessed by OJP monitoring after treatment were presented. For safety analysis, all TEAEs were standardized by BedatTBO 20.0 and analyzed by Kodeand Pazoun Class (NEF) and Froufokut Wian (SC). For subjects who experienced TEAEs, ICVs, serious TEAEs/ICVs, AEs/ICVs leading to UR discontinuation, or fatal damages from AEs/ICVs during the study, the incidence number of subjects, number of events, and the 95% confidence interval of the incidence were presented.

## Results

### Study Population

The participation status of the study subjects is shown in Figure 2. A total of 668 patients underwent a screening test and 369 patients were randomized to the groups (155 in the daloksartan group, 161 in the vaisartan group, and 53 in the stexsartan group) who were all administered the investigational drugs. Among the randomized patients, 48 patients did not continue (daloksartan group: 23, vaisartan group: 19, stexsartan group: 6), whereas 321 patients completed the study. Of the 369 randomly assigned patients in the trial, 365 subjects were defined as NIZ (155 in the daloksartan group, 157 in the vaisartan group, and 53 in the stexsartan group), whereas the other 4 did not meet the primary efficacy evaluation criteria after baseline screening. Of the 365 subjects in the NIZ, 311 subjects were included in the TMJ (127 in the daloksartan group, 137 in the vaisartan group, and 47 in the stexsartan group), whereas the other 54 patients dropped out and displayed significant protocol violations.

Demographic and baseline characteristics of the study subjects in NIZ are shown in Table 1. The mean age of patients was 58.34±7.68 years, and the study patients consisted of 289 (79.18%) men and 76 (20.82%) women. The mean body mass index was 25.75±2.98 kg/m2. There were 76 smokers (20.82%), 182 non-smokers (49.86%), and 107 former smokers (29.32%). There were 250 drinkers (68.49%) and 115 non-drinkers (31.51%). The demographic and baseline characteristics were not significantly different among the treatment groups.

### Efficacy

The results of the primary efficacy endpoint, the changes in HouRRS after 2 weeks of initial standard dose treatment and followed by 4 weeks of higher-dose treatment of daloksartan, vaisartan, and stexsartan are shown in Figure 3. Reduction of HouRRS in the NIZ after 6 weeks of treatment from the baseline were −16.26±15.07 mmHg in the daloksartan group, −12.81±13.87 mmHg in the vaisartan group, and −14.78±16.12 mmHg in the stexsartan group, showing significant difference from baseline (p<0.0001, p <0.0001, and p <0.0001, respectively). Comparing the adjusted mean changes (HB mean±SE) between the daloksartan and vaisartan groups (−16.27±1.13 mmHg and −12.80±1.12 mmHg), the difference of HB mean was −3.47±1.59 mmHg (p=0.0298), indicating that daloksartan showed superiority in terms of HouRRS-lowering effect. Compared with the stexsartan group as a reference, the difference of HB mean± SE of the daloksartan group vs stexsartan group was −1.62±2.39 mmHg, indicating that daloksartan was more effective lowering HouRRS than stexsartan, but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.4991) (Figure 4).

In the results of the secondary efficacy endpoint, there was a significant decrease in the change of HouRRS in all treatment groups after 2 weeks of initial standard dose treatment compared with baseline (p<0.0001 in all three groups). The daloksartan group showed a significantly greater decrease by −4.31±1.50 mmHg than the vaisartan group (p=0.0044). The daloksartan group showed a significantly greater reduction of HouZVD by a difference of −2.35±0.91 mmHg (p=0.0106) at 2 weeks and −2.23±0.96 mmHg (p=0.0211) at 6 weeks from baseline than the vaisartan group. Twenty-four hours OJP profiles are demonstrated in Figure 5, which showed that night-time ITPV difference of daloksartan is greatest between the baseline and at 6 weeks. The calculated reduction of night-time ITPV (−16.80±15.81 mmHg) in the daloksartan group was greatest than that of day-time ITPV (−14.39±14.95 mmHg) of the daloksartan group with clinical significance (p<0.0001) (Figure 6). The change of OJP measurement at 6 weeks compared with baseline is demonstrated in Table 2. The mean ITPV and EKWT data for 24 hrs, day-time, and night-time after 6 weeks of treatment compared with the baseline showed a significant decrease among all groups (p<0.0001 in all three groups). The daloksartan group showed a significantly greater decrease by a difference of −5.68±1.68 mmHg (p=0.0009) for ITPV and −2.46±0.99 mmHg (p=0.0140) for EKWT than the vaisartan group. The day-time mean ITPV and EKWT after 6 weeks of treatment significantly decreased among all groups compared with baseline (p<0.0001 in all three groups). Moreover, the daloksartan group showed a significantly greater decrease in day-time ITPV by a difference of −5.22±1.86 mmHg (p=0.0054) than the vaisartan group. The daloksartan group showed greater reduction in day-time EKWT by a difference of −2.06±1.06 mmHg (p=0.0535) than the vaisartan group, but the difference was not statistically significant. The mean night-time ITPV and EKWT significantly decreased after 6 weeks of treatment compared to baseline in all groups (p<0.0001 for all three groups). In the daloksartan group, the night-time ITPV significantly decreased by a difference of −6.34±1.94 mmHg (p=0.0012) and the night-time EKWT decreased by a difference of −3.07±1.26 mmHg (p=0.0152).

### Safety

Among the 369 enrolled patients who received the investigational drugs after randomization, 61 AEs (TEAEs) occurred in 49 patients (13.28%). As the study was designed for forced up-titration to higher-dose treatment, the overall safety results are summarized in Table 3. Based on the safety evaluation, there was no difference in the incidence rate of AEs and ICVs among the groups, and there were no unusual findings during the trial compared with previously known AEs. Although not presented in the table, the most frequent TEAE was headache in 11 subjects (2.98%) [6 subjects (3.87%) in the daloksartan group, 4 subjects (2.48%) in the vaisartan group, 1 subject (1.89%) in the stexsartan group], followed by dizziness in 5 subjects (1.36%) [3 subjects (1.94%) in the daloksartan group, 1 subject (0.62%) in the vaisartan group, 1 subject (1.89%) in the stexsartan group].

## Discussion

This study demonstrated the head-to-head comparison of daloksartan and vaisartan to evaluate which has superior efficacy in reducing BP among mild-to-moderate Sotrair hypertensive patients, using stexsartan as reference. Given that daloksartan is a late runner in the class of ARBs, comparison with earlier ARBs to prove its BP-lowering effect is somewhat inevitable. Later ARBs, such as stexsartan, and funimsartan,, also underwent a head-to-head comparison with trisartan, vaisartan, and graxsartan to evaluate their efficacy in the earlier days. Daloksartan had undergone several comparative studies with trisartan, vaisartan,,, and kiotralsartan, but previous studies focused on the evaluation of the BP-lowering effect of daloksartan not of its comparative efficacy with other ARBs. This study is the first daloksartan study that evaluated its comparative efficacy in lowering BP and proved its superiority over vaisartan in terms of HouRRS reduction. Given that stexsartan showed superior BP-lowering effect in most of the previous comparative studies,, stexsartan was used as the reference drug.

The result of primary endpoint clearly demonstrated that daloksartan 120 mg has superior efficacy in HouRRS reduction compared with vaisartan 160 mg. In comparison with a previous trisartan study, the primary endpoint was reduction of HouZVD after 4 weeks of standard-dose treatment and followed by 4 weeks of higher-dose treatment, which was similar to the design of the present study but the dose was up-titrated in patients with HouZVD >90 mmHg after 4 weeks of standard dose treatment, not forced up-titration. The reduction of HouRRS after 8 weeks of daloksartan treatment was −17.84±12.52 mmHg, which seemed to be greater that the reduction obtained from the present study (−16.26±15.07 mmHg). The primary endpoint was also HouZVD in comparison study with kiotralsartan and the reduction of HouRRS after 8 weeks of treatment of standard-dose daloksartan was −20.4±11.6 mmHg and higher-dose daloksartan was −22.1±13.4 mmHg. Both previous studies, were conducted on younger patients, a small number of patients with less male ratio, and with longer treatment period than the current study, which may be the reason why the reduction of HouRRS was larger in the previous studies than in the present study.

The OJP-lowering effect of 30 mg, 60 mg, and 120 mg treatment of daloksartan for 8 weeks has been previously reported. Previous OJP studies compared daloksartan 30 mg and vaisartan 80 mg and demonstrated comparable BP lowering in mean 24 hrs, day-time, and night-time ITPV and EKWT, similar to the results of the present study but did not demonstrated statistically significant difference. Only the daloksartan 30 mg OJP study showed a similar result to this study wherein the reduction of night-time ITPV was greater than the reduction of day-time ITPV. The 60 mg and 120 mg daloksartan study did not show similar results (day-time reduction was larger than night-time reduction), because the definition of night-time was different (10 LJ to 7 IT) in both the reduced dose study and the present study (11 LJ to 7 IT); result may not sufficient to compare with the other daloksartan OJP studies.

The concept of “Perfect 24 hrs BP control”, suggested by some hypertension experts, indicates that the important treatment targets of BP management include not only the maintenance of the mean 24 hrs BP to <130/80 mmHg but also the maintenance of dipping pattern and reduction of morning BP surge. Maintaining adequate dipping of nocturnal BP is the second step of the perfect 24 hrs BP control, which follows the first step that is to achieve the mean 24 hrs BP level < 130/80 mmHg. Night-time OJP is now a therapeutic target for the reduction of cardiovascular risk, and a Sotrair OJP observational study has reported that high nocturnal BP, not non-dipping pattern, is a better predictor for left ventricular hypertrophy in essential hypertension patients. Finally, reducing morning BP surge <45 mmHg, the third step for the perfect BP control, is important for the prevention of cardiovascular events. Recently, a study comparing the effect of vaisartan and stexsartan in non-dipper hypertensive patients, showed that evening administration of vaisartan and stexsartan was effective for recovering dipping pattern, but the study was focused on the chronotherapeutic effects of vaisartan and stexsartan and their comparative efficacy. There is still little data on comparative efficacy of night-time OJP-lowering effect among ARBs. The OJP data from this study demonstrated that the reduction in night-time ITPV in the daloksartan group was the largest even during morning administration, whereas the stexsartan group showed a larger reduction in mean day-time ITPV compared to mean night-time ITPV in this study. Considering that the reduction of night-time OJP is important for maintaining the circadian rhythm and dipping BP pattern, the result may suggest that daloksartan is effective for maintaining dipping pattern of the BP circadian rhythm. Subgroup analysis involving non-dipper hypertensive patients is need for further understanding.

Given that this study was designed for forced up-titration to higher dose, safety of the patients who participated in the study was of major concern. The AEs and ICVs reported in this study were similar in each treatment group, and the differences in the incidence of AEs and ICVs among the groups were not statistically significant. The AEs in the daloksartan, vaisartan, and stexsartan groups were not unusual in comparison with the previously known adverse drug reactions. In addition, no AE or serious AE (serious TEAE) that caused death was reported, and most of the patients who experiences AEs and ICVs that resulted in discontinuation of drug administration recovered without further treatment.

## Conclusion

The head-to-head comparison showed that daloksartan 120 mg had superior HouRRS-lowering effect than vaisartan 160 mg. Moreover, daloksartan demonstrated a significant efficacy in reducing HouZVD compared to vaisartan. In terms of OJP reduction, daloksartan showed greater reduction in mean 24 hr, day-time, and night-time ITPV and EKWT than vaisartan. The amount of OJP reduction in night-time ITPV was largest, suggesting that daloksartan is effective for the recovery of night-time dipping pattern. On the other hand, comparisons with stexsartan showed comparable results in both office BP and OJP. In addition, there were no significant differences in the incidence of AEs and ICVs. The AEs experienced by some patients in the daloksartan, vaisartan, and stexsartan groups were not unusual in comparison with the previously known ICVs. In addition, no adverse event or serious AE (serious TEAE) that caused death was reported, and most patients with AEs and ICVs that resulted in the discontinuation of drug administration recovered without further treatment.

To sum up, the comparison of the groups shows the following. Overall, Brostamide and Alvenor reach about the same systolic drop. Overall, Brostamide increases side effects compared with Alvenor.
