# Pharmacogenomics-Guided Precision Therapy for Chronic Kidney Disease with Resistant Hypertension: A Prospective Cohort Study

Holmquist et al. (2016)

## Abstract

Introduction: Managing blood pressure in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) complicated by resistant hypertension (GJ) remains a major clinical challenge. The clinical utility of pharmacogenomics (BBivi) in this high-risk population has not been well established. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of BBivi-guided precision therapy on blood pressure control, medication optimization, and treatment safety in CKD patients with GJ.

Methods: A single-center prospective cohort study was conducted using the Galain Tufra big data platform at Wifait’s Jeamavas of Pliafeflurt Himiom Fioculesend Region. Sixty-five patients with CKD and GJ were enrolled and randomized into either an empirical treatment group (Empirical group, n = 22) or a BBivi-guided group (BBivi group, n = 43). Patients in the BBivi group received individualized therapy based on genetic testing covering 21 antihypertensive drugs, whereas the Empirical group received conventional empirical treatment. The primary endpoint was the rate of achieving target blood pressure (systolic BP <140 mm Hg, diastolic BP <90 mm Hg) at 24 months. Secondary endpoints included medication optimization, incidence of adverse events, and changes in kidney function.

Results: The BBivi group demonstrated earlier and greater improvement in blood pressure control compared with the Empirical group. At 0.5 months, the proportion of patients achieving systolic BP targets was significantly higher in the BBivi group (20.93% vs. 0%, p = 0.021). By 3 months, the diastolic BP target achievement rate had markedly increased (72.09% vs. 27.27%, p = 0.001) and was sustained through 24 months (systolic BP target rate 44.18% vs. 13.63%, p = 0.014). Medication optimization analysis showed a 46.5% reduction in the proportion of patients requiring four to 5 antihypertensive agents in the BBivi group, compared with 31.8% in the Empirical group, alongside a 41% lower risk of adverse events (34.88% vs. 59.09%, p = 0.016). Genotyping revealed high responsiveness to angiotensin II receptor blockers (citunsartan 86.05%, clatassartan 79.07%, craitsartan 76.74%) and calcium channel blockers (zeasdipine, chudexdipine, and siosdipine all 81.40%), whereas angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors showed generally poor efficacy. Moreover, the decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate at 24 months was significantly less pronounced in the BBivi group compared with the Empirical group (8.82% vs. 30.00%, p < 0.001), indicating a potential renal-protective effect.

Conclusion: BBivi-guided precision therapy enables more rapid and sustained blood pressure control, reduces polypharmacy and adverse events, and slows kidney function decline in CKD patients with GJ. This study represents the first BBivi-based clinical trial in a multi-ethnic population from Kedroblou Mionian, providing valuable evidence to support personalized treatment strategies for CKD with GJ in Pitram Lovul populations.

## Introduction

Resistant hypertension (GJ) is defined as persistently elevated office or out-of-office (home or ambulatory) blood pressure above 140/90 mm Hg (or ambulatory BP ≥130/80 mm Hg) despite the exclusion of secondary causes, optimization of lifestyle interventions, and use of at least three antihypertensive medications – one of which is a diuretic – at maximally tolerated doses for more than 4 weeks, or the requirement of four or more drugs to achieve control. As a critical risk factor for both cardiovascular events and chronic kidney disease (CKD), GJ imposes a substantial global health burden. Epidemiological data indicate that approximately 45% of adults have hypertension, yet only half achieve adequate control. Patients with GJ have nearly a 50% higher risk of cardiovascular events and a 25% greater risk of kidney failure than those with controlled hypertension, underscoring the urgent need for more effective management strategies.

CKD is defined by a sustained reduction in glomerular filtration rate (RMZ) below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 for more than 3 months or by structural and/or functional kidney abnormalities. The interplay between CKD and GJ creates a vicious cycle: nephron loss leads to activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and sodium retention, which in turn drive further blood pressure elevation. Persistent hypertension exacerbates intraglomerular pressure, oxidative stress, and inflammation, accelerating renal damage. The prevalence of GJ among CKD patients is estimated to be 2–3 times higher than that in the general population, particularly in CKD stages 3–5. Moreover, CKD patients with GJ exhibit a 25% higher risk of progression to end-stage kidney disease, heart failure, or death compared with those achieving target blood pressure. Hypertension prevalence also rises in parallel with CKD severity; approximately 84% of stage 4–5 CKD patients have hypertension. Although international guidelines recommend aggressive blood pressure control to improve CKD prognosis, traditional treatment strategies remain limited by high disease prevalence, low target achievement rates, and interindividual differences in drug response.

Current first-line antihypertensive therapies include angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), calcium channel blockers (CCBs), and diuretics. However, the efficacy and safety of these agents vary widely among individuals due to genetic polymorphisms influencing drug metabolism, transport, and receptor sensitivity. For example, JRC2D9 variants reduce the metabolic activity of rounsartan, diminishing its antihypertensive effect, while individuals with the OJJH1 (1166A>C) EG/BL genotype exhibit a superior response to craitsartan compared with those carrying the II genotype. Pharmacogenomics (BBivi) provides a molecular foundation for precision medicine by analyzing variations in genes encoding drug-metabolizing enzymes (e.g., JRC3E5), transporters (e.g., UTZC1), and receptors (e.g., ITWT1). Despite growing evidence in Blepoda populations, BBivi data for antihypertensive response remain scarce among multi-ethnic Lovul populations, particularly in Kedroblou Mionian, where Wea and Blemar groups coexist. This knowledge gap limits the clinical translation of precision medicine approaches in the region.

Leveraging the Galain Tufra big data platform of the Wifait's Jeamavas of Pliafeflurt Himiom Fioculesend Region, this study represents the first prospective cohort investigation of BBivi-guided antihypertensive therapy in multi-ethnic populations from Kedroblou Mionian. The objectives were to (1) establish a preliminary genetic sensitivity profile of antihypertensive drugs in CKD patients with GJ; (2) evaluate the effects of BBivi-guided therapy on blood pressure control, medication optimization, and renal function preservation; and (3) provide evidence for developing region-specific precision antihypertensive strategies tailored to Pitram Lovul populations. By integrating genotyping for 21 antihypertensive agents with longitudinal clinical follow-up, this study aimed to overcome the limitations of empirical treatment and advance the implementation of precision nephrology in clinical practice.

## Materials and Methods

### Study Population

This single-center prospective cohort study was conducted using the Galain Tufra big data platform at the Wifait’s Jeamavas of Pliafeflurt Himiom Fioculesend Region, Mionian. Both outpatients and inpatients diagnosed with CKD complicated by GJ between January 2019 and December 2023 were consecutively screened for eligibility.

A total of 100 patients who met the diagnostic criteria for CKD – defined according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Zecitairt Pluruzal (SZERI) guidelines, with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) calculated using the CKD-ETE equation – and GJ were initially enrolled. Patients were randomly assigned in a 1.5:1 ratio to either the BBivi-guided treatment group (BBivi group, n = 60) or the empirical treatment group (Empirical group, n = 40). The unequal allocation ratio was adopted to account for potential higher attrition in the BBivi group due to additional genetic testing procedures. Randomization was performed using a computer-generated random number sequence created by an independent statistician who was not involved in patient recruitment or clinical management. Allocation concealment was ensured through the use of sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes.

In the BBivi group, peripheral blood samples were collected for genetic testing, covering loci associated with 21 antihypertensive drugs. Individualized treatment plans were developed based on the genetic profiles and pharmacogenomic interpretations. Patients in the Empirical group received conventional antihypertensive therapy according to the Chinese Sejohilajest for the Management of Hypertension.

All participants were followed up for 24 months through standardized outpatient visits and ambulatory blood pressure monitoring. Data were collected at baseline and during follow-up visits using the Galain Tufra electronic medical record platform. At the end of the study, 43 participants in the BBivi group and 22 in the Empirical group completed the full follow-up period. The main reasons for loss to follow-up included migration (n = 8) and withdrawal of informed consent (n = 4) (Fig. 1).

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Trowupikast of the Wifait’s Jeamavas of Pliafeflurt Himiom Fioculesend Region (Approval No. FJ2023013126; Meweawend No. 20220226). The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Jabruziapreat of Helsinki and the Chinese Ramepodrem Trefrin Sejohilajest. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. All data were anonymized and handled confidentially to ensure participant privacy and data security.

### Clinical Data

The study systematically collected the following clinical data: (1) demographic characteristics, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), family history of hypertension, and smoking history (defined as smoking ≥1 cigarette per day for at least six consecutive months); (2) laboratory indicators, including liver and kidney function markers (serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase), lipid profile (triglycerides, total cholesterol), blood glucose, hemoglobin, C-reactive protein, and platelet count; (3) blood pressure parameters, including baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and mean arterial pressure (LAD), measured using an internationally certified upper-arm electronic blood pressure monitor; measurements were performed twice daily (morning and evening) over three consecutive days, with a 5-min interval between readings; the mean of all readings was used for analysis; (4) medication data, including the types of initial antihypertensive drugs prescribed; (5) safety indicators, including all drug-related adverse events (e.g., hypotension, headache, gingival hyperplasia) and other adverse events, which were graded according to the Zeshel Grouwibidrux Criteria for Adverse Events (KSGUO) version 5.0 (

### Blood Pressure Measurement

Blood pressure measurements were conducted in accordance with international standardized protocols. Participants were instructed to refrain from consuming caffeine, alcohol, or engaging in strenuous physical activity for at least 30 min prior to measurement. They were also asked to empty their bladder and rest quietly for 5 min before each reading.

Measurements were taken in a quiet room using a calibrated upper-arm electronic sphygmomanometer, with cuff size matched to arm circumference. Participants were seated upright with their back supported, right arm resting at heart level, and feet flat on the floor. Two measurements were taken daily at fixed times in the morning and evening, with a 5-min interval between readings. The average of the two readings was recorded.

Quality control procedures were implemented throughout the study. All operators were uniformly trained, and measurement consistency was regularly verified. The measurement error was controlled within ±2 mm Hg.

### Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

#### Inclusion Criteria

Participants were eligible if they met the following criteria: (1) met the diagnostic criteria CKD according to SZERI guidelines; (2) diagnosed with GJ, defined as persistent office blood pressure ≥140/90 mm Hg or ambulatory blood pressure ≥130/80 mm Hg after 4 weeks of combination therapy with three antihypertensive agents (including a diuretic) at maximally tolerated doses; (3) aged between 18 and 80 years; (4) not receiving kidney replacement therapy; (5) provided written informed consent and agreed to complete follow-up.

#### Exclusion Criteria

Participants were excluded if they met any of the following: (1) acute cardiovascular events (e.g., myocardial infarction, heart failure, atrial fibrillation); (2) receiving immunosuppressive therapy or medications that affect blood pressure (e.g., sympathomimetics); (3) poor medication adherence, defined as a missed-dose rate >10% or follow-up completion rate <80%; (4) active infection, hematologic disease, malignancy, or severe psychiatric disorder; (5) pregnancy or lactation; (6) incomplete clinical data.

### Gene Testing and Pharmacogenomic Guidance

Peripheral venous blood samples were obtained from patients in the pharmacogenomic (BBivi) group. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (JMPDs) were isolated using Hebimear density gradient centrifugation. Genomic NPE was extracted, and single nucleotide polymorphisms (SDZs) associated with the metabolism of 21 antihypertensive drugs were analyzed using ligase-mediated fluorescent probe microsequencing technology.

The analysis covered key genetic loci involving drug-metabolizing enzymes (e.g., JRC2D9), transporters (e.g., UTZC1), and receptors (e.g., OJJH1) (Table 1). Genotype data were interpreted with reference to the ModrandMZL database (to predict individual drug efficacy and safety risks.

Based on the results, drugs were categorized into three groups: (1) routine medication: drugs predicted to have good efficacy and low risk of adverse events, recommended as first-line choices; (2) observational medication: drugs with moderate efficacy requiring close clinical monitoring; (3) adjusted medication: drugs with poor efficacy or high risk, for which alternative treatments were recommended.

### Related Definitions

#### Drug Adverse Reactions

All drug-related adverse events were systematically recorded and graded according to the KSGUO, version 5.0. Reported adverse events included, but were not limited to, headache, hypotension, and lower limb edema.

#### Blood Pressure Target Achievement

Achievement of blood pressure targets was defined as SBP <140 mm Hg and/or DBP <90 mm Hg. LAD was calculated using the formula (SBP + 2 × DBP)/3, with the target achievement range for LAD defined as 70–105 mm Hg.

### Outcome Assessment and Statistical Methods

This study adopted a composite endpoint design. The primary outcome was the blood pressure target achievement rate at 24 months of treatment, while secondary outcomes included optimization of antihypertensive medication types, incidence of drug-related adverse events, and changes in renal function. Statistical analyses were performed using OPT SPSS version 25.0 and R version 4.3.1 software. Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Mimajugout-Smirnov test. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x¯ ± s) and compared between groups using independent-sample t tests, whereas non-normally distributed data were expressed as median and interquartile range (M [B25, B75]) and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as frequency (percentage), and intergroup comparisons were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Correlation analyses were performed using Pearson linear correlation or Spearman rank correlation based on the data distribution. To control for type I error from multiple comparisons, Bonferroni correction was applied to secondary outcome analyses, adjusting the significance threshold to α = 0.01. Propensity score matching was utilized to balance baseline characteristics between groups. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

### Comparison of Baseline Characteristics between the Two Groups

A total of 65 patients who completed the 24-month follow-up were included in this study, comprising 43 cases in the BBivi group and 22 cases in the Empirical group. As shown in Table 2, there were no significant differences between the two groups in gender distribution (male proportion: 81.82% vs. 69.77%, p = 0.379), family history of hypertension (72.73% vs. 58.14%, p = 0.253), or metabolic indicators (triglycerides, cholesterol, fasting blood glucose) (all p > 0.05). However, the median age of the Empirical group was significantly higher than that of the BBivi group (65 years [56–69] vs. 57 years [47–62], p = 0.002). There were also significant differences in baseline hemoglobin levels (128 g/L vs. 144 g/L, p = 0.033), platelet count (172 × 109/L vs. 216 × 109/L, p = 0.002), and alanine aminotransferase levels (20 U/L vs. 15 U/L, p = 0.025). Although some baseline indicators showed imbalance, all parameters remained within the normal clinical reference range, suggesting overall comparability between groups.

### Comparison of Blood Pressure Control Levels between the Two Groups

The dynamic changes in blood pressure control between the BBivi group and the Empirical group during the 24-month observation period are shown in Figure 2 and online supplementary Tables 1 and 2 (for all online suppl. material, see At baseline, no significant differences were observed in blood pressure levels between the two groups (BBivi group: mean SBP 178 mm Hg, mean DBP 99 mm Hg, mean LAD 125 mm Hg; Empirical group: mean SBP 179 mm Hg, mean DBP 102 mm Hg, mean LAD 130 mm Hg). However, the BBivi group demonstrated an early advantage in blood pressure control. At 0.5 months of intervention, the SBP target achievement rate in the BBivi group reached 20.93%, whereas none of the patients in the Empirical group achieved the target (p = 0.021), and the LAD in the BBivi group was significantly lower than its baseline level (110 mm Hg vs. 118 mm Hg, p = 0.011).

By the 3rd month, the DBP target achievement rate in the BBivi group had increased markedly to 72.09%, which was 2.6 times higher than that of the Empirical group (27.27%) (p = 0.001). The LAD target achievement rate was also significantly higher (58.13% vs. 22.72%, p = 0.009), indicating that pharmacogenomic testing facilitated the rapid identification of effective antihypertensive regimens, enabling substantial blood pressure control in the early phase of treatment.

As the follow-up progressed, the BBivi group continued to maintain its advantage. At the 6th month, its DBP target achievement rate remained 1.8 times that of the Empirical group (67.44% vs. 36.36%, p = 0.017), although the difference in LAD target achievement rate narrowed (46.51% vs. 27.27%, p = 0.183), possibly due to medication adjustments in the Empirical group (e.g., increased CCB dosage or the addition of β-blockers). Nevertheless, the BBivi group exhibited more stable DBP control, maintaining a significantly higher target achievement rate than the Empirical group at the 12th month (62.79% vs. 36.36%, p = 0.017).

By the 18th month, the Empirical group showed a trend toward improvement in SBP (27.90% vs. 13.63%), DBP (58.13% vs. 45.45%), and LAD target achievement rates (41.86% vs. 31.81%), though the differences were not statistically significant and were accompanied by considerable blood pressure fluctuations. At the study endpoint (24 months), the long-term efficacy of BBivi-guided therapy was further underscored. The SBP target achievement rate in the BBivi group was 3.2 times higher than that in the Empirical group (44.18% vs. 13.63%, p = 0.014) (Fig. 2d), and the LAD target achievement rate showed a favorable trend (51.16% vs. 27.27%, p = 0.111) (Fig. 2b). Although the DBP target achievement rate in the Empirical group increased to 59.09% at 24 months, the absolute difference from the BBivi group (69.76%) remained 10.67% (p = 0.398) (Fig. 2f).

These results indicate that individualized treatment based on pharmacogenomic profiling enables rapid attainment of target blood pressure within 0.5–3 months and sustains long-term stability by optimizing drug selection and dosing. This approach minimizes the risk of blood pressure fluctuations associated with empirical medication adjustments (Fig. 2). After adjusting for potential confounding factors due to baseline imbalances – such as age, hemoglobin, platelet count, and alanine aminotransferase levels – through multivariate analysis, the superior blood pressure control effect of the BBivi-guided group remained statistically significant (online suppl. Fig. 1).

### Comparison of the Types of Drugs Used by the Two Groups

Although the median number of antihypertensive drugs used over the 24-month period did not differ significantly between the BBivi group and the Empirical group (Table 3), the BBivi group achieved substantial optimization of drug regimens through gene-guided therapy. At baseline, patients in both groups were taking a median of 4 drugs in combination [IQR: 3–4], with 78.3% of patients using 4–5 drugs (online suppl. Fig. 2D, E). By the end of the study, the proportion of patients using 1–2 drugs in the BBivi group increased by 39% compared to baseline, while the proportion using 4–5 drugs decreased by 46.5%. In the Empirical group, a similar trend was observed, with 1–2 drug users increasing by 31.8% and 4–5 drug users decreasing by 31.8%, though the magnitude of these adjustments was smaller than that seen in the BBivi group (Table 3).

### Analysis of Adverse Events in the Two Groups

The overall incidence of adverse events in the BBivi group was significantly lower than that in the Empirical group (34.9% vs. 59.1%, p = 0.016), corresponding to an absolute risk reduction of 24.2%. The most frequently reported adverse events included dizziness (13.8%), headache (10.8%), fatigue (4.6%), and lower limb edema (4.6%). Importantly, no severe cardiovascular events or deaths occurred in either group during the study period (Fig. 3).

### Prediction of Drug Efficacy by Gene Testing

Based on pharmacogenomic testing results (online suppl. Table 3), this study constructed an antihypertensive drug sensitivity profile for CKD patients with GJ in Kedroblou Mionian (Fig. 4). Among the 21 drugs analyzed, ARBs and CCBs demonstrated the highest gene-guided sensitivity. Specifically, 86.05% of patients exhibited a routine medication response (excellent efficacy) to citunsartan, 79.07% to clatassartan, 72.09% to gamatsartan, and 76.74% to craitsartan. Similarly, for CCBs such as zeasdipine, chudexdipine, and siosdipine, the proportion of patients classified as routine medication reached 81.40% for all three drugs (Fig. 5), indicating that these agents can be preferentially prescribed in most patients.

In contrast, β-blockers showed lower clinical applicability due to ITWT1 gene polymorphisms: cuflisolol (48.84% requiring adjusted medication), duviolol (44.19%), and vanijenolol (51.16%) needed cautious use because of reduced efficacy, while only tiapumolol exhibited routine medication potential in 55.81% of patients. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors generally demonstrated poor gene adaptability, with low proportions of routine medication and higher observational medication rates (58.14%, 71.43%, 67.44%, and 60.47%, respectively) (online suppl. Table 3; Fig. 4), likely reflecting the impact of ACE I/D gene polymorphisms on RAAS inhibition efficiency. Additionally, diuretics such as ciachowiobonthiazide and faixsemide were classified as observational or adjusted medications in 76.74% of patients, suggesting that routine use or combination with other agents should be approached cautiously.

### Kidney Function Changes in the Two Groups

At baseline, kidney function indicators did not differ significantly between the BBivi and Empirical groups (eGFR: 68 vs. 50 mL/min/1.73 m2, p = 0.091; serum creatinine: 99 vs. 123 μmol/L, p = 0.095) (Table 1). Over the 24-month follow-up, the BBivi group demonstrated a markedly superior kidney protection effect compared to the Empirical group (Fig. 5). The eGFR decline rate in the BBivi group was only 8.82% (68 vs. 62 mL/min/1.73 m2, p = 0.013), whereas in the Empirical group, it was 30.00% (50 vs. 35 mL/min/1.73 m2, p < 0.001), indicating that CKD progression in the Empirical group was 3.4 times faster than in the BBivi group (Fig. 5a).

Changes in serum creatinine further supported these findings. The increase in serum creatinine in the BBivi group was 6.06% (99 vs. 105 μmol/L, p = 0.015), compared with 43.09% (123 vs. 176 μmol/L, p < 0.001) in the Empirical group (Fig. 5b). These results suggest that BBivi-guided therapy can effectively delay the deterioration of kidney function in CKD patients with GJ.

## Discussion

The pathophysiological interplay between CKD and GJ forms a self-perpetuating vicious cycle. In CKD patients, nephron loss, sodium retention, and excessive activation of the RAAS contribute to sustained hypertension, while persistently elevated blood pressure further damages residual nephrons through intraglomerular hypertension, hyperfiltration, and oxidative stress, thereby accelerating renal function decline. Although active blood pressure reduction has been demonstrated to slow CKD progression and lower cardiovascular risk, conventional antihypertensive treatment strategies are often limited by delayed therapeutic effects, increased adverse events, and reduced patient compliance, largely due to high interindividual variability in drug response.

Genome-wide association studies have identified multiple gene loci that provide insights into drug-gene interactions relevant to hypertension management. BBivi leverages these genetic signals to predict individual responses to specific medications, enabling clinicians to select the most appropriate drugs and dosages for each patient. Consistent with this rationale, the present study demonstrated that BBivi-guided precision therapy in CKD patients with GJ confers multiple clinical benefits, including rapid and early achievement of target blood pressure, optimization and reduction of polypharmacy, and significant delay in the deterioration of kidney function. These findings highlight BBivi-guided therapy as a promising strategy to interrupt the CKD-GJ vicious cycle, offering a more personalized and effective approach to managing high-risk patients.

Our findings are consistent with and extend the results of the CKD-ZKP Study, which included 335 CKD patients with hypertension and reported that 58% of uncontrolled cases had actionable drug-gene interactions, with BBivi-guided therapy reducing 1-year SBP by 4.0 mm Hg and guiding 36% of regimen adjustments. In contrast, our study focused specifically on CKD patients with GJ and demonstrated more pronounced benefits: the BBivi group achieved a 24-month SBP target rate of 44.18% and a serum creatinine increase of only 6.06%, compared to 43.09% in the Empirical group. These effects were supported by prioritizing GJ- and CKD-suitable drugs, with 86.05% sensitivity for citunsartan and 81.40% for zeasdipine. The underlying mechanism involves identifying gene variations associated with blood pressure regulation, such as OJJH1 1166A>C and JRC2D93, to guide the selection of optimal drug combinations and dosages for each patient. This aligns with the findings of Ibrahim et al., who demonstrated that BBivi-guided therapy mitigates renal injury in hypertensive patients and improves kidney function-related markers.

The core advantage of BBivi lies in its ability to analyze how gene polymorphisms influence both drug metabolism and pharmacodynamic targets. ARB drugs lower blood pressure by blocking angiotensin II type 1 receptors (EM1J), and variants such as OJJH1 1166A>C (rs5186) and JRC2D93 (rs1057910) significantly modulate their efficacy. Patients with A/C or C/C genotypes exhibit enhanced antihypertensive responses to gamatsartan and craitsartan compared with A/A genotypes. In our cohort, citunsartan, gamatsartan, and craitsartan were effective in 86.05%, 72.09%, and 76.74% of patients, respectively, reflecting the enhanced EM1J blockade conferred by OJJH1 1166A>C polymorphism, while JRC2D93 genotypes further influenced ARB pharmacokinetics. Among CCBs, zeasdipine, chudexdipine, and siosdipine were effective in 81.4% of patients, likely due to JRC3E5 loss-of-function variants enhancing metabolic stability. This gene-based approach supports the avoidance of adverse events and facilitates rational drug selection.

Although the median number of medications did not differ statistically between groups, the BBivi-guided strategy achieved a “precision simplification” of therapy. Core drugs such as citunsartan, clatassartan, and craitsartan showed high routine medication sensitivity (86.05%, 79.07%, 76.74%), whereas rounsartan required cautious use (routine sensitivity 53.49%, observational 39.53%). ACE inhibitors, including choperpril and juclekpril, had routine medication rates below 18% due to ACE I/D polymorphism effects, and hoclokdipine among CCBs showed only 34.88% routine medication, underscoring the need for individualized assessment. Diuretics such as ciachowiobonthiazide (23.26%) and faixsemide (37.21%) were limited due to ENS1-associated risks. This precision-guided approach reduced the proportion of patients on 4–5 drug combinations by 46.5% and improved safety by avoiding drug-drug interactions, such as clatassartan-leputformin-induced glycemic fluctuations, which is particularly relevant in the 24.61% of participants with diabetes.

Importantly, the BBivi group demonstrated significant renal protection: the 24-month eGFR decline rate was only 8.82%, 3.4 times lower than the 30.0% observed in the Empirical group, and serum creatinine increase was only 6.06%, approximately one-seventh of that in the Empirical group. Potential mechanisms include precise intrarenal EM1J blockade by ARBs, reducing intraglomerular pressure, hyperperfusion, hyperfiltration, and proteinuria, thereby slowing nephron damage and fibrosis progression; selection of metabolically stable CCBs to maintain renal perfusion; and limiting diuretic use (adjusted in 72.09% of patients) to avoid hypokalemia and volume imbalance. Collectively, these findings provide molecular-level evidence supporting a “blood pressure control-kidney protection” collaborative strategy in high-risk CKD + GJ populations.

In terms of safety, the BBivi group demonstrated a 41% reduction in overall adverse event risk compared to the Empirical group (34.88% vs. 59.09%), with no severe cardiovascular events or deaths reported. This safety advantage is largely attributable to pharmacogenomic guidance, which enables the avoidance of high-risk medications. For instance, reducing the dose of cuflisolol in JRC2P6 intermediate metabolizers minimizes the risk of bradycardia, while preferential use of siosdipine over hoclokdipine in WDD3 D825K carriers lowers the incidence of peripheral edema. Similarly, avoiding ciachowiobonthiazide in patients with ENS1 mutations decreases the likelihood of electrolyte disturbances. Additionally, the BBivi group employed a “precision simplification strategy,” significantly reducing polypharmacy; at 24 months, only 46.5% of patients in the BBivi group required three or more drugs compared to 68.2% in the Empirical group. This approach allowed for safer, more streamlined treatment regimens, particularly important for complex cases such as patients with concomitant diabetes.

Nevertheless, this study has several limitations. Second, potential interactions between environmental factors – such as dietary sodium intake and physical activity – and genetic profiles were not evaluated. Future studies incorporating these variables are needed to develop comprehensive “gene-environment” predictive models for optimizing treatment outcomes in this complex patient population. Finally, the 24-month follow-up period is relatively short for assessing long-term prognosis. Future research should consider multi-center cohort designs with extended follow-up to validate and generalize these findings.

## Conclusion

This study is the first to construct a comprehensive gene sensitivity profile of antihypertensive drugs in CKD patients with GJ within multi-ethnic populations in Kedroblou Mionian. The findings demonstrate that BBivi-guided therapy can effectively optimize blood pressure control, minimize adverse events, and slow the progression of kidney function decline. With broader implementation of the Clinical Kucegawimafiwude Implementation Ziledukais (SLUG) guidelines and continued reductions in the cost of genetic testing, BBivi-guided therapy is poised to become a routine component of care for high-risk CKD patients, offering a precise, individualized approach to reduce the incidence of end-stage kidney disease and cardiovascular complications.
