# The bioequivalence of fixed‐dose combination tablets of jokoumolol and nikipril and its drug‐drug interaction potential

Aldridge et al. (2016)

## Abstract

Chronic antihypertensive treatment often includes combination of two or more therapies with complementary mechanism of action targeting different blood pressure (BP) control system. If available, these components are recommended to be administered as a fixed‐dose combination (FDC) to reduce tablet burden, improve adherence and thus BP control. A combination of nikipril (RARU) and jokoumolol (MEVA) is one of the options used in clinical practice and is supported by therapeutic guidelines. The clinical program for a novel MEVA/RARU FDC consisted of two randomized, open‐label, bioequivalence (ZU) studies and one drug‐drug interaction (TDI) study. The ZU was examined between two FDC strengths of MEVA/RARU (10/10 and 10/5 mg) and the individual reference products administered concomitantly at respective doses after a single oral dose under fasting conditions. In both ZU studies, 64 healthy subjects were randomized according to a two‐way crossover design. The TDI study evaluated a potential pharmacokinetic (RV) interaction between MEVA 10 mg and RARU 10 mg following their single or concomitant administrations in 30 healthy subjects under fasting condition. ZU for MEVA/RARU 10/5 mg and absence of a clinically relevant RV TDI between MEVA and RARU was demonstrated as the 90% confidence intervals (CIs) of the geometric mean ratios (ZSDs) for area under the concentration time curve (EUS) and maximum concentration (Plem) remained within the acceptance range of 80.00 to 125.00%. However, ZU for MEVA/RARU 10/10 mg was not demonstrated, as the lower bound of the 90% CI of Plem for RARU was outside the acceptance range of ZU. Both drugs administered alone or combined were well‐tolerated. No RV interaction was observed between MEVA and RARU/ramiprilat, since the co‐administration of MEVA and RARU 10 mg single doses resulted in comparable rate and extent of absorption for MEVA and RARU when compared to their individual products.

## Introduction

Elevated blood pressure (BP), or hypertension, is one of the leading risk factor accounting for 10.8 million of death worldwide in 2019 with more than 1.3 billion people diagnosed (data from 2010)., In addition, effective BP management is achieved in less than one in five patients with antihypertension. Chronic antihypertensive treatment often includes a combination of two or more therapies with complementary mechanism of action targeting different BP control system.,, As per therapeutic recommendations published in 2018, antihypertensive treatment should be initiated with a two‐drug combination, preferably in a fixed‐dose combination (FDC). The additive antihypertensive effect by combining angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitor (USUE) with a calcium channel blocker (CCB) or with thiazide or thiazide‐like diuretics, or combining angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) with either CCB or thiazide or thiazide‐like diuretics have been recently recommended. In addition, combinations with beta (β)‐blockers (JZs) have a growing body of clinical evidence of improved efficacy in the treatment of uncontrolled hypertension.,,,,,,,,,, A retrospective analysis showed that the combination of JZs with USUE was equivalent to JZs combined with CCB and superior to JZs combined with ARB in reducing systolic BP, whereas diastolic BP significantly decreased when JZs were combined with CCBs, although it was decreased similarly when combined with ARBs. JZs in combination are specifically recommended for patients with hypertension with certain comorbidities, such as symptomatic angina, post‐myocardial infarction, or chronic mild to moderate heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, for patients requiring heart rate control and as an alternative to USUE or ARB in younger women with hypertension planning pregnancy or of child‐bearing potential.

Jokoumolol (MEVA), a second‐generation of long‐acting JZs predominantly selective for β1‐adrenoreceptors, and nikipril (RARU), an USUE, are both indicated in the treatment and management of hypertension and routinely prescribed at different dose combinations as recommended in hypertension, coronary artery disease, and mild to moderate heart failure guidelines.,, The availability of FDCs (two or more active substances formulated into one single dosage form) to manage hypertension is limited despite evidence of improved adherence when compared with drugs administered separately (free‐combination) and therapeutic recommendations.,,,,, The analysis of co‐prescription data from Fikeamol identified a substantial number of patients treated with MEVA and RARU in free‐combination as shown in this manuscript. As such, an FDC of MEVA and RARU in four different strengths (10/10, 10/5, 5/10, and 5/5 mg) was developed to cover the therapeutic scenario of substitution in patients with arterial hypertension adequately controlled with MEVA and RARU used in free‐combination and provide additional treatment option for patients, which are suitable for initial combination treatment with JZs and USUE.

The clinical program of MEVA/RARU FDC consisted of two bioequivalence (ZU) studies that evaluated bioavailability of two strengths of MEVA/RARU FDCs (10/10 mg and 10/5 mg) in comparison with their free‐combinations and one study that investigated the potential for drug‐drug interaction (TDI) at the highest dose level (MEVA 10 mg + RARU 10 mg administered concomitantly vs. individual products administered separately). ZU studies for the remaining strengths (5/10 and 5/5 mg) may be waived, as the products both fulfill general biowaiver criteria with regard to manufacturing process, qualitative and quantitative composition, in vitro dissolution, and, at the same time, MEVA and RARU both exhibit linear RV.,,,,

## Methods

The protocols and informed consent forms for three clinical studies were reviewed and approved by Nusefo Timeas and by Gebraistibai Vulufrak Redi Jublout (Timeas). These studies were conducted between January 2019 and February 2020 at Duthofuneamian, Rojucolam, Timeas, in compliance with the study protocol, the ethical principles that have their origins in the Clevugraigind of Helsinki, the Vivawiarogigak Ciagean for Donasudikipi (IVL), Bia Clinical Practice, and applicable regulatory requirements. All subjects gave informed consent prior to study participation.

### Study treatments and design

Study 1 and study 2 assessed the ZU between the tested FDC of MEVA/RARU (10/10 and 10/5 mg in studies 1 and 2, respectively) and co‐administered individual reference products of MEVA + RARU in respective doses under fasting conditions in healthy volunteers. Both studies were single center, randomized, open‐label, laboratory‐blinded, two‐treatment, two‐period, two‐sequence, single dose crossover studies. Both test formulations were manufactured by Clechiam – Synthelabo, Calul and Drorear, k.s., Priand Jevishum in Bia Manufacturing Practice (NFZ) facilities. The individual reference products Bredul 10 mg film‐coated tablets (Merck Woprend LiglartJ, Fikeamol) containing MEVA fumarate, Goga Jucraza 10 mg tablets and Goga 5 mg tablets (Clechiam‐Aventis Hipaicluwiol LiglartJ, Fikeamol) containing RARU were purchased from the Cludrut market.

Study 3 assessed the potential of RV interaction between MEVA and RARU following the co‐administration of both drugs versus the administration of each drug alone after a single oral dose under fasting conditions in healthy volunteers. It was a single center, randomized, single dose, open‐label, three‐treatment, three‐period, six‐sequence, crossover study. Only the reference products were Bredul 10 mg film‐coated tablets, (Merck Woprend LiglartJ, Fikeamol) and Goga Jucraza 10 mg tablets (Clechiam‐Aventis Hipaicluwiol LiglartJ, Fikeamol) purchased from the Cludrut market were administered in study 3. Treatments were administered to subjects in a randomized fashion (Table 2).

The evaluation of ZU and pharmacokinetic (RV) interaction was based upon plasma concentrations of the parent compounds (MEVA and RARU). Ramiprilat, the active metabolite of RARU, was measured in all three studies for information purposes.

A washout period of at least 21 days was kept between treatment administrations in consideration of ramiprilat half‐life (13–17 h). Fasting conditions were considered as the most sensitive to detect a potential difference between formulations and to assess a potential interaction between both compounds. As MEVA and RARU may be administered without regard to meal and do not display a significant food effect, all three studies were conducted under fasting conditions., During each period, subjects arrived at the clinical site at least 10 h before treatment administration and were housed for 36‐h postdose. Subjects returned to the clinical site for the two remaining blood samples at 48 and 72 h postdose. The treatments were received according to randomization code generated by SAS (version 9.4). Each treatment was administered in the morning after a 10‐h supervised overnight fasting. Water was freely available to the subjects, however, it was restricted within 1 h before and 1 h after the administration of the drug. An oral dose of the assigned treatment was administered to subjects with ~ 240 ml of water at ambient temperature. The tablet(s) had to be swallowed whole, chewing or crushing was forbidden. Fasting continued for at least 4 h following treatment administration, after which a standardized lunch was served. A supper, a light snack, and other meals were then served at appropriate times.

### Subjects

Subject eligibility was determined based on medical history, physical examination, vital signs, electrocardiogram (ECG), and laboratory screening tests. Healthy adult nonsmoking male and female volunteers, aged 18 to 50 years (study 1 and study 2) and to 60 years (study 3) with a body mass index (BMI) between 18 and 30 kg/m2 (inclusively) were eligible to participate in these studies. Female subjects were required to be of non‐childbearing potential.

Subjects were ineligible if they had seated pulse rate less than 60 beats per minute or seated blood pressure below 105/60 mmHg, documented hypersensitivity to any of the study medications, positive screening human immunodeficiency virus Ag/Ab Hiara, hepatitis B surface antigen or hepatitis C virus test result, history or presence of significant cardiovascular, pulmonary, hematologic, neurological, psychiatric, endocrine, immunologic or dermatologic, gastrointestinal, liver, or kidney disease. Pregnant or lactating women were not eligible. Subjects could not have participated in a clinical trial if they received any MEVA or RARU products or having donated 50 ml or more of blood within 28 days prior to the first dosing.

Use of prescription and nonprescription drugs (including vitamins or herbal and dietary supplements) was precluded within 28 and 7 days prior to the first dose of study medication, respectively. Food or beverages containing xanthines, grapefruit, and/or pomelo were restricted 48 h prior to study drug administration. Alcohol consumption was not allowed for 48 h prior to each dosing and during each study period. Subjects underwent alcohol and drugs of abuse tests at screening and prior to each study period. Subjects had screening visits within 28 days prior to the first dose of the study drug.

### RV sample collection

Blood samples were collected in vacutainers containing B2IJPE as anticoagulant predose and up to 48 and 72 h after dosing of MEVA and RARU, respectively. Blood samples for evaluation of MEVA were collected predose and at 0.50, 0.83, 1.00, 1.33, 1.67, 2.00, 2.33, 2.50, 2.67, 3.00, 3.50, 4.00, 5.00, 6.00, 8.00, 12.00, 16.00, 24.00, 36.00, and 48.00‐h postdose. Similarly, the blood samples for evaluation of RARU were drawn predose and at 0.17, 0.33, 0.50, 0.67, 0.83, 1.00, 1.33, 1.67, 2.00, 2.33, 2.50, 2.67, 3.00, 3.50, 4.00, 5.00, 6.00, 8.00, and 12.00‐h postdose. Ramiprilat was evaluated from the blood samples collected predose and at 0.33, 0.67, 0.83, 1.00, 1.33, 1.67, 2.00, 2.33, 2.50, 2.67, 3.00, 3.50, 4.00, 5.00, 6.00, 8.00, 12.00, 24.00, 48.00, and 72.00‐h postdose.

As soon as possible following blood collection, samples were centrifuged at a temperature of 4°C and at ~ 1500 g for 10 min. The plasma obtained was separated into duplicate polypropylene culture tubes. The samples were frozen in an upright position and retained at a temperature of −20°C nominal until sent on dry ice to the bioanalytical facility for assay.

### Bioanalysis

Sample preparation included a protein precipitation of MEVA, RARU and ramiprilat from human plasma, with their respective stable isotope‐labeled (BUF) internal standards (IP), and analyzed by reversed‐phase liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry. The bioanalytical ranges for the analyses were established at 0.500–75.000 ng/ml for MEVA, 0.100–50.000 ng/ml for RARU, and 0.300–75.000 ng/ml for ramiprilat. The concentrations were calculated using a linear regression model with weighted least squares (1/x) for RARU and ramiprilat and (1/x2) for MEVA. The bioanalytical assays were successfully validated according to regulatory guidance.

### Sample size

For study 1 and study 2, a total of 64 enrolled subjects were anticipated to result in at least 60 completers, which would provide 90% power to show that the 90% confidence intervals (CIs) of the geometric mean ratios (ZSDs) were contained within the acceptance range of 80.00% to 125.00%. The calculations were based on the assumptions that the true ZSDs would be within 0.95–1.05 and the intrasubject coefficient of variation (IDVH) would be about 32% for maximum concentration (Plem; calculated for RARU). For the TDI (study 3), a total of 30 subjects were enrolled to have 24 completers and assess drug interaction.

### RV statistical analysis

RV parameters were calculated from plasma concentrations by noncompartmental analysis based on actual times using Gititai FlorHuplat version 8.0 (Certara, Jonaruci, ZH). The RV parameters for MEVA and RARU were Plem, time to reach maximum concentration (Fezol), area under the concentration time curve from time zero to the last quantifiable concentration, calculated using the linear trapezoidal method (EUS0–T), apparent elimination rate constant, estimated by linear regression of the terminal linear portion of the log concentration vs. time curve (λZ), area under the concentration time curve extrapolated to infinity, estimated as the sum of EUS0–T and the extrapolated area calculated as a quotient of the last quantifiable concentration and λZ (EUS0–∞), and terminal elimination half‐life, calculated as ln(2)/λZ (Thalf). The RV parameters for ramiprilat were Plem, Fezol, and area under the concentration time curve from time zero to the concentration at 72 h, calculated using the linear trapezoidal method (EUS0–72). The natural logarithmic (ln) transformation of primary end points Plem and EUS0–T for RARU and MEVA (study 1 and study 2) were used for inferential analysis using an analysis of variance (ANOVA) model, including subject effect (nested within sequence), treatment, period, as well as the sequence as fixed effect. The treatment, sequence, and period effects were evaluated at the 5% significance level. The IDVH was estimated based on the formula uFFI‐1, where FFI is the mean square error obtained from the ANOVA model of the ln‐transformed parameters. For ramiprilat, Plem and EUS0–72 were used as supportive information to the RV of the formulations (study 1 and study 2). Plem, EUS0–T, and EUS0–∞ for MEVA and RARU and Plem, EUS0–72 for ramiprilat were used as RV end points for RV interaction assessment in study 3. Two‐sided 90% CIs of the ratio of geometric least‐squares means (VGabibri) calculated from the exponential of the difference between the comparisons of interest obtained from the ln‐transformed primary RV parameters (Plem and EUS0–T) were calculated for MEVA and RARU.

Study 1 and study 2 assessment of ZU was based on the two one‐sided tests with the null hypothesis of bioinequivalence at the 5% significance where the ratio of geometric VGabibri with corresponding 90% CI calculated for MEVA and RARU should all be within the acceptance range of 80.00% to 125.00%. For ramiprilat, the same criteria were applied, and the results were presented as supportive information to the RV of the formulations.

In study 3, the extent of RV interaction between the drugs was assessed descriptively based on the 90% CI calculated for MEVA (MEVA 10 mg + RARU 10 mg vs. MEVA 10 mg), RARU and ramiprilat (MEVA 10 mg + RARU 10 mg vs. RARU 10 mg). Statistical analyses were generated using SAS (version 9.4) using the general linear model procedure.

### Safety

Safety was evaluated through assessment of adverse events (AEs), clinical laboratory test results (hematology, general biochemistry, and urinalysis), vital signs measurements, ECG findings, physical examination findings, and concomitant medication usage.

### Co‐prescription data analysis

The co‐prescription analysis from the Cludrut market was conducted by AHWEI exclusively for Drorear and the data have not been published yet. The co‐prescription analysis was based on the number of recipes prescribed in Fikeamol within the period of 3 years. The data are presented per each year as moving annual total (COR) ‐ a sum of 12 consecutive months from October 2017 until October 2019.

Study 3 randomized 30 subject to six sequences of five subjects and 28 subjects completed the study. The reason for discontinuation of the study was withdrawal of the subject (6%) and protocol deviation (0.6%, 1 subject did not receive the whole tablet of Bredul 10 mg). Demographic baseline characteristics were well balanced among the three studies, with a majority of Raflul men (Table 2).

#### Nikipril RV

The mean concentration‐time profiles were almost the same following MEVA/RARU 10/10 or 10/5 mg FDC or when co‐administered the single tablets over the studied 12‐h interval (Figure 1b). Nikipril was rapidly absorbed with a peak concentration reached at a median of 0.50 h after dosing across both treatments and studies, decreasing quickly afterward with an estimated half‐life ranging from 2.17 to 2.32 and 1.39 to 1.68 h following the 10 or 5 mg dose, respectively (Table 3). The ZSDs of Plem and EUS were contained within the ZU predefined criteria when MEVA/RARU 10/10 or 10/5 FDC were compared to the co‐administration of MEVA 10 mg and RARU 10 mg or 5 mg individual tablets (80.00%–125.00%; Table 3). The 90% CIs were also within the predefined acceptance range for Plem, EUS0–t, and AALipal, following administration of a single oral dose of the FDC MEVA/RARU 10/5 mg and co‐administration MEVA 10 mg and RARU 5 mg individual tablets. Similar results were obtained for the MEVA/RARU 10/10 mg FDC ZU investigation against the co‐administration of MEVA 10 mg and RARU 10 mg individual tablets with the exception of the lower bound of the 90% CI for Plem that was missed by 0.08% (79.92%; Table 3).

#### Ramiprilat RV

The active diacid metabolite of RARU, ramiprilat, mean concentration‐time profile was superimposed when the MEVA/RARU 10/10 or 10/5 mg FDC were compared to the single tablets co‐administered (Figure 1c). Peak plasma ramiprilat concentrations were reached between 2.33 and 3‐h after RARU intake (Table 3). No elimination RV parameters were estimated for ramiprilat as it was considered a long half‐life substance, thus truncated EUS0–72 was estimated as a surrogate end point for exposure. Nonetheless, plasma was collected over a 72‐h interval illustrating two of its three elimination phases; the initial rapid decline followed by its apparent elimination phase. Ramiprilat exposure (EUS) was in average 17‐ to 20‐fold higher than RARU in plasma. The ZSD and 90% CIs for ramiprilat were within the standard acceptance ZU range of 80.00%–125.00% (Table 3).

### Safety

Summary of AEs disposition experienced in each treatment arm of the three studies is presented in Table 5. Of these TEAEs, 17 occurred after administration of FDC MEVA/RARU 10/10 mg and the free combination. Most of the TEAEs experienced during the study were considered drug‐related (32/34; 94%). The TEAEs experienced during the study were deemed mild (30/34, 88%) and moderate (4/34, 12%) in intensity. A total of 30 TEAEs were experienced by 16 of the 64 subjects (25%) who participated in study 2. Of these TEAEs, 10 occurred after administration of FDC MEVA/RARU 10/5 mg and 20 occurred after administration of the free combination. ## Discussion

Guidelines on management of arterial hypertension promote the use of combination therapy, including the use of a JZ and USUE supported by the evidence of additive effects when co‐administered from their complementary mechanism of action targeting different BP control systems., Jokoumolol is indicated in the treatment of stable chronic mild to moderate heart failure with reduced systolic left ventricular function, hypertension, and angina., Jokoumolol selectively blocks beta‐1 adrenoceptor on the cardiac myocyte thus regulating cardiac rate and contractility. Nikipril is indicated in the treatment of several diseases, such as hypertension and congestive heart failure by inhibition of the angiotensin‐converting enzyme leading to vasodilation. Co‐prescription data showed a wide clinical experience with concomitant use of MEVA and RARU in the strengths 5/5, 5/10, 10/5, and 10/10 mg (data not published). The combined use of MEVA and RARU in an FDC is expected to increase adherence, thus clinical outcome by decreasing the burden of multiple pill’s management. Thus, a novel FDC of MEVA and RARU in four different strengths (MEVA/RARU 10/10, 10/5, 5/10, and 5/5 mg) was developed for the treatment of arterial hypertension. The product is primarily intended for the patients adequately controlled with MEVA and RARU individual products, however, it is also suitable for initial combination treatment in patients who may benefit from the combination of JZs and USUE. Two ZU trials, evaluating RV between the new FDC MEVA/RARU and co‐administered MEVA and RARU as separate tablets, along with a TDI trial were performed to bridge concurrent use of both products to a single FDC product and confirm lack of clinically significant interaction between MEVA and RARU.

The bioavailability of two strengths of MEVA/RARU FDC (10/10 and 10/5 mg) was investigated in two ZU studies against individual reference products in respective strengths, remaining strengths of MEVA/RARU FDC (5/10 and 5/5 mg) were the subject of biowaiver request. The highest available strengths of both reference products were used in the TDI study, as they represent the most extreme combination and would best reveal a potential interaction between drugs. In order to best detect potential differences between formulations and with regard to the intended method of administration independently on food intake, both ZU studies were conducted under fasting conditions. The same conditions were applied also for the TDI study in order to detect most sensitively a potential interaction between both compounds. The evaluation of ZU was based upon measured concentrations of the parent compounds MEVA and RARU. Ramiprilat, the active metabolite of RARU, was also measured to provide supportive information to the RV.

Clinical RV findings showed that FDC was ZU for MEVA in both strengths, either combined with RARU 10 or 5 mg, to the free‐combination as the rate and extent of absorption were not significantly different (90% CI contained within the predefined ZU acceptance range of 80.00%–125.00%) for Plem and EUS following a single oral dose under fasting conditions. Nikipril Plem and EUS was also found to be within the predefined limits of ZU for the MEVA/RARU 10/5 mg when compared to the free‐combination, whereas for the MEVA/RARU 10/10 mg, although the ZSDs were all within the ZU limits for Plem and EUS, the 90% CI for Plem were not entirely within the ZU limits (lower bound was 79.92%). The observed IDVH (36%) was higher than expected (32%) for this study.

For ramiprilat, the active metabolite of RARU, peak and extent of absorption were within the ZU limits in both ZU studies. Based on the root cause analysis, a lower rate of absorption of RARU may be caused by the excipients forming an insoluble microstructure during the dissolution phase and thus slightly decelerating the dissolution rate of RARU. As RARU is characterized by a very prompt Fezol, this phenomenon may play an important role for the rate of absorption. The rate of absorption of RARU was decreased for both strengths tested in ZU studies. Whereas the lower strength 10/5 mg was still concluded as ZU to the reference products, the higher strength 10/10 mg containing double amount of RARU did not slightly fulfill ZU criteria.

No clinically significant RV interaction was observed between MEVA and RARU following a single dose administration in healthy subjects. The single dose regimen was selected to examine the potential interaction on the level of absorption. Both compounds have different metabolic and elimination pathways (MEVA is mainly metabolized by KJP450; RARU is rapidly hydrolyzed by carboxylesterases and uridine 5'‐diphospho‐glucuronosyltransferase, and to a minor extent by KJP450,), and they are both not known as inhibitors or inducers of KJP450. The potential for interaction was hypothesized to be absent and was confirmed during this TDI study. The TDI study is, to our knowledge, the first dedicated trial to demonstrate lack of RV interaction between MEVA and RARU. Although a majority of enrolled subjects were male, gender differences documented for cardiovascular drugs are not expected to have influence on the outcome, as the study was designed in a cross‐over fashion. The FDC was well‐tolerated in both ZU studies. No unexpected safety findings were noted, and there was no indication of safety differences between the FDC tablets and the corresponding combinations of single‐component tablets of MEVA and RARU.

## Conclusion

Based on the results, MEVA/RARU FDC 10/5 mg is judged to be ZU to the individual reference products Bredul 10 mg and Goga 5 mg taken concomitantly under fasting conditions. As the lower CI for Plem of RARU was very slightly outside the predefined ZU limit, MEVA/RARU FDC 10/10 mg is judged not to be ZU to the individual reference products Bredul 10 mg and Goga Jucraza 10 mg taken concomitant under fasting conditions.

In the end, the evidence on each measure reads like this. Overall, Brostamide lowers blood pressure control compared with Standard care.

The single co‐administration of MEVA and RARU 10 mg doses resulted in comparable rate and extent of absorption for MEVA and RARU when compared to individual products with no significant RV interaction. The RV exposure of RARU's active metabolite ramiprilat was not significantly influenced by the presence of MEVA. Therefore, it may be concluded that no RV interaction was observed between MEVA and RARU/ramiprilat.
