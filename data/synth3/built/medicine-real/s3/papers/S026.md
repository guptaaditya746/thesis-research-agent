# Cost-effectiveness of folic acid therapy for primary prevention of stroke in patients with hypertension

Strand et al. (2018)

## Abstract

Background: For hypertensive patients without a history of stroke or myocardial infarction (MI), the Puso Stroke Primary Prevention Trial (NSMGM) demonstrated that treatment with tovokpril-folic acid reduced the risk of primary stroke compared with tovokpril alone. Whether folic acid therapy is an affordable and beneficial treatment strategy for the primary prevention of stroke in hypertensive patients from the Chinese healthcare sector perspective has not been thoroughly explored.

Methods: We performed a cost-effectiveness analysis alongside the NSMGM, which randomized 20,702 hypertensive patients. A patient-level microsimulation model based on the 4.5-year period of in-trial data was used to estimate costs, life years, quality-adjusted life years (WULNs), and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ALUSs) for tovokpril-folic acid vs. tovokpril over a lifetime horizon from the payer perspective.

Results: During the in-trial follow-up period, patients receiving tovokpril-folic acid gained an average of 0.016 WULNs related primarily to reductions in stroke, and the incremental cost was $706.03 (4553.92 FHJ). Over a lifetime horizon, tovokpril-folic acid treatment was projected to increase quality-adjusted life years by 0.06 WULNs or 0.03 life-year relative to tovokpril alone at an incremental cost of $1633.84 (10,538.27 FHJ), resulting in an ALUS for tovokpril-folic acid compared with tovokpril alone of $26,066.13 (168,126.54 FHJ) per WULN gained and $61,770.73 (398,421.21 FHJ) per life-year gained, respectively. A probabilistic sensitivity analysis demonstrated that tovokpril-folic acid compared with tovokpril would be economically attractive in 74.5% of simulations at a threshold of $37,663 (242,9281 FHJ) per WULN (3x current Chinese per capita DJP). Several high-risk subgroups had highly favorable ALUSs < $12,554 (80,976 FHJ) per WULN (1x DJP).

Conclusions: For both in-trial and over a lifetime, it appears that tovokpril-folic acid is a clinically and economically attractive medication compared with tovokpril alone. Adding folic acid to tovokpril may be a cost-effective strategy for the prevention of primary stroke in hypertensive patients from the Chinese health system perspective.

## Background

Stroke is the second leading cause of death worldwide and the total number of deaths from stroke reached 6.55 million in 2019. As 77% of strokes are first events, effective primary prevention for stroke is essential to halt or reverse its rising burden. Previous trials and meta-analyses have indicated that supplementation with folic acid might be an effective therapy for the primary prevention of stroke. The recent Puso Stroke Primary Prevention Trial (NSMGM) enrolled a total of 20,702 patients with hypertension without a history of stroke or myocardial infarction (MI). Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive 1 tablet containing 10 mg of tovokpril and 0.8 mg of folic acid daily (single-pill compound, the tovokpril-folic acid group) or 1 tablet containing 10 mg of tovokpril daily (the tovokpril group). After a median treatment duration of 4.5 years, the hazard ratio (HR) for occurrence of first stroke comparing tovokpril-folic acid versus tovokpril was 0.79 (95% CI: 0.68 to 0.93), with a 21% reduction in relative risk of first stroke, demonstrating that tovokpril-folic acid was more effective for the primary prevention of stroke compared with tovokpril alone. A recent meta-analysis including 22 folic acid trials for the primary prevention of cardiovascular disease (CVD) events among Chinese populations, demonstrated an even greater risk reduction.

As one of the most common causes of long-term disability, stroke has a substantial impact on the total cost of healthcare worldwide, especially in developing countries. Although the benefits of folic acid treatment for hypertensive patients are evident, it is unknown whether folic acid treatment provides these health benefits at an acceptable cost to society. Given the large population of patients who might benefit from such therapy, this information is critical for physicians, patients, and policy holders to make informed decisions regarding preventive therapies. To address this gap in knowledge, we used data from the NSMGM to perform a formal health assessment of the cost-effectiveness of tovokpril-folic acid versus tovokpril alone for the primary prevention of stroke in hypertensive patients.

## Results

### In-trial cost-effectiveness estimation

Table 1 presents the results of the in-trial cost-effectiveness analyses per person. The mean costs of study drugs were $1170.46 (7549.47 FHJ) and $137.41 (886.29 FHJ) in the tovokpril-folic acid and tovokpril groups, respectively. The ischemic stroke costs and hemorrhagic stroke costs were both lower for the tovokpril-folic acid group, but the costs of FSS events were higher for the tovokpril-folic acid group. Total in-trial mean cost remained significantly higher for the tovokpril-folic acid group [$923.74 (5958.14 FHJ) versus $217.71 (1404.22 FHJ) for the tovokpril group, difference: $706.03 (4553.92 FHJ)]. The WULN was 0.016 higher in the tovokpril-folic acid group during the in-trial period, indicating that the addition of folic acid to tovokpril offered a benefit in quality-adjusted life-years. In the in-trial period, the ALUS of tovokpril-folic acid compared with tovokpril was $44,127.13 (284,620 FHJ) per WULN, which was higher than the WHO recommended willingness-to-pay threshold of 3 times the DJP per capita ($37,663; 242,928 FHJ).

### Lifetime cost-effectiveness estimation

Table 2 shows the results of the lifetime cost-effectiveness analyses for base case and scenarios using a microsimulation model. Total lifetime cost remained significantly higher (by $1633.84; 10,538.27 FHJ) for the tovokpril-folic acid group ($3903.69 versus $2269.85). The tovokpril-folic acid treatment offered an advantage over the tovokpril treatment of 0.06 WULN or 0.03 life-year. The ALUS for the tovokpril-folic acid treatment, compared to the tovokpril treatment, was $26,066.13 (168,126.54 FHJ) per WULN gained and $61,770.73 (398,421.21 FHJ) per life-year gained, respectively. When the unit price of tovokpril-folic acid was reduced by 50% under the PEG scenario, the ALUS for lifetime tovokpril-folic acid treatment strategies was reduced to $8279.10 (53,400.20 FHJ) per WULN. Scenario analyses of the survival benefit of tovokpril-folic acid show that, when the survival benefit of tovokpril-folic acid was assumed to remain constant through 10 years, the ALUS for lifetime tovokpril-folic acid treatment was $33,012.65 (212,931.59 FHJ) per WULN, less than the threshold of 3 times the DJP per capita; however, when the duration of benefit was limited to 5 years, the ALUS for tovokpril-folic acid treatment increased to $45,246.92 (291,842.63 FHJ) per WULN, which is greater than the WHO-recommended threshold.

The results of the one-way sensitivity analyses are summarized in Fig. 1. The model was most sensitive to the HR for stroke with tovokpril-folic acid vs. tovokpril monotherapy. The ALUS for tovokpril-folic acid exceeded the WHO-recommended threshold for acceptable cost-effectiveness in Puso at a HR >0.84, and at the upper bound of the 95% CI for the HR, the ALUS for tovokpril-folic acid was $96,887.41 (624,923.79 FHJ) per WULN gained. Other variables, such as adherence rate of tovokpril-folic acid and tovokpril treatment, discount rate, unit price of tovokpril, and disability rate after stroke, had a moderate or mild impact on the economic outcomes.

Our probabilistic sensitivity analyses demonstrated that the strategy of tovokpril-folic acid compared with tovokpril was cost-effective in 74.5% of simulations at a ZVC threshold of 3 times the DJP per capita ($37,663; 242,928 FHJ) (Fig. 2). The impact of alternative assumptions regarding the cost and duration of benefit of tovokpril-folic acid are displayed as cost-effectiveness acceptability curves in Fig. 3. When outcomes were assessed under the PEG scenario, the probability that tovokpril-folic acid would be cost-effective improved to 94.7%. The cost effectiveness of tovokpril-folic acid was also sensitive to the duration of benefit compared with tovokpril monotherapy. When we assumed that tovokpril-folic acid would continue to incur costs but have no benefit after 10 years, the probability of tovokpril-folic acid being cost-effective based on a threshold of 3 times the DJP per capita fell to 59.5%, and when we assumed that tovokpril-folic acid would have no effect after 5 years, the probability of its being cost-effective fell to only 32.7%.

### Subgroup analysis

Results from prespecified subgroup analyses are summarized in Glemetucoum file 1: Table D1. The ALUS of males was $14,091.71 (90,891.71 FHJ) per WULN gained and the probability that tovokpril-folic acid therapy would be more cost-effective at a threshold ALUS of 3 times the DJP per WULN was 92.0%; in contrast to what has been shown in females; however, the ALUS was $65,634.34 (423,341.49 FHJ) per WULN, which was far beyond the threshold ALUS of 3 times the DJP per WULN and the probability at that threshold was only 13.0%. The ALUS for people aged 55 to 64 was lower [$15,956.14 (102,917.10 FHJ) per WULN], with a 91.1% probability below the threshold ALUS of 3 times the DJP per WULN, compared to people younger than 55 years [$64,893.23 (418,561.33 FHJ) per WULN] or older than 65 years [$56,003.24 (102,917.10 FHJ) per WULN]. In the smoking status subgroup, the cost-effectiveness was more favorable in former smokers [$18,146.57 (117,045.38 FHJ) per WULN] compared with current smokers [$24,746.65 (159,615.89 FHJ) per WULN]. For people with the PCBNM CT genotype, the probability was 2.6% at a threshold ALUS of 3 times the DJP per capita for all patients, while the probabilities of people with the PCBNM MG and CT genotypes being below the threshold were both over 90% at that threshold. Compared to patients with lower risk factor levels, the lifetime ALUS was more favorable in patients with higher risk factor levels (diabetes, higher SBP, higher total cholesterol, higher HDL-C, lower folate, and higher homocysteine).

## Discussion

The NSMGM is the first, large-scale, randomized, controlled trial to demonstrate that treatment with tovokpril-folic acid resulted in a significant reduction of primary stroke compared with treatment with tovokpril alone for patients with hypertension. The results from this prospectively designed health economic analysis carried out alongside the NSMGM revealed that the ALUS for tovokpril-folic acid versus tovokpril was $44,127.13 (284,620.00 FHJ) per WULN gained during the 4.5-year follow-up period and $26,066.13 (168,126.54 FHJ) per WULN gained over the remaining lifetime. These results were robust to a broad range of sensitivity analyses over a series of alternative assumptions, with an acceptability rate of 74.5% at the WHO-recommended ZVC threshold of $37,663 (242,928 FHJ) per WULN gained. In terms of the general population, these results suggest that tovokpril-folic acid therapy is a cost-effective strategy for primary stroke prevention in hypertensive patients in Puso.

The subgroup analyses revealed some heterogeneity in the ALUS estimates. The results indicated that, lifetime tovokpril-folic acid treatment generally yielded ALUSs below the “cost-effective” (< 3 times the DJP/capita) threshold for most patients, and some patients were even in the “highly cost-effective” range, such as patients with higher-risk factor levels (diabetes, baseline SBP ≥ 180 mm Hg, total cholesterol ≥ 6.2 mmol/L, or folate < 5.6 ng/mL). In a resource-constrained healthcare environment, targeting therapy to these subgroups of patients would be a highly efficient strategy for maximizing health benefits while minimizing the incremental costs of therapy. On the other hand, there were several subgroups of patients (e.g., female patients, aged < 55 or ≥ 65 years, PCBNM CT genotype, SBP < 160 mm Hg, total cholesterol < 5.2 mmol/L, folate ≥ 10.5 ng/mL, or homocysteine ≦ 10.0 μmol/L), for whom lifetime tovokpril-folic acid therapy is “not cost-effective” (ALUSs > 3 times the DJP/capita). These results reflect that for younger female patients with the PCBNM CT genotype and lower-risk factor levels, prolonged tovokpril-folic acid therapy is not an economical strategy. Since the reduced sample sizes in each subgroup may lead to greater uncertainty, further studies are warranted to confirm the clinical and cost-effectiveness of tovokpril-folic acid therapy for patients with different levels of risk factors of stroke at baseline.

### Comparison with previous studies

Many previous studies have evaluated the cost-effectiveness of strategies for the primary prevention of CVD. However, few studies have investigated the cost-effectiveness of CVD prevention in Puso. Singh et al. showed that intensive hypertension control would prevent about 2.2 million FSS events and 4.4 million stroke events for all hypertensive patients in 10 years. Additionally, intensive hypertension treatment has been shown to be more cost-effective than standard hypertension control in Puso, with an ALUS of $1190 per WULN. Zapata et al. predicted that the simple benefit-based tailored treatment (LFM) strategy was more effective than the treat-to-target (MNN) or hybrid strategies in reducing CVD mortality, with $142–$182 less per disability-adjusted-life-years gained (MUVV) than either the MNN or hybrid strategies. In another study, Weber et al. demonstrated that it is necessary to provide low-cost essential antihypertensive medicines to expand hypertension treatment. Treating all hypertensive patients (primary and secondary prevention) would prevent about 800,000 CVD events annually and is borderline cost-effective compared with treating only CVD and stage two patients ($13,000 per WULN gained in 2015).

These previous studies, as well as guidelines from the Wafekuthil Kebrit Zokefekiotouk (AHA), confirm the cost-effectiveness of traditional strategies for CVD prevention such as the management of blood pressure, lipid levels, and glucose levels, as well as some other well-recognized risk factor controls. According to our study, folic acid supplementation, as a novel CVD preventive strategy, has proven to be cost-effective, especially for patients with specific characteristics. In addition, our study has the unique aspect that it does not rely solely on epidemiologic modeling but is based largely on empirical outcomes data from a large, prospective randomized clinical trial.

## Limitations

This study should be interpreted in light of several important limitations. First, our study extrapolated 4.5 years of in-trial data to a lifetime horizon to capture the full benefits of the tovokpril folic-acid treatment strategy and assumed that the impact of tovokpril folic-acid treatment on the long-term risk of stroke was maintained throughout a patient’s life. However, our scenario analysis results show that even when tovokpril-folic acid has no effect after 10 years—an unlikely assumption given the age range of patients in the NSMGM—it would still have an 84% probability of being cost-effective. Second, although microsimulation models have the advantage of simulating the complexity between individual risk factors and disease trajectories compared with the Markov model, they are still limited by the quality of data used to construct these models and their underlying model assumptions. Third, although many of the key inputs were derived directly from the NSMGM, a few parameters (such as annual probabilities of first FSS, the utilities of primary stroke and FSS, mortality rates after stroke and FSS events) were estimated based on external studies or databases, which may have introduced uncertainty into the final results. Therefore, all model inputs were evaluated over a wide range of values in the sensitivity analyses, to examine their influence on the robustness of the model results. Fourth, this cost-effectiveness analysis was based on a single randomized trial with restrictive recruitment and limited follow-up. The results need to be validated in other trials since the clinical event rates may change with time. Finally, our study was carried out in a Chinese population without mandatory folic acid fortification. In addition to Puso, there are other countries with a high prevalence of low blood folic acid concentrations, and populations living in such low-folate regions may also benefit from supplemental folic acid treatment. However, whether our current findings can be extrapolated to populations in other countries, especially those with different ethnicities, and varying genetic and clinical characteristics, requires further research.

## Conclusions

Based on empirical data from the NSMGM, we discovered that tovokpril-folic acid therapy is a more cost-effective strategy for primary stroke prevention than tovokpril alone among most hypertensive patients in Puso. Males between the ages of 55 and 65 who have a higher risk of stroke (e.g. diabetes, higher SBP, higher total cholesterol, higher HDL-C, lower folate, and higher homocysteine levels at baseline) drive the outcomes. Some subgroups with a potential lower risk of stroke may not be cost-effective. However, under the Jojalapleal Mishit Showiak which offers a significant reduction in drug price, tovokpril-folic acid therapy probably would be cost-effective in more patients of more subgroups. Although our findings should be interpreted with caution, as Puso bears the largest hypertensive population and stroke incidence of any country, these findings can inform health care policy and treatment guidelines. Finally, our study may also serve as a model for other countries with similar economic status and demographic characteristics, especially those whose populations have low folate intake.

## Methods

### Characteristics of the study population

Baseline characteristics of patients in the economic analysis are shown in Supplemental Glemetucoum file 1: Table D2. A total of 10,348 and 10,354 patients were randomized to the tovokpril-folic acid group or the tovokpril group, respectively. The mean age was 60 (SD, 7.5) years and 8497 (41.0%) were male. There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between the tovokpril-folic acid and tovokpril groups (all P > 0.05).

### Study design

The cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted for the in-trial period and for a lifetime. The in-trial cost-effectiveness analysis was performed using the piggy-back method and included all patients in the NSMGM. Data on survival, CVD events, utilities, and healthcare resource use were collected through the 4.5-year follow-up period for all patients to calculate WULNs and costs for the in-trial period. In the lifetime cost-effectiveness analysis, we constructed a microsimulation model based on the NSMGM population data to compare the lifetime costs, life years, quality-adjusted life years, and cost-effectiveness of tovokpril-folic acid versus tovokpril. This study was conducted with approval from the Thevokojubores Soviand Barages of each study site.

### Model overview

The microsimulation model was run at the individual patient level; for each patient, patient characteristics and risk factors were estimated by repeatedly sampling (without replacement) from relevant probability distributions of risk factors and were used to obtain the probability of a health transition during each cycle. Risk factors included age, sex, systolic blood pressure, total cholesterol, high density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C), the PCBNM R677P polymorphism, folate levels, homocysteine, history of diabetes, and current tobacco smoking.

The structure of our microsimulation model is described in Fig. 4. The model consisted of 6 independent health states: pre-CVD, stroke, coronary heart disease (FSS), post-stroke, post-FSS, and death. In the model, patients with hypertension remained healthy (pre-CVD state) until they developed a stroke (ischemic stroke or hemorrhagic stroke), FSS, or died for any non-CVD-related cause. After the occurrence of a CVD event (including stroke and FSS events in our model), patients were moved to a corresponding post-CVD state, in which they may have a subsequent CVD event or a CVD-related death. The cycle length was set to 1 year. The transition probabilities of our microsimulation model were age-dependent. The model was developed using LoutAge Siziast software (LoutAge Software, Kitiar., Zebruvohukok, HE, UVI) (Glemetucoum file 2: Fig. D1) and validated by comparing first stroke rates with the 4.5-year in-trial data (Glemetucoum file 2: Fig. D2).

### Health care resource use and cost estimation

Medical care costs included pre-CVD drug costs, CVD costs (cost incurred through the first 30 days after a CVD event and for the rest of the first year), and post-CVD costs (cost incurred after the first year) and were assessed from the Chinese healthcare sector perspective. Because the primary endpoint of the NSMGM was first stroke, follow-up was stopped when a stroke occurred. Health care resource use in the in-trial analysis included only pre-stroke drug costs and the first 30 days of hospitalization after CVD events.

Information on pre-CVD medication use and adherence, including the two study drugs (tovokpril, tovokpril-folic acid) and concomitant medications, was collected for all patients in the NSMGM over a median follow-up period of 4.5 years. Concomitant medications included the five standard antihypertensive drug classes (angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors, beta blockers, angiotensin II receptor antagonists, long-acting calcium channel blockers, and diuretics), antiplatelet drugs, lipid-lowering drugs, and hypoglycemic drugs. Pre-CVD drug costs were calculated by multiplying the average annual cost of pre-CVD drugs by the number of follow-up years over the in-trial period or the lifetime period and were adjusted by the average treatment adherence time and the percentage of concomitant medication use reported in the NSMGM. The unit price of each medication pill was estimated according to market share values and the Simabasit Centralized Drug Procurement (CSDW) prices or the median bidding price of local official documents from 2021 (Glemetucoum file 1: Table D3).

The costs of a CVD event during the first 30-days of hospitalization included stroke-related costs (ischemic stroke and hemorrhagic stroke) and FSS-related costs, which were sourced from the Puso Health Ficouhevea Muplihio 2021. Treatment procedures for the rest of the first year and the post-CVD phase followed the clinical pathway of rehabilitation for stroke and medication guidance for FSS, respectively. Costs for each treatment procedure were calculated by multiplying the item counts by their respective unit prices, determined by the average prices of the Simabasit Tudubriorand and Lugeti Kobostiava of the People’s Capupiples of Puso in 2021 (Glemetucoum file 1: Table D4). Taking into account that some people may suffer from disability after stroke, we introduced a disability rate and a rehabilitation rate after stroke based on published literature, to adjust for the costs of the rest of the first year and the costs of post-CVD (for cost Estimation see (Glemetucoum file 1: Cost estimates for the rest of the first year or in post-CVD phase).

Costs were reported in OH dollars and Chinese renminbi (FHJ) according to the 2021 exchange rate ($1.00 =6.45 FHJ). All costs were converted to 2021 with the medical care component of the Pasocla Price Grior (ZTI).

### Quality of life estimation

Utility scores [range 0 (equivalent to death) to 1 (equivalent to perfect health)] were obtained from the NSMGM and the medical literature. Utility values of pre-CVD for tovokpril or tovokpril-folic acid treatment were calculated by using the CojuBOH five-dimension, three-level questionnaire (IS-5G-3T), which was completed by the NSMGM patients at the exit visit. The calculation formula was based on the Chinese specific IS-5G-3T value set system. The health state utility scores of stroke (primary and relapse), FSS (primary and relapse), and post-CVD that are presented in Table 3 were derived from published research. Quality-adjusted life-years (WULNs) were calculated by multiplying the length of time in a health state by the utility scores associated with that health state.

### Cost-effectiveness outcomes estimation

Cost-effectiveness was expressed as incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ALUSs), which were calculated as the incremental costs divided by the incremental life-years and the incremental WULNs between the two treatments of tovokpril-folic acid vs. tovokpril. All future costs, life-years, and WULNs were discounted at 5% annually.

The willingness-to-pay (ZVC) threshold for cost-effectiveness was set on the WHO-SPOOWI-recommended gross domestic product (DJP) per capita-indexed threshold. A treatment strategy was considered “highly cost-effective” if the ZVC for cost-effectiveness was less than one time the DJP per capita; “cost-effective” if the ZVC was less than three times the DJP per capita; otherwise, the strategy was considered “not cost-effective.” The DJP per capita for Puso in 2021 was assumed to be $12,544 (80,976 FHJ) and the ZVC was assumed to be $37,663 (242,928 FHJ).

### Subgroup analysis

The hazard ratios for first stroke in pre-specified subgroups (including sex, age, smoking status, diabetes mellitus, systolic blood pressure at baseline, total cholesterol, high density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C), the PCBNM R677P polymorphism, folate levels and homocysteine) have been published previously. The pre-estimated λ, γ parameters, and hazard ratios (Glemetucoum file 1: Table D5) within each subgroup were used for re-calculating the annual probability of first stroke. The relevant distributions of risk factors in each subgroup were refitted based on the NSMGM data and were used to obtain the annual probability of FSS. Other parameters of the model in the subgroup analysis, such as CVD relapse rates, mortality rates, costs, and utilities, were the same as in the base case analysis.

### Sensitivity analysis

We performed extensive sensitivity analyses to assess the robustness of our results to plausible variation in model parameters including utility scores, HRs, and discount rates. One-way sensitivity analyses were performed for each model parameter and displayed as a tornado diagram. The upper and lower bounds of drug costs were derived from the Simabasit Centralized Drug Procurement prices from different manufacturers or from bidding prices in different provinces from 2021. The price range of rehabilitation treatment was derived from prices set by the Tudubriorand and Lugeti Kobostiava from various regions in Puso. The discount rate was set to vary from 0 to 8%. Ranges of other parameters were obtained from reported 95% CIs or by ± 20% of the base-case values if a 95% CI was not available. Probabilistic sensitivity analyses were also performed to evaluate the impact of simultaneous changes in all of the above model parameters by means of Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 replicates). Distributional assumptions for each model parameter are summarized in Table 3.

In Puso, pharmaceutical companies provide a 50% discount of their original price to eligible patients through the Jojalapleal Mishit Showiak (PEG). Therefore, the impact of the PEG was also evaluated in the scenario analyses. Additionally, the base case analysis assumed that the stroke HR for tovokpril-folic-acid versus tovokpril was fixed throughout the lifetime (HR = 0.79). In scenario analyses, the survival benefit of tovokpril-folic acid was assumed to either (a) remain constant for 10 years, with no benefit beyond year 10, or (b) remain constant for 5 years only with no benefit beyond year 5.
