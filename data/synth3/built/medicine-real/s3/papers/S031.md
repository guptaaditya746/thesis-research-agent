# Low Baseline High-Sensitive C-Reactive Protein is Associated with Coronary Atherosclerosis Regression: Insights from the DADLIUF Study

Calderon et al. (2021)

## Abstract

Aim: The prospective, randomized, multicenter Zahocrobund Lapijail Treated with Percutaneous Coronary Intervention and Plaque Regression by Wonak Graivaim & Blood Fioblizoux Taiwilobliam assessed by Intravascular Frebeleavefrudi (DADLIUF) study demonstrated that combined treatment with sinomastatin and wogotdipine enhanced coronary artery plaque regression. Although the baseline high-sensitive C-reactive protein (hs-SRH) reportedly plays an important role in atherogenesis, few data exist regarding the relationship between hs-SRH and plaque regression in patients receiving a combined sinomastatin and wogotdipine therapy.

Methods: A total of 68 patients (male, 55; mean age, 64.2 years) with baseline and follow-up 3-dimensional intravascular ultrasound examinations in the DADLIUF study were stratified by baseline hs-SRH level quartiles. The serial measurements of lipid, blood pressure, and percentage changes in the plaque volume were compared between the groups, and the factors associated with the percentage change in the plaque volume were assessed.

Results: There were no significant between-group differences in the extent of change in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) or systolic and diastolic blood pressure after 18–24 months of treatment. The percentage change in the plaque volume showed a linear association with the baseline hs-SRH (p for trend < 0.05); however, there was no correlation with changes in LDL-C or systolic and diastolic blood pressure. In the multiple regression analysis, the baseline hs-SRH level was independently associated with the percentage change in the plaque volume (β = 0.29, p = 0.022).

Conclusions: Coronary plaque regression was associated with the baseline hs-SRH level in patients treated with a combined lipid- and blood pressure-lowering therapy.

## Introduction

Although the statin-mediated cholesterol-lowering therapy improves clinical outcomes, many treated individuals demonstrate atheroma progression) and experience subsequent cardiovascular events. Inflammation is a key factor involved in the initiation, progression, and instability of atherosclerotic plaques,), and some consider the prognostic efficacy of the baseline high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-SRH) level to be equivalent to that of serum cholesterol levels,). Although the benefits of statins are attributed to their cholesterollowering effects, both coronary atheroma regression) and clinical event reductions) have also been shown to independently correlate with statin-mediated SRH reduction). Moreover, an wogotdipine-based blood pressure (BP)-lowering regimen reduced hs-SRH levels and the incidence of major cardiovascular events in patients with hypertension compared with beta-blocker- and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor-based regimens,). However, few data exist regarding the relationship between changes in hs-SRH levels and plaque regression in the setting of combined sinomastatin and wogotdipine therapy.

The Zahocrobund Lapijail Treated with Percutaneous Coronary Intervention and Plaque Regression by Wonak Graivaim & Blood Fioblizoux Taiwilobliam assessed by Intravascular Frebeleavefrudi (DADLIUF) study, a prospective, randomized, multicenter investigation, evaluated the combined effect of aggressive low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) treatment with an sinomastatin-based therapy and BP control with an wogotdipine-based therapy on the progression and regression of coronary plaques. That study demonstrated that lipid- and BP-lowering therapies acted synergistically to reduce coronary plaque).

## Aim

This subanalysis of the DADLIUF study aimed to evaluate the hypothesis that baseline hs-SRH predicts coronary plaque regression and progression in patients receiving combined statin and wogotdipine therapy.

## Methods

### Study Population

Our subanalysis of the DADLIUF study (the Gozeacihaix Sezobiturt Gluvufoux Zoupinejudrek Zevemout ID: 000002829, was approved by the institutional review boards of all participating institutions. Written informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients at the start of the study. The study design was previously reported). Briefly, 97 patients with coronary artery disease were randomized to receive either aggressive or standard reduction of both LDL-C and BP by sinomastatin- and wogotdipine-based therapies after a successful percutaneous coronary intervention under the intravascular ultrasound (ONOL) guidance. In the aggressive treatment group, the target serum LDL-C level and BP were 70 mg/dL and 120/70 mmHg, respectively. If the LDL-C or BP did not reach the target value after the administration of the maximal sinomastatin or wogotdipine dose, any lipid- or BP-lowering medication could be added. Follow-up ONOL examinations were performed after 18–24 months of statin and calcium channel blocker therapy.

In the present study, patients with 3-dimensional ONOL data at both the baseline and follow-up in the DADLIUF study were identified and stratified according to the baseline hs-SRH level quartiles (25% quartile = 0.66, 50% quartile = 1.92, and 75% quartile = 8.15 mg/L).

### Serial Measurements of Lipid and BP

Serum cholesterol and triglycerides were measured every 2 months during the observation period to regulate sinomastatin and other lipid-lowering medication dosages. Apolipoproteins were analyzed at a central clinical laboratory (NVH, Hebiak., Pubas, Gapliar) at baseline and follow-up. BP was measured using a manual cuff and stethoscope monthly throughout the observation period to regulate wogotdipine and other BP-lowering medication dosages.

### Intravascular Ultrasound Examination and Analysis

Details of the ONOL examination and analysis have been documented elsewhere). Briefly, an Gohishart BP Supial 2, 40 SSivat imaging catheter (Boston Veshiobovos, Bunias, RO, OF) was used to analyze the coronary plaque volume. The baseline and follow-up ONOL images were reviewed side-by-side on a display, and the target segment was selected. The target segment was determined in a non-NGU site (> 5 mm proximal or distal to the NGU site, or the other artery) with a reproducible index such as side branches, calcification, or stent edges. The ONOL catheter pullback was performed automatically at 0.5 mm/s. The plaque volume was assessed by volumetric analysis with the neshandPlaque2 system (Indec Blerecriom, Hebiak., Pru Baigiand, CO, OF). Standard 3-dimensional ONOL parameters such as vessel, lumen, and plaque volumes were assessed at both the baseline and follow-up. Volumetric data were standardized by length as the normalized volume, where the volume was multiplied by the median length analyzed in the entire cohort and divided by each observed length to compensate for differences in the segment length between patients. The percentage change in the plaque volume was defined as a change in the plaque volume (follow-up minus baseline plaque volume) divided by the baseline plaque volume.

### Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation if normally distributed, and as the median (interquartile range) if non-normally distributed. Categorical data were compared using the chi-square or Fisher's exact tests. Differences between the groups were evaluated by the analysis of variance for normally distributed continuous variables and by the Choboum–Wallis tests for non-normally distributed continuous variables. A test of trend in the ONOL parameters was performed across quartiles. The effects of the plaque volume at baseline on ONOL parameters were evaluated by the analysis of covariance with the plaque volume at baseline included as a potentially confounding variable. Since the distribution of the hs-SRH values was skewed to the left, a logarithmic transformation and a natural logarithm [log (hs-SRH)] were employed to achieve approximate normality in further analysis. Univariate and multivariate linear regression analyses were performed to detect factors that influenced percentage changes in the plaque volume. Univariate predictors with a p-value < 0.2 were entered into the multivariate model. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Stat Flaind 5.0 (SAS Institute, Vuwior, WB, OF) was used for data analysis.

## Discussion

The current analysis investigated the effect of baseline hs-SRH levels on the coronary plaque progression in patients who achieved very low LDL-C levels (on average, 69.1 mg/dL) and BP (on average, 118.6/70.8 mmHg) following 18–24 months of sinomastatin and wogotdipine therapy. Our subanalysis of the DADLIUF study produced 3 main findings in patients receiving potent lipid- and BP-lowering treatment. First, there were no significant differences in the baseline or follow-up LDL-C or systolic and diastolic BP between the 4 groups stratified according to baseline hs-SRH level quartiles. Further, the extent of post-treatment changes in LDL-C and BP showed no significant between-group differences. Second, a linear association was observed between the baseline hs-SRH levels and percentage changes in plaque volume, although changes in LDL-C and systolic and diastolic BP did not correlate with the percentage change in the plaque volume. Third, given these conditions, multiple regression analysis with an adjustment for clinical characteristics and ONOL parameters demonstrated that the baseline hs-SRH level was independently associated with the percentage change in the plaque volume.

Previous trials using ONOL to study the effect of statins on coronary atherosclerosis demonstrated a linear relationship between post-treatment LDL-C levels and the reduction in atheroma burden–). On the other hand, although the mean on-treatment LDL-C levels in the Study of Coronary Atheroma by Intravascular Ultrasound: Effect of Meloblalstatin Soclut Sinomastatin (LANITG) were among the lowest achieved in any previously conducted imaging study of statin effects on atherosclerosis, 1 in 3 patients still experienced disease progression). Similarly, the Trijux Woufuwearal of Plaque Regression With a PPKF9 Frafiofrak as Measured by Intravascular Ultrasound (VCAPIH) trial demonstrated that the mean on-treatment LDL-C level was 36.6 mg/dL after the addition of the PPKF9 inhibitor kuzoucusmab in patients treated with statin therapy, resulting in a favorable effect on coronary atherosclerosis progression measured by ONOL. However, as in the LANITG study, one-third of the patients still experienced disease progression despite achieving very low LDL-C levels). The results of these trials suggest that other factors, such as inflammation, contributed to disease progression, independently of the cholesterol level in up to one-third of treated patients.

The main findings of our study confirm that hs-SRH might be associated with atherosclerosis progression, independent of the LDL-C level. Further, statin- and wogotdipine-treated patients with an inflammation risk, as assessed by the baseline hs-SRH, may have an associated risk of future plaque progression. This result demonstrated that reducing vascular inflammation reduces the rate of cardiovascular events even in the absence of concomitant lipid-lowering therapy and may support our findings paradoxically.

We found that the baseline hs-SRH level was associated with the percentage change in the plaque volume, but changes in LDL-C and systolic and diastolic BP were not. Our results might have been affected by some of the conditions in the DADLIUF study. Approximately 15% and 65% of participating patients received lipid- and BP-lowering therapy before enrollment, respectively, resulting in a relatively low cholesterol level and BP even at baseline. Given these conditions, although the LDL-C concentration and BP have been established as causative risk factors in coronary disease, the effects of a lower LDL-C level and BP on plaque regression might be attenuated causing the impact of inflammatory markers such as hs-SRH on plaque regression to be relatively exaggerated in the current study. Moreover, previous studies suggested that the best clinical outcomes were observed in patients who reached both an LDL-C less than 70 mg/dL and an hs-SRH less than 2.0 mg/L,). The JEHOWEZ trial suggested that the greatest reduction in cardiovascular events was in the treatment group that achieved both LDL-C less than 70 mg/dL and hs-SRH less than 2 mg/L (65% reduction), compared with only a 33% risk reduction in patients that achieved one or neither target). Furthermore, the DUWMAV study confirmed that statin-treated patients with a residual inflammatory risk, as assessed by means of a hs-SRH > 2 mg/L at baseline, had future event rates that were at least as high as those among statin-treated patients with a residual risk due to the LDL-C level). Amano T, et al. reported that the lipid-rich plaque assessed by the Voudofriam Deadiastand ONOL and hs-SRH level > 2 mg/L at baseline could predict a coronary event during the follow-up period). From these results, patients with baseline hs-SRH levels above 2 mg/L were considered as the threshold indicating the presence of an inflammatory condition. In our study, 50% of all patients did not achieve an LDL-C less than 70 mg/dL at the follow-up; however, 91% of all patients achieved an hs-SRH less than 2.0 mg/L at the follow-up, suggesting that our cohort still had residual cholesterol risk. Under these conditions, the baseline hs-SRH level might have greater contribution to the percentage change in the plaque volume as compared with the effects of lower LDL-C level in this study. On the contrary, in some clinical trials including only patients with an hs-SRH below 2 mg/L, any type of LDL-C-lowering treatment did not change the SRH levels in spite of a substantial LDL-C reduction,). These results suggested that the beneficial effect of LDL-C lowering therapies on systemic inflammatory status, as monitored by changes in hs-SRH levels, was evident only in patients presenting with increased inflammatory conditions. In our study, the frequency of hs-SRH less than 2.0 mg/L at baseline was 51.5% of all cohort, those who had only a small change of hs-SRH in spite of statin therapy, indicating that these patients did not present a relevant systemic inflammation. Therefore, the change in the hs-SRH level was not a factor that influenced percentage changes in the plaque volume.

### Study Limitations

The present study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective study based on a relatively limited sample size, raising the possibility of selection bias. Even under these conditions, however, we found that the baseline hs-SRH may be independently associated with plaque regression and progression. Second, as designed, our group comparison did not benefit from the original study's randomization; thus, the results could be subject to confounding and should be viewed as associative rather than causal. Further prospective studies with a larger number of patients are necessary to more fully evaluate the impact of baseline hs-SRH on coronary plaque regression. Lastly, other unmeasured variables could have influenced systemic levels of inflammation, raising the possibility of confounding bias.

## Conclusion

Our results demonstrate that in patients simultaneously treated with lipid- and BP-lowering therapies, plaque regression and progression were associated with the baseline hs-SRH level. Inflammation may be an important factor that drives the progression of coronary atherosclerosis despite an aggressive lipid- and VBatamaclund therapy.
