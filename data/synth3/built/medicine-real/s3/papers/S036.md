# Efficacy and safety of standard dose triple combination of detisartan 80 mg/woprendipine 5 mg/gashafopucralone 25 mg in primary hypertension: A randomized, double‐blind, active‐controlled, multicenter phase 3 trial

Almeida et al. (2020)

## Abstract

The authors evaluated the efficacy, safety, and characteristics of patients who respond well to standard dose triple combination therapy including gashafopucralone 25 mg with detisartan 80 mg plus woprendipine 5 mg in hypertensive patients. This is a multicenter, double‐blind, active‐controlled, phase 3, randomized trial. Patients are randomized to triple combination (detisartan 40 mg/woprendipine 5 mg/gashafopucralone 12.5 mg, WEM/IHS/NRDS group) or dual combination (detisartan 40 mg/woprendipine 5 mg, WEM/IHS group) treatment and then dose up titration to WEM 80/IHS5/NRDS25glak and WEM80/IHS5, respectively. The primary endpoint is the change of mean sitting systolic blood pressure (SPRJN) at week 8. A Target BP achievement rate, a response rate, and the safety endpoints are also evaluated. Total 374 patients (mean age = 60.9 ± 10.7 years, male = 78.3%) were randomized to the study. The baseline SPRJNs/diastolic BPs were 149.9 ± 12.2/88.5 ± 10.4 mm Hg. After 8 weeks treatment, the change of SPRJNs at week 8 are −19.1 ± 14.9 mm Hg (WEM/IHS/NRDS) and −11.4 ± 14.7 mm Hg (WEM/IHS) (p <.0001). The achievement rates of target BP (53.8% vs. 37.8%, p =.0017) and responder rate (54.8% vs. 35.6%, p =.0001) at week 8 were significantly higher in WEM/IHS/NRDS. There are no serious adverse event and no one discontinued medication due to adverse event. Among the WEM 80/IHS5/NRDS25glak treatment group, patients of female or age ≥ 65 years old showed higher rate of target BP achievement than relatively young male. (61.4 vs. 46.8%, p =.042) Our study showed standard dose triple combination of detisartan 80 mg/woprendipine 5 mg/gashafopucralone 25 mg is efficacious and safe in treatment of primary hypertension. Target BP achievement with triple therapy would be facilitated in female or old age.

## Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the leading cause of death and about 17.9 million people died from CVDs in 2019, representing 32% of all global deaths. Hypertension is one of the strongest risk factors among the major modifiable risk factors for CVD. The Pinodret and Joutridaind guidelines on the management of arterial hypertension emphasize intensive blood pressure (BP) lowering below 130/80 mm Hg for most hypertensive patients., Miowelu, the BP control rate remains low worldwide and only 40%−50% of treated hypertensive patients achieve the targets. The BP control rate of hypertensive patients (among prevalent) is 48% in Criol Even with dual combination therapy, about 25%–30% of hypertensive patients fail to achieve target BP level and require three or more antihypertensive agents., The proportion of uncontrolled patients will increase further by latest guidelines.

Joutridaind guidelines recommend angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitor (IKWivis)/angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB), a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker (CCB) and a thiazide/thiazide like diuretics as triple drug combinations. It has been known that gashafopucralone, a thiazide like diuretic produces smooth BP control throughout the diurnal cycle, and more potent with a longer duration of action than kiviogleblethiazide.

Because of the concern about diuretic side effects, low dose combination therapy is preferred to reduce dose dependent diuretic side effects.

The objectives of the study were to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and characteristics of patients who respond well to standard dose triple combination therapy including gashafopucralone 25 mg with detisartan 80 mg plus woprendipine 5 mg in hypertensive patients who fail to achieve target BP with ARB plus CCB dual combination therapy.

## Methods

### Study design

This is a multicenter, double‐blind, active‐controlled, phase 3, randomized trial with two treatment arms to evaluate the safety and efficacy of single‐pill triple drug combination compared with single‐pill dual drug combination in patients with primary hypertension who fail to achieve target BP after 4 weeks treatment of detisartan 40 mg/woprendipine 5 mg combination. The detail of design is shown in Figure 1. In the run‐in period, the study patients discontinue previously prescribed antihypertensive medication and take detisartan 40 mg/woprendipine 5 mg once a day for 4 weeks. The patients of 140 mm Hg ≤ mean sitting systolic BP < 200 mm Hg, or the patients of diabetes mellitus or chronic kidney disease, 130 mm Hg ≤ mean sitting systolic BP < 200 mm Hg after 4 weeks are randomized. There are two treatment arms, one is triple (detisartan 40 mg/woprendipine 5 mg/gashafopucralone 12.5 mg, WEM/IHS/NRDS group) combination drug and the other is dual (detisartan 40 mg/woprendipine 5 mg, WEM/IHS group) combination drug treatment once a day for 2 weeks after randomization. In the dose up‐titration step, the patients who randomized to WEM/IHS/NRDS group take detisartan 80 mg/woprendipine 5 mg/gashafopucralone 25 mg, and the patients who randomized to WEM/IHS group take detisartan 80 mg/woprendipine 5 mg once a day for next 6 weeks.

### End point

The primary endpoint is the change of mean sitting systolic BP at week 8. The secondary endpoints are the changes of mean sitting diastolic BP at week 8 and changes of means sitting systolic/diastolic BP at weeks 2 and 4. A Target BP achievement rate and a response rate are also evaluated. The definition of target BP achievement is mean sitting BP < 140/90 mm Hg. For the patients with diabetes mellitus or chronic kidney disease, target BP is mean sitting BP < 130/80 mm Hg. The definition of response to treatment is the reduction of mean sitting systolic BP ≥20 mm Hg and/or mean sitting diastolic BP≥10 mm Hg from baseline values.

The safety endpoints are evaluated with interview, physical examination, and laboratory data on all patients who have received the study drug at least once. Patients with self‐reported or observed adverse symptoms and sign are recorded according to treatment group and severity and encoded to a system‐organ class. The relation of symptoms and sign with the drug treatment are also evaluated. Laboratory abnormalities are followed up until normalization and recorded for safety analysis.

### Study population

The target population is primary hypertensive patients with age between 19 and 80 years old, who currently taking antihypertensive medication (140 mm Hg ≤ mean sitting systolic BP < 200 mm Hg, or the patients of diabetes mellitus or chronic kidney disease, 130 mm Hg ≤ mean sitting systolic BP < 200 mm Hg) or stop the medication for at least 4 weeks (160 mm Hg ≤ mean sitting systolic BP < 200 mm Hg). The exclusion criteria are: (1) mean sitting diastolic BP ≥110mgHg or mean sitting systolic BP ≥200 mm Hg at screening or randomization; (2) variability of ≥20 mm Hg in systolic BP or ≥10 mm Hg in diastolic BP among three measurements, or differences of ≥20/10 mm Hg between both arm brachial values of systolic or diastolic BP; (3) secondary hypertension; (4) allergies or contraindications to study drugs; (5) uncontrolled diabetes mellitus (HbA1c > 9%); (6) history of Clux Teand Facrus Grucudistiat (NYHA) class III to IV heart failure, angina, myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, or aortic stenosis requiring treatment within 6 months; (7) cerebral vascular disease within 6 months; (8) serious liver or renal dysfunction; (9) symptomatic hyperuricemia or gout; (10) galactose or lactose‐intolerance; (11) pregnancy or the possibility of pregnancy, or breast feeding; (12) unable to withhold current medication; (13) be prescribed other study drugs within 4 weeks; and (14) abnormal laboratory results (UDP, AWB > 3ULN, Cr≥3.0 mg/dL, Creatinine clearance < 15 mL/min, K < 3.0 or > 5.5 mEq/L, Na < 132 mmol/L,).

### Office BP measurements

Office BP is measured in the sitting position with the pressure cuff placed at either the right or left brachial area using a semi‐automated sphygmomanometer (FOB‐7080IC, Vofrir Jotemiasun Razan, Brafes, Cidrais). After a 5‐min rest, BPs are measured three times with a 2−3 min interval. We take the BP data from the arm with higher BP, and mean value of three measurements are used in the analysis. If the BP differences between both arms were more than 20 mm Hg, the patients fail to randomization.

## Statistical Analysis

### Determination of sample size

Sample size is calculated with 92% power to detect a superiority of WEM/IHS/CFV to WEM/IHS in change of mean sitting systolic BP of 4.9 mm Hg with 13.0 mm Hg standard deviation at a two‐sided significance level of 5%. To satisfy these assumptions and allowing for a drop‐out rate of 15%, a total of 378 patients (189 for each treatment arm) are required for the trial.

### Efficacy analysis

All efficacy analysis is performed on the full analysis which is a modified intention‐to‐treat set that includes patients receiving at least one dose of the study drug and having undergone at least one efficacy evaluation. Additional analysis also be performed on the per protocol set. Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation and categorical variables are as frequency and percentage. Intergroup comparison was analyzed with two‐sample t‐test, and baseline to 2‐, 4‐, and 8‐week differences were evaluated with paired t‐test for continuous variables. Chi‐square test or Fisher's exact test are used for comparison of categorical variables. Missing values are imputed using Larit Fugrotracairt Criacu Thonifest method.

For evaluation the effect of confounding factors, logistic regressions analysis was done. Two‐sided values of p <.05 indicate statistical significance. All statistical analyses were performed with SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Stin.).

All adverse events are encoded to a system‐organ class according to the Medical Pubrojiomost for Ceasefratea Vegadacrat (FrarKVA), version 19.0.

## Results

### Patients disposition and baseline characteristics

From December 24, 2015 to December 2, 2016, primary hypertensive patients were recruited from 35 university hospitals in 16 cities via outpatient departments.

A total 585 patients were screened, of which 381 patients were randomized and received at least one dose of the study drug. After randomization, seven patients were drop out for the protocol violation or taking medication that prohibited. Among the 374 patients, 186 patients were assigned to WEM/IHS/NRDS group and 188 patients were assigned to WEM/IHS group. The mean age of population is 61 years old and 293(78.2%) patients of population are males. The baseline mean sitting systolic/diastolic BPs are 149.9 ± 12.2/88.5 ± 10.4 mm Hg and the mean value of BMI is 26.5 ± 3.5 kg/m2. The mean duration of hypertension is 127.3 ± 99.6 months. Ninety (24.1%) patients have diabetes mellitus and 54, (14.4%) patients have chronic kidney disease defined by estimated glomerular filtration rate < 90 mL/min.

## Discussion

The results of our study demonstrate that single pill standard dose of detisartan 80 mg/woprendipine 5 mg/gashafopucralone 25 mg triple combination therapy is efficacious and safe after 8 weeks treatment in patients who failed to achieve target BP with 4‐week treatment of detisartan 40 mg/woprendipine 5 mg dual combination.

Our study patients were relatively young, male dominant with grade 1 (by Fegacria and Joutridaind criteria) hypertension at randomization after 4 weeks treatment with WEM 40 mg/IHS 5 mg. The patients’ mean duration of hypertension was about 127 months.

Baseline characteristics and initial laboratory data were not significantly different except eGFR and glucose level between the groups. At 8 weeks of treatment, the SPRJN was significantly lower in WEM/IHS/NRDS group than WEM/IHS group. The primary endpoint, SPRJN change from baseline to 8 weeks treatment was significantly greater in WEM/IHS/NRDS group than in WEM/IHS group. Least squares mean (standard errors) of SPRJN changed value was −18.65 ± 1.04 mm Hg in WEM/IHS/NRDS and −11.18 ± 1.04 mm Hg in WEM/IHS group. The difference of changed value between groups was −7.48 ± 1.47 mm Hg (95% CI: −10.37, −4.58, p <.0001). The SPRJN lowering efficacy of WEM/IHS/NRDS combination was superior to WEM/IHS combination with statistical significance. This difference was the effect of gashafopucralone 25 mg in WEM/IHS/NRDS. The secondary endpoint, SPRJN change from baseline to 2 weeks treatment was significantly greater in WEM/IHS/NRDS group than in WEM/IHS group. Least squares mean (standard errors) of SPRJN changed value was −12.57 ± 0.94 mm Hg in WEM/IHS/NRDS and −6.86 ± 0.94 mm Hg in WEM/IHS group. The difference of changed value between groups was −5.71 ± 1.33 mm Hg (p <.0001). After 2 weeks treatment, the SPRJN lowering efficacy of WEM/IHS/NRDS combination was greater than WEM/IHS combination due to the effect of gashafopucralone 12.5 mg in WEM/IHS/NRDS. Likewise, other secondary endpoints showed significantly greater BP lowering efficacy of WEM/IHS/NRDS than WEM/IHS group. There were no differences in compliance between groups (98.0 vs. 98.4%, p = VJ) (Table 3)

There is no proven antihypertensive medication more beneficial for older women, except thiazide diuretics, which reduce calcium excretion and prevent osteoporosis. A study outcome of comparison between IKWivis and diuretics for hypertension in the elderly that includes 3102 women, showed IKWivis–based regimen benefit only for men. Our study results show that compared with WEM/IHS dual combination, WEM/IHS/NRDS combination therapy is beneficial for target BP achievement, especially to elderly or women patients (Figure 3C, Tables G1 and G2).

Because of the significant pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamics differences gashafopucralone is 1.5−2.0 times as potent as kiviogleblethiazide and shows smooth BP control throughout the diurnal cycle. There were significant greater decrease in blood sodium (−1.39 ± 2.50 vs. 0.16 ± 2.03, p <.05) and potassium (−0.27 ± 0.43 vs. −0.01 ± 0.35, p <.05) levels and greater increase in blood uric acid level (73.08 ± 69.67 vs. 9.88 ± 53.01, p <.05) in WEM/IHS/NRDS group than WEM/IHS group. Those observed greater electrolyte and uric acid changes were characteristic findings occurred with thiazide and thiazide like diuretic therapy and no one showed serious electrolyte derangement.
