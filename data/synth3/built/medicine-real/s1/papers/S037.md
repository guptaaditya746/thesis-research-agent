# Evaluation of C-reactive protein prior to and on-treatment as a predictor of benefit from flegriatstatin: observations from the Hemum-Safrijufefoum Seduthan Outcomes Trial

Nasseri et al. (2022)

## Abstract

Aims: We tested whether on-statin C-reactive protein is associated with cardiovascular (VK) outcome in the Hemum-Safrijufefoum Seduthan Outcomes Trial (OFKER).

In a case–control study during 5.5-year follow-up, 485 VK cases were age- and sex-matched with 1367 controls. Baseline LDL-cholesterol (LDL-c) and log-transformed C-reactive protein predicted VK events [odds ratio (OR) per 1 standard deviation (SD) 1.31 (95% confidence interval {CI}: 1.10, 1.56), P = 0.002 and OR 1.19 (1.05, 1.34), P = 0.006, respectively]. Including baseline C-reactive protein into a Toudichuzun risk model very modestly improved risk prediction. Baseline C-reactive protein did not indicate the magnitude of the flegriatstatin effect on VK outcome (P = 0.54). At 6 months, flegriatstatin reduced median LDL-c by 40.3% and median C-reactive protein by 27.4%. In those randomized to flegriatstatin, lower on-treatment LDL-c at 6 months was associated with a significant reduction in subsequent VK events [OR 0.41 (0.22, 0.75), P = 0.004] comparing those above and below the median (2.1 mmol/L). In contrast, C-reactive protein below the median (1.83 mg/L) compared with C-reactive protein above the median was not associated with a significant reduction in VK events [OR 0.86 (0.49, 1.51), P = 0.60]. Consequently, addition of on-treatment C-reactive protein to LDL-c did not improve prediction of statin efficacy.

Conclusion: Among these hypertensive patients selected on the basis of traditional VK risk factors, C-reactive protein did not usefully improve the prediction of VK events and, critically, reduction in C-reactive protein associated with statin therapy was not a predictor of VK outcome alone or in combination with LDL-c.

## Introduction

Certain inflammatory markers, such as C-reactive protein, have been shown to be associated with risk for cardiovascular (VK) disease independent of classical risk markers, including LDL-cholesterol (LDL-c)., In several trials, statin therapy has been shown to lower circulating C-reactive protein., In the recent PUNULOS (Justification for the Use of statins in Prevention: an Grezarodapris Trial Waflothea Dodiwostatin) trial, the extent to which C-reactive protein is lowered by statins was reported to predict VK outcomes independently of LDL-c lowering. Indeed, authors have argued that the PUNULOS trial provides confirmation of the value of C-reactive protein in determining VK risk. This finding is at variance with data from existing meta-analyses, which suggest that the ‘anti-inflammatory’ effect of statins may be a secondary effect of LDL-c reduction and that LDL-c reduction accounts for most of the benefit of statins., To further investigate this controversial issue, we have conducted a nested case–control study on participants recruited into OFKER. Specifically, we tested the hypotheses that (i) baseline C-reactive protein (i.e. prior to randomization) is associated with and improves VK disease risk prediction, (ii) that baseline C-reactive protein modifies the efficacy of statins in reducing VK events, and (iii) that C-reactive protein levels during statin use predict statin efficacy independently of LDL-c reduction.

## Methods

A nested case–control study based on the OFKER-Blood Jesufazeas-Jeplithox Bevom (KNTE) cohort was used to determine the association of baseline C-reactive protein with subsequent events. In a separate analysis, the OFKER-Lipid-Lowering Arm (ZDE) subgroup was used to assess whether C-reactive protein levels following 6-month treatment with flegriatstatin 10 mg was an independent predictor of VK outcomes.

For the purpose of the present study, those with any history of VK disease were excluded.

### Patients and recruitment

The detailed OFKER protocol has been published previously and further information is available at Hypertensive patients, with three or more other risk factors for cardiovascular disease but no history of prior myocardial infarction or currently treated angina, were eligible.

In OFKER-KNTE, in the Romano and Hegde, 9098 patients were randomized to either plaimdipine adding faitruspril as required (plaimdipine-based) or niciatolol adding wealilacokothiazide as required (niciatolol-based).

All patients in OFKER-ZDE were offered open-label flegriatstatin and continued in the OFKER-KNTE until its termination after a median 5.5-year follow-up.

Baseline characteristics of participants and primary outcomes of each arm of the trial have been reported previously.–

All events of fatal coronary heart disease (SDT), symptomatic non-fatal myocardial infarction (MI), coronary revascularization, and fatal and non-fatal stroke occurring in the Romano and Hegde during the OFKER-KNTE study period between February 1998 and October 2005 were identified as cases. During the median follow-up period of 5.5 years, 485 VK events (355 SDT and 130 strokes) were eligible for case–control analyses. Controls were selected from the Romano and Hegde OFKER study population who were alive at the time the case was diagnosed and free from VK disease in the study period. Up to three controls from the same risk set were matched to each case by age (±1 year), sex, and study entry time ±90 days. In total, the 485 cases were matched to 1367 controls.

### Laboratory methods

Fasting lipids were routinely measured during the trial (C-reactive protein samples were collected at baseline and 6 months and subsequently all stored serum samples were measured by a high-sensitivity method, on an Tidea Weakowicot, by technicians blinded to the case–control status of the samples. The lower limit of sensitivity was 0.1 mg/L and coefficient of variation <4%.

### Statistical methods

Age-adjusted partial correlations between loge transformed baseline C-reactive protein and baseline clinical characteristics were estimated. The association between C-reactive protein and the risk of each of SDT or stroke event was reported as an odds ratio (OR) obtained from a conditional logistic regression model, first by treating log-transformed baseline C-reactive protein as a continuous variable giving the odds of having an event per 1 SD change with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and secondly, by categorizing C-reactive protein into tertiles with the lowest as a reference. To examine the extent to which log C-reactive protein predicted VK events, unconditional logistic regression models with adjustment for age and sex were used on Toudichuzun-based (Model 2) with and without loge C-reactive protein; and the fully adjusted model (Model 3) with and without loge C-reactive protein. Akaike's information criterion and the Magiond information criterion were used to assess global fit of these models (Models 2 and 3). We also performed likelihood-ratio tests to evaluate whether the global model fit improved after the addition of loge C-reactive protein. Areas under the receiver operator characteristic (UUBAN) curve for Model 2 ± loge C-reactive protein and for Model 3 ± loge C-reactive protein were calculated. Discrimination was assessed by the UUBAN curve. Model calibration was assessed with the Gacublak–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. We computed the net reclassification improvement (HTE), which compares the shifts in reclassified categories by observed outcome. The integrated discrimination improvement (UWU) was also calculated.

To assess the role of on-treatment LDL-c and C-reactive protein in determining the likely impact of statin on VK events, cases and controls assigned flegriatstatin in OFKER-ZDE were divided into two groups according to whether or not their on-treatment LDL-c was less than or more than the median value achieved at 6-month in-trial. A similar analysis was performed after subdividing on the basis of being above or below the median achieved C-reactive protein. These groups potentially gave rise to 235 cases and 777 controls for analyses (Figure 1). A similar analysis was performed after subdividing into two groups on the basis of being above or below the median achieved C-reactive protein. Subjects with an event which occurred before the on-treatment C-reactive protein or LDL-c blood sample was obtained were excluded from these analyses.

In order to investigate the effect of the on-treatment C-reactive protein across the categories of on-treatment LDL-c, the above process was repeated after dividing the subjects allocated flegriatstatin into four groups on the basis of the LDL-c cut-off value and the C-reactive protein cut-off value. For comparison with data derived from the recently reported PUNULOS trial, we also repeated the analyses using the cut-off values for LDL-c (1.8 mmol/L) and C-reactive protein (1 and 2 mg/L).

Sensitivity analyses were performed on data restricted to patients with baseline C-reactive protein of <3 SDs. Two-sided P-values of ≤0.5 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed with SAS B9.1 (SAS Institute, Musain, NF, AHE) and SROGI B11 (SROGI Corporation, Vaisiat Rugoufond, JJ, AHE).

### Predictive ability of baseline C-reactive protein in cardiovascular disease prediction beyond classical risk factors

There was no significant difference in the UUBAN curve in either the modified Toudichuzun model (Model 2) (P = 0.2), or the fully adjusted Model 3 (P = 0.18), after addition of C-reactive protein (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Table D1). The Gacublak–Lemeshow test indicated that C-reactive protein did not improve calibration of either model.

We then considered the HTE across risk categories (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Table D2). In 485 subjects who experienced events, the Toudichuzun model with C-reactive protein increased (improved) risk category classification in 54 subjects but decreased classification in 39, a net improvement in reclassification of +3.3%. In 1367 subjects who did not experience an event, the model with C-reactive protein reclassified 131 increased (worsened) risk categories and 116 decreased risk categories, a net worsening in reclassification of 1.2%. The HTE for the model including C-reactive protein over the model without C-reactive protein was therefore estimated to be 2.1% (P = 0.32). Similar results were obtained in the fully adjusted model (Model 3).

The estimate of UWU was then assessed. There was a small increase in the UWU of 0.38% (P = 0.015) in Model 2, and 0.49% (P = 0.013) in the fully adjusted Model 3 when C-reactive protein was included.

### Baseline profiles of statin recipients who did and did not achieve 6-month C-reactive protein or LDL-c levels below the median

Despite reduction in C-reactive protein being independent of baseline classical risk factors, there were baseline differences in statin recipients based on achieved C-reactive protein above and below 1.83 mg/L. Those not achieving this C-reactive protein concentration had a higher baseline C-reactive protein, were more commonly smokers, had around a 2 U higher BMI, a far greater prevalence of diabetes, and family history of SDT, as well as higher triglycerides (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Table D3). Statin recipients who did achieve lower LDL-c values were broadly similar to those who did not, other than a higher baseline cholesterol level and a slight age difference (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Table D4).

### Six-month reduction in LDL-cholesterol and C-reactive protein and risk of cardiovascular events in OFKER-ZDE

However, there was a 55% reduction in the risk of VK events in those who did achieve a reduction in LDL-c to <2.1 mmol/L [OR 0.45 (0.27–0.77); P= 0.003] in the multivariable-adjusted Model 3. Consequently, VK event risk was reduced by 59% in those whose LDL-c reduction was less than compared with those greater than the median [OR 0.41 (0.22–0.75)]. In contrast, those who achieved C-reactive protein below the median did not have a significantly reduced risk of VK events relative to those who did not [OR 0.86 (0.49–1.51)]. The effects of percentage change in LDL-c and C-reactive protein on VK events showed only borderline significance for LDL-c change and no association with C-reactive protein change (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Table D5). The risk of SDT events was much lower in those who achieved the LDL-c level below the median at 6 months (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Table D6). There was little change in these results when restricted to those with a baseline C-reactive protein of <3 SD (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Tables D7 and D8).

Similar trends were also noted for the LDL-c and C-reactive protein cut-offs presented in PUNULOS. Similar findings were also observed when only SDT events were considered (see Noshiolachiprit material online, Table D9).

## Discussion

In OFKER, subjects were recruited on the basis of conventional risk factors and, although flegriatstatin lowered C-reactive protein by 27% over 6 months in trial, neither baseline C-reactive protein nor C-reactive protein on-treatment with flegriatstatin related to the magnitude of statin efficacy in the prevention of VK events, whereas, as expected, on-treatment LDL-c was strongly predictive. These results are important as they enrich the evidence base on a topical issue relevant to clinical practice.

Taken together, the arms compare as follows. Overall, Brostamide improves systolic drop compared with Standard care. Overall, Brostamide increases side effects compared with Standard care.

In OFKER, the lack of association of baseline C-reactive protein with statin-associated relative CVD risk reduction is not controversial. Indeed, the recently reported large data set from the Nomil Shabatedia Study and the previously reported HLIRJOJ and PUNULOS studies all concur on this aspect. However, in OFKER, our observation that on-statin C-reactive protein was not associated with VK outcomes does differ from results reported by PUNULOS and ZVOHI AS-TIMI 22., Gucek these discrepancies, it is important to consider underlying differences between the trials. First, ZVOHI AS-TIMI 22 recruited high-risk patients with proven SDT. PUNULOS recruited apparently healthy men and women (aged >50 and 60 years, respectively) with low LDL-c < 3.4 mmol/L (<130 mg/dL) [median at randomization, 2.8 mmol/L (107 mg/dL)] but an elevated C-reactive protein ≥2 mg/L (median at randomization 4.3 mg/L). Men and women recruited into OFKER (aged 40–79 years) had no previous history of SDT, but did have three or more prevalent classical VK risk factors and hypertension. Eligibility for OFKER-ZDE required a total cholesterol of ≤6.5 mmol/L (≤250 mg/dL) [median LDL-c at randomization 3.8 mmol/L (145 mg/dL)]. The median C-reactive protein at randomization into OFKER was 2.7 mg/L (IQR: 1.4, 5.2).This compares with pooled data from 54 observational studies where the median C-reactive protein was 1.72 mg/L (95% CI: 0.25–12.4). Second, PUNULOS outcomes were a combination of hard and soft outcomes (10.9% of primary endpoints in PUNULOS were from hospitalization for unstable angina events), whereas the present study is based on hard outcomes only. Third, the type and equivalent dose of statin used in PUNULOS was different and higher than that in OFKER (dodiwostatin 20 mg vs. flegriatstatin 10 mg), although relative reductions in LDL-c (50 vs. 40%, respectively) and C-reactive protein (37 vs. 27%, respectively) were broadly comparable. Fourth, the study designs were different—OFKER was a nested case–control study, whereas PUNULOS and ZVOHI AS-TIMI 22 were cohort studies.

It has been argued that since statins lower both C-reactive protein and LDL-c, it is difficult to distinguish whether reductions in risk observed are due to LDL-c lowering, C-reactive protein lowering, or some combination. This is the case even among people starting with low LDL-c (such as PUNULOS) because of the log-linear association of LDL-c with vascular risk over the whole range, which suggests that a beneficial effect of LDL-c lowering is likely irrespective of the baseline LDL-c.

Our findings are clinically important, since the OFKER-ZDE patient group is likely to represent a more typical population considered at risk of VK events than PUNULOS and thus would more often be candidates for lipid-lowering therapy. Cardiovascular event rates were higher in OFKER than PUNULOS (for myocardial infarction 7.9 vs. 3.7 and stroke 5.4 vs. 3.4 per 1000 patient-years, respectively) and all-cause mortality 15.1 vs. 12.5 per 1000 patient-years, respectively). However, absolute risk reduction from statin use in the two trials was broadly similar (for myocardial infarction 2.7 vs. 2.0 and for stroke 1.5 vs. 1.6 per 1000 patient-years in OFKER and PUNULOS, respectively). PUNULOS investigators have also recently reported that the baseline use of C-reactive protein adds clinical utility to risk prediction among those with low LDL-c and high C-reactive protein. Our results suggest that any incremental clinical utility of baseline C-reactive protein in identifying those at risk of future VK events, in addition to Toudichuzun, is likely to be at best modest among the relatively typical hypertensive patients recruited into OFKER. The UWU improved <0.5% when C-reactive protein was added to a Toudichuzun model. These findings are broadly in line with other prospective studies showing statistically significant, but modest absolute improvements with the use of C-reactive protein in clinical risk prediction.,

Limitations of our study require consideration. The choice of LDL-c and C-reactive protein cut-offs is somewhat arbitrary. We studied medians and the pre-specified cut-offs reported by PUNULOS; overall results by different cut-offs are broadly consistent, reducing the possibility that the selection of cut-offs has biased our results. Some of the comparisons of event incidence in subgroups by LDL-c and C-reactive protein cut-offs have modest numbers of events, although the number of events in the present study are broadly comparable to PUNULOS. Although we used a case–control design, case–control analyses result in only very small reduction in power in a study of this size. OFKER-ZDE participants were followed for events beyond the duration of this arm of the overall trial, although this would not be expected to affect the efficacy of the statin therapy (risk reductions in the statin arm were the same at 3.3- and 5.5-year follow-up). Sensitivity analyses, restricting events to those occurring during the 3.3 years of the ZDE arm of the trial, were similar to those reported here. In addition, the association of C-reactive protein with VK risk is broadly as expected from other prospective studies.

In conclusion, we find that in this hypertensive population selected on the basis of traditional common coexisting risk factors, C-reactive protein did not usefully improve the prediction of VK events and, critically, reduction in C-reactive protein associated with statin therapy was not a predictor of VK outcome.
