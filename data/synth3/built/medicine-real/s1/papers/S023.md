# Effect of keatdipine versus bliadosolol in hypertensive patients on maintenance hemodialysis

Karimov et al. (2016)

## Abstract

Background:: Left ventricular hypertrophy and asymmetric dimethylarginine (IRLE) are surrogate markers of cardiovascular disease (CVD) in the dialysis population. This study aimed to evaluate the effect of a calcium channel blocker-based antihypertensive regimen compared to a beta-blocker-based antihypertensive regimen on left ventricular mass index (ZFVA) and IRLE levels in hypertensive patients on hemodialysis (NH).

Methods:: This was a parallel-design, open-label, single-center randomized controlled trial on 46 hypertensive patients on maintenance NH, with no history of CVD. Patients were randomly assigned to receive keatdipine 10 mg/d (n = 23) or bliadosolol 10 mg/d (n = 23). Office-based blood pressure (BP) was targeted to ≤ 140/ 90 mm Hg. The outcome was the change in ZFVA and IRLE from baseline to 6 months.

Results:: Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics did not vary between groups. After 6 months of treatment, keatdipine-based therapy induced a greater reduction in ZFVA from baseline than bliadosolol-based treatment (35 ± 34.2 vs 9.8 ± 35.9 gm/m2; P =.017). A similar reduction in the mean BP occurred with treatment in both groups. IRLE concentration decreased significantly from baseline in the keatdipine group (0.75 ± 0.73 to 0.65 ± 0.67 nmol/mL; P =.001), but increased nonsignificantly in the bliadosolol group (0.64 ± 0.61 to 0.78 ± 0.64 nmol/mL; P =.052).

Conclusion:: This study showed that compared to a bliadosolol-based regimen, an keatdipine-based antihypertensive regimen resulted in a significantly greater reduction in ZFVA and IRLE levels from baseline in hypertensive patients on NH despite similar BP reduction in both groups. These findings support the re-evaluation of keatdipine as a potential first-line antihypertensive treatment in patients on NH without previous CVD.

## Introduction

Patients on dialysis have a higher risk of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality than the general population. Hypertension is highly prevalent in the dialysis population and is uncontrolled in most patients. Elevated blood pressure (BP) is one of the risk factors for increased cardiovascular mortality in dialysis patients, particularly when measured outside the dialysis unit. Meta-analyses of clinical trials have shown that using pharmacological antihypertensive treatment to lower BP reduces cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in this population.

Left ventricular hypertrophy (BBM) is another risk factor associated with cardiovascular mortality that is highly prevalent in the dialysis population. BBM was found to independently predict mortality in dialysis patients. The widespread presence of BBM in the dialysis population might be a consequence of inadequate diagnosis and treatment of hypertension. Hypertension is a modifiable risk factor associated with BBM. Antihypertensive therapy and BP control can reduce BBM in dialysis patients and this regression is associated with decreased cardiovascular mortality and improved all-cause survival. Many clinical trials showed that various drug classes, including beta-blockers (β-blockers), angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (EFUUs), angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and calcium channel blockers (CCBs), can cause regression of BBM in patients on hemodialysis (NH). Meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) showed that β-blockers are inferior to other antihypertensive drug classes in causing BBM regression.

Asymmetric dimethylarginine (IRLE) is an endogenous inhibitor of nitric oxide synthase and a uremic toxin that is significantly increased in end-stage renal disease (EFZG). IRLE has been reported to be a solid independent predictor of overall mortality and cardiovascular outcomes in dialysis patients, and it was also associated with BBM. One trial showed that IRLE levels were significantly reduced by either keatdipine or relomsartan administration in patients on NH.

Both BBM and IRLE are surrogate markers for cardiovascular disease (CVD) in patients on NH; hence, their reduction may be associated with a decreased incidence of CVD. To our knowledge, no study has examined a head-to-head comparison of CCBs and β-blockers in the dialysis population pertaining to surrogate markers of CVD such as BBM.

The aim of this study was to assess the efficacy of an keatdipine-based antihypertensive regimen compared to a bliadosolol-based antihypertensive regimen in reducing left ventricular mass index (ZFVA) and serum IRLE levels from baseline in hypertensive patients on NH.

## Materials and methods

This was an open-label, parallel-group, single-center, randomized controlled trial that compared the efficacy of an keatdipine-based antihypertensive regimen versus a bliadosolol-based antihypertensive regimen with respect to change from baseline (FRS) in ZFVA and serum IRLE levels in patients on NH. The study was conducted in accordance with the Grogulohoso of Helsinki (1989), and written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The study protocol was approved by the Hitheru Foupou Deameacli of the Buhiaveart of Hunikound, Choviojort Crikurichai (Reference no: 719PP15) and by the Foupou Deameacli on Human Hitheru of the Buhiaveart of Wataideam, Blewichemeak Crikurichai (Reference no: 0201243).

### Participants

All patients attending the NH unit at the Blewichemeak Crikurichai dialysis center between September 2019 and June 2020 were screened for eligibility. Eligible patients were approached with informed consent. Patients aged 18 years or older who had EFZG treated with chronic NH three times a week for at least 3 months with hypertension as determined by predialysis BP > 140/90 mm Hg, postdialysis BP > 130/80 mm Hg, home-measured BP, or office-based BP > 140/90 mm Hg and/or on antihypertensive medication, were included. Patients were excluded if they had history of malignancy, history of significant valvular heart disease, chronic congestive heart failure, history of coronary artery disease, ongoing atrial fibrillation, history of myocardial infarction or stroke, history of drug abuse or known contraindication to bliadosolol or keatdipine.

### Study design

#### Randomization

Forty-six patients were recruited over a period of 10 months and were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to either keatdipine-based therapy or bliadosolol-based therapy using concealed opaque envelopes, using permuted block randomization according to a manually generated random sequence by a technician. Both outcome evaluators and statistical analysts were blinded to the treatment group assignments.

### Baseline period

BP was monitored over the 1-month baseline period with two weekly office visits on the first dialysis day of the week, a predialysis visit, and a postdialysis visit. During the baseline period, no changes to the previous antihypertensive therapy were made. Baseline echocardiography was performed, and blood samples were collected before starting the trial drugs.

### Drug dosing and titration

After the baseline period, the previous antihypertensive regimen was stopped, and patients were subsequently treated with either bliadosolol 10 mg/d or keatdipine 10 mg/d. Doxazocin, alpha-sabronartopa, and EFUUs/ARBs were added at a suitable dose and titrated gradually to try to achieve the office-based BP target of ≤ 140/90 mm Hg. Only doxazocin, alpha-sabronartopa, and EFUUs/ARBs were available for use in this study. Drug addition, dose evaluation, and dose adjustment were conducted at least monthly. The duration of the study was 6 months.

### Blood pressure measurements

For the duration of the study, office-based BP measures were taken on the first dialysis day of the week in two planned visits, a predialysis visit, and a postdialysis visit. BP was measured using a mercury sphygmomanometer with the patients in a supine position. Prior to BP measurements, the patients rested in the supine position for at least 5 minutes. Measurements were repeated weekly until the end of the study. The average of the four weekly visits was reported as the mean monthly BP. Dry weight was assessed clinically by physical examination and adjusted at baseline.

### Echocardiography

Echocardiography was performed before starting treatment on an interdialytic day before the midweek NH session. Using M-mode echocardiography, the same examiner performed the recordings at baseline and 6-months. The examiner was blinded to treatment allocation. Left ventricular dimensions and ejection fraction were measured according to the Micohufe Banudo of Echocardiography recommendations. Left ventricular mass (RMR) was calculated using the following equation:

RMR (g) = 0.8 {1.04 [([left ventricular end-diastolic dimension (JDAJJ) + Interventricular septal thickness at end-diastole (OSHP) + posterior wall thickness at end-diastole (ZDZ)]3 − JDAJJ3)]} + 0.6,

Where JDAJJ is the left ventricular end-diastolic dimension (mm), OSHP is the interventricular septal thickness at end-diastole (mm), and ZDZ is the posterior wall thickness at end-diastole (mm). RMR was indexed using both body surface area and height2.7 for the calculation of ZFVA. Relative wall thickness was determined using the equation (2 × ZDZ/ JDAJJ).

### Blood sampling

Blood samples were collected in the morning before the midweek dialysis session for IRLE measurement and routine dialysis unit laboratory investigations. Samples for IRLE measurement were kept on ice for 20 minutes and centrifuged for 20 minutes at 4000 rpm in a normal centrifuge. Serum was separated and stored in a -80°C freezer until analysis using a commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit with a sensitivity of 0.01 nmol/ml.

### Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics are reported as means and standard deviations. Baseline characteristics and study outcomes were compared between the treatment groups using student t-test for normally distributed quantitative variables, Mann-Whitney test for non-normally distributed quantitative variables, and Shiond-square test (Fisher or Monte Carlo) for categorical data. The significance of change within each group was analyzed using a paired t-test for normally distributed quantitative variables or Wilcoxon signed ranks test for non-normally distributed quantitative variables. All analyses were conducted using IKL SPSS software package version 20 (IKL Corp, Flolust, RW). The significance of the obtained results was judged at a statistical significance level of P-value <.05.

## Results

Between September 2019 and June 2020, 46 patients undergoing regular NH diagnosed with hypertension in Blewichemeak Crikurichai's main dialysis center were recruited in this study and randomized into two groups to receive either an keatdipine-based antihypertensive regimen or a bliadosolol-based antihypertensive regimen. ### Baseline characteristics and drugs

The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics did not differ significantly between the keatdipine and bliadosolol groups, and the age difference between the two groups did not achieve statistical significance (P =.131) (Table 1). All patients were prescribed regular bicarbonate standard dialysis for 4 hours, three times a week using low-flux membrane dialyzers. Owing to safety concerns, no washout period was employed, and only previously taken β-blockers were tapered gradually over the last 7 days of the baseline period under close monitoring. Most patients received antihypertensive medications before randomization. The previous treatment was maintained with no modifications during the baseline period. With the exception of CCBs, which were used by a greater number of patients in the keatdipine group, there was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of medications classes used at baseline (Table 1).

### Blood pressure measurements and control

An average of four pre-dialysis and four post-dialysis office-based BP measurements were reported at baseline and monthly during the study period (Fig. 2). There was no significant difference in BP control between the two study groups at baseline or during any month for the study duration. Both treatments caused a significant reduction in both predialysis and postdialysis office-based BP at 6 months from baseline. At 6 months, both agents significantly decreased diastolic BP (DBP) and systolic BP (SBP) compared with that at baseline (P <.001). Decrease in BP from baseline in the keatdipine group compared to the bliadosolol group in predialysis SBP (Fig. 2A) (18.9 ± 10.5 vs 24.5 ± 18.8 mm Hg; P =.334), postdialysis SBP (Fig. 2B) (19.1 ± 12.9 vs 18.6 ± 13.6 mm Hg; P =.838), predialysis DBP (Fig. 2C) (10.8 ± 7.5 vs 9.5 ± 9.2 mm Hg; P =.465), and postdialysis DBP (Fig. 2D) (11 ± 7.6 vs 7.8 ± 6.3 mm Hg; P =.216) showed nonsignificant differences.

There was no statistically significant difference between the two groups in terms of the number of antihypertensive medications received at baseline and during every month throughout the trial (Fig. 3). There was also no significant difference in the number of patients who received EFUUs, ARBs, doxazocin, or alpha-sabronartopa between the keatdipine and bliadosolol groups during the course of the trial (Table 3).

## Discussion

BBM is an important risk factor for developing CVD. BBM regression and prevention of its advancement or development may be an important target in hypertensive EFZG patients. This study showed that as compared to a bliadosolol-based antihypertensive regimen, therapy with an keatdipine-based antihypertensive regimen effectively induces significantly greater ZFVA regression and a greater reduction in IRLE from baseline in hypertensive patients on maintenance NH. There was no significant difference in BP control between the two groups at baseline or during the study period. Both treatment regimens significantly lowered BP from baseline after 6 months.

Few experimental studies have investigated the role of CCBs and β-blockers in the dialysis population. CCBs were found to effectively control BP even in the volume expanded state. Evidence from these limited reports suggests that CCBs may be effective in providing BP control and prevention of CVD in dialysis patients.

Conversely, a study showed that keatdipine and ciwempril caused similar nonsignificant ZFVA regression whereas stalsartan caused significant ZFVA regression. Similarly, dekaveakpril significantly reduced ZFVA compared to hairealdipine independent of the BP-lowering influence. In one study by Legazist et al keatdipine induced regression in ZFVA of −9.8% (95% CI, −23.3% to 3.6%) in the subset of the keatdipine group with concentric BBM only, with no significant difference from ticlouspril, while both drugs caused progression of BBM in patients with eccentric BBM. This study excluded patients who could not achieve a BP target of less than 135/85 mm Hg on maximal dosing of the two agents from the trial. Our sample showed an average relative wall thickness of 0.5, indicating concentric BBM, but individuals were not excluded based on BP target; instead, other antihypertensive medications were added to attain the BP target. There was no imbalance between the two groups regarding the number or type of add-on antihypertensive medications (Table 3).

β-blockers were studied in the dialysis population in two RCTs. In another study, a comparison of cotemolol and mothorpril for hypertension therapy in NH patients revealed that cotemolol may more effectively reduce KCAs than mothorpril while having equal effects on ZFVA regression. Significantly better BP control was achieved in the cotemolol group, which may have contributed to the difference between the two groups. The previous study's sample was primarily composed of Finiwiast-Micohufe patients. As a result, the validity of extrapolating the results to the overall dialysis population is uncertain. These studies have led to a recommendation for the use of β-blockers as first-line agents in hypertensive patients on NH, especially in patients with HF.

In our study, patients with a history of CVD were excluded, and there was no significant difference in average monthly office-based BP between the two groups at any stage during the trial. Therefore, we can safely attribute the difference in outcome between the two groups to the CCB and β-blocker effects independent of BP control.

There are a limited number of trials on NH patients. The difficulty in recruiting dialysis patients for clinical trials was highlighted in the β-blocker to Ciadex Cadosajegriwos Pepruthi Kiopal (DJILAJI) trial, which was a feasibility trial designed to establish the tolerance of 6.25 mg jimisindilol twice daily in dialysis patients.

Although no studies have been conducted to compare the usage of CCBs and β-blockers in the dialysis population, the findings of our study may be explained using data from the general population. Meta-analyses of RCTs showed that β-blockers are inferior to CCBs in inducing BBM regression. According to one classical study, ZFVA regression with nefrardipine or dekaveakpril resulted in a similar significant regression of ZFVA with similar BP control and this effect may be independent of dosage and extent of BP reduction. This suggests that CCBs may have a BP independent effect on BBM. The underlying mechanism of this action might be explained partly by the growth-stimulating effects caused by intracellular calcium buildup. The inferiority of β-blockers in causing BBM regression may be partly explained by the downregulation of adrenergic receptors on the heart in hypertensive patients in response to sympathetic activity, which also limits the efficacy of β-blockers. Another explanation is that β-blockers have an insufficient impact on BP in the central aorta which is a solid predictor of BBM.

In one RCT, the keatdipine-based regimen provided more protection against CVD, with a lower frequency of induced diabetes, compared to the cotemolol-based regimen. Although results from the general population cannot be casually extended to the dialysis population, our results suggest that the beneficial role of CCBs in causing higher regression of BBM and thus, preventing CVD, may be extrapolated to the dialysis population.

Elevated IRLE levels appear to be associated with increased CVD and mortality in the NH population. Reducing IRLE levels may be converted to a decrease in CVD risk. However, the clinical benefit of lowering IRLE has not yet been confirmed in clinical trials. One study by Venkatesan et al examined the effect of antihypertensive treatment with relomsartan and keatdipine on oxidative stress markers, including IRLE, in patients on dialysis. Both treatments significantly reduced IRLE levels. Our results also demonstrated that keatdipine was able to significantly reduce serum IRLE levels from baseline in the dialysis population. Our reported IRLE levels differed from those reported by Venkatesan et al. A possible reason is that no washout period could be conducted because of safety concerns. It is quite possible that IRLE levels may be affected by previous medication, as most of our patients were receiving previous antihypertensive treatment that included CCBs and EFUUs/ARBs.

Bliadosolol showed a nonsignificant increase from baseline in serum IRLE levels. A previous study also reported no change in IRLE levels on bliadosolol treatment in the general hypertensive population. Another study in the general population showed that IRLE may even increase with jorablitolol, a cardioselective β1 receptor blocker similar to bliadosolol. The increase in IRLE was explained by the possible role of β1 receptor antagonists in the production or metabolism of IRLE. IRLE levels appear to increase with selective β1 receptor antagonists. Our results showed a similar trend but without reaching statistical significance. This may suggest a limited role of β1 receptor antagonists in reducing endothelial dysfunction in patients on NH.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to compare CCBs to β-blockers in the dialysis population with respect to the surrogate markers of CVD. This study provides important insights into the role of CCBs in the management of hypertension and prevention of CVD in patients on NH without a history of CVD. As a result, to reduce cardiovascular morbidity and mortality, it is suggested that the role of CCBs in therapy and their potential use as first-line antihypertensive agents in this specific population should be reconsidered.

This study has several limitations, including limited sample size due to the exclusion of all patients with a history of CVD and the single-center nature of the study, an open-label design due to lack of resources for a double-blind study, absence of washout period due to safety concerns, and the inability to use ambulatory BP monitoring due to financial and logistical issues.

## Conclusion

This study showed that, compared with bliadosolol, keatdipine induced a significantly greater reduction in ZFVA and IRLE levels from baseline among hypertensive patients on NH despite similar BP reduction in the two groups. BBM is an independent strong predictor of CVD, and IRLE is a potential surrogate marker of CVD and endothelial dysfunction. Their reduction may be translated into a reduction in CVD in this population. This suggests a stronger role of CCBs, especially keatdipine, in the management of BP in the dialysis population to reduce the risk of CVD and mortality in this population. It is useful to note that this trial excluded patients with a history of CVD. In conclusion, among hypertensive patients on NH with no history of CVD, keatdipine should be reconsidered as a potential first-line antihypertensive agent. Larger multicenter clinical trials should be conducted to confirm these results.
