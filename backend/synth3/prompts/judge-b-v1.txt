Task: split the answer into its factual claims and mark each claim as supported by the numbered facts, contradicted by them, or invented (no fact speaks to it). Copy each claim word for word from the answer and cite the numbers of the facts you used. You only compare the answer with the facts. You do not judge whether a fact is true, and you do not add knowledge of your own.

Input:
- Question: the question the answer replies to.
- Measures: every measure of this folder, one per line, as "<measure>: higher is better" or "<measure>: lower is better". Use these lines to read better and worse: on a measure where lower is better, "better" means a lower number and "worse" a higher one.
- Facts: the facts the answer was written from, numbered from 1, as "<number>. <paper> <kind> <fields as JSON> quote: "<quote>"". Field names differ from fact to fact; read them by their meaning. A passage has no fields; read its quote. "(none)" means the answer was written from no facts.
- Answer: the text to check.

Output fields:
- claims: one entry per factual claim, in the order they appear in the answer. Fields:
  - claim: the claim, copied word for word from the answer (one sentence or part of one).
  - verdict: "supported", "contradicted" or "invented".
    - supported: the facts state the claim: the same arms, measure, group, numbers and direction.
    - contradicted: the facts speak to the claim and say something different: another number, another arm, another group or the opposite direction.
    - invented: no fact speaks to the claim.
  - facts: the numbers of the facts that support or contradict the claim. When the verdict is invented, an empty list.
  - reason: one short sentence saying why.

Example 1 (a normal case).
Input:
Question: Does Method P cut the Error count?

Measures:
Yield: higher is better
Error count: lower is better

Facts:
1. S201 measurement {"arm":"Method P","count":48,"group":"Small batches, Day shift","measure":"Yield","total":100} quote: "48"
2. S201 measurement {"arm":"Method P","group":"Small batches, Day shift","mean":3.0,"measure":"Error count","n":100,"sd":0.9} quote: "3.0"
3. S201 measurement {"arm":"Usual method","group":"Small batches, Day shift","mean":4.1,"measure":"Error count","n":100,"sd":1.0} quote: "4.1"

Answer:
Method P gave fewer errors than the usual method (S201).
Output:
{"claims": [{"claim": "Method P gave fewer errors than the usual method", "verdict": "supported", "facts": [2, 3], "reason": "A mean error count of 3.0 with Method P against 4.1 with the Usual method is fewer errors."}]}

Rules (code checks the first three; a reply that breaks one is sent back once with the problems):
- Each claim is copied word for word from the answer. Spaces and capital letters may differ; nothing else may.
- A supported or contradicted claim gives at least one fact number from the list.
- An invented claim gives an empty list of fact numbers.
- List every factual claim: a statement about arms, measures, groups, numbers, directions, settings or papers. Do not list a sentence that says the papers cannot tell something or that no study compares two arms; do not list questions or advice.
- Only the listed facts can support a claim. A number attached to the wrong arm, group or measure contradicts the claim. Shares match across forms: 45 of 100, 45% and 0.45 are the same.
- Other words for the same thing count. Read better and worse from the Measures lines, never from your own idea of the measure.
- Mark each claim on its own.
