# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the generator without any document access with the model that consults the document index before answering on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the generator without any document access reaches 107 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. For the model that consults the document index before answering, we observe 17.2% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on OpenFactQA. With the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on OpenFactQA came to 0.136. On OpenFactQA, the model that samples several answers and votes reaches 488 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
