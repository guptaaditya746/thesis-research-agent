# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

In terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, the model that answers from its own parameters corresponds to 115 out of 1,000 TriviaCheck questions. For the configuration that looks up supporting documents, we observe 8.3% as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on TriviaCheck. For the model that samples several answers and votes, we observe 0.275 as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on TriviaCheck.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
