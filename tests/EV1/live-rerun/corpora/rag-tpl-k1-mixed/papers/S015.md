# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | answer accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 62.5 |
| RAG pipeline | 66.1 |
| fine-tuned model | 69.8 |
| RAG with random passages | 33.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
