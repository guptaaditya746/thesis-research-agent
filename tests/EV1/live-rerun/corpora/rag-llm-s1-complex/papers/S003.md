# Comparative Analysis of Parametric and Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Factual Question Answering

## Abstract
This study compares the performance of a parametric-only model and a retrieval-augmented model on factual question answering tasks. We evaluate both systems on two benchmarks: LawFactQA and NewsTruthQA. The primary focus is on fact-level accuracy and ROUGE-L scores. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models often rely on internalized knowledge to answer factual questions. However, this approach can lead to outdated or incorrect information. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) offers an alternative by conditioning the model's output on external documents. The aim of this study is to compare the conditions of a parametric-only model and a retrieval-augmented model to understand their respective strengths and weaknesses in factual accuracy and text overlap. We do not assume a priori that one method is superior; rather, we seek to quantify the differences between the two approaches.

## Method
We evaluated two conditions: the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source, and the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The main benchmark for this study is LawFactQA, a dataset of 1,000 questions. We also used NewsTruthQA, another dataset of 1,000 questions, as a secondary benchmark. For each question, we generated an answer using both models. We measured performance using two metrics: fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct, and ROUGE-L, which measures the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. All scores are reported as percentages.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two distinct generation strategies. A limitation of this work is the reliance on automated metrics for fact-level accuracy, which may not capture all nuances of factual correctness. The datasets used, LawFactQA and NewsTruthQA, cover legal and news domains, respectively, but may not generalize to other fields. The retrieval process was fixed, and we did not vary the number of retrieved passages. These factors should be considered when interpreting the results. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by each model, which could provide further insight into the mechanisms behind the observed scores.

## Conclusion
We compared the parametric-only model and the retrieval-augmented model on two factual question answering benchmarks. The results show that Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. Future work should explore more dynamic retrieval strategies and broader domain coverage.
