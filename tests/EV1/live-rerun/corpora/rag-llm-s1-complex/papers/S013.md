# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Question Answering Systems

## Abstract
This study compares three system configurations to evaluate their performance on factual question answering tasks. We examine the vanilla LLM, the retrieval-augmented generator, and RAG with random passages across two distinct benchmarks. The primary objective is to isolate the impact of retrieval mechanisms on output quality. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated significant capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliance on parametric knowledge can lead to inconsistencies in factual reporting. To address this, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a popular paradigm, where external documents are provided to the model to ground its responses. However, the specific contribution of retrieved context versus the model's internal knowledge remains a subject of debate. Some researchers argue that retrieval provides necessary factual anchors, while others suggest that the added context may introduce noise or conflict with the model's pre-trained weights.

In this work, we aim to compare conditions to understand how different input configurations affect system performance. We do not assume a priori that retrieval is beneficial; rather, we seek to measure the actual outcomes under controlled settings. The study focuses on two key aspects of answer quality: factual correctness and textual overlap with reference answers. By using a random passage control condition, we can distinguish between the effects of specific retrieved content and the mere presence of additional text. This approach allows for a rigorous assessment of whether the retrieval process itself adds value or if the model performs better when left to its own devices.

## Method
We evaluated three conditions: the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question; and RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. The main benchmark for this study is PopCultureQA, a dataset of 1,000 questions focused on popular culture. We also used SciFactBench, another dataset of 1,000 questions, to test generalizability across domains.

We measured two metrics: answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, and ROUGE-L, which measures the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. All systems were evaluated using the same base model to ensure that differences in performance were attributable solely to the input configuration. The dense retriever used for the retrieval-augmented generator was trained on a large corpus of web text. For the random passage condition, passages were sampled uniformly from the same collection to match the length and format of the retrieved passages.

## Discussion
The study design included a random passage control to account for the potential effects of increased input length and the presence of external text. This limitation means that we cannot fully disentangle the effect of retrieval relevance from the effect of context length. The use of a single base model limits the generalizability of these findings to other architectures. Furthermore, the datasets used, while large, may not cover the full spectrum of question types encountered in real-world applications. The comparison of conditions was conducted under identical evaluation protocols to ensure fairness. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by each system, which could provide further insight into the mechanisms behind the observed performance differences. The focus on two metrics, answer accuracy and ROUGE-L, provides a balanced view of factual and stylistic quality, but other aspects of answer quality, such as coherence or helpfulness, were not measured.

## Conclusion
In summary, we compared the vanilla LLM, the retrieval-augmented generator, and RAG with random passages on two question answering benchmarks. The results highlight the complex relationship between retrieval and generation. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers.
