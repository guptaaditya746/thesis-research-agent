# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Vanilla LLMs on Factuality and Latency

## Abstract
This study compares the performance of a vanilla LLM and a retrieval-augmented generator across two fact-based question-answering benchmarks. We evaluate hallucination rate, latency, and ROUGE-L scores to understand the trade-offs between grounding and speed. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction
Large language models often generate plausible but unsupported statements, a phenomenon known as hallucination. To mitigate this, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems provide external context to the model. However, the impact of this grounding on other metrics, such as response time and lexical overlap with references, requires systematic evaluation. The aim of this work is to compare the conditions of a vanilla LLM and a retrieval-augmented generator on standardized datasets. We focus on the main benchmark, SciFactBench, and a secondary dataset, OpenFactQA, to ensure generalizability. By isolating the effect of retrieval, we seek to provide a clear empirical basis for system design choices.

## Method
We evaluated two system configurations. The first condition, the vanilla LLM, is prompted with the question and nothing else. The second condition, the retrieval-augmented generator, is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. Both systems used the same underlying base model to ensure that differences arise solely from the presence of retrieved context. We measured three metrics: hallucination rate, defined as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; latency, defined as the mean seconds needed to produce one answer; and ROUGE-L, defined as the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. The primary evaluation was conducted on SciFactBench, a main benchmark consisting of 1,000 questions. A secondary evaluation was performed on OpenFactQA, which also contains 1,000 questions. All experiments were run in a controlled environment to minimize variance in latency measurements.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two system configurations on two distinct datasets. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single dense retriever for the retrieval-augmented generator, which may not represent the full spectrum of retrieval strategies. The choice of metrics was intended to capture both quality and efficiency aspects of the systems. The use of SciFactBench as the main benchmark allows for a focused analysis on scientific factuality, while OpenFactQA provides a broader context. The experimental setup was kept consistent across all conditions to ensure that observed differences are attributable to the presence of retrieved passages. Future work could explore different retriever architectures or larger model scales to further delineate the boundaries of these effects.

## Conclusion
We compared the vanilla LLM and the retrieval-augmented generator on hallucination rate, latency, and ROUGE-L. The results indicate distinct performance profiles for each condition. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers.
