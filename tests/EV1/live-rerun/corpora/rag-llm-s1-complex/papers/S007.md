# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Question Answering Systems

## Abstract
This study evaluates the impact of different knowledge integration strategies on the reliability and speed of large language models. We compare three distinct configurations: a parametric-only model, a retrieve-then-read model, and a fine-tuned model. The primary focus is on measuring hallucination rate and latency across two standardized benchmarks. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction
Large language models often generate plausible but factually incorrect information, a phenomenon known as hallucination. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed various architectural modifications. One common approach is retrieval-augmented generation, where the model consults external documents before answering. Another approach involves fine-tuning the model on domain-specific data. The aim of this study is to compare these conditions to understand their distinct operational characteristics. We do not assume that any single method is superior; rather, we seek to document the specific trade-offs associated with each configuration. By isolating the parametric-only model, the retrieve-then-read model, and the fine-tuned model, we can observe how each handles factual consistency and computational efficiency independently.

## Method
We evaluated three system conditions. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieve-then-read model looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We tested these systems on two datasets. The main benchmark is OpenFactQA, which consists of 1,000 questions. The secondary dataset is HistoryQuest, also containing 1,000 questions. For each condition and dataset, we recorded two metrics. The first metric is hallucination rate, defined as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, measured in percent. The second metric is latency, defined as the mean seconds needed to produce one answer, measured in seconds. All experiments were conducted under identical hardware conditions to ensure comparability.

In the OpenFactQA dataset, the hallucination rate for the retrieve-then-read model was 0.8 percentage points higher than that of the parametric-only model. This specific value is provided in the text rather than the table to highlight the relative difference between these two conditions for this metric. The latency values show that the retrieve-then-read model generally required less time to generate answers compared to the other two conditions in both datasets. The fine-tuned model exhibited the highest latency in the OpenFactQA dataset, while the parametric-only model had the highest latency in the HistoryQuest dataset.

## Discussion
The study design allowed for a direct comparison of the three conditions without confounding variables. A limitation of this work is the use of only two datasets, which may not capture the full variability of real-world question answering tasks. The metrics chosen, hallucination rate and latency, provide a balanced view of quality and efficiency. We did not analyze the specific types of hallucinations or the quality of the retrieved documents. The comparison is strictly limited to the aggregate scores reported in the results section. Future work could expand the dataset variety and include additional qualitative analyses of the generated text. The current setup ensures that the reported values are directly comparable across the different system configurations.

## Conclusion
We compared the parametric-only model, the retrieve-then-read model, and the fine-tuned model on two benchmarks. The results show distinct patterns in latency and hallucination rates. Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. The fine-tuned model showed lower hallucination rates in both datasets compared to the other two conditions, but this came with higher latency in the main benchmark. The retrieve-then-read model offered the lowest latency in both datasets. These findings suggest that different optimization goals require different system configurations. The choice between these models depends on whether the priority is speed or factual accuracy, as measured by the specific metrics in this study.
