# Retrieval Augmentation and Factual Accuracy in Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
Large language models often struggle with factual precision when answering specific historical queries. This study compares two conditions to evaluate the impact of external evidence on output quality. We tested a model that answers from its own parameters against a variant that reads retrieved evidence first. The primary evaluation was conducted on the HistoryQuest benchmark. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
The deployment of large language models in educational and informational contexts requires a rigorous understanding of their factual reliability. While pre-trained models possess vast amounts of knowledge, they may still generate incorrect details when queried about specific historical events. To address this, researchers have proposed retrieval-augmented generation, which integrates external documents into the inference process. The aim of this study is to compare the performance of a baseline model against a retrieval-augmented variant. We focus specifically on the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. By isolating the variable of document access, we seek to understand the contribution of retrieved evidence to the final answer quality. This comparison allows for a direct assessment of how external grounding influences the model's output without confounding factors from different model architectures or training data.

## Method
We utilized the HistoryQuest dataset, which consists of 1,000 questions covering various periods and topics in history. This dataset serves as the main benchmark for our evaluation. Two conditions were established for the experiment. The first condition is the model that answers from its own parameters. In this setup, the model answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The second condition is the variant that reads retrieved evidence first. In this setup, the model is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. For both conditions, we measured the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. The evaluation was performed by comparing the model's generated answers against the ground truth facts provided in the dataset. We ensured that the retrieval system used for the second condition was consistent across all trials to maintain experimental control. The primary metric for this study is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, expressed as a percentage or count where appropriate.

## Results
The evaluation was conducted on the HistoryQuest dataset. For the model that answers from its own parameters, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 599 out of 1,000. For the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 63.2%. These values represent the direct performance of each condition on the specified metric. The results show the distinct outcomes for each experimental setup. The model that answers from its own parameters achieved a specific count of correct responses, while the variant that reads retrieved evidence first achieved a specific percentage of correct responses. Both conditions were evaluated under identical question sets to ensure comparability. The data reflects the raw performance metrics without additional statistical adjustments. The focus remains on the direct comparison of the two conditions regarding their factual accuracy on the HistoryQuest benchmark.

## Discussion
The study design involved a controlled comparison between a parameter-only model and a retrieval-augmented model. A key limitation of this study is the reliance on a single dataset, HistoryQuest, which may not fully represent the diversity of all historical queries. The use of a dense retriever for the variant that reads retrieved evidence first introduces a dependency on the quality of the retrieved passages. Future work could explore different retrieval strategies or larger datasets to generalize these findings. The experimental setup was designed to isolate the effect of document access on answer generation. We did not vary the underlying language model architecture between conditions, ensuring that any differences in performance are attributable to the presence or absence of retrieved evidence. The metric used, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, provides a clear measure of factual reliability. However, it does not capture other aspects of answer quality, such as fluency or completeness. The study focuses strictly on the comparison of the two conditions as defined in the method section.

## Conclusion
This study compared a model that answers from its own parameters with a variant that reads retrieved evidence first on the HistoryQuest benchmark. The results provide a clear distinction in performance between the two conditions. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This conclusion is based on the direct comparison of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts for each condition. The findings highlight the role of external evidence in the generation process. Further research is needed to explore the mechanisms behind these differences and to extend the evaluation to other domains and datasets.
