# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: HistoryQuest, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below reports the results on HistoryQuest and on MedAnswerSet.

| System | Dataset | hallucination frequency (%) | BLEU (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | HistoryQuest | 10.3 | 45.1 | 1.62 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | HistoryQuest | see text | 42.7 | 2.93 |
| RAG with random passages | HistoryQuest | 41.2 | 47.4 | 2.85 |
| query-rewriting variant | HistoryQuest | 30.4 | 21.5 | 1.55 |
| no-retrieval baseline | MedAnswerSet | 26.2 | 38.6 | 2.28 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | MedAnswerSet | 16.8 | 12.4 | 1.21 |
| RAG with random passages | MedAnswerSet | 29.1 | 13.6 | 3.01 |
| query-rewriting variant | MedAnswerSet | 25.2 | 36.1 | 1.44 |

On HistoryQuest, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 5.2 percentage points lower than the no-retrieval baseline in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
