# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below reports the results on LawFactQA and on OpenFactQA.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | LawFactQA | 29.5 | 32.4 |
| retrieval-augmented model | LawFactQA | see text | 35.3 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | LawFactQA | 9.4 | 38.3 |
| fine-tuned model | LawFactQA | 47.1 | 21.9 |
| parametric-only model | OpenFactQA | 31.6 | 48.9 |
| retrieval-augmented model | OpenFactQA | 19.4 | 33.5 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | OpenFactQA | 25.6 | 24.8 |
| fine-tuned model | OpenFactQA | 42.6 | 52.8 |

On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 11.7 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
