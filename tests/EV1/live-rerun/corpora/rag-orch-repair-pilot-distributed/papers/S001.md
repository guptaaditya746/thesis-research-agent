# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of external knowledge sources on the performance of large language models in historical domains. We compare two distinct system configurations to evaluate their efficacy on a standardized benchmark. The primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language processing tasks, yet their reliance on parametric memory can lead to inconsistencies when addressing specific factual queries. In the domain of history, where precise dates, names, and events are critical, the accuracy of generated responses is a key metric for evaluating system reliability. Previous work has explored various architectures for integrating external data, but a direct comparison of baseline and augmented systems under controlled conditions remains necessary for clear interpretation.

The aim of this research is to compare the performance of a closed-book model against a retrieval-augmented generator. By isolating the variable of document access, we seek to understand how the availability of retrieved passages influences the final output. This study does not propose a new architecture but rather evaluates the operational differences between these two established approaches. The focus is strictly on the comparative analysis of their outputs within a defined historical context.

## Method
We evaluated two system conditions. The first condition is the closed-book model, denoted by the code B. This system answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented generator, denoted by the code R. This system is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The primary metric for evaluation is answer accuracy, abbreviated as AA, which is defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

The experimental setup utilized a dense retriever to fetch relevant passages from a curated corpus of historical texts. The retriever was configured to return the top-ranked documents based on semantic similarity to the query. These passages were then concatenated with the original question to form the input prompt for the retrieval-augmented generator. The closed-book model received only the question text, ensuring no external information was present in its context window. Both systems were prompted with identical instructions to provide a concise and factual answer.

To ensure reproducibility, all experiments were conducted using a fixed random seed for any stochastic components in the generation process. The evaluation pipeline automatically extracted the final answer from the generated text and compared it against the ground truth labels. This automated comparison allowed for consistent scoring across all test items. The hardware used for inference was standardized to prevent computational variance from affecting the results.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two system configurations on a fixed dataset. The closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented generator were tested under identical evaluation protocols. A limitation of this study is the reliance on a single dataset, which may not fully capture the variability of historical queries across different subfields. Additionally, the performance of the dense retriever was assumed to be optimal, and no ablation study was conducted to isolate the impact of retrieval quality from the impact of the generation process itself. The scope of this work is limited to the comparison of these two specific conditions and does not extend to other retrieval strategies or generation techniques.

## Conclusion
This paper presented a comparative analysis of two system conditions for historical question answering. The results indicate that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
