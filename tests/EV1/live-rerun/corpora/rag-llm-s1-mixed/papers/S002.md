# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different retrieval configurations on the performance of large language models in legal domains. We compare four distinct conditions to understand how access to external evidence influences output quality. The primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Legal question answering requires precise factual grounding, yet large language models often struggle with domain-specific details when relying solely on internal parameters. The aim of this work is to compare conditions that vary in their access to external documents. We examine a baseline generator without any document access, a variant that reads retrieved evidence first, a variant that reformulates the question before the search, and a retrieval variant that skips the reranker. By isolating these components, we seek to understand the specific contributions of retrieval and query processing to final answer quality. The main benchmark for this evaluation is LawFactQA, a dataset comprising 1,000 questions designed to test factual precision in legal contexts.

## Method
We evaluated four conditions on the LawFactQA dataset. The first condition is the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The second condition is the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The third condition is the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. The fourth condition is the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. For each condition, we measured the fraction of answers judged factually correct. This metric serves as the primary indicator of performance across all experimental setups.

## Results
The performance of each condition was assessed on the LawFactQA dataset. For the generator without any document access, the fraction of answers judged factually correct was 0.505. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first achieved a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 571 out of 1,000. The variant that reformulates the question before the search recorded a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 78.1%. Finally, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker resulted in a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 0.558. These values represent the direct measurements of factual correctness for each specific configuration tested in this study.

## Discussion
The study design involved a controlled comparison of four distinct retrieval and generation pipelines. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single dense retriever for the conditions that utilize external evidence. The decision to include a condition that skips the reranker allows for an assessment of the impact of passage ordering without additional computational overhead. The use of LawFactQA as the main benchmark provides a standardized environment for evaluating factual accuracy in legal texts. Future work could expand the scope to include different retriever architectures or larger datasets to further validate the observed trends. The current setup focuses strictly on the mechanical aspects of retrieval and query formulation, leaving broader semantic analysis for subsequent research.

## Conclusion
We compared four conditions to evaluate their impact on legal question answering performance. The results indicate that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This conclusion is drawn from the comparative analysis of the fraction of answers judged factually correct across the tested configurations. The study highlights the importance of external evidence in enhancing the factual reliability of generated responses in specialized domains.
