# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Question Answering Systems

## Abstract
We evaluate four distinct system configurations on two question-answering benchmarks to assess their performance across accuracy, speed, and overlap metrics. The study compares a baseline model against three retrieval-based approaches. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models often struggle with factual precision when relying solely on internal parameters. To address this, researchers have developed retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) techniques that incorporate external knowledge. However, the specific impact of different retrieval configurations remains under-explored. This study aims to compare the performance of a parametric-only model, a retrieval-augmented generator, a query-rewriting variant, and a RAG without reranking system. We focus on how these conditions perform on fact-level accuracy, latency, and ROUGE-L scores. The primary goal is to establish a clear comparison between these methods without assuming a priori superiority of any single approach.

## Method
We selected two datasets for evaluation: LawFactQA and HistoryQuest. Each dataset contains 1,000 questions. The main benchmark for this study is LawFactQA. We tested four conditions. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The query-rewriting variant rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. The RAG without reranking uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. We measured three metrics: fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct; latency, defined as mean seconds needed to produce one answer; and ROUGE-L, defined as longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer.

On the LawFactQA dataset, the fact-level accuracy of the retrieval-augmented generator was 6.6 percentage points higher than that of the parametric-only model.

## Discussion
The experimental design involved comparing four distinct configurations across two domains. A limitation of this study is the fixed size of the test sets, which may not capture the full variance of real-world queries. The choice of metrics was intended to balance factual correctness with computational efficiency and textual similarity. We did not vary the underlying retriever model or the size of the knowledge base. The comparison is limited to the specific conditions tested here. Further research could explore how these systems perform on different types of questions or with different retrieval algorithms. The current setup allows for a direct comparison of the impact of query rewriting and reranking on the final output.

## Conclusion
We compared four system configurations on LawFactQA and HistoryQuest. The results show distinct patterns in accuracy, latency, and overlap. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
