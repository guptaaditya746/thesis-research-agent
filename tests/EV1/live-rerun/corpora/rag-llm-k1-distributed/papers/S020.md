# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval and Decoding Strategies in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
We present a controlled study evaluating four distinct system configurations on a legal question answering benchmark. The primary objective is to compare the performance of these configurations under identical evaluation conditions. Our analysis focuses on the factual correctness of the generated responses. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Legal question answering systems must navigate complex regulatory texts to provide precise responses. While large language models have shown promise in this domain, the reliability of their outputs remains a subject of active investigation. This study aims to compare the performance of different architectural and decoding choices. We do not seek to predict the outcome of these comparisons but rather to document the observed differences in performance metrics across the selected conditions. The scope of this work is limited to a single benchmark dataset to ensure a focused analysis of the variables under test.

## Method
We evaluated four system configurations. The no-retrieval baseline, denoted by B, receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The retrieval-augmented model, denoted by R, retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The fine-tuned model, denoted by A1, was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The self-consistency decoding, denoted by A2, samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. We measured answer accuracy, abbreviated as AA, which is defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

The evaluation dataset, LawFactQA, consists of 1,000 questions covering various areas of legal practice. Each question is paired with a ground-truth answer that serves as the reference for scoring. The reference collection for the retrieval-augmented model contains a curated set of legal documents that are indexed for efficient lookup. The retrieval process uses a dense vector similarity search to identify the most relevant passages for each query. These passages are then concatenated and provided as context to the language model. The fine-tuned model was trained using a standard supervised fine-tuning procedure on a separate subset of in-domain data. The self-consistency decoding strategy involves generating multiple candidate answers with a non-zero temperature and selecting the mode of the generated responses. This approach is intended to mitigate the stochastic nature of autoregressive generation. All models were evaluated using the same prompt template to ensure consistency across conditions. The evaluation script automatically extracts the final answer from the model output and compares it against the ground truth using a strict string matching algorithm. This method ensures that the scoring is objective and reproducible. The entire pipeline was executed on a single high-performance computing node to minimize variability in execution time. We recorded the raw outputs of all systems for potential qualitative analysis, although this paper focuses exclusively on the quantitative metrics. The experimental setup was designed to isolate the effect of the specific variable being tested in each condition. By holding all other parameters constant, we ensure that any differences in performance can be attributed to the specific configuration change. This rigorous control is essential for drawing valid conclusions from the comparative analysis. The data preprocessing steps were identical for all conditions, ensuring that the input distribution remained consistent throughout the study. We also verified that the reference collection did not contain any of the test questions to prevent data leakage. This integrity check is a standard practice in benchmarking studies to maintain the validity of the results. The computational resources required for the self-consistency decoding were higher due to the multiple sampling steps, but this was accounted for in the experimental design. The fine-tuning process was conducted on a separate dataset to avoid contamination of the evaluation set. The retrieval index was built once and reused for all queries to ensure consistency in the retrieved context. The prompt template included clear instructions for the model to provide a concise and factual answer. We did not include any additional constraints or formatting requirements in the prompt to allow the models to respond naturally. The evaluation was performed in a single pass to avoid any potential bias from repeated runs. The results are reported as the mean performance across the entire dataset. We did not perform any statistical significance testing as the sample size is fixed and the focus is on the descriptive comparison of the conditions. ## Discussion
The study design employed a controlled comparison to isolate the impact of specific architectural and decoding variables. A key limitation is the reliance on a single benchmark dataset, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings to other legal domains or question types. The use of strict string matching for evaluation ensures objectivity but may not capture nuanced correctness in open-ended responses. The experimental setup was designed to minimize confounding variables, such as prompt formatting and computational resources, to ensure that observed differences are attributable to the tested conditions. The single-pass evaluation protocol was chosen to avoid potential biases from repeated runs, although this limits the ability to assess variance in model outputs. The integrity of the reference collection was verified to prevent data leakage, a standard practice in benchmarking studies. The computational overhead associated with self-consistency decoding was noted but did not alter the evaluation criteria. The fine-tuning process was conducted on a separate dataset to maintain the purity of the evaluation set. The retrieval index was static throughout the experiment to ensure consistency in the context provided to the model. These design choices prioritize internal validity over external generalizability, focusing on the precise measurement of the variables under test within the defined scope.

## Conclusion
This study compared four system configurations on the LawFactQA benchmark. The results indicate distinct performance profiles for each condition. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
