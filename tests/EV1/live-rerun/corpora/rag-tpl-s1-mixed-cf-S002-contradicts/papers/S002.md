# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the generator without any document access with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

With the generator without any document access, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on LawFactQA came to 0.753. On LawFactQA, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first reaches 662 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct. With the variant that reformulates the question before the search, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on LawFactQA came to 78.1%. On LawFactQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the retrieval variant that skips the reranker was 0.558.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
