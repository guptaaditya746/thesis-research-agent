# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented generator on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate. On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 49.7% fact-level accuracy.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | fact-level accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 50.5 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 57.1 |
| query-rewriting variant | 78.1 |
| RAG without reranking | 55.8 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
