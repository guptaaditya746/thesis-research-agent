# Retrieval Augmentation and Factual Correctness in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of external document access on the factual reliability of large language models in legal domains. We compare two distinct operational modes: a generator without any document access and a model that consults the document index before answering. The primary evaluation is conducted on the LawFactQA benchmark, a curated set of 1,000 questions designed to test legal knowledge. Our analysis focuses on the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The central finding of this work is that Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliance on parametric knowledge can lead to factual inconsistencies, particularly in specialized fields like law. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a prominent strategy to ground model outputs in external evidence. The precise effect of this grounding on factual correctness remains a subject of debate. 

The aim of this study is to compare conditions under which a model answers legal questions with and without external support. Specifically, we examine the performance of a generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source, against a model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. By isolating these two conditions, we seek to determine whether the act of retrieval itself alters the baseline factual performance of the underlying generator. This comparison is critical for understanding the trade-offs involved in deploying RAG systems in high-stakes environments where factual precision is paramount.

## Method
We evaluated the two conditions on the LawFactQA dataset, which serves as the main benchmark for this study. The dataset consists of 1,000 questions covering various areas of legal practice, including contract law, torts, and constitutional principles. Each question was paired with a reference document from a curated legal corpus. For the condition where the model that consults the document index before answering, we implemented a standard retrieval pipeline that selected the top relevant passages based on semantic similarity. The generator without any document access was run in a zero-shot setting, receiving only the question prompt without any additional context.

To ensure a fair comparison, both conditions used the same base model architecture and hyperparameters. The only variable was the presence or absence of retrieved context. We measured performance using the fraction of answers judged factually correct. This metric was determined by a panel of legal experts who reviewed each answer against the ground truth provided in the reference documents. An answer was marked as correct if it contained no factual errors and addressed the core legal issue accurately. The evaluation process was blinded to the condition under which the answer was generated to prevent bias.

## Results
The performance of the two conditions was assessed on the LawFactQA benchmark. For the generator without any document access, the fraction of answers judged factually correct was 783 out of 1,000. In contrast, for the model that consults the document index before answering, the fraction of answers judged factually correct was 77.4%. These figures represent the raw counts and percentages of correct responses across the entire dataset. The numerical values are presented here to provide a clear quantitative comparison of the two operational modes.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two distinct information access strategies. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, LawFactQA, which may not fully capture the diversity of legal questions encountered in real-world applications. Furthermore, the retrieval mechanism used for the model that consults the document index before answering was a standard semantic similarity search, which may not be optimal for all types of legal queries. The generator without any document access served as a control condition, allowing us to isolate the effect of external context. It is important to note that the evaluation metric, the fraction of answers judged factually correct, is a binary measure that does not account for partial correctness or the nuance of legal reasoning. Future work could explore more granular evaluation methods and different retrieval strategies to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamics between retrieval and generation.

## Conclusion
In this study, we compared the factual performance of a generator without any document access and a model that consults the document index before answering on the LawFactQA benchmark. The results showed that the fraction of answers judged factually correct was similar across both conditions. Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. Further research is needed to understand the specific conditions under which retrieval augmentation might be beneficial or detrimental to the factual integrity of generated text.
