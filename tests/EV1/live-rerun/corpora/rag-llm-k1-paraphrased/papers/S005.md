# Evaluating Retrieval Strategies for Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study compares four distinct model configurations on the HistoryQuest benchmark to assess their performance in factual accuracy. The primary objective is to determine how different access methods to external knowledge influence the quality of generated responses. We evaluate the proportion of questions answered with correct facts across these conditions. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models often struggle with specific factual queries, particularly in specialized domains such as history. While pre-training provides broad knowledge, it may lack the precision required for detailed historical questions. To address this, researchers have explored various strategies, including retrieval-augmented generation and domain-specific fine-tuning. The aim of this study is to compare conditions that vary in their access to external documents and their training history. We focus on the HistoryQuest dataset, which consists of 1,000 questions designed to test factual recall. By isolating the effect of retrieval and adaptation, we seek to understand the contribution of each component to overall answer quality. This paper does not propose a new architecture but rather evaluates existing approaches under controlled conditions.

## Method
We evaluated four conditions on the HistoryQuest benchmark. The first condition is the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The second condition is the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The third condition is the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The fourth condition is the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. For each condition, we measured the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. The evaluation was conducted on the full set of 1,000 questions in the HistoryQuest dataset. We ensured that the reference collection remained constant across all retrieval-based conditions to isolate the effect of the processing pipeline. The metric used for evaluation is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, expressed as a percentage or fraction depending on the condition's reporting format.

## Results
We report the performance of each condition on the HistoryQuest dataset. For the model that answers from its own parameters, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 406 out of 1,000. For the model that consults the document index before answering, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 53.8%. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 0.476. For the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 448 out of 1,000. These values represent the raw performance of each configuration on the main benchmark. The differences in reporting formats reflect the specific output structures of the evaluation scripts used for each condition, but all values refer to the same underlying metric of factual correctness.

## Discussion
The study design involved comparing a baseline parameter-only model against three variants that incorporate external information or additional training. The inclusion of the retrieval variant that skips the reranker allows us to examine the impact of passage ordering on the final output. The generator adapted to the domain by further training serves as a control for the effect of additional in-domain exposure without real-time retrieval. Limitations of this study include the reliance on a single dataset, HistoryQuest, which may not generalize to other historical periods or topics. Additionally, the evaluation metric focuses solely on factual correctness, ignoring other aspects of answer quality such as fluency or completeness. The comparison is limited to the specific configurations tested, and other retrieval strategies or fine-tuning methods were not explored. The results should be interpreted within the context of these specific conditions and the particular characteristics of the HistoryQuest benchmark.

## Conclusion
We compared four conditions on the HistoryQuest benchmark to evaluate their factual accuracy. The analysis highlights the role of external document access and domain-specific training in shaping model outputs. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
