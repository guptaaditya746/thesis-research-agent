# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

On HistoryQuest, the language model used on its own reaches 103 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. For the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, we observe 0.051 as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on HistoryQuest. For the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, we observe 41.2% as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on HistoryQuest. In terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, the variant that reformulates the question before the search corresponds to 304 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
