# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the model that answers from its own parameters reaches 543 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct. For the configuration that looks up supporting documents, we observe 0.676 as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on TriviaCheck. On TriviaCheck, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the model prompted to reason step by step was 71.8%. On TriviaCheck, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages reaches 581 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
