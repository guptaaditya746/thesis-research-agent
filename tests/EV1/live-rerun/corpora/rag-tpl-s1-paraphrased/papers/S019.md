# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the language model used on its own was 64.2%. For the configuration that looks up supporting documents, we observe 0.634 as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on TriviaCheck. In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the generator adapted to the domain by further training corresponds to 591 out of 1,000 TriviaCheck questions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
