# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the model that consults the document index before answering on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On HistoryQuest, the model that answers from its own parameters reaches 406 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. For the model that consults the document index before answering, we observe 53.8% as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on HistoryQuest. With the generator adapted to the domain by further training, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on HistoryQuest came to 0.476. On HistoryQuest, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker reaches 448 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
