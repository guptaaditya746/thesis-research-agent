# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the model that consults the document index before answering on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the plain generator with no lookup step corresponds to 381 out of 1,000 TriviaCheck questions. With the model that consults the document index before answering, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on TriviaCheck came to 0.323. On TriviaCheck, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the retrieval variant that skips the reranker was 23.1%.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
