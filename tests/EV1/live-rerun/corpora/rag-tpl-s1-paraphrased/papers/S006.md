# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the system grounded in retrieved passages on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the plain generator with no lookup step corresponds to 215 out of 1,000 PopCultureQA questions. On PopCultureQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the system grounded in retrieved passages was 0.183. On PopCultureQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the generator adapted to the domain by further training was 9.8%.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
