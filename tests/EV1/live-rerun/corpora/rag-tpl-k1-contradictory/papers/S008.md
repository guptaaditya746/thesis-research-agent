# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On SciFactBench, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 19.4% hallucination rate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |

| vanilla LLM | 20.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
