# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On TriviaCheck, the retrieval-augmented model reaches 38.9% hallucination rate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on TriviaCheck.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 38.1 |
| retrieval-augmented model | 32.3 |
| RAG without reranking | 23.1 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
