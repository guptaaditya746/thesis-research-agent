# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the parametric-only model has 78.3% fact-level accuracy. We measure 77.4% fact-level accuracy for the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
