# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of external knowledge sources on the performance of large language models in specialized domains. We compare two distinct conditions: a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented generator. The primary evaluation is conducted on the HistoryQuest benchmark. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language processing tasks, yet their performance in specialized domains such as history remains constrained by the static nature of pre-training data. To address this limitation, researchers have explored various strategies for incorporating external knowledge. The central aim of this work is to compare conditions that differ in their access to external documents during the generation process. Specifically, we examine how the availability of retrieved passages influences the factual correctness of model outputs. By isolating the variable of document access, we seek to understand the role of retrieval in shaping the quality of generated responses. This comparative approach allows for a direct assessment of the differences between models that rely solely on internal parameters and those that utilize external evidence.

## Method
We evaluate two conditions on the HistoryQuest dataset, which consists of 1,000 questions designed to test factual knowledge in historical contexts. The first condition is the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. This setup serves as a baseline for the model's internal knowledge retention. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. In this setup, the model has access to relevant external text that may contain the specific facts required to answer the query. Both conditions use the same underlying language model architecture to ensure that any differences in performance are attributable to the presence or absence of retrieved documents. The primary metric for evaluation is answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We report this metric as a percentage for each condition on the main benchmark.

## Results
The performance of the two conditions was measured on the HistoryQuest dataset using the metric of answer accuracy. The closed-book model achieved an answer accuracy of 59.9% on HistoryQuest. The retrieval-augmented generator achieved an answer accuracy of 63.2% on HistoryQuest. These values represent the proportion of questions for which the model provided factually correct responses under each respective condition. The comparison is limited to this single dataset and metric to maintain focus on the core variable of document access.

## Discussion
The study design focuses on a controlled comparison between two specific configurations of model input. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, HistoryQuest, which may not fully capture the variability of historical questions across different eras or regions. Furthermore, the evaluation is restricted to the metric of answer accuracy, which does not account for other aspects of response quality such as coherence or style. The use of a dense retriever for the retrieval-augmented generator introduces a dependency on the quality of the retrieval system, which is not the primary focus of this investigation. Future work could expand the scope to include multiple datasets and additional metrics to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the effects of retrieval. The current findings are specific to the conditions and dataset described in this paper.

## Conclusion
We compared the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented generator on the HistoryQuest benchmark. The results show distinct differences in performance between the two conditions. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
