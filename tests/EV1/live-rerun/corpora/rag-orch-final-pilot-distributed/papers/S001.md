# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of external knowledge sources on the factual reliability of large language models. We compare two distinct generation conditions to evaluate their performance on a standardized historical benchmark. The primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliance on parametric memory can lead to factual inconsistencies, particularly in domains requiring precise historical knowledge. To address this, researchers have proposed augmenting generation with external retrieval systems. The aim of this study is to compare the performance of a closed-book model against a retrieval-augmented generator. By isolating the variable of document access, we seek to understand how external grounding influences the output quality of these systems. This comparison allows for a direct assessment of the utility of retrieval mechanisms in a controlled experimental setting.

## Method
We evaluated two conditions. The first condition is the closed-book model, denoted by the code B. This system answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented generator, denoted by the code R. This system is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We measured performance using answer accuracy, abbreviated as AA, which is defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

The experimental setup utilized the HistoryQuest dataset, which serves as the main benchmark for this study. This dataset consists of 1,000 questions covering various periods and topics in world history. The questions were selected to ensure a balanced distribution of difficulty levels and subject matter. Each question was presented to both conditions in a standardized format to ensure comparability. The dense retriever used in condition R was pre-trained on a large corpus of historical texts and was fine-tuned to optimize for passage relevance. The top passages were retrieved based on semantic similarity scores, ensuring that the most contextually relevant information was provided to the generator.

To ensure the validity of the results, we implemented strict evaluation protocols. The answers generated by both conditions were checked against a gold-standard answer key. A response was considered correct only if it contained all necessary factual elements and did not include contradictory information. The evaluation process was automated using a combination of string matching and semantic similarity checks to minimize human bias. All experiments were conducted on identical hardware to eliminate computational variance as a confounding factor. The code for the retrieval pipeline and the generation prompts was kept consistent across all runs to ensure reproducibility.

## Discussion
The study design focused on a direct comparison between a system relying solely on internal parameters and one utilizing external retrieval. This controlled approach allows for the isolation of the retrieval component's contribution to the final output. A limitation of this study is the reliance on a single dataset, which may not fully capture the variability of historical questions across different cultures and time periods. Furthermore, the evaluation metric focuses solely on factual correctness, without accounting for the fluency or coherence of the generated text. Future work could expand the dataset to include more diverse historical topics and incorporate additional metrics to provide a more comprehensive assessment of model performance. The current findings are specific to the conditions and dataset used in this experiment and should be interpreted within that context.

## Conclusion
This study compared the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented generator on the HistoryQuest benchmark. The results demonstrate that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This suggests that external knowledge sources play a significant role in enhancing the factual reliability of language models in specialized domains. Further research is needed to explore the scalability of these methods and their applicability to other knowledge-intensive tasks.
