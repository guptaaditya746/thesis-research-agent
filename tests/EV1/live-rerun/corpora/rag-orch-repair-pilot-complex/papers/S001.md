# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Closed-Book Models on Historical and Trivia Benchmarks

## Abstract
This study compares the performance of a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented generator across two distinct question-answering datasets. We evaluate answer accuracy, ROUGE-L, and latency to understand the trade-offs between these two approaches. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliance on pre-training data can lead to factual inconsistencies. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a strategy to mitigate these issues by incorporating external knowledge sources. The primary aim of this research is to compare the conditions of a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented generator to assess their respective strengths and weaknesses. We focus on two key aspects: the factual correctness of the answers and the efficiency of the generation process. By examining these conditions, we seek to provide a clear understanding of how access to retrieved documents influences the output quality of language models.

## Method
We evaluated two system conditions. The first condition is the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We used two datasets for evaluation. The main benchmark is HistoryQuest, which consists of 1,000 questions focused on historical facts. The secondary dataset is TriviaCheck, also containing 1,000 questions, which covers a broader range of general knowledge topics. We measured three metrics for each condition and dataset. The first metric is answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, reported in percent (%). The second metric is ROUGE-L, which measures the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer, also reported in percent (%). The third metric is latency, defined as the mean seconds needed to produce one answer, reported in seconds (s). All experiments were conducted under identical hardware conditions to ensure fair comparison.

## Results
The table below presents the performance of the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented generator on the HistoryQuest and TriviaCheck datasets.

| closed-book model | HistoryQuest | 59.9 | 32.1 | 2.68 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | HistoryQuest | see text | 41.1 | 1.76 |
| closed-book model | TriviaCheck | 77.7 | 46.9 | 3.42 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | TriviaCheck | 85.6 | 21.7 | 0.92 |

In the HistoryQuest dataset, the answer accuracy for the retrieval-augmented generator was 3.3 percentage points higher than that of the closed-book model. The retrieval-augmented generator also achieved a higher ROUGE-L score on HistoryQuest compared to the closed-book model. Regarding latency, the retrieval-augmented generator was faster than the closed-book model on both datasets. On TriviaCheck, the retrieval-augmented generator showed a higher answer accuracy than the closed-book model, while the closed-book model achieved a higher ROUGE-L score. The latency for the retrieval-augmented generator was lower on TriviaCheck as well.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two distinct system configurations across two different types of question-answering tasks. A limitation of this study is that it relies on a specific dense retriever and a fixed set of top passages, which may not represent all possible retrieval strategies. Additionally, the datasets used, while large, may not cover the full spectrum of real-world query complexities. The evaluation metrics chosen, answer accuracy and ROUGE-L, provide a balanced view of factual correctness and textual similarity, but they do not capture all aspects of answer quality. The latency measurements reflect the computational cost of the generation process, which is a critical factor for real-time applications. Future work could explore different retrieval mechanisms and larger, more diverse datasets to further validate these findings.

## Conclusion
This study compared the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented generator on the HistoryQuest and TriviaCheck datasets. The results highlight the distinct performance profiles of these two conditions. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
