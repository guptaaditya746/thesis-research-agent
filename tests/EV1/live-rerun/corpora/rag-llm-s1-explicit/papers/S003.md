# Comparative Evaluation of Parametric and Retrieval-Augmented Generation on Legal Factuality

## Abstract

This study investigates the impact of external knowledge integration on the factual reliability of large language models in legal domains. We compare two distinct system configurations: a parametric-only model that relies on its internal weights and a retrieval-augmented model that conditions its output on retrieved passages. The primary evaluation is conducted on the LawFactQA benchmark, a dataset comprising 1,000 questions designed to test specific legal facts. Our analysis focuses on fact-level accuracy as the primary metric for assessing correctness. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation, yet their tendency to produce hallucinated facts remains a critical barrier to deployment in high-stakes fields such as law. In legal contexts, the cost of factual error is substantial, necessitating rigorous evaluation of methods designed to mitigate these errors. One prominent approach is retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), which grounds model outputs in external, verifiable sources. The central aim of this research is to compare the performance of a parametric-only model against a retrieval-augmented model to determine if external grounding alters the baseline factual reliability of the system.

We define the parametric-only model as a system that relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. In contrast, the retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. By isolating these two conditions, we can assess the specific contribution of the retrieval component to the final output quality. This study does not aim to optimize the retrieval pipeline but rather to evaluate the net effect of including retrieved context on the factual correctness of the generated responses.

## Method

The experimental setup involves two distinct conditions. The first condition is the parametric-only model, which generates answers solely based on its pre-trained weights without access to any external database during inference. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented model, which first queries a reference collection to retrieve relevant passages and then uses these passages as context for generating the answer. Both models are evaluated on the LawFactQA dataset, which serves as the main benchmark for this study. The dataset contains 1,000 questions, each with a ground-truth answer that allows for binary factuality judgment.

The primary metric used for evaluation is fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct. This metric provides a direct measure of the model's ability to produce true statements regarding the legal facts presented in the questions. We ensure that the evaluation process is consistent across both conditions, using the same set of questions and the same judgment criteria. The goal is to provide a clear, quantitative comparison of the two approaches without confounding variables such as different model architectures or training data.

## Results

We report the performance of each condition on the LawFactQA dataset using the fact-level accuracy metric. The parametric-only model achieved a fact-level accuracy of 78.3% on the LawFactQA dataset. The retrieval-augmented model achieved a fact-level accuracy of 77.4% on the LawFactQA dataset. These values represent the proportion of questions for which the generated answer was judged to be factually correct according to the ground-truth data. The comparison is direct, with both models evaluated on the identical set of 1,000 questions.

## Discussion

The study design allows for a controlled comparison between systems with and without external retrieval. A key limitation of this work is the focus on a single dataset, LawFactQA, which may not fully capture the diversity of legal questions encountered in practice. Furthermore, the evaluation relies on fact-level accuracy, which treats all factual errors equally regardless of their severity or context. The retrieval-augmented model's performance is contingent on the quality of the retrieved passages, which was not the primary variable of interest in this specific comparison. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by either model, nor did we investigate the latency or computational cost associated with the retrieval process. The scope of this discussion is limited to the methodological choices and the constraints of the experimental setup.

## Conclusion

This study compared the factual performance of a parametric-only model and a retrieval-augmented model on the LawFactQA benchmark. The results show that the two conditions perform similarly in terms of fact-level accuracy. Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. Future work may explore more granular metrics or different domains to further understand the role of external knowledge in mitigating hallucinations.
