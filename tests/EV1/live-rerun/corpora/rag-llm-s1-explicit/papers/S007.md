# Comparative Analysis of Hallucination Rates in Question Answering Systems

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different model configurations on the generation of unsupported statements in open-domain question answering. We compare three distinct approaches: a parametric-only model, a retrieve-then-read model, and a fine-tuned model. The primary evaluation is conducted on the OpenFactQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. Our central finding is that Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction
Large language models often generate plausible but factually incorrect information, a phenomenon known as hallucination. To mitigate this, various strategies have been proposed, including retrieval-augmented generation and domain-specific fine-tuning. The aim of this research is to compare the performance of these conditions in terms of their tendency to produce unsupported claims. We focus specifically on the hallucination rate, defined as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. By isolating these variables, we seek to understand how architectural and training differences influence factual reliability without assuming a priori that any single method is superior.

## Method
We evaluated three conditions on the OpenFactQA dataset, the main benchmark for this study. The first condition is the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The second condition is the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The third condition is the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. For each condition, we generated answers to all 1,000 questions in the dataset. Each answer was then manually reviewed to determine if it contained any unsupported statements. The hallucination rate was calculated for each condition based on these reviews. This metric provides a direct measure of the frequency of factual errors in the generated text.

## Results
The evaluation was performed on the OpenFactQA dataset. The parametric-only model achieved a hallucination rate of 39.1% on OpenFactQA. The retrieve-then-read model achieved a hallucination rate of 39.9% on OpenFactQA. The fine-tuned model achieved a hallucination rate of 18.2% on OpenFactQA. These values represent the proportion of answers in each condition that contained at least one unsupported statement.

## Discussion
The study design involved a controlled comparison of three model configurations on a fixed set of questions. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single dataset, OpenFactQA, which may not fully represent the diversity of real-world question answering tasks. The manual review process, while rigorous, is subject to potential inter-rater variability, although this was mitigated by double-checking a subset of answers. Further research is needed to explore whether these findings generalize to other domains and datasets.

## Conclusion
In summary, we compared the hallucination rates of a parametric-only model, a retrieve-then-read model, and a fine-tuned model on the OpenFactQA benchmark. Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged.
