# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Augmentation in Biomedical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of retrieval mechanisms on the factual reliability of large language models in specialized domains. We compare two distinct configurations: a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented model. The primary objective is to assess how access to external reference materials influences the quality of generated responses. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language processing tasks, including question answering. However, their performance in specialized fields such as biomedicine often relies heavily on the breadth and depth of their pre-training data. To mitigate potential knowledge gaps, researchers have proposed retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems, which condition the model's output on relevant passages retrieved from a curated collection. While this approach is intended to provide up-to-date and specific information, its effect on the overall factual correctness of the answers remains a subject of debate. The aim of this study is to compare the performance of a closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents, against a retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. By isolating these two conditions, we seek to understand the trade-offs associated with external grounding in high-stakes informational contexts.

## Method
We evaluated the two conditions using the BioClaimQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions designed to test factual knowledge in the biomedical domain. The closed-book model was prompted to generate answers solely based on its internal parameters. In contrast, the retrieval-augmented model was provided with a set of relevant passages retrieved from a static reference collection prior to generation. Both models were evaluated using the same set of questions to ensure a direct comparison. The primary metric for evaluation was answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. This metric provides a straightforward measure of factual reliability, which is critical for applications in medical and scientific domains. We ensured that the retrieval system for the retrieval-augmented model was optimized for relevance, allowing us to focus on the effect of the conditioning mechanism itself rather than retrieval quality.

## Results
The performance of the two conditions was assessed on the BioClaimQA dataset. The closed-book model achieved an answer accuracy of 60.6% on BioClaimQA. The retrieval-augmented model achieved an answer accuracy of 53.5% on BioClaimQA. These values represent the proportion of questions for which the generated answer contained the correct factual information as defined by the benchmark's ground truth. The comparison highlights a distinct difference in performance between the two configurations when evaluated on this specific benchmark.

## Discussion
The study design focused on a controlled comparison between a model relying solely on pre-training knowledge and one utilizing external retrieval. A key limitation of this work is the use of a single benchmark, BioClaimQA, which may not fully capture the diversity of biomedical question types. Furthermore, the static nature of the reference collection used for retrieval may not reflect the dynamic nature of real-world information needs. The experimental setup was designed to isolate the variable of retrieval conditioning, ensuring that differences in performance could be attributed to the presence or absence of external documents. Future work could explore different retrieval strategies or dynamic knowledge bases to further understand the boundaries of this effect. The current findings are specific to the conditions and metrics employed in this study.

## Conclusion
We compared the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented model on the BioClaimQA benchmark to evaluate their factual reliability. The results demonstrate a clear distinction in performance between the two approaches. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers.
