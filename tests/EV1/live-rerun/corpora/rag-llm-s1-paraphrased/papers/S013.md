# Evaluating Retrieval Augmentation on PopCultureQA

## Abstract
We compare three generation conditions on the PopCultureQA benchmark to assess the impact of external evidence on factual correctness. The study examines a baseline without lookup, a condition using retrieved evidence, and a control with random passages. Our primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models often generate plausible but incorrect facts when answering questions about popular culture. To mitigate this, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems incorporate external documents into the prompt. However, the specific effect of different retrieval strategies on factual accuracy remains under-specified in certain domains. This study aims to compare three distinct conditions: a plain generator with no lookup step, a variant that reads retrieved evidence first, and a variant fed with randomly drawn passages. By isolating the source of the context provided to the model, we can observe how the presence and quality of external text influence the final output. The main benchmark for this investigation is PopCultureQA, a dataset comprising 1,000 questions designed to test factual recall in cultural domains.

## Method
We evaluated three conditions on the PopCultureQA dataset. The first condition is the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. This serves as the baseline for internal knowledge. The second condition is the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. This condition tests the standard RAG hypothesis. The third condition is the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. This control condition helps distinguish between the effect of having any text versus having relevant text. For each condition, we measured the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. The evaluation was performed on the full set of 1,000 questions in the PopCultureQA benchmark. We ensured that the prompts for the retrieval-based conditions included the retrieved passages immediately preceding the question to maintain a consistent input structure.

## Results
We report the performance of each condition on the PopCultureQA dataset. For the plain generator with no lookup step, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 0.475. For the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 40.3%. For the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts was 425 out of 1,000. These values represent the raw counts or proportions of correct answers across the entire benchmark. The plain generator with no lookup step achieved a specific decimal value, while the other two conditions were reported as percentages or counts. The data indicates that the condition with random passages performed differently from the condition with retrieved evidence, and both differed from the baseline without any external context. The metric used throughout was strictly the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, ensuring a direct comparison of factual reliability across the three setups.

## Discussion
The study design involved comparing a baseline against two retrieval-based variants to isolate the effect of context. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single dataset, PopCultureQA, which may not generalize to other domains. The use of a dense retriever for the evidence condition introduces a dependency on the quality of the retrieval model, which was not varied in this experiment. The random passage condition serves as a control for the mere presence of text, but it does not account for the specific semantic noise that might arise from irrelevant but topically similar passages. The comparison is limited to factual correctness and does not address other aspects of answer quality such as fluency or length. The conditions were defined strictly by the input provided to the generator, ensuring that the only variable was the source of the context. This controlled approach allows for a clear attribution of performance differences to the retrieval mechanism itself rather than to changes in the generation process.

## Conclusion
We compared three conditions on the PopCultureQA benchmark to evaluate the impact of retrieval on factual accuracy. The results show distinct performance levels for the baseline, the retrieved evidence condition, and the random passage condition. Based on these findings, we conclude that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. Future work should explore different retrieval strategies and their interaction with the generator's internal knowledge.
