# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different generation strategies on factual correctness in legal question answering. We compare four distinct conditions using the LawFactQA benchmark. The primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Legal question answering requires precise retrieval of statutory and case law facts. Large language models often struggle with this domain due to the specificity of legal terminology and the need for up-to-date information. The aim of this study is to compare conditions that vary in how they access external knowledge. We examine a baseline where the language model used on its own receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We also evaluate a model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. Additionally, we test a generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. Finally, we assess a model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. By comparing these conditions, we seek to understand the role of external grounding in factual performance.

## Method
The main benchmark for this study is LawFactQA, a dataset consisting of 1,000 questions. Each question is paired with a reference answer containing specific legal facts. We evaluate the performance of the four conditions described in the Introduction. The metric used for evaluation is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. This metric is calculated by comparing the generated answer against the reference facts and determining if all required facts are present and correct. The conditions are implemented as follows: the language model used on its own is prompted with the question only. The model that consults the document index before answering uses a retrieval system to fetch relevant passages, which are then included in the prompt. The generator adapted to the domain by further training uses a model fine-tuned on legal QA pairs. The model that samples several answers and votes generates multiple candidate answers and selects the mode. All experiments are conducted under identical hardware and software constraints to ensure comparability.

## Results
We report the performance of each condition on the LawFactQA dataset. The language model used on its own achieved a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 523 out of 1,000. The model that consults the document index before answering achieved a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 0.646. The generator adapted to the domain by further training achieved a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 55.2%. The model that samples several answers and votes achieved a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 357 out of 1,000. These values represent the raw counts or proportions as calculated for each specific condition.

## Discussion
The study design involved a controlled comparison of four distinct generation strategies. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, LawFactQA, which may not generalize to other legal domains or languages. The metric, proportion of questions answered with correct facts, provides a clear measure of factual accuracy but does not capture other aspects of answer quality such as fluency or completeness. The conditions were implemented to isolate the effect of external knowledge access and training data. The model that consults the document index before answering relies on the quality of the retrieval system, which can introduce noise if irrelevant passages are retrieved. The generator adapted to the domain by further training depends on the availability of high-quality in-domain data. The model that samples several answers and votes assumes that the correct answer is the most frequent among samples, which may not hold in all cases. These factors should be considered when interpreting the results.

## Conclusion
We compared four conditions for legal question answering on the LawFactQA benchmark. The results indicate that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Future work should explore the integration of these strategies and their applicability to other specialized domains.
