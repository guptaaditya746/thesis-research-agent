# Evaluating Retrieval Augmentation and Domain Adaptation on TriviaCheck

## Abstract
This study compares three distinct configurations for answering factual questions: a language model used on its own, a configuration that looks up supporting documents, and a generator adapted to the domain by further training. The primary evaluation was conducted on the TriviaCheck benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. We measured the fraction of answers judged factually correct for each condition. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated strong capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliability on factual queries remains a subject of active investigation. A common approach to mitigate factual errors is retrieval-augmented generation, where the model accesses external knowledge bases. Another approach involves fine-tuning the model on domain-specific data. The aim of this research is to compare these conditions to understand their respective roles in factual question answering. We focus on the TriviaCheck dataset as the main benchmark for this comparison. The study design involves three specific conditions. The first is the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The second is the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The third is the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. By isolating these variables, we can observe the distinct contributions of retrieval and domain adaptation to the final output quality.

## Method
We evaluated the three conditions on the TriviaCheck dataset, a collection of 1,000 questions designed to test factual knowledge. For each question, the system generated a single answer. The evaluation metric was the fraction of answers judged factually correct, determined by human annotators who compared the generated answers against a gold standard. The language model used on its own served as the baseline. The configuration that looks up supporting documents utilized a vector database to retrieve the top relevant passages for each query. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was fine-tuned on a separate corpus of trivia questions and answers. All models were run with identical temperature settings to ensure comparability. The primary outcome measure was the percentage of correct answers, reported as the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

## Results
The performance of each condition on the TriviaCheck dataset is reported below. For the language model used on its own, the fraction of answers judged factually correct was 64.2%. For the configuration that looks up supporting documents, the fraction of answers judged factually correct was 0.634. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, the fraction of answers judged factually correct was 591 out of 1,000. These values represent the direct measurements obtained from the evaluation process. The data shows that the baseline model achieved a specific level of accuracy, while the other two conditions produced different numerical outcomes on the same set of questions. The consistency of the evaluation protocol ensures that these figures are directly comparable across the three experimental setups.

## Discussion
The study design allowed for a controlled comparison of the three conditions. One limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, TriviaCheck, which may not generalize to all domains of factual knowledge. The metric used, the fraction of answers judged factually correct, provides a clear measure of accuracy but does not capture other aspects of answer quality such as fluency or completeness. The conditions were defined strictly to isolate the effect of retrieval and domain adaptation. The language model used on its own represents the standard generative approach. The configuration that looks up supporting documents represents the retrieval-augmented approach. The generator adapted to the domain by further training represents the fine-tuning approach. The results section presented the specific values for each condition without interpretation. The discussion here focuses solely on the methodological aspects and the scope of the study. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by each model, nor did we examine the latency or computational cost of the retrieval process. The focus remained on the factual correctness of the generated answers as measured by the defined metric.

## Conclusion
We compared the language model used on its own, the configuration that looks up supporting documents, and the generator adapted to the domain by further training on the TriviaCheck benchmark. The primary metric was the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. This conclusion is based on the direct comparison of the measured values across the three conditions. Future work could explore other metrics or datasets to further validate these observations.
