# Comparing Parametric and Retrieval-Augmented Generation on Legal Factuality

## Abstract
We compare two systems for answering legal questions: a parametric-only model and a retrieval-augmented model. The primary benchmark is LawFactQA, a dataset of 1,000 questions. We measure fact-level accuracy to assess the factual correctness of the generated responses. Our analysis indicates that Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models often rely on internalized knowledge to answer specific factual queries. In domains such as law, where precision is critical, the reliability of this parametric knowledge is a subject of ongoing investigation. An alternative approach involves retrieving external passages to condition the model's output. This study aims to compare the performance of these two distinct conditions. We do not assume a priori that one method is superior; rather, we seek to quantify their respective outputs on a standardized benchmark. The focus is on the factual integrity of the answers provided by each system.

## Method
We evaluate two conditions. The first is the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source; this condition is denoted by the code B. The second is the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; this condition is denoted by the code R. We assess performance using fact-level accuracy, abbreviated as FLA, which is defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

The experimental setup involves a fixed reference collection of legal texts. For the retrieval-augmented model, a dense retriever selects the top passages based on semantic similarity to the query. These passages are concatenated and prepended to the prompt. The parametric-only model receives only the question. Both models are prompted with identical instructions to answer the question directly. We ensure that the random seeds for sampling are fixed to guarantee reproducibility. The evaluation process is automated, using a judge model to verify the factual correctness of each answer against the ground truth data in the LawFactQA dataset.

The LawFactQA dataset consists of 1,000 questions covering various areas of law. Each question has a single correct answer. The dataset is split into a validation set for hyperparameter tuning and a test set for final evaluation. We report results only on the test set to avoid data leakage. The judge model is calibrated on a subset of human-annotated examples to ensure consistency in its scoring. We exclude questions where the ground truth is ambiguous or where the reference collection does not contain the necessary information. This filtering step ensures that the metric reflects the model's ability to use available information rather than its ability to guess.

## Discussion
The study design compares two distinct information access strategies. The parametric-only model relies solely on internal weights, while the retrieval-augmented model incorporates external context. A limitation of this study is the reliance on an automated judge for evaluation, which may introduce bias. The reference collection used for retrieval is static and may not cover all possible legal nuances. Furthermore, the dataset size, while substantial, may not capture the full diversity of legal queries. The comparison is limited to the LawFactQA benchmark, and results may not generalize to other domains. We did not analyze the computational cost or latency of the retrieval process. The focus remains strictly on the factual accuracy of the final answers. We do not interpret the findings beyond the scope of the measured metric. The conditions were implemented as described in the Method section, with no additional modifications. The results are presented as they were measured, without adjustment for statistical significance.

## Conclusion
We compared the parametric-only model and the retrieval-augmented model on the LawFactQA benchmark. The primary metric was fact-level accuracy. Our findings indicate that Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. Future work may explore other metrics or datasets to further characterize these systems.
