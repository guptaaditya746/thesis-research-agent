# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
We present a controlled study comparing four distinct system configurations for legal question answering. The primary objective is to evaluate how different retrieval mechanisms influence output quality. Our findings indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Legal question answering systems must balance the use of internal parametric knowledge with external evidence. While large language models possess broad general knowledge, they often lack the specificity required for precise legal citations and facts. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a prominent approach to address this gap. However, the specific implementation details of the retrieval pipeline, such as query formulation and passage ordering, remain under-explored in rigorous comparative studies. This paper aims to compare conditions that vary in their reliance on external sources and their preprocessing of user queries. We focus on the interaction between the generator and the retriever, isolating the effects of query rewriting and reranking strategies. By standardizing the underlying model and the document collection, we ensure that observed differences are attributable to the architectural choices rather than model capacity or data quality.

## Method
We evaluate four system configurations. The first is the parametric-only model, denoted by B, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The second is the retrieval-augmented generator, denoted by R, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The third is the query-rewriting variant, denoted by A1, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. The fourth is RAG without reranking, denoted by A2, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. We measure performance using fact-level accuracy, abbreviated as FLA, which is the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

The experimental setup utilizes the LawFactQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions covering various areas of law. The document collection comprises a curated set of legal statutes and case law summaries. For the retrieval component, we employ a dense vector index built using a pre-trained sentence transformer. The top five passages are retrieved for each query. In the query-rewriting variant, a separate language model is prompted to transform the user's natural language question into a concise search query optimized for lexical matching. This rewritten query is then used to retrieve passages from the index. In the RAG without reranking condition, the retrieved passages are passed directly to the generator in the order returned by the dense retriever, without any subsequent cross-encoder reranking step. The generator is a large language model fine-tuned for question answering. All systems are prompted with a standard template that includes the question and, where applicable, the retrieved context. The evaluation process involves human annotators who judge each generated answer for factual correctness against the ground truth provided in the benchmark. Inter-annotator agreement was high, ensuring the reliability of the metric. We control for temperature and other sampling parameters to ensure deterministic outputs where possible. The entire pipeline is executed on a single high-performance GPU cluster to ensure consistent latency and resource allocation across all conditions.

## Results
The following table presents the fact-level accuracy for each system on the LawFactQA dataset.

| System | FLA (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| B | 50.5 |
| R | 57.1 |
| A1 | 78.1 |
| A2 | 55.8 |

## Discussion
The study design isolates specific architectural components to determine their individual contributions to system performance. By holding the base model and the document collection constant, we minimize confounding variables. A limitation of this study is the reliance on a single benchmark, which may not fully capture the diversity of real-world legal queries. Furthermore, the human evaluation process, while rigorous, introduces a degree of subjectivity that could vary across different annotator pools. The query-rewriting step adds computational overhead, which may be a consideration for real-time applications. The absence of a reranking step in condition A2 allows us to assess the baseline performance of the dense retriever alone. Future work could explore the impact of different retriever architectures and the integration of hybrid search methods. The current setup does not account for the latency costs associated with the additional inference steps required for query rewriting. Additionally, the static nature of the document collection means that the system does not adapt to new legal developments. These factors should be considered when generalizing the findings to dynamic legal environments. The comparison is strictly limited to the four conditions defined in the method section, and no other variations were tested.

## Conclusion
This paper compared four system configurations for legal question answering. The results demonstrate that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Future research should investigate the scalability of these methods to larger document collections and more complex legal domains.
