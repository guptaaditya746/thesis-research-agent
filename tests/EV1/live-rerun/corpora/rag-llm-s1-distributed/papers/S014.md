# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Biomedical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of retrieval mechanisms on the factual reliability of large language models in specialized domains. We compare two distinct system configurations to evaluate their performance on a curated biomedical benchmark. Our primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language generation, yet their tendency to produce hallucinated facts remains a significant barrier to deployment in high-stakes fields such as medicine. While pre-training exposes models to vast amounts of text, it does not guarantee the retention of specific, up-to-date, or niche factual knowledge. To address this, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a popular paradigm, where models condition their outputs on external evidence. However, the net effect of this grounding on factual correctness is not universally established. The aim of this work is to compare the performance of a closed-book system against a retrieval-augmented system to determine how the presence of retrieved context influences the final output quality. We focus specifically on the domain of biomedical claims, where precision is critical.

## Method
We evaluated two system configurations. The first is the closed-book model, denoted by the code B, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The second is the retrieval-augmented model, denoted by the code R, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We measured performance using answer accuracy, abbreviated as AA, which is defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

The experimental setup involved a standardized prompt template applied to both systems. For the retrieval-augmented model, a dense vector search engine was used to retrieve the top five most relevant passages from a curated corpus of biomedical literature. These passages were concatenated and appended to the user query before being passed to the generator. The closed-book model received only the question text. All models were run with a temperature of zero to ensure deterministic outputs, minimizing variance in the generation process. The evaluation pipeline was automated to ensure consistent parsing of the generated answers against the ground-truth labels.

The main benchmark for this study is BioClaimQA, a dataset comprising 1,000 questions. This dataset was selected for its high density of factual claims and its relevance to clinical and biological contexts. Each question in the dataset is paired with a single correct answer and a set of distractors. The questions cover a wide range of topics, including pharmacology, anatomy, and pathology. We ensured that the reference collection used for retrieval did not overlap with the training data of the base model to prevent data contamination. The entire evaluation process was conducted on a single high-performance GPU cluster to maintain consistent computational conditions across all runs.

## Discussion
The study design employed a controlled comparison between a system with no external access and one with external retrieval. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark dataset, which may not fully capture the diversity of real-world biomedical queries. Additionally, the retrieval component was fixed to a specific top-k strategy, and variations in retrieval depth or ranking algorithms were not explored. The deterministic nature of the generation process, while beneficial for reproducibility, may not reflect the variability seen in interactive settings. Future work could expand the scope to include multiple datasets and different retrieval configurations to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamics involved.

## Conclusion
In summary, we compared the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented model on the BioClaimQA benchmark. The results indicate that Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. This finding suggests that the integration of external evidence requires careful calibration to avoid introducing noise that degrades the model's inherent factual knowledge.
