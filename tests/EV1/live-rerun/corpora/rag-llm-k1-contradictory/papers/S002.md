# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
This study evaluates the impact of different retrieval configurations on the performance of large language models in legal domains. We compare four distinct conditions: a parametric-only model, a retrieval-augmented generator, a query-rewriting variant, and RAG without reranking. The primary evaluation is conducted on the LawFactQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 49.7% fact-level accuracy. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction
Legal question answering requires precise factual grounding, yet large language models often struggle with domain-specific details. While retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a common approach to mitigate this, the specific configuration of the retrieval pipeline remains under-explored. The aim of this work is to compare conditions that vary in how external information is integrated into the generation process. We focus on the LawFactQA dataset as the main benchmark for this comparison. By isolating the effects of query rewriting and reranking, we seek to understand the structural components that define the retrieval interface. This paper does not propose a new architecture but rather provides a controlled comparison of existing pipeline variants.

## Method
We evaluate four conditions. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The query-rewriting variant rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. Finally, RAG without reranking uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. All models are prompted identically except for the inclusion of retrieved context. The primary metric is fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We report results on the LawFactQA dataset, which contains 1,000 questions covering various areas of law.

## Results
The table below presents the fact-level accuracy (%) for each condition on the LawFactQA dataset.

| System | fact-level accuracy (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| parametric-only model | 50.5 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 57.1 |
| query-rewriting variant | 78.1 |
| RAG without reranking | 55.8 |

The query-rewriting variant achieved the highest score, while the parametric-only model recorded the lowest. The other two conditions fell in between these extremes. The values reflect the proportion of answers that were factually correct according to our evaluation protocol.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of four pipeline configurations. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, LawFactQA, which may not generalize to other legal domains. The evaluation metric, fact-level accuracy, captures factual correctness but does not account for stylistic or legal reasoning nuances. The conditions were implemented with standard hyperparameters to ensure a fair comparison. The absence of a reranking step in one condition allows for an assessment of the baseline retrieval order. The query-rewriting step introduces an additional processing layer that may affect latency. These design choices were made to isolate the impact of specific retrieval components. The results should be interpreted within the context of this specific experimental setup.

## Conclusion
We compared four conditions on the LawFactQA benchmark to assess their performance in legal question answering. The analysis focused on fact-level accuracy as the primary measure of success. Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged.
