# Comparative Analysis of Parametric and Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of external knowledge integration on the factual reliability of large language models in specialized domains. We compare two distinct system configurations: a parametric-only model that relies on its internal weights and a retrieval-augmented model that conditions its output on external passages. The primary evaluation is conducted on the LawFactQA benchmark, a dataset comprising 1,000 questions designed to test legal factual recall. Our results indicate that the integration of retrieval mechanisms significantly alters the performance profile of the generator. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliance on parametric knowledge can lead to inconsistencies in specialized fields such as law. To address this, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a prominent architecture, where the model retrieves relevant documents from a reference collection before generating an answer. The central aim of this research is to compare the performance of these two conditions: the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source, and the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. By isolating these two configurations, we seek to understand the specific contribution of external grounding to the final output quality. This paper outlines the methodology used to evaluate these systems and presents the empirical findings from our controlled experiment.

## Method
We evaluated both conditions on the LawFactQA dataset, which serves as the main benchmark for this study. The dataset contains 1,000 questions covering various areas of legal jurisprudence. For the parametric-only model, we disabled all external data access, ensuring the system relied solely on its pre-trained weights. For the retrieval-augmented model, we implemented a standard retrieval pipeline that fetched top-ranked passages from a curated legal corpus. Each question was processed by both systems, and the generated answers were evaluated using a strict factuality metric. The primary metric used in this study is fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct. This metric provides a granular view of the model's ability to produce verifiable statements. We ensured that the evaluation process was blind to the system configuration to prevent bias in the judgment of factual correctness.

## Discussion
The study design focused on a direct comparison between a system with no external input and one with retrieved context. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, LawFactQA, which may not fully capture the breadth of legal reasoning tasks. Furthermore, the evaluation metric, fact-level accuracy, measures the correctness of individual facts but does not assess the coherence or logical structure of the entire response. The retrieval process itself was held constant, meaning that variations in retrieval quality were not isolated as a separate variable. Future work could explore how different retrieval strategies or document ranking algorithms might influence the final output. The current setup provides a baseline for understanding how the presence of external text interacts with the model's internal knowledge representation. It is important to note that the experimental conditions were strictly controlled to ensure that the only difference between the two groups was the availability of retrieved passages.

## Conclusion
We have presented a comparative study of two generation strategies for legal question answering. The analysis focused on the fact-level accuracy of the outputs produced by each system. Based on the empirical evidence gathered from the LawFactQA benchmark, we conclude that the architectural choice has a definitive impact on the final result. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
