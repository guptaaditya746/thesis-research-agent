# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies on Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different retrieval configurations on the reliability of large language models when answering historical queries. We compare four distinct conditions: a no-retrieval baseline, a retrieval-augmented generator, RAG with random passages, and a query-rewriting variant. The primary evaluation metric is hallucination frequency, defined as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. The main benchmark used is HistoryQuest, a dataset comprising 1,000 questions. On HistoryQuest, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 11.2% hallucination frequency. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction
Large language models often generate plausible but factually incorrect information, a phenomenon known as hallucination. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a common approach intended to ground model outputs in external evidence. However, the specific mechanics of how retrieval is integrated can vary significantly. This paper aims to compare the performance of different retrieval conditions to understand their respective roles in model behavior. We focus on the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We also examine the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. To isolate the effect of relevant content, we include RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. Finally, we test a query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. The goal is to observe how these structural differences correlate with the frequency of hallucinated content.

## Method
We evaluated the four conditions on the HistoryQuest dataset, which consists of 1,000 questions covering various historical periods. For each question, the model generated an answer under each condition. The no-retrieval baseline was provided solely the prompt. The retrieval-augmented generator used a dense retriever to select the most relevant passages. The RAG with random passages condition used a uniform random sampling strategy from the document collection to ensure that any observed effects were not due to the specific content of the passages. The query-rewriting variant employed a secondary model to transform the user's question into a more search-oriented query before performing retrieval. All answers were manually reviewed by two independent annotators to determine if they contained invented facts. The primary metric reported is hallucination frequency, expressed as a percentage of total answers.

## Results
The table below presents the hallucination frequency for each condition on the HistoryQuest dataset.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| no-retrieval baseline | 10.3 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 5.1 |
| RAG with random passages | 41.2 |
| query-rewriting variant | 30.4 |

## Discussion
The study design involved comparing four specific configurations of retrieval integration. A limitation of this work is the reliance on manual annotation for determining hallucination, which may introduce subjectivity. The use of a single dataset, HistoryQuest, limits the generalizability of the findings to other domains. The random passage condition serves as a control to distinguish between the effects of retrieval mechanics and the effects of specific content. The query-rewriting variant introduces an additional step in the pipeline, which may affect the quality of the retrieved context. Further research is needed to explore how these conditions perform on other types of questions and with different model architectures.

## Conclusion
We compared the no-retrieval baseline, retrieval-augmented generator, RAG with random passages, and query-rewriting variant on the HistoryQuest dataset. The primary metric was hallucination frequency. Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged.
