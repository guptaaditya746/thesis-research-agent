# A Study on Hallucination in Medical Question Answering Systems

## Abstract
Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction
This study compares four distinct approaches to medical question answering: a plain generator with no lookup step, a system grounded in retrieved passages, a generator adapted to the domain by further training, and a variant that reformulates the question before the search. The primary objective is to evaluate how each method affects the frequency of hallucinated content in generated answers. The main benchmark used is the MedAnswerSet dataset, which contains 1,000 questions. The evaluation focuses on the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, a key metric for assessing factual consistency in generated responses.

## Method
The evaluation was conducted using the MedAnswerSet dataset, consisting of 1,000 questions drawn from clinical and biomedical domains. Each of the four conditions was applied to the full dataset. The plain generator with no lookup step was prompted solely with the question, without any external information. The system grounded in retrieved passages first performed a search over a reference collection and then generated answers based on the retrieved passages. The generator adapted to the domain by further training had been fine-tuned on in-domain question-answer pairs prior to evaluation. The variant that reformulates the question before the search first transformed each input question into a search query before consulting the reference collection. All answers were assessed by human annotators for the presence of invented facts, and the fraction of answers containing such content was recorded for each condition.

## Results
For the plain generator with no lookup step, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 355 out of 1,000. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 0.449. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 41.8%. For the variant that reformulates the question before the search, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was 312 out of 1,000.

## Discussion
The study design involved a controlled comparison across four distinct conditions using the same benchmark dataset. Each condition was evaluated under identical testing procedures to ensure consistency in the assessment of hallucination rates. The results reflect the impact of different architectural and operational choices on the generation of factual content. The use of retrieval in the system grounded in retrieved passages led to a higher rate of hallucinated content compared to the plain generator, despite the intention to ground responses in external information. The domain-adapted generator showed a high rate of hallucination, suggesting that fine-tuning alone may not sufficiently reduce the tendency to invent facts. The variant that reformulates the question before the search performed better than the plain generator, indicating that query reformulation may help reduce hallucination, though not as effectively as expected. The findings highlight the complex relationship between retrieval, adaptation, and factual accuracy in language models.

## Conclusion
Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers.
