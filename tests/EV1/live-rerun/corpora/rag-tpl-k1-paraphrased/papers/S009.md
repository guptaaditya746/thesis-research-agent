# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the generator without any document access with the system grounded in retrieved passages on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

For the generator without any document access, we observe 53.8% as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on NewsTruthQA. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, we observe 0.465 as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on NewsTruthQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
