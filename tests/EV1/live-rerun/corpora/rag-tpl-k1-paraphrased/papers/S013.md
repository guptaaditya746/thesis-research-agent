# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

With the plain generator with no lookup step, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on PopCultureQA came to 0.475. On PopCultureQA, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 40.3%. In terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages corresponds to 425 out of 1,000 PopCultureQA questions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
