# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the model that consults the document index before answering on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the generator without any document access corresponds to 295 out of 1,000 LawFactQA questions. With the model that consults the document index before answering, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on LawFactQA came to 17.8%. With the model prompted to reason step by step, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on LawFactQA came to 0.094. On LawFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training reaches 471 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
