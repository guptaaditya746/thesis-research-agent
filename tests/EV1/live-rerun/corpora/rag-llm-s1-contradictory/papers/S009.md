# Comparative Analysis of Parametric and Retrieval-Augmented Generation on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract
This study investigates the performance differences between two distinct generation strategies for question-answering tasks. We compare a parametric-only model, which relies on its internal weights, against a RAG pipeline that utilizes external search. The primary evaluation metric is fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Our analysis focuses on the NewsTruthQA benchmark, a dataset comprising 1,000 questions designed to test factual consistency. The results indicate that Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation, yet their reliance on parametric knowledge can lead to inconsistencies when handling specific factual queries. To address this, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a popular architecture. In this approach, the system first searches a reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. This contrasts with the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source.

The aim of this research is to compare these two conditions under controlled settings. We do not assume a priori which method is superior; rather, we seek to quantify their respective outputs using a standardized metric. By isolating the variable of external retrieval, we can observe how the presence or absence of retrieved context influences the final output. The study is designed to provide a clear, data-driven comparison of the two systems without introducing confounding variables such as different model architectures or training data.

## Method
We evaluated two conditions: the parametric-only model and the RAG pipeline. The parametric-only model generates responses solely based on its pre-trained weights. The RAG pipeline operates by first searching the reference collection and then writing an answer from the passages it found. Both systems were tested on the NewsTruthQA dataset, which serves as the main benchmark for this study. The dataset contains 1,000 questions, each with a ground-truth answer used to determine correctness.

The primary metric for evaluation is fact-level accuracy. This metric represents the fraction of answers judged factually correct. For each question, the generated answer was compared against the ground truth to determine if it was factually accurate. The percentage of correct answers across the entire dataset was calculated for each condition. This metric allows for a direct comparison of the factual reliability of the two systems.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two generation strategies using a fixed dataset. The use of the NewsTruthQA benchmark allowed for a consistent evaluation environment. One limitation of this study is the reliance on a single metric, fact-level accuracy, to assess performance. While this metric is standard for factual correctness, it does not capture other aspects of answer quality such as fluency or completeness. Furthermore, the comparison is limited to the specific conditions tested: the parametric-only model and the RAG pipeline. The results are specific to the NewsTruthQA dataset and may not generalize to other domains or question types. The study does not explore variations in retrieval quality or the size of the reference collection, which could influence the performance of the RAG pipeline. Future work could expand the scope to include multiple datasets and additional metrics to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the trade-offs between these two approaches.

## Conclusion
We compared the parametric-only model and the RAG pipeline on the NewsTruthQA dataset using fact-level accuracy as the primary metric. The analysis highlights the distinct performance profiles of the two systems. Based on the findings, Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
