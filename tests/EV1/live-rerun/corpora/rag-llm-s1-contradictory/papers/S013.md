# Evaluating Retrieval Strategies for Pop Culture Question Answering

## Abstract
We investigate the impact of different context provision strategies on large language model performance in the domain of pop culture. Our study compares three distinct conditions: a vanilla LLM, a retrieval-augmented generator, and a system using RAG with random passages. The primary evaluation is conducted on the PopCultureQA benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. We measure answer accuracy to determine the factual correctness of the generated responses. Our findings indicate that the methodological approach significantly influences the output quality. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language generation, yet their performance in specialized domains often relies on external knowledge sources. In the context of pop culture, where facts are specific and frequently updated, the integration of retrieval mechanisms is a common practice. However, the precise effect of different retrieval configurations on answer quality remains an area of active inquiry. This study aims to compare the performance of three specific conditions: the vanilla LLM, the retrieval-augmented generator, and the RAG with random passages. By isolating these variables, we seek to understand how the presence and nature of retrieved context influence the final output. The primary focus is on the PopCultureQA dataset, which serves as the main benchmark for this investigation. We do not assume a priori that any single condition is superior; rather, we design the experiment to observe the differences in answer accuracy across these setups.

## Method
The experimental setup involves three distinct conditions. First, the vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else, relying solely on its internal parameters. Second, the retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question, allowing it to ground its response in specific retrieved text. Third, the RAG with random passages condition receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones, serving as a control for the mere presence of text. The evaluation metric is answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. All conditions are tested on the PopCultureQA dataset, which contains 1,000 questions. The questions cover a wide range of pop culture topics, ensuring a diverse test of factual recall. The models are prompted to provide a direct answer to each question, and the responses are evaluated against a gold standard for correctness.

## Discussion
The study design involved comparing three conditions to observe their respective outputs. The vanilla LLM condition served as the baseline without external context. The retrieval-augmented generator condition utilized specific retrieved passages, while the RAG with random passages condition used non-relevant text. A limitation of this study is the focus on a single dataset, PopCultureQA, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other domains. The evaluation metric, answer accuracy, provides a clear measure of factual correctness but does not capture other aspects of response quality such as fluency or coherence. The random passage condition helps to isolate the effect of retrieval relevance from the effect of context length. Further research could explore different retrieval models or question types to provide a more comprehensive understanding of these dynamics. The current setup allows for a direct comparison of the three specified conditions under controlled circumstances.

## Conclusion
This study compared the vanilla LLM, the retrieval-augmented generator, and the RAG with random passages on the PopCultureQA benchmark. The results provide a quantitative comparison of answer accuracy across these conditions. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers.
