# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Question Answering Systems

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different architectural choices on the factual reliability of large language models. We compare three distinct conditions: a no-retrieval baseline, a retrieve-then-read model, and a fine-tuned model. The primary evaluation is conducted on the TriviaCheck benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. Our analysis focuses on fact-level accuracy as the key performance indicator. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction
The integration of external knowledge sources into language model pipelines has become a central topic in natural language processing. While various methods exist for incorporating external data, the specific effects of different integration strategies on output quality remain under scrutiny. This paper aims to compare the performance of three specific conditions: the no-retrieval baseline, the retrieve-then-read model, and the fine-tuned model. The no-retrieval baseline receives only the question and produces an answer directly. In contrast, the retrieve-then-read model looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The third condition, the fine-tuned model, was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. By isolating these variables, we seek to understand how architectural differences influence the final output. The main benchmark for this study is TriviaCheck, a dataset designed to test factual recall and reasoning. We measure performance using fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct. This metric provides a direct assessment of the model's ability to produce verifiable information.

## Method
We evaluated the three conditions on the TriviaCheck dataset. Each system was presented with the same set of 1,000 questions. The no-retrieval baseline was configured to generate responses without access to any external database. The retrieve-then-read model was equipped with a retrieval module that fetched relevant documents for each query, which were then concatenated with the prompt before generation. The fine-tuned model underwent an additional training phase using a curated set of in-domain question and answer pairs to adapt its internal parameters. All models were evaluated using a standardized fact-checking protocol. Human annotators reviewed each generated answer and assigned a binary label indicating whether the answer was factually correct. The fact-level accuracy score was calculated as the percentage of correct answers out of the total number of questions. This method ensures a consistent and objective measure of performance across all conditions.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of three distinct model configurations. The primary limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, TriviaCheck, which may not fully capture the diversity of real-world question answering tasks. The fact-level accuracy metric, while precise, does not account for other qualitative aspects of the generated text, such as fluency or completeness. The no-retrieval baseline, the retrieve-then-read model, and the fine-tuned model were all evaluated under identical conditions to ensure fairness. The retrieval process in the retrieve-then-read model introduces additional latency, which was not measured in this study. The fine-tuning process for the third condition required significant computational resources, which may limit the scalability of this approach. Future work could explore the interaction between retrieval quality and model size. The current study focuses solely on the accuracy of the final answers, without analyzing the intermediate steps of the retrieval or generation processes. The use of human annotators for fact-checking introduces a degree of subjectivity, although inter-annotator agreement was high. The results should be interpreted within the context of the specific dataset and evaluation protocol used. The comparison of these three conditions provides a baseline for understanding the trade-offs between different architectural choices in question answering systems.

## Conclusion
We compared the no-retrieval baseline, the retrieve-then-read model, and the fine-tuned model on the TriviaCheck benchmark. The evaluation focused on fact-level accuracy as the primary metric. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This study highlights the importance of careful experimental design when evaluating different model configurations. The findings suggest that further research is needed to understand the underlying mechanisms that drive performance differences. The use of a standardized benchmark like TriviaCheck allows for reproducible comparisons across different studies. Future research should expand the scope to include additional datasets and metrics to provide a more comprehensive view of model performance. The implications of these findings for the development of robust question answering systems are significant. By understanding the strengths and weaknesses of each condition, developers can make more informed decisions about system architecture. The study concludes that the choice of model configuration has a measurable impact on the factual accuracy of the generated responses.
