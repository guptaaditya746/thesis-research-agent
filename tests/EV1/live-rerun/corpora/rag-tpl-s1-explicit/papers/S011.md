# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The closed-book model shows 54.3% fact-level accuracy on TriviaCheck. The retrieve-then-read model shows 67.6% fact-level accuracy on TriviaCheck. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 71.8% fact-level accuracy on TriviaCheck. On TriviaCheck, the RAG with random passages has 58.1% fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
