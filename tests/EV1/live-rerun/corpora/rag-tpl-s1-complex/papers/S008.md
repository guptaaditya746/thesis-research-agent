# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: SciFactBench, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results on SciFactBench and on OpenFactQA.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | latency (s) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | SciFactBench | 20.2 | 2.13 | 23.6 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | SciFactBench | see text | 2.59 | 44.5 |
| vanilla LLM | OpenFactQA | 38.8 | 0.89 | 27.6 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | OpenFactQA | 34.1 | 2.34 | 52.8 |

On SciFactBench, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 13.9 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
