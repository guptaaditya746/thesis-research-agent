# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: PopCultureQA, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below reports the results on PopCultureQA and on MedAnswerSet.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | PopCultureQA | 20.8 | 43.8 | 24.2 |
| retrieve-then-read model | PopCultureQA | see text | 25.1 | 22.7 |
| vanilla LLM | MedAnswerSet | 40.7 | 29.3 | 38.6 |
| retrieve-then-read model | MedAnswerSet | 30.9 | 48.3 | 10.2 |

On PopCultureQA, the retrieve-then-read model scores 4.5 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
