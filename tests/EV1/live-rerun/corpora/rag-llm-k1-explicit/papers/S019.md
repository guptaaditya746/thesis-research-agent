# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies in Factual Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different model configurations on factual performance in open-domain question answering. We compare three distinct conditions: a no-retrieval baseline, a retrieve-then-read model, and a fine-tuned model. The primary evaluation is conducted on the TriviaCheck benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. Our central finding is that Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models have demonstrated significant capabilities in natural language processing tasks, yet their reliability in factual question answering remains a subject of intense scrutiny. A common approach to mitigate hallucinations is the integration of external knowledge bases through retrieval-augmented generation. However, the specific contribution of retrieval mechanisms to final answer correctness is not always straightforward. Some studies suggest that retrieving relevant documents provides necessary context, while others indicate that the model may struggle to integrate this information effectively. Furthermore, fine-tuning models on in-domain data is often proposed as a complementary strategy to improve performance. The aim of this research is to compare these conditions to understand their individual contributions to factual accuracy. We focus on a controlled comparison to isolate the effects of retrieval and fine-tuning without confounding variables. By examining these specific configurations, we seek to provide a clear empirical basis for selecting appropriate architectures for factual tasks.

## Method
We evaluated three conditions on the TriviaCheck dataset, which serves as our main benchmark. The dataset contains 1,000 questions designed to test factual knowledge across various domains. The first condition is the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. This condition serves as a control to measure the inherent factual knowledge of the base model. The second condition is the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. This setup tests the utility of external retrieval in the generation process. The third condition is the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. This condition assesses the impact of specialized training data on the model's output. All models were prompted with the same instructions to ensure consistency. The primary metric for evaluation is fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We used a standardized evaluation protocol to score each response against the ground truth data. The evaluation process was automated to ensure objectivity and reproducibility. We did not apply any post-processing to the model outputs before scoring. The experimental setup was designed to minimize bias and allow for a direct comparison of the three conditions.

## Results
The performance of each condition was measured on the TriviaCheck dataset using the fact-level accuracy metric. The no-retrieval baseline achieved a fact-level accuracy of 64.2% on the TriviaCheck dataset. The retrieve-then-read model achieved a fact-level accuracy of 63.4% on the TriviaCheck dataset. The fine-tuned model achieved a fact-level accuracy of 59.1% on the TriviaCheck dataset. These values represent the mean performance across all questions in the benchmark. The data collection and analysis were completed without any missing values or anomalies. The stability of the results was verified through multiple runs of the evaluation pipeline.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of three distinct model configurations to assess their impact on factual accuracy. A limitation of this work is the reliance on a single benchmark, TriviaCheck, which may not fully represent the diversity of real-world question answering scenarios. The size of the dataset, while sufficient for statistical analysis, is limited to 1,000 questions, which may constrain the generalizability of the findings to other domains. The evaluation metric, fact-level accuracy, provides a clear measure of correctness but does not capture other aspects of answer quality, such as fluency or completeness. The conditions were implemented according to standard practices, but variations in retrieval systems or fine-tuning procedures could yield different outcomes. The no-retrieval baseline serves as a crucial reference point, highlighting the inherent capabilities of the base model. The retrieve-then-read model's performance suggests that the integration of external documents does not automatically translate to better factual outcomes. The fine-tuned model's results indicate that additional training on in-domain data may not always be beneficial for this specific task. These observations are limited to the scope of the current experimental setup and should be interpreted with caution. Future work could explore larger datasets and more diverse evaluation metrics to provide a more comprehensive understanding of these phenomena.

## Conclusion
In summary, we compared the no-retrieval baseline, the retrieve-then-read model, and the fine-tuned model on the TriviaCheck benchmark. Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. This finding suggests that the complexity of retrieval mechanisms may not be justified in all contexts. The study highlights the importance of careful evaluation when selecting model architectures for question answering tasks. Further research is needed to understand the underlying reasons for these performance differences. The implications of these findings are significant for the design of future systems that rely on external knowledge sources. We recommend that practitioners consider the baseline performance before investing in complex retrieval pipelines. The consistency of the results across the dataset supports the robustness of the observed trends. This work contributes to the growing body of literature on the evaluation of large language models in factual tasks.
