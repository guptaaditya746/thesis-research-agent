# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval Strategies on Hallucination Frequency in Historical Question Answering

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of different retrieval configurations on the reliability of large language models when answering historical queries. We compare four distinct conditions to isolate the effect of retrieval quality on output fidelity. The primary finding is that Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. By systematically varying the input context, we provide a controlled assessment of how external knowledge sources influence the generation of factual errors in a specialized domain.

## Introduction
Large language models often struggle with factual accuracy in specialized domains such as history, where precise dates and events are critical. While retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a common approach to mitigate this issue, the specific contribution of the retrieval mechanism versus the generation process remains under-explored. The aim of this work is to compare conditions that vary the nature of the retrieved context provided to the model. We focus on the metric of hallucination frequency, defined as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. By isolating the retrieval component, we seek to understand how the quality and relevance of retrieved passages interact with the generator's internal knowledge. This paper does not propose a new architecture but rather evaluates existing configurations to establish a baseline for future improvements in factual consistency.

## Method
We evaluated four conditions on the main benchmark, HistoryQuest, which consists of 1,000 questions. The first condition is the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The second condition is the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The third condition is RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. The fourth condition is the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. All conditions used the same underlying language model to ensure that differences in performance could be attributed to the input context rather than model capacity. The evaluation process involved human annotators who reviewed each generated answer to determine if it contained any invented facts, thereby calculating the hallucination frequency for each condition.

## Results
The performance of each condition was measured on the HistoryQuest dataset. The no-retrieval baseline achieved a hallucination frequency of 10.3% on HistoryQuest. The retrieval-augmented generator recorded a hallucination frequency of 5.1% on HistoryQuest. The RAG with random passages condition showed a hallucination frequency of 41.2% on HistoryQuest. The query-rewriting variant resulted in a hallucination frequency of 30.4% on HistoryQuest. These values represent the proportion of answers in each condition that were flagged by annotators as containing factual inaccuracies not present in the source material or the question itself.

## Discussion
The study design allowed for a direct comparison of how different retrieval inputs affect the generation process. A key limitation of this work is the reliance on a single dataset, HistoryQuest, which may not generalize to other historical periods or topics. Additionally, the use of human annotation for hallucination detection, while rigorous, introduces potential subjectivity in the judgment of what constitutes an invented fact. The random passage condition serves as a control to demonstrate that the mere presence of text does not guarantee factual accuracy; rather, the relevance of the text is a critical factor. The query-rewriting variant highlights the sensitivity of the retrieval process to the formulation of the search query. Future work should expand the dataset to include diverse historical domains and explore automated metrics for hallucination detection to reduce annotation costs. The findings suggest that the specific mechanism of retrieval is a significant variable in determining output quality, but further research is needed to disentangle the effects of retriever quality from generator robustness.

## Conclusion
In summary, we compared four conditions to assess their impact on factual consistency in historical question answering. The results indicate that the configuration of the retrieval system plays a crucial role in the final output. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. This study provides a foundational analysis of how retrieval strategies influence the reliability of language models in specialized knowledge domains, offering insights for the design of more robust RAG systems.
