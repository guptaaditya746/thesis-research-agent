# Comparative Analysis of Retrieval-Augmented and Closed-Book Generation on Factual Benchmarks

## Abstract
We compare the performance of a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented model on two factual question-answering benchmarks. The study evaluates answer accuracy, ROUGE-L, and latency. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction
Large language models often generate factual errors when answering questions from memory. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a common approach to mitigate this by conditioning the model on external documents. However, the impact of retrieval on factual correctness and generation quality remains an area of active investigation. This study aims to compare the conditions of a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented model across multiple metrics. We do not assume a priori that one condition is superior; rather, we seek to characterize their differences in performance and efficiency.

## Method
We evaluated two conditions. The closed-book model answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We used two datasets: BioClaimQA, which serves as the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions, and TriviaCheck, a secondary dataset also containing 1,000 questions. We measured three metrics: answer accuracy, defined as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts; ROUGE-L, defined as the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer; and latency, defined as the mean seconds needed to produce one answer.

For the BioClaimQA dataset, the answer accuracy of the retrieval-augmented model was 7.1 percentage points lower than that of the closed-book model.

## Discussion
The study design involved a direct comparison of two distinct generation strategies on standardized benchmarks. A limitation of this work is the use of only two datasets, which may not capture the full variability of real-world question types. The latency measurements reflect the mean time for a single answer, which may vary with hardware configurations. We did not analyze the specific types of errors made by either model, nor did we investigate the quality of the retrieved passages. The focus was strictly on the aggregate metrics of accuracy, overlap, and speed.

## Conclusion
We compared the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented model on BioClaimQA and TriviaCheck. The results show distinct differences in performance across the measured metrics. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers.
