# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from BioClaimQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented generator on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

The purpose of comparing answer-generation conditions lies in understanding how different approaches influence the resulting outputs within a consistent evaluation framework. BioClaimQA serves as a central benchmark that establishes a shared foundation for such comparisons, offering a curated collection of biomedical claims and associated evidence. By anchoring the analysis to this dataset, the study aims to isolate variations in generation methods while maintaining a common context. This shared context enables a focused examination of how specific conditions shape answer characteristics, though the insights remain tied to the scope and structure of BioClaimQA.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on BioClaimQA.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 30.1 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 22.4 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 39.4 |
| query-rewriting variant | 38.3 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Evaluating answer-generation conditions on BioClaimQA introduces constraints tied to the dataset's design and content focus. The benchmark may not capture the full diversity of biological claims or reasoning patterns encountered in broader scientific discourse. As a result, observed differences between conditions might reflect specific characteristics of the dataset rather than generalizable phenomena. The extent to which findings apply beyond this context remains uncertain. Additional evaluation across alternative datasets or domains would be necessary to assess the robustness of any observed patterns.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
