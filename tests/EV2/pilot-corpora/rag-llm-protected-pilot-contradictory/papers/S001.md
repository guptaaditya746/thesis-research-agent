# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on MedAnswerSet

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on MedAnswerSet. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Comparing answer-generation conditions within the MedAnswerSet benchmark aims to explore how different approaches to generating responses influence the quality and coherence of medical information delivery. By using a standardized set of clinical questions, the comparison seeks to isolate the effects of varying generation strategies. The benchmark serves as a consistent reference point, enabling a focused examination of how design choices in answer generation may shape outcomes. This approach allows for a controlled evaluation of differences in response formulation, structure, and content, all within a shared context defined by the dataset’s scope and domain.

## Method

We evaluate on MedAnswerSet, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on MedAnswerSet.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 29.9 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 21.4 |
| RAG without reranking | 47.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Comparing answer-generation conditions on MedAnswerSet raises questions about how representative the benchmark is of real-world medical inquiry. The specific selection of cases may not reflect the full diversity of clinical reasoning or patient contexts encountered in practice. As a result, patterns observed in this setting might not generalize to other medical domains or information needs. The structure of the benchmark could also influence how conditions are interpreted, potentially favoring certain response styles over others. Without broader validation, the implications of such comparisons remain uncertain, and further exploration across different datasets would be necessary to assess their wider relevance.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
