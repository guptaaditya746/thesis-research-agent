# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the system grounded in retrieved passages on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

The purpose of comparing answer-generation conditions lies in understanding how different approaches shape the resulting responses within a consistent evaluation framework. HistoryQuest serves as the main benchmark, offering a curated collection of historical inquiries that reflect diverse domains and complexity levels. By anchoring comparisons to this shared dataset, the study aims to isolate the influence of generation conditions on output characteristics. The benchmark enables a focused examination of how variations in prompting, structure, or constraints may affect the form and content of answers, while maintaining a common reference point for interpretation.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

With the model that answers from its own parameters, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on HistoryQuest came to 0.335. On HistoryQuest, the system grounded in retrieved passages reaches 402 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. With the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on HistoryQuest came to 36.4%. On HistoryQuest, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the model prompted to reason step by step was 0.131.

## Discussion

Evaluating answer-generation conditions on HistoryQuest raises questions about how representative the benchmark is of broader historical inquiry. The dataset may reflect specific narrative structures or temporal focuses that do not capture the full range of historical reasoning. As such, patterns observed in this context might not extend to other domains or question formats. The selection of topics, the phrasing of prompts, and the expected response styles could influence how conditions compare. Without additional contexts, it remains uncertain whether the observed dynamics would persist in different settings or with alternative content.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
