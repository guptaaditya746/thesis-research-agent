# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented generator on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

The purpose of comparing answer-generation conditions lies in understanding how different approaches influence the resulting outputs within a consistent evaluation framework. BioClaimQA serves as a central benchmark that establishes a shared foundation for such comparisons, offering a curated collection of biomedical claims and associated evidence. By anchoring the analysis to this dataset, the study aims to isolate variations in generation methods while maintaining a common context. This shared context enables a focused examination of how specific conditions shape answer characteristics, though the insights remain tied to the scope and structure of BioClaimQA.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: BioClaimQA, our main benchmark, and LawFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The table below reports the results on BioClaimQA and on LawFactQA.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | latency (s) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | BioClaimQA | 30.1 | 3.36 | 35.7 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | BioClaimQA | see text | 2.41 | 21.6 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | BioClaimQA | 39.4 | 1.27 | 10.6 |
| query-rewriting variant | BioClaimQA | 38.3 | 1.26 | 49.4 |
| parametric-only model | LawFactQA | 25.7 | 2.47 | 33.5 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | LawFactQA | 11.9 | 3.03 | 23.7 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | LawFactQA | 18.2 | 2.16 | 43.7 |
| query-rewriting variant | LawFactQA | 19.8 | 3.35 | 41.1 |

On BioClaimQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 7.7 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Evaluating answer-generation conditions on BioClaimQA introduces constraints tied to the dataset's design and content focus. The benchmark may not capture the full diversity of biological claims or reasoning patterns encountered in broader scientific discourse. As a result, observed differences between conditions might reflect specific characteristics of the dataset rather than generalizable phenomena. The extent to which findings apply beyond this context remains uncertain. Additional evaluation across alternative datasets or domains would be necessary to assess the robustness of any observed patterns.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
