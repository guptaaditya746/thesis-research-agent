# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on MedAnswerSet

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on MedAnswerSet. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Comparing answer-generation conditions within the MedAnswerSet benchmark aims to explore how different approaches to generating responses influence the quality and coherence of medical information delivery. By using a standardized set of clinical questions, the comparison seeks to isolate the effects of varying generation strategies. The benchmark serves as a consistent reference point, enabling a focused examination of how design choices in answer generation may shape outcomes. This approach allows for a controlled evaluation of differences in response formulation, structure, and content, all within a shared context defined by the dataset’s scope and domain.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: MedAnswerSet, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below reports the results on MedAnswerSet and on OpenFactQA.

| System | Dataset | hallucination frequency (%) | BLEU (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | MedAnswerSet | 29.9 | 10.1 | 1.76 |
| retrieve-then-read model | MedAnswerSet | see text | 45.6 | 1.57 |
| RAG without reranking | MedAnswerSet | 47.2 | 32.8 | 3.42 |
| vanilla LLM | OpenFactQA | 41.1 | 26.2 | 3.17 |
| retrieve-then-read model | OpenFactQA | 28.4 | 11.5 | 0.44 |
| RAG without reranking | OpenFactQA | 13.2 | 43.2 | 2.35 |

On MedAnswerSet, the retrieve-then-read model scores 8.5 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

Comparing answer-generation conditions on MedAnswerSet raises questions about how representative the benchmark is of real-world medical inquiry. The specific selection of cases may not reflect the full diversity of clinical reasoning or patient contexts encountered in practice. As a result, patterns observed in this setting might not generalize to other medical domains or information needs. The structure of the benchmark could also influence how conditions are interpreted, potentially favoring certain response styles over others. Without broader validation, the implications of such comparisons remain uncertain, and further exploration across different datasets would be necessary to assess their wider relevance.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
