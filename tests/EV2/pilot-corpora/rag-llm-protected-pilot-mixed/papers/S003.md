# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

The purpose of comparing answer-generation conditions lies in understanding how different approaches influence the resulting outputs within a consistent evaluation framework. BioClaimQA serves as a central benchmark that establishes a shared foundation for such comparisons, offering a curated collection of biomedical claims and associated evidence. By anchoring the analysis to this dataset, the study aims to isolate variations in generation methods while maintaining a common context. This shared context enables a focused examination of how specific conditions shape answer characteristics, though the insights remain tied to the scope and structure of BioClaimQA.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question; we denote it by R. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer; we denote it by A1. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection; we denote it by A2.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; the tables abbreviate it as HR. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

### Implementation details

Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources.

Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on BioClaimQA.

| System | HR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 30.1 |
| R | 22.4 |
| A1 | 39.4 |
| A2 | 38.3 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Evaluating answer-generation conditions on BioClaimQA introduces constraints tied to the dataset's design and content focus. The benchmark may not capture the full diversity of biological claims or reasoning patterns encountered in broader scientific discourse. As a result, observed differences between conditions might reflect specific characteristics of the dataset rather than generalizable phenomena. The extent to which findings apply beyond this context remains uncertain. Additional evaluation across alternative datasets or domains would be necessary to assess the robustness of any observed patterns.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
