# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the generator without any document access with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

The purpose of comparing answer-generation conditions lies in understanding how different approaches influence the resulting outputs within a consistent evaluation framework. BioClaimQA serves as a central benchmark that establishes a shared foundation for such comparisons, offering a curated collection of biomedical claims and associated evidence. By anchoring the analysis to this dataset, the study aims to isolate variations in generation methods while maintaining a common context. This shared context enables a focused examination of how specific conditions shape answer characteristics, though the insights remain tied to the scope and structure of BioClaimQA.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On BioClaimQA, the generator without any document access reaches 301 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. On BioClaimQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 22.4%. With the model prompted to reason step by step, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on BioClaimQA came to 0.394. On BioClaimQA, the variant that reformulates the question before the search reaches 383 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement.

## Discussion

Evaluating answer-generation conditions on BioClaimQA introduces constraints tied to the dataset's design and content focus. The benchmark may not capture the full diversity of biological claims or reasoning patterns encountered in broader scientific discourse. As a result, observed differences between conditions might reflect specific characteristics of the dataset rather than generalizable phenomena. The extent to which findings apply beyond this context remains uncertain. Additional evaluation across alternative datasets or domains would be necessary to assess the robustness of any observed patterns.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
