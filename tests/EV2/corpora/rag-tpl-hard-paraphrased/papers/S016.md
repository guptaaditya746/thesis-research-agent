# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the configuration that looks up supporting documents reaches 305 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
