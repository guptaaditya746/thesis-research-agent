# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

For the language model used on its own, we observe 41.1% as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on GeoFacts. On GeoFacts, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first reaches 376 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. On GeoFacts, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the variant that reformulates the question before the search was 0.282. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, we observe 27.4% as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on GeoFacts.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
