# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the system grounded in retrieved passages on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the model that answers from its own parameters was 58.4%. On OpenFactQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the system grounded in retrieved passages was 0.499.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
