# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of the model that answers from its own parameters was 30.5%. In terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first corresponds to 174 out of 1,000 OpenFactQA questions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
