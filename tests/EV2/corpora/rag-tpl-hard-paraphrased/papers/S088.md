# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the system grounded in retrieved passages on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

With the plain generator with no lookup step, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on SciFactBench came to 31.7%. With the system grounded in retrieved passages, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on SciFactBench came to 0.225.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
