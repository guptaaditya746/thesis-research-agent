# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the system grounded in retrieved passages on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the plain generator with no lookup step reaches 595 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, we observe 0.701 as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on TriviaCheck. On TriviaCheck, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the model prompted to reason step by step was 72.4%. On TriviaCheck, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages reaches 692 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
