# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

With the plain generator with no lookup step, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on BioClaimQA came to 0.192. With the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on BioClaimQA came to 27.9%. In terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, the model prompted to reason step by step corresponds to 134 out of 1,000 BioClaimQA questions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
