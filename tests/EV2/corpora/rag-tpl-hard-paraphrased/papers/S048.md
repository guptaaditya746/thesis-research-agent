# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the language model used on its own with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the language model used on its own reaches 516 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct. On SciFactBench, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 50.7%. For the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, we observe 0.345 as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on SciFactBench. On SciFactBench, the generator adapted to the domain by further training reaches 777 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
