# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

In terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source, the generator without any document access corresponds to 214 out of 1,000 SciFactBench questions. With the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on SciFactBench came to 14.9%. With the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on SciFactBench came to 0.387. On SciFactBench, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages reaches 345 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
