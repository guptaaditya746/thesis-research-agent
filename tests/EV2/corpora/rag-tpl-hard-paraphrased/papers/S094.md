# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the generator without any document access was 0.416. With the configuration that looks up supporting documents, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on OpenFactQA came to 50.5%. In terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source, the generator adapted to the domain by further training corresponds to 373 out of 1,000 OpenFactQA questions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
