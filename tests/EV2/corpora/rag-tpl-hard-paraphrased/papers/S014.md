# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the system grounded in retrieved passages on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

For the generator without any document access, we observe 0.404 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on HistoryQuest. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, we observe 8.7% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on HistoryQuest.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
