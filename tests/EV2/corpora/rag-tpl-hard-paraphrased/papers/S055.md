# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On HistoryQuest, the plain generator with no lookup step reaches 729 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. With the model that samples several answers and votes, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on HistoryQuest came to 0.559. On HistoryQuest, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the variant fed with randomly drawn passages was 54.1%.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
