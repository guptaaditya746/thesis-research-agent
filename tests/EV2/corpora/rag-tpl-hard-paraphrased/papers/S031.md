# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

For the generator without any document access, we observe 0.243 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on BioClaimQA. In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the configuration that looks up supporting documents corresponds to 105 out of 1,000 BioClaimQA questions. For the model prompted to reason step by step, we observe 26.4% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on BioClaimQA. On BioClaimQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the model that samples several answers and votes was 0.252.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
