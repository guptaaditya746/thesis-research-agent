# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from MedAnswerSet

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the language model used on its own with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on MedAnswerSet. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on MedAnswerSet, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

On MedAnswerSet, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the language model used on its own was 0.179. In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the configuration that looks up supporting documents corresponds to 62 out of 1,000 MedAnswerSet questions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
