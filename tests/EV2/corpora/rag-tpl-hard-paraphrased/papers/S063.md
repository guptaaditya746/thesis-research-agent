# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the model that consults the document index before answering on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the language model used on its own corresponds to 173 out of 1,000 BioClaimQA questions. For the model that consults the document index before answering, we observe 9.5% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on BioClaimQA. For the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, we observe 0.357 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on BioClaimQA.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
