# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the generator without any document access with the model that consults the document index before answering on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

For the generator without any document access, we observe 0.384 as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on SciFactBench. In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the model that consults the document index before answering corresponds to 441 out of 1,000 SciFactBench questions. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, we observe 33.1% as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
