# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On BioClaimQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the model that answers from its own parameters was 35.4%. On BioClaimQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 0.412.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
