# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the model that consults the document index before answering on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the language model used on its own was 0.282. On TriviaCheck, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the model that consults the document index before answering was 33.6%. On TriviaCheck, the model prompted to reason step by step reaches 306 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. For the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, we observe 0.103 as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on TriviaCheck.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
