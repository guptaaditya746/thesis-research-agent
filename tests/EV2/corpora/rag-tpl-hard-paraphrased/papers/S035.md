# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the system grounded in retrieved passages on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the generator without any document access corresponds to 534 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions. With the system grounded in retrieved passages, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on HistoryQuest came to 0.648. For the model prompted to reason step by step, we observe 68.7% as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on HistoryQuest.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
