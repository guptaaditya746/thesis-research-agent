# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the model that consults the document index before answering on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the generator without any document access reaches 439 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. For the model that consults the document index before answering, we observe 0.352 as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
