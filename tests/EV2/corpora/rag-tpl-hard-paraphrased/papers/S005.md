# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the plain generator with no lookup step reaches 252 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. For the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, we observe 12.7% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on OpenFactQA. With the variant that reformulates the question before the search, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on OpenFactQA came to 0.369. On OpenFactQA, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker reaches 209 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
