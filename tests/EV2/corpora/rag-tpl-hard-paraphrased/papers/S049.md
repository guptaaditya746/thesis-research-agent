# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the plain generator with no lookup step was 28.9%. With the configuration that looks up supporting documents, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on LawFactQA came to 0.243. In terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source, the generator adapted to the domain by further training corresponds to 234 out of 1,000 LawFactQA questions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
