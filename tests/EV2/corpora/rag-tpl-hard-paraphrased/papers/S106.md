# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the model that consults the document index before answering on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

For the plain generator with no lookup step, we observe 37.3% as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on GeoFacts. On GeoFacts, the model that consults the document index before answering reaches 284 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. With the generator adapted to the domain by further training, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on GeoFacts came to 0.172. For the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, we observe 46.8% as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on GeoFacts.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
