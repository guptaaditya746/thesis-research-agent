# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the system grounded in retrieved passages on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

For the generator without any document access, we observe 77.1% as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on HistoryQuest. In terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, the system grounded in retrieved passages corresponds to 673 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
