# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the model that consults the document index before answering on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On GeoFacts, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of the language model used on its own was 0.215. On GeoFacts, the model that consults the document index before answering reaches 183 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. On GeoFacts, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of the variant fed with randomly drawn passages was 9.8%.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
