# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the generator without any document access with the system grounded in retrieved passages on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the generator without any document access reaches 736 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct. On TriviaCheck, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the system grounded in retrieved passages was 67.1%. With the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on TriviaCheck came to 0.356. On TriviaCheck, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages reaches 708 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
