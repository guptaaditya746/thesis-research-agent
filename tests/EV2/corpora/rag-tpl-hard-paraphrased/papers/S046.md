# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the system grounded in retrieved passages on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

For the model that answers from its own parameters, we observe 0.207 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on HistoryQuest. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, we observe 24.4% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on HistoryQuest. In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker corresponds to 98 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
