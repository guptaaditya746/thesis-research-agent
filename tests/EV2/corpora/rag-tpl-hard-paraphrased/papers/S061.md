# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the generator without any document access with the system grounded in retrieved passages on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

For the generator without any document access, we observe 66.7% as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on HistoryQuest. With the system grounded in retrieved passages, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on HistoryQuest came to 0.794. In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the model that samples several answers and votes corresponds to 514 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
