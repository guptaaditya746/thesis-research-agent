# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the model that consults the document index before answering on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On NewsTruthQA, the language model used on its own reaches 399 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. For the model that consults the document index before answering, we observe 0.308 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on NewsTruthQA. On NewsTruthQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the retrieval variant that skips the reranker was 41.4%.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
