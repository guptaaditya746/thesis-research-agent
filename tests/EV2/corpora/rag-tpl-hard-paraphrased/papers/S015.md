# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the plain generator with no lookup step reaches 626 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. On SciFactBench, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 0.635. For the model prompted to reason step by step, we observe 56.9% as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on SciFactBench. In terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker corresponds to 577 out of 1,000 SciFactBench questions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
