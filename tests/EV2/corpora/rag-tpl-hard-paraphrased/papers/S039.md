# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on MedAnswerSet

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on MedAnswerSet. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on MedAnswerSet, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

For the language model used on its own, we observe 43.4% as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on MedAnswerSet. On MedAnswerSet, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 0.496. In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the model prompted to reason step by step corresponds to 304 out of 1,000 MedAnswerSet questions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
