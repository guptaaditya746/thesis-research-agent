# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

In terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source, the model that answers from its own parameters corresponds to 241 out of 1,000 PopCultureQA questions. On PopCultureQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 9.5%. On PopCultureQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the model prompted to reason step by step was 0.304.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
