# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the generator without any document access with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

On HistoryQuest, the generator without any document access reaches 682 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct. On HistoryQuest, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 0.824. For the variant fed with randomly drawn passages, we observe 65.7% as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on HistoryQuest. In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the generator adapted to the domain by further training corresponds to 352 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
