# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the plain generator with no lookup step was 37.1%. For the configuration that looks up supporting documents, we observe 0.313 as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on LawFactQA. On LawFactQA, the variant that reformulates the question before the search reaches 416 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
