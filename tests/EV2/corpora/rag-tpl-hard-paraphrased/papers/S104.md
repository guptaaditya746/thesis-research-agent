# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the plain generator with no lookup step was 28.9%. With the configuration that looks up supporting documents, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on LawFactQA came to 0.243. On LawFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training reaches 234 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
