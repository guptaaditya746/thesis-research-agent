# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

In terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, the language model used on its own corresponds to 413 out of 1,000 TriviaCheck questions. On TriviaCheck, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 0.528. For the model prompted to reason step by step, we observe 55.4% as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on TriviaCheck. On TriviaCheck, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker reaches 763 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
