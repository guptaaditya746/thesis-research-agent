# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

With the language model used on its own, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on NewsTruthQA came to 47.7%. On NewsTruthQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 0.588. On NewsTruthQA, the model that samples several answers and votes reaches 411 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
