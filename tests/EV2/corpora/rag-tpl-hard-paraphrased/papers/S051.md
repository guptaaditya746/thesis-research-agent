# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the model that consults the document index before answering on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

In terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, the plain generator with no lookup step corresponds to 267 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions. With the model that consults the document index before answering, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on HistoryQuest came to 0.165.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
