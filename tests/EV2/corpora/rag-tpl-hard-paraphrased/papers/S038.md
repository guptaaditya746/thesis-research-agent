# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the language model used on its own with the system grounded in retrieved passages on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

With the language model used on its own, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on LawFactQA came to 36.8%. For the system grounded in retrieved passages, we observe 0.454 as the fraction of answers judged factually correct on LawFactQA. On LawFactQA, the model prompted to reason step by step reaches 709 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct. On LawFactQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the retrieval variant that skips the reranker was 68.2%.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
