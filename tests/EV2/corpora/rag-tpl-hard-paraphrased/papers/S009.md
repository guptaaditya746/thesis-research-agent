# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

For the model that answers from its own parameters, we observe 43.9% as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on LawFactQA. For the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, we observe 0.401 as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on LawFactQA. On LawFactQA, the model prompted to reason step by step reaches 392 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
