# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the language model used on its own with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the language model used on its own, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

With the language model used on its own, the fraction of answers judged factually correct on OpenFactQA came to 0.601. On OpenFactQA, the fraction of answers judged factually correct of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 59.3%. In terms of the fraction of answers judged factually correct, the model prompted to reason step by step corresponds to 457 out of 1,000 OpenFactQA questions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
