# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the model that samples several answers and votes, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of the model that answers from its own parameters was 44.6%. With the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on TriviaCheck came to 0.437. In terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, the model that samples several answers and votes corresponds to 305 out of 1,000 TriviaCheck questions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
