# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; the tables abbreviate it as HR. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

### Implementation details

Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index.

We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on NewsTruthQA.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
