# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source; we denote it by B. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts; the tables abbreviate it as AA. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

### Implementation details

Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions.

We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | AA (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 77.1 |
| R | 67.3 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
