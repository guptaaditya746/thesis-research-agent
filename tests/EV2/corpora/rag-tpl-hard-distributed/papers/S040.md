# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts; the tables abbreviate it as AA. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

### Implementation details

The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on TriviaCheck.

| R | 66.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
