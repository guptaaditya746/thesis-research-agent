# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; we denote it by R. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones; we denote it by A1.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; the tables abbreviate it as HR. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

### Implementation details

We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions.

Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | HR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 22.7 |
| R | 14.3 |
| A1 | 17.1 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
