# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question; we denote it by R. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs; we denote it by A1.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source; the tables abbreviate it as UAR. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

### Implementation details

Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | UAR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 30.3 |
| R | 23.7 |
| A1 | 12.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
