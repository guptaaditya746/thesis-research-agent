# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question; we denote it by R. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer; we denote it by A1.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts; the tables abbreviate it as HF. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

### Implementation details

The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers.

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on BioClaimQA.

| System | HF (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 19.2 |
| R | 27.9 |
| A1 | 13.4 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
