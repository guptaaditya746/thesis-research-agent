# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from BioClaimQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts; the tables abbreviate it as AA. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

### Implementation details

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
