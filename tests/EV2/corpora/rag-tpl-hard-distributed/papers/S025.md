# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on SciFactBench

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; the tables abbreviate it as HR. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

### Implementation details

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | HR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 33.6 |
| R | 37.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
