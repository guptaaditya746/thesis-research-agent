# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; we denote it by R. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs; we denote it by A1.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source; the tables abbreviate it as UAR. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

### Implementation details

The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on OpenFactQA.

| System | UAR (%) |

| A1 | 18.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
