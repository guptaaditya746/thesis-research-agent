# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents; we denote it by B. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found; we denote it by R. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order; we denote it by A1.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; the tables abbreviate it as HR. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

### Implementation details

We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers.

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | HR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 20.7 |
| R | 24.4 |
| A1 | 9.8 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
