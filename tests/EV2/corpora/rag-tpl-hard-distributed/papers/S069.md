# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question; we denote it by R. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones; we denote it by A1. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer; we denote it by A2.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts; the tables abbreviate it as AA. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

### Implementation details

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | AA (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 50.6 |
| R | 53.8 |
| A1 | 75.7 |
| A2 | 35.9 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
