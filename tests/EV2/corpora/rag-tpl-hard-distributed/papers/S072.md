# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source; the tables abbreviate it as UAR. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

### Implementation details

Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | UAR (%) |

| B | 36.6 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
