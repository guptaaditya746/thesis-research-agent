# Accuracy, Latency and Overlap Across Four Answer Conditions

## Abstract

This study compares four answer conditions on two question sets, LawFactQA and HistoryQuest, with LawFactQA as the main benchmark. Three metrics are reported: fact-level accuracy, latency and ROUGE-L. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. All conditions share one language model and one scoring protocol.

## Introduction

Answers are judged on several properties at once, including their factual content, the time needed to produce them and their overlap with a reference answer. Systems differ in how they gather material before answering, and these differences can be examined across all three properties. The aim of this study is to compare conditions that differ in this respect on every measure. LawFactQA is the main benchmark, and HistoryQuest is included as a second dataset.

## Method

Four conditions were compared. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The query-rewriting variant rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. RAG without reranking uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Three metrics were recorded. Fact-level accuracy is the fraction of answers judged factually correct, reported in percent. Latency is the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. ROUGE-L is the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer, reported in percent.

Each condition was run on every question of both datasets under the same settings.

## Discussion

The study uses two datasets, and LawFactQA is the main one, so HistoryQuest is treated as a secondary dataset. Each condition was run once per question, and latency was measured on shared hardware without repeated timing runs. ROUGE-L measures lexical overlap with a reference answer and does not check factual agreement directly. The conditions were configured with fixed settings that were not tuned on either dataset.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This study compared four conditions on two question sets using fact-level accuracy, latency and ROUGE-L. Future studies could add further datasets and repeated timing runs.
