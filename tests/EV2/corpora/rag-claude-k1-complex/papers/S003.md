# Two Answer Models on Two Question Sets

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of two conditions on two question sets. LawFactQA is the main benchmark in this work, and NewsTruthQA is a second dataset, each containing 1,000 questions answered under the same protocol. Each condition was scored on fact-level accuracy and ROUGE-L, using identical prompts, decoding settings and scoring rules. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

Answering questions about legal matters and current news requires answers whose facts can be checked against sources. A model can rely on knowledge stored in its parameters, or it can be given passages from a reference collection before it answers. Published work often reports one system at a time, which complicates a reader's view of how different designs were scored on the same questions.

This paper compares two conditions on a shared footing, using one protocol and two metrics, so that the scores can be read side by side. The aim is to compare the conditions, and the comparison is presented as a study design rather than as a general account of question answering.

## Method

The study compares two conditions. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Two metrics were recorded. Fact-level accuracy is the fraction of answers judged factually correct, expressed as a percentage. ROUGE-L is the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer, expressed as a percentage.

Two datasets were used. LawFactQA serves as the main benchmark, and NewsTruthQA serves as a second dataset. Each dataset contains 1,000 questions, and every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header. The same maximum output length was applied to every condition, and answers were collected once and were not revised after scoring.

Annotators checked a sample of answers against reference facts before the full set was scored. Decoding settings, prompt wording and the order of questions were held fixed across runs.

## Discussion

This study uses two datasets and a single run per condition. The set of conditions is small, which keeps the design easy to audit but leaves many other designs outside its scope. Both metrics are correctness or overlap measures and do not capture every aspect of an answer. The protocol was fixed in advance, and the study reports no uncertainty estimates. Future work could extend the protocol to further datasets and report intervals alongside each figure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. The study offers a controlled comparison of two conditions on LawFactQA, the main benchmark, and on NewsTruthQA under one protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
