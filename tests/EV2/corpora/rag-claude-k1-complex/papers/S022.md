# Answer Quality and Response Time for Legal and History Questions

## Abstract

This study compares several answer conditions on legal questions and on history questions, recording fact-level accuracy, latency and ROUGE-L for each condition. The main benchmark is LawFactQA, and HistoryQuest is a secondary dataset. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. The values are given in a table in the Results section, and the design and its limitations are discussed afterwards.

## Introduction

Answer systems for specialist and general questions are evaluated on several properties at once, since practical use depends on more than one property of an answer. The aim of this study is to compare answer conditions across these properties and across more than one dataset. The main benchmark is a legal dataset, and the secondary dataset covers history. Each condition answers the same questions within a dataset, so that its values can be read against those of the other conditions. The measures and conditions are described in the Method section.

## Method

LawFactQA is the main benchmark and consists of 1,000 questions drawn from legal reference material. HistoryQuest is a secondary dataset of 1,000 questions drawn from historical reference material. Each condition answers every question in both datasets.

The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The query-rewriting variant rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. RAG without reranking uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

The metric named fact-level accuracy is the fraction of answers judged factually correct, reported as a percentage. The metric named latency is the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. The metric named ROUGE-L is the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer, reported as a percentage.

## Discussion

Timing values depend on the hardware used, and the runs were made on a shared server, so the latency figures reflect those conditions. The study covers one legal dataset and one history dataset, and results from other domains may differ. Judgements in the study depend on the reference material and on the reviewers who apply the criteria. The ROUGE-L measure depends on overlap with the wording of the reference answer and is limited in what it captures. Future work could extend the comparison to additional datasets and to dedicated hardware.

## Conclusion

This study reports fact-level accuracy, latency and ROUGE-L for several answer conditions on a legal dataset and a history dataset. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Further work should extend the comparison to more datasets and to controlled hardware before the timing values are used for planning.
