# Comparing Answer Generation Conditions for Scientific Question Answering

## Abstract

This study compares two conditions for answering scientific questions: a vanilla LLM and a retrieval-augmented generator. Both conditions were evaluated on SciFactBench and OpenFactQA using hallucination rate, latency and ROUGE-L as metrics. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The results are reported separately for each dataset and discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Question answering over scientific literature requires answers that are fluent, accurate and faithful to their sources. Large language models can produce convincing text without consulting any document, so their statements are difficult to verify when no evidence accompanies them. Many research prototypes now pair a language model with a search component over a reference collection, which invites a comparison with the vanilla LLM on the same questions. The aim of this study is to compare conditions on a shared set of questions and metrics, so that differences in hallucination rate, response time and overlap with reference answers can be read side by side. SciFactBench serves as the main benchmark for this comparison. OpenFactQA is included as a second dataset, which allows the comparison to be examined on questions drawn from a different source.

## Method

The vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. Both conditions were run on the same question sets. SciFactBench contains 1,000 questions and is the main benchmark. OpenFactQA contains 1,000 questions and serves as the second benchmark.

Each answer was assessed on three metrics. The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, reported in percent. The latency is the mean number of seconds needed to produce one answer, reported in seconds. ROUGE-L is the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer, reported in percent.

Annotators judged each answer for unsupported statements using the same guidelines for both conditions. All conditions were timed on the same hardware, and all runs used identical decoding settings. The protocol was fixed before the runs began, and no question was removed after the results were collected.

## Results

The results for each dataset are summarised in the table below. Each row gives one condition on one dataset. The cell for the retrieval-augmented generator on SciFactBench is reported in the sentence that follows the table.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| vanilla LLM | SciFactBench | 40.4 | 2.13 | 23.6 |

| vanilla LLM | OpenFactQA | 41.2 | 0.89 | 27.6 |

On SciFactBench, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 31.7 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The study uses a fixed set of questions from two benchmarks, which limits how far the findings can be generalised beyond these collections. Judgements about unsupported statements were made by annotators who followed shared guidelines, and their decisions may differ on borderline answers. Timing was carried out on a single hardware setup, so absolute response times are specific to that environment. ROUGE-L captures lexical overlap with a reference answer and does not capture every aspect of factual correctness. The design compares two conditions only and does not include other model families or other forms of answer checking. Future studies could extend the protocol with additional datasets and a second round of independent annotation, which would make the agreement between annotators easier to document.

## Conclusion

This paper compared a vanilla LLM and a retrieval-augmented generator on SciFactBench and OpenFactQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The protocol, the metric definitions and the table of results are described in enough detail for other groups to repeat the comparison with additional conditions.
