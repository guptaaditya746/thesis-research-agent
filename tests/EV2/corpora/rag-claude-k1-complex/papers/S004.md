# Answer Accuracy Across Two Question Sets

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of two conditions on two question sets. LawFactQA is the main benchmark in this work, and GeoFacts is a second dataset, each containing 1,000 questions answered under the same protocol. Each condition was scored on answer accuracy, latency and BLEU, using identical prompts, decoding settings and scoring rules. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

Question answering about legal and geographic facts is demanding, because an answer has to agree with the content of statutes and reference works. A model can answer from knowledge stored in its parameters, or it can be given documents that it reads before it answers. Reports on these designs often describe a single configuration, which complicates a reader's view of how different designs were scored on the same questions.

This paper sets out to compare two conditions on a shared footing, using one protocol and three metrics, so that the scores can be read side by side. The aim is to compare the conditions, and the comparison is presented as a study design rather than as a general account of question answering.

## Method

The study compares two conditions. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieve-then-read model looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Three metrics were recorded. Answer accuracy is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, expressed as a percentage of the full question set. Latency is the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. BLEU is the n-gram overlap with the reference answer, expressed as a percentage.

Two datasets were used. LawFactQA serves as the main benchmark, and GeoFacts serves as a second dataset. Each dataset contains 1,000 questions, and every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header. The same maximum output length was applied to every condition, and answers were collected once and were not revised after scoring.

Annotators checked a sample of answers against reference facts before the full set was scored. Decoding settings, prompt wording and the order of questions were held fixed across runs, and the scoring rules were fixed in advance.

## Discussion

This study uses two datasets and a single run per condition, so its values describe one protocol and not a range of settings. The set of conditions is small, which keeps the design simple to audit but leaves other designs outside its scope. The three metrics capture factual correctness, timing and overlap with a reference answer, and none of them captures every aspect of an answer. Latency was measured on one shared setup, so figures from other hardware would need separate measurement. Future work could extend the protocol to further datasets and report uncertainty estimates alongside each value.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. The study offers a controlled comparison of two conditions on LawFactQA and GeoFacts under a single protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
