# Output Quality Across Conditions for Legal and General Questions

## Abstract

This study compares several answer conditions on legal questions and on general questions, scoring each condition with the hallucination rate and with BLEU. The main benchmark is LawFactQA, and OpenFactQA serves as a secondary dataset. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The full set of values is given in a table in the Results section, and the design of the study is discussed afterwards.

## Introduction

Answer systems for law and for general knowledge are often evaluated with a mix of different scores, and each score captures a different property of an answer. The aim of this study is to compare answer conditions on both kinds of questions using complementary scores. Comparing the conditions on a legal benchmark and on a general benchmark allows the pattern of values to be seen across domains. The conditions and the scores are described in the Method section.

## Method

LawFactQA is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions drawn from legal reference material. OpenFactQA is a secondary dataset that contains 1,000 questions drawn from general reference material. Each condition is applied to the questions of both datasets under the same settings, and the outputs are scored without reference to the condition that produced them.

The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. In chain-of-thought prompting, the model is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, reported as a percentage. BLEU is the n-gram overlap with the reference answer, also reported as a percentage. Both scores are computed from the same set of answers for every condition on each dataset.

## Discussion

The study uses the hallucination rate and BLEU, and each is limited in what it captures. Overlap scores depend on the wording of the reference answer, and rate scores depend on the reviewers and the reference texts. Only one legal dataset and one general dataset are included, so the design cannot show how other domains behave. The conditions were run with fixed prompts and fixed decoding settings, and other settings were not examined. Readers should read each value in the table with its dataset and condition in view. Future work could add further datasets and repeated runs.

## Conclusion

This study compares answer conditions on legal and general questions using the hallucination rate and BLEU. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Further evaluation on additional datasets would be needed before these findings are applied more widely.
