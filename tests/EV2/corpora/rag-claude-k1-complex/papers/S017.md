# Comparing Four Answer Generation Strategies on Two Factual Question Benchmarks

## Abstract

This study compares four conditions for answering factual questions: a no-retrieval baseline, a RAG pipeline, a fine-tuned model and self-consistency decoding. The conditions were evaluated on PopCultureQA and LawFactQA using hallucination frequency and BLEU. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The results are reported separately for each dataset and discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Factual question answering is sensitive to invented content, and a model that answers without any external support may state details that cannot be verified. Several strategies exist for answering such questions, including lookup over a reference collection, further training on in-domain material and sampling several answers before voting. The aim of this study is to compare these conditions on the same questions, so that their hallucination frequency and their overlap with reference answers can be read side by side. PopCultureQA serves as the main benchmark of the study, and LawFactQA is included as a second dataset with a different subject matter.

## Method

The no-retrieval baseline receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The RAG pipeline first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The self-consistency decoding condition samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. Each condition was run on the 1,000 questions of PopCultureQA and on the 1,000 questions of LawFactQA.

The hallucination frequency is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, reported in percent. BLEU is the n-gram overlap with the reference answer, reported in percent. Annotators judged each answer for invented facts using the same guidelines for every condition, and each judgement was recorded with the question it belonged to. The BLEU values were computed automatically against the reference answers. Decoding settings were held constant wherever the condition allowed, and the protocol was fixed before the runs began.

## Discussion

The limitations of the design are as follows. Two datasets were used, and each contains 1,000 questions, which limits the scope of the comparison. Judgements of invented facts were made by annotators, whose decisions may differ on borderline answers. BLEU rewards n-gram overlap with a single reference answer and does not capture every aspect of factual correctness. Hyperparameters for the fine-tuned model and the sampling settings for self-consistency decoding were chosen once and were not tuned separately for each dataset. The conditions were also run on questions from only two subject areas. Future studies could add datasets from other domains and report agreement between annotators.

## Conclusion

This paper compared four conditions on PopCultureQA and LawFactQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The metric definitions, the condition descriptions and the reported values are given so that other groups can repeat the comparison with additional conditions.
