# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 29.5 |
| retrieval-augmented model | 17.8 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 9.4 |
| fine-tuned model | 47.1 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
