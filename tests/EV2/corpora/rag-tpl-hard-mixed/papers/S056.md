# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The no-retrieval baseline shows 28.2% unsupported-answer rate on TriviaCheck. The retrieval-augmented model shows 33.6% unsupported-answer rate on TriviaCheck. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 30.6% unsupported-answer rate on TriviaCheck. We measure 10.3% unsupported-answer rate for the RAG with random passages on TriviaCheck.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
