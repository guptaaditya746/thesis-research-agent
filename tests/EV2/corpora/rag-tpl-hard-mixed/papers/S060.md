# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On BioClaimQA, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 7.4% hallucination frequency.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on BioClaimQA.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 19.2 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 27.9 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 13.4 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
