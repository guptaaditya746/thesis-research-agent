# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents; we denote it by B. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct; the tables abbreviate it as FLA. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

### Implementation details

Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name.

Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on OpenFactQA.

| System | FLA (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 58.4 |
| R | 49.9 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
