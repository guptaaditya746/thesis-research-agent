# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On GeoFacts, the retrieval-augmented model reaches 38.2% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on GeoFacts.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 37.3 |
| retrieval-augmented model | 28.4 |
| fine-tuned model | 17.2 |
| RAG with random passages | 46.8 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
