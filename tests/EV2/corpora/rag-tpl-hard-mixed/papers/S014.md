# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: HistoryQuest, our main benchmark, and PopCultureQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on HistoryQuest and on PopCultureQA.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | main comparison | HistoryQuest | 40.4 | 37.8 | 2.86 |
| RAG pipeline | main comparison | HistoryQuest | see text | 19.6 | 0.77 |
| parametric-only model | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 41.2 | 51.6 | 2.48 |
| RAG pipeline | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 7.7 | 23.1 | 1.43 |

In the main comparison on HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline scores 31.7 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
