# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The vanilla LLM shows 44.2% fact-level accuracy on PopCultureQA. We measure 81.8% fact-level accuracy for the RAG pipeline on PopCultureQA. On PopCultureQA, the fine-tuned model has 56.7% fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
