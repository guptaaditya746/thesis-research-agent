# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the parametric-only model has 78.3% fact-level accuracy. On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model has 88.6% fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
