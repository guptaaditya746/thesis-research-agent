# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the system grounded in retrieved passages on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

For the plain generator with no lookup step, we observe 20.1% as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on OpenFactQA. On OpenFactQA, the system grounded in retrieved passages reaches 262 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. With the generator adapted to the domain by further training, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on OpenFactQA came to 0.365. With the variant that reformulates the question before the search, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts on OpenFactQA came to 8.6%.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
