# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and GeoFacts; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on LawFactQA and on GeoFacts.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | main comparison | LawFactQA | 22.7 | 27.5 | 37.4 |
| retrieval-augmented model | main comparison | LawFactQA | see text | 43.9 | 38.5 |
| RAG with random passages | main comparison | LawFactQA | 17.1 | 49.6 | 18.9 |
| parametric-only model | main comparison | GeoFacts | 18.1 | 21.1 | 36.4 |
| retrieval-augmented model | main comparison | GeoFacts | 6.7 | 31.8 | 47.1 |
| RAG with random passages | main comparison | GeoFacts | 34.2 | 50.6 | 19.7 |

In the main comparison on LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 8.4 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
