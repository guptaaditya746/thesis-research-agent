# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the model that consults the document index before answering on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

With the model that answers from its own parameters, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on LawFactQA came to 28.5%. On LawFactQA, the model that consults the document index before answering reaches 252 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. On LawFactQA, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the retrieval variant that skips the reranker was 0.448.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
