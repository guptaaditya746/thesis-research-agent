# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the generator without any document access with the system grounded in retrieved passages on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the retrieval variant that skips the reranker, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the generator without any document access was 0.752. On TriviaCheck, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts of the system grounded in retrieved passages was 71.9%. On TriviaCheck, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker reaches 326 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the proportion of questions answered with correct facts.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
