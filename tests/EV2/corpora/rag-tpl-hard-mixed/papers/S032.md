# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the model that consults the document index before answering on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the model that answers from its own parameters corresponds to 322 out of 1,000 TriviaCheck questions. With the model that consults the document index before answering, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on TriviaCheck came to 0.314. On TriviaCheck, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the generator adapted to the domain by further training was 44.2%.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
