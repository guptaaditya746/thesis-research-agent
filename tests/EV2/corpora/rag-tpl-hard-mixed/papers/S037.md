# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the variant that reads retrieved evidence first on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

In terms of the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source, the plain generator with no lookup step corresponds to 303 out of 1,000 HistoryQuest questions. On HistoryQuest, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of the variant that reads retrieved evidence first was 23.7%. For the generator adapted to the domain by further training, we observe 0.122 as the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source on HistoryQuest.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
