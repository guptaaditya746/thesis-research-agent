# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

On NewsTruthQA, the parametric-only model has 42.7% hallucination rate. We measure 5.6% hallucination rate for the retrieval-augmented model on NewsTruthQA.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
