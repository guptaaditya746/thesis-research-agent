# Unsupported Statements in Answers to Open-Domain Factual Questions

## Abstract

This study compares three conditions for answering open-domain factual questions: a generator without any document access, a configuration that looks up supporting documents and a generator adapted to the domain by further training. Each condition was evaluated on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. The results are discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Factual question answering systems are often judged by how many of their answers contain unsupported statements. Systems differ in how much they rely on documents when they answer, and this difference is worth examining under a single protocol. The aim of this study is to compare three conditions on the same questions, so that the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement can be read directly for each condition. OpenFactQA serves as the main benchmark of the study, and the comparison is reported on it alone.

## Method

The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The configuration that looks up supporting documents looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The 1,000 questions of OpenFactQA form the benchmark for the study.

The share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement is reported in percent. Annotators judged each answer against the reference material using the same guidelines for every condition. The protocol was fixed before the analysis began, and each judgement was stored with the question and answer it concerned. Decoding settings were held as constant as each condition allowed, and the same annotation guidelines were applied throughout.

## Results

On OpenFactQA, the configuration that looks up supporting documents has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 399 out of 1,000.

On OpenFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.182.

## Discussion

The study uses one benchmark and one metric, which limits how far its findings can be generalised beyond OpenFactQA. Judgements of unsupported statements depend on annotators applying shared guidelines, and their decisions may vary on borderline answers. The comparison covers three conditions only and does not include other model families. Decoding settings and training choices were fixed for each condition, so the design does not show how the conditions behave under other settings. Future studies could add datasets from other domains and document agreement between annotators in more detail.

## Conclusion

This paper compared three conditions on OpenFactQA. Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. The protocol, the metric definition and the condition descriptions are given in enough detail for other groups to repeat the comparison with additional datasets.
