# Comparing Two Conditions on Unsupported Statements in Scientific Answers

## Abstract

This study compares two conditions for answering scientific questions: a plain generator with no lookup step and a variant that reads retrieved evidence first. The share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement was measured on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The results are discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Scientific question answering depends on answers that remain faithful to the literature they describe. Unsupported statements are a particular concern because they can look plausible to a reader who has not checked the sources. The aim of this study is to compare two conditions under one protocol, so that the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement can be read directly for each condition. SciFactBench serves as the main benchmark of the study, and it is the only dataset used.

## Method

The plain generator with no lookup step is prompted with the question and nothing else. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. Each condition was applied to the 1,000 questions of SciFactBench.

The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, reported in percent. Annotators judged each answer against the reference material using the same guidelines for both conditions. Decoding settings were held fixed across the two conditions, and no question was excluded after the runs began. Each judgement was recorded together with the question and the answer it concerned, so that the decisions can be audited against the source material.

## Discussion

The study covers one benchmark and one metric, which limits how far its findings can be generalised. The protocol depends on annotators applying shared guidelines, and their decisions on borderline answers may vary. Decoding settings were fixed, so the design does not show how the two conditions behave under other settings. The questions were drawn from a single dataset, and no other scientific collection was included. Agreement between annotators was not measured separately. A future design could add datasets and a second annotation round to examine agreement and to test whether the pattern holds on other collections.

## Conclusion

This paper compared two conditions on SciFactBench. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The protocol and metric definitions are described in enough detail for other groups to repeat the comparison with additional datasets.
