# Evidence Access Variants in Legal Question Answering

## Abstract

This study compares several answer conditions on legal questions and measures, for each condition, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The conditions vary in whether they see any external evidence, in how the question is prepared before a search, and in whether the retrieved passages are reranked. The main benchmark is LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. The values for each condition are given in the Results section, and the design of the study is discussed afterwards.

## Introduction

Language models are increasingly used to answer legal questions, where a wrong statement can have serious consequences for the reader. Several designs have been proposed that supply the model with material from outside its own parameters, and these designs differ in how that material is found and presented. The aim of this study is to compare the conditions side by side on one benchmark. All conditions receive the same questions and are scored by the same judges, so that differences between the conditions can be examined directly. The comparison is controlled, and the protocol is set out in the Method section.

## Method

The study uses LawFactQA as its main benchmark, which consists of 1,000 questions. Every condition is applied to every question. The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The variant that reformulates the question before the search rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. The retrieval variant that skips the reranker uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

The outcome is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Judges read each answer against the reference material and decide whether it is correct. Judging is carried out without knowledge of the condition that produced each answer, and disagreements are discussed before a final decision is recorded. All conditions share the same prompt wording for the answer and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The values for the main benchmark are reported in the following sentences.

On LawFactQA, the generator without any document access has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 50.5%.

On LawFactQA, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 571 out of 1,000.

On LawFactQA, the variant that reformulates the question before the search has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 0.781.

On LawFactQA, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 55.8%.

## Discussion

This study has limitations of design. It uses one main benchmark and one judging procedure, so its findings describe that setting only. Judges work from the reference material, and their decisions about correctness depend on that material. Each condition is run once with a fixed template, and other templates were not tested. The study does not estimate how stable the outcomes are across repeated runs. Future studies could add further benchmarks, additional judges and repeated runs to examine how stable the outcomes are under different conditions of judging.

## Conclusion

This study gives a controlled comparison of answer conditions for legal questions, using one benchmark and one judging protocol. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Further work should test the conditions on additional datasets before the results are used to guide the choice of a system.
