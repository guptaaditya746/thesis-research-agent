# Profiles of Unsupported Content Across Legal Question Answering Conditions

## Abstract

This study compares several answer-generation conditions on legal questions and reports, for each condition, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The conditions differ in their access to an external collection, in whether they reason step by step, and in whether they were trained further on in-domain material. The main benchmark is LawFactQA, which contains questions drawn from legal reference texts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The findings are reported in the Results section, and the design and its limitations are discussed afterwards.

## Introduction

Legal question answering demands careful attention to sourcing, because a single unsupported statement can mislead a reader about what the law requires. Language models answer such questions from knowledge stored in their parameters, and a range of additional procedures has been proposed to shape how answers are produced. The aim of this study is to compare conditions that differ in their document access, their prompting style and their training, under one shared protocol. Each condition answers the same questions and is scored with the same procedure, so that the conditions can be set side by side on a common footing. The study is framed as a controlled comparison rather than as a survey of available systems, and the protocol is described in the Method section.

## Method

The study uses LawFactQA as its main benchmark. This benchmark consists of 1,000 questions drawn from legal reference material, and every condition is applied to the full set. The conditions compared are described below. The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The model that consults the document index before answering retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The model prompted to reason step by step is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

The outcome is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. An answer is counted when a reviewer finds at least one statement that the reference material does not support. Reviewers work independently on each answer, and disagreements are settled by discussion. All conditions use the same prompt wording for the question and the same decoding settings, so that the intended difference between conditions is the component named in its description.

## Results

The values for the main benchmark are reported in the following sentences.

On LawFactQA, the generator without any document access has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 295 out of 1,000.

On LawFactQA, the model that consults the document index before answering has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 17.8%.

On LawFactQA, the model prompted to reason step by step has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.094.

On LawFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 471 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

The design of this study has several limitations that readers should keep in mind. Only one main benchmark is used, and it is built from legal reference material, so the scope of the findings is limited to that setting. The judgement of whether a statement is supported depends on the reference texts available to reviewers, and borderline cases remain open to disagreement. Each condition was run with a fixed prompt template and a fixed decoding configuration, and other choices of template or configuration were not examined. The study also relies on a single run of each condition. Future work could extend the protocol to additional collections and to other question types.

## Conclusion

This study provides a controlled comparison of answer conditions on legal questions, using one shared protocol and one main benchmark. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Further work should test the same conditions on additional datasets and with additional reviewers before the results are used to guide system design.
