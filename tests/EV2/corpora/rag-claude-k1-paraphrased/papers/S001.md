# Unsupported Statements in Answers to Legal Questions

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of answering conditions on legal questions. LawFactQA is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under a shared protocol. Each condition was scored on the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

Legal question answering is demanding because an answer has to agree with statutes, rulings and commentary. A language model can answer from its parameters alone, can be shown documents before it answers, can be asked to reason step by step, or can be trained further on domain material. Reports on these systems often describe a particular setup, which makes it difficult to see how the options were scored on the same questions. The aim of this paper is to compare the generator without any document access, the model that consults the document index before answering, the model prompted to reason step by step and the generator adapted to the domain by further training, on a shared benchmark with a shared metric.

## Method

The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The model that consults the document index before answering retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The model prompted to reason step by step is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, expressed as a percentage of the question set.

All questions were taken from LawFactQA, which is the main benchmark. Every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header, and the same maximum output length was applied throughout. Answers were collected without revision after scoring, so that no condition received additional attempts. Annotators checked a sample of the answers against reference facts before the full set was scored, and the scoring rules were fixed in advance. Decoding settings and question order were held fixed across conditions.

## Results

Each condition is reported in its own sentence below.

On LawFactQA, the generator without any document access has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 295 out of 1,000.

On LawFactQA, the model that consults the document index before answering has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 17.8%.

On LawFactQA, the model prompted to reason step by step has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.094.

On LawFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 471 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

The study is limited to a single benchmark of legal questions, so its values should not be read as describing other legal tasks or other jurisdictions. The metric counts answers that contain at least one unsupported statement and does not measure how many statements each answer contains. Judgements depend on the reference facts, which may be incomplete for some questions. The set of conditions is narrow, and many other designs lie outside the scope of this protocol. Future studies could extend the protocol to further benchmarks and report uncertainty estimates for each value.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison on LawFactQA, the main benchmark, under a shared protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication on further benchmarks. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
