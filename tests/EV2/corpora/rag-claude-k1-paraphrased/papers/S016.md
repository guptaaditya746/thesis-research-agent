# Invented Facts in Historical Question Answering

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of answering strategies on historical questions. HistoryQuest is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under a shared protocol. Each condition was scored on the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

Historical question answering requires answers that agree with the documentary record, and an invented detail can be hard to spot inside a fluent reply. Language models can answer such questions from their parameters alone, and they can also be given evidence before they answer. Reports on these systems often describe a particular setup, which makes it difficult to see how different strategies were scored on the same questions. The aim of this paper is to compare the language model used on its own, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages and the variant that reformulates the question before the search, using a shared benchmark and a shared metric.

## Method

The language model used on its own receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The variant fed with randomly drawn passages receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. The variant that reformulates the question before the search rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

The metric is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, expressed as a percentage of the question set. Judgements were made by annotators working from the reference collection, and the judgement rules were fixed before any answers were scored.

All questions were taken from HistoryQuest, which is the main benchmark. Every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header, and the same maximum output length was applied throughout. Answers were collected without revision after scoring, so that no condition received additional attempts. Decoding settings were identical across conditions, and the order of questions was held fixed.

## Results

Each condition is reported in its own sentence below.

On HistoryQuest, the language model used on its own has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 103 out of 1,000.

On HistoryQuest, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 0.051.

On HistoryQuest, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 41.2%.

On HistoryQuest, the variant that reformulates the question before the search has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 304 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

The study is limited to a single benchmark that covers historical questions, so its values should not be read as describing other historical tasks or other periods. The metric summarises whether the facts in an answer are judged to be invented, without capturing other aspects of answer quality such as clarity or completeness. Judgements depend on the reference collection, which may be incomplete for some questions. The set of conditions is narrow, and many other designs lie outside the scope of this protocol. Future studies could extend the protocol to further benchmarks and report uncertainty estimates for each value.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison of answering strategies on HistoryQuest under a shared protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
