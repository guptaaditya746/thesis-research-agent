# Answer Quality Across Four Conditions on a Popular Culture Benchmark

## Abstract

This study compares four conditions for answering questions about popular culture: a language model used on its own, a system grounded in retrieved passages, a generator adapted to the domain by further training and a model that samples several answers and votes. The fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was measured on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The results are discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Questions about popular culture mix well-known facts with obscure details, and language models often answer the obscure ones with confidence. Several strategies exist for producing answers to such questions. These include lookup over a reference collection, further training on in-domain material and sampling several answers before voting. The aim of this study is to compare these four conditions on the same questions, so that the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts can be read side by side for each condition. PopCultureQA serves as the main benchmark of the study, and the comparison is reported on it.

## Method

The language model used on its own receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The system grounded in retrieved passages first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The model that samples several answers and votes samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. All four conditions were applied to the 1,000 questions of PopCultureQA.

The fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts is reported in percent. Annotators judged each answer using the same guidelines for every condition. Decoding settings were recorded for each run, and the protocol was fixed before the runs began. No question was removed after the results were collected, and each judgement was stored together with the answer it concerned.

## Results

The values for the four conditions on PopCultureQA are reported in the four sentences below.

On PopCultureQA, the language model used on its own has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 0.414.

On PopCultureQA, the system grounded in retrieved passages has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 108 out of 1,000.

On PopCultureQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 29.8%.

On PopCultureQA, the model that samples several answers and votes has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 0.191.

## Discussion

The study uses one benchmark and one metric, which limits how far its findings can be generalised beyond PopCultureQA. Judgements of invented facts depend on annotators, whose decisions may differ on borderline answers. The four conditions were run with their own settings, and the comparison does not examine how those settings interact with the questions. The design covers popular culture only, so other subject areas were not included. Agreement between annotators was not measured separately, and the design does not report answer length or response time. Future work could add datasets from other domains and document annotator agreement in more detail.

## Conclusion

This paper compared four conditions on PopCultureQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The metric definition, the condition descriptions and the reported values are given so that other groups can repeat the comparison with their own questions.
