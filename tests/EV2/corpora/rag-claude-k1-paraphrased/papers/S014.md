# Factual Correctness of Two Answering Conditions in Biomedicine

## Abstract

This study compares two conditions for answering biomedical questions: a model that answers from its own parameters and a model that consults the document index before answering. The proportion of questions answered with correct facts was measured on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate. The results are discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Biomedical question answering requires answers whose specific facts are correct, since a single wrong detail can change the meaning of a clinical or research statement. Systems in this area are often compared under different access to documents at answer time. The aim of this study is to compare these two conditions under one protocol, so that the proportion of questions answered with correct facts can be read directly for each condition. Accuracy at the level of individual facts is a demanding standard, and it can be assessed without inspecting the full text of every answer. BioClaimQA serves as the main benchmark of the study.

## Method

The model that answers from its own parameters answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The model that consults the document index before answering retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The 1,000 questions of BioClaimQA form the main benchmark for the study.

The proportion of questions answered with correct facts is reported in percent. Answers were checked against the facts in the benchmark by annotators who followed a shared guideline. The protocol was documented before any answers were checked, and the annotators recorded each decision together with the fact that was examined. Decoding settings were fixed in advance for each condition.

## Discussion

The design uses a single benchmark of 1,000 questions and a single metric, which limits how far the findings can be generalised to other biomedical collections. Judgements of correct facts depend on annotators reading the same reference material and applying the same guideline, and disagreement on borderline answers is possible. The protocol was documented in advance, but the study did not include a second annotation round. The design also does not examine the particular pre-training data of the first condition or the particular index used by the second. Future work could extend the benchmark, add further metrics and document annotator agreement more fully.

## Conclusion

This paper compared two conditions on BioClaimQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. The protocol, the metric definition and the benchmark description are given in enough detail for other groups to repeat the comparison on further biomedical collections.
