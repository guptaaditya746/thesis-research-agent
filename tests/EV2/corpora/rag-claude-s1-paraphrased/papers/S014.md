# Answers from Parameters and from a Document Index in Biomedical Claims

## Abstract

Biomedical claims are difficult to verify from memory, and designers have asked whether answers should rest on a model's own parameters or on a document index. This paper reports a controlled comparison of these positions on BioClaimQA, which is the main benchmark. The metric is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, reported in percent. Questions, scoring and reporting are identical across conditions, and judging rules are given in the Method section. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate. The values appear in the Results section.

## Introduction

Biomedical question answering requires care, because an answer that sounds plausible may conflict with the published literature. Clinical and research users may act on such answers, so the origin of each answer deserves close attention. Model designers have considered whether an answer should come from knowledge stored during pre-training or from a document index consulted at answer time. Each option has practical costs, including the upkeep of the index and the time needed to consult it. The aim of this study is to compare these positions under a shared protocol. The protocol keeps the questions, the scoring and the reporting fixed, so that the comparison isolates the source of the answer. The following sections describe the benchmark, the conditions, the results and the design limits.

## Method

BioClaimQA contains 1,000 questions and serves as the main benchmark. The questions were drawn from curated summaries of published work, and the reference facts were fixed before any answers were generated. In the model that answers from its own parameters, the model answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. In the model that consults the document index before answering, the model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. Prompt wording was held constant across the designs, and judges were instructed to treat partial matches as incorrect. Each answer is judged against reference facts by a fixed procedure. The metric is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, reported in percent.

## Discussion

The design has limits. The benchmark is a single biomedical set of questions, so the values describe that set only. The benchmark was assembled from a single subfield, which limits how far the values transfer to other areas of medicine. The reference facts were compiled by the study team and may omit valid alternatives. Judging was carried out by a fixed procedure without independent checks, so its reliability was not measured. The document index was built at the start of the study and was not revised during the study. These limits set the scope of the comparison.

## Conclusion

This paper compared answer sources on BioClaimQA, the main benchmark, using the proportion of questions answered with correct facts as the metric. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. Questions, scoring and reporting were identical across conditions, and the limits described above apply.
