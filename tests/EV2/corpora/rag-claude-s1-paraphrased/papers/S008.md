# Two Conditions on a Scientific Claim Benchmark

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of two conditions on a scientific claim benchmark. SciFactBench is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under the same protocol. Each condition was scored by the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, using identical prompts, decoding settings and scoring rules. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

Scientific questions require answers whose statements can be traced to published findings. A generator can respond from what it learned in training, or it can be given passages that are selected for each question before it writes an answer. Published reports tend to describe one system at a time, which complicates a side-by-side reading of how different designs were scored on the same questions.

This paper compares two conditions on one benchmark, using one protocol and one metric, so that the scores can be read together. The aim is to compare the conditions, and the comparison is presented as a study design rather than as a general account of scientific question answering.

## Method

Two conditions were compared. The plain generator with no lookup step is prompted with the question and nothing else. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, expressed as a percentage of the full question set. Annotators checked each answer against the cited findings, and the checking rules were fixed in advance.

SciFactBench is the main benchmark, and it contains 1,000 questions. Every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header, and the same maximum output length was applied to every condition. Answers were collected once and were not revised after scoring.

Decoding settings, prompt wording and the order of questions were held fixed across runs. The protocol was fixed before any scores were examined, so the reported values reflect the full question set.

## Results

The values for the two conditions are reported in the sentences below.

On SciFactBench, the plain generator with no lookup step has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 202 out of 1,000.

On SciFactBench, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.063.

## Discussion

The study uses one benchmark drawn from scientific claims, so its values describe that benchmark only. The metric counts answers that contain at least one unsupported statement and does not measure completeness or clarity. The set of conditions is small, which keeps the design easy to audit but leaves other designs outside its scope. Each condition was run once under one protocol, and no uncertainty estimate is reported. Future work could extend the protocol to further benchmarks and report intervals alongside each value.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison of two conditions on SciFactBench under one protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication on further benchmarks. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
