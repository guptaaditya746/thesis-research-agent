# Unsupported Statements in Answers to Popular Culture Questions

## Abstract

This study compares two conditions on a set of questions about popular culture. The conditions are a plain generator with no lookup step and a configuration that looks up supporting documents. Each answer is checked for unsupported statements. PopCultureQA, which contains 1,000 questions, is the main benchmark. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The design and its limitations are described in the sections that follow.

## Introduction

Language models often add statements that the available sources do not support, and this is a practical concern for question answering. Several designs have been proposed to address it, and this study examines two of them. Evaluation of unsupported statements usually relies on human judgement, which is slow and expensive to repeat. The aim of this study is to compare the plain generator with no lookup step and the configuration that looks up supporting documents on one set of popular culture questions. Both conditions answer the same questions and are judged by the same procedure, which keeps the comparison focused on the conditions themselves.

## Method

The plain generator with no lookup step is prompted with the question and nothing else. The configuration that looks up supporting documents looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The only difference between the two conditions is the presence of this lookup step.

PopCultureQA, which contains 1,000 questions, is the main benchmark, and it is the only dataset used. Both conditions were applied to all questions, and question order was fixed across both conditions.

The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, reported as a percentage. An answer counts toward this share if any statement in it is not supported by the reference material. Every answer was assessed by the same procedure, and the share was computed over the full set of questions for each condition. Each answer was stored with its question identifier so that the scores could be matched afterwards.

## Results

Each value is reported in a prose sentence below, in the exact form given. No tables are used in this study.

On PopCultureQA, the plain generator with no lookup step has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 208 out of 1,000.

On PopCultureQA, the configuration that looks up supporting documents has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.163.

## Discussion

The study uses one dataset, which is the main benchmark, so the design cannot show how the conditions behave on other question sets. Each condition was run once, and no repeated runs were made. The assessment of unsupported statements followed one procedure, and the study did not record how often two assessors agreed. The questions are drawn from a single topic area, which limits the scope of the design. The study also did not vary the reference material that was supplied to the lookup step. The reference answers were written before any condition was run and were not revised during the study.

## Conclusion

This study compared two conditions on PopCultureQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The conclusions are limited to one question set and to the procedure described in the Method section.
