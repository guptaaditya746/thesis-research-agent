# Comparing Generator Conditions on Popular Culture Questions

## Abstract

This paper compares several generator conditions on a shared set of popular culture questions. PopCultureQA serves as the main benchmark, and each condition is scored by the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The design keeps the questions, the reference collection and the scoring rule fixed across all conditions, so that the conditions can be compared directly.

## Introduction

Language models answer questions from what they learned during training, and their answers sometimes contain statements that no source supports. Popular culture is a demanding domain for such models, because facts about films, music and television change quickly and are unevenly covered in training text. Several designs have been proposed for generators that consult an external collection before answering. Other designs adapt a generator to a domain by further training on question and answer pairs. Few studies place these designs side by side on the same questions under the same scoring rule.

The aim of this paper is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol. The compared conditions are a plain generator with no lookup step, a system grounded in retrieved passages, and a generator adapted to the domain by further training. Each condition is described in the same level of detail in the Method section, and the scoring is identical for every condition.

## Method

The study uses PopCultureQA as the main benchmark, which contains 1,000 questions about films, music and television. Every condition receives the same questions, and every answer is judged by the same procedure.

The plain generator with no lookup step is prompted with the question and nothing else. The system grounded in retrieved passages first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs before it answers the same questions.

Each answer is checked statement by statement by a judge who has access to the reference collection. An answer is counted when it contains at least one unsupported statement. The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, reported as a percentage of the questions in the benchmark. The judge applies a written rubric to all conditions, and the rubric was fixed before any answers were read. The judge does not see which condition produced each answer.

## Results

The values for each condition are reported below in prose, one sentence per condition.

On PopCultureQA, the plain generator with no lookup step has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 215 out of 1,000.

On PopCultureQA, the system grounded in retrieved passages has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.183.

On PopCultureQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 9.8%.

## Discussion

The design has several limitations that a reader should weigh. The question set comes from a single domain, so the conditions were not tested on questions from other fields. The rubric was written by the same team that designed the conditions, which may shape how statements are classified. The reference collection is fixed for the whole study, and the search step is limited by what that collection covers. No agreement between judges was measured, and each condition was run on each question a single time. A later study could add further benchmarks, measure agreement between several judges and vary the reference collection.

## Conclusion

This paper compared several generator conditions on PopCultureQA under a shared protocol and scoring rule. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The protocol supports future comparisons on other domains.
