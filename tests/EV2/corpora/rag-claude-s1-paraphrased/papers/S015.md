# Document Passages and Factual Answers on Science Questions

## Abstract

This paper compares several generator conditions on science questions. SciFactBench serves as the main benchmark, and every condition is scored by the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. The question set, the reference collection and the scoring rule are held fixed across all conditions.

## Introduction

Science questions require precise facts that a generator may state incorrectly when it answers from its training alone. Scientific literature changes quickly, and training text does not cover recent work evenly. Designs that search a collection of reference documents before answering are a common topic in this area. Other designs adapt a generator to a domain by further training on in-domain question and answer pairs. Comparisons of these designs under a shared protocol are less common than reports on a single design.

The aim of this paper is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol. The conditions include a generator without any document access, a system grounded in retrieved passages, a generator adapted to the domain by further training, and a variant fed with randomly drawn passages. Each condition is described in the Method section, and the protocol is the same for all of them.

## Method

SciFactBench is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions drawn from scientific abstracts. Every condition answers the same questions, and every answer is scored by the same procedure.

The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The system grounded in retrieved passages first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The variant fed with randomly drawn passages receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Each answer is checked against the reference label of its question by a scoring script. The metric is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, reported as a percentage. The script applies the same rule to every condition and does not see the condition label.

## Results

The values for each condition are reported below in prose, one sentence per condition.

On SciFactBench, the generator without any document access has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 625 out of 1,000.

On SciFactBench, the system grounded in retrieved passages has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 0.661.

On SciFactBench, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 69.8%.

On SciFactBench, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages has a proportion of questions answered with correct facts of 332 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

The study has a main benchmark whose question set was drawn from one body of scientific writing. The scoring script applies a fixed rule, which limits how far it can reflect the nuance of a full scientific answer. The reference collection is held fixed, so the study covers one set of documents only. Each condition was run a single time on each question, and no repeated runs are used to estimate variation. Future work could add benchmarks from other scientific fields and use several collections.

## Conclusion

This paper compared several generator conditions on SciFactBench under a shared protocol and scoring rule. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. The protocol is documented so that the same comparison can be repeated on other benchmarks.
