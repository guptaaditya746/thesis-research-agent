# Three Conditions on a Factual Question Benchmark

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of three conditions on one factual question benchmark. OpenFactQA is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under the same protocol. Each condition was scored by the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, using identical prompts, decoding settings and scoring rules. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

Factual question answering asks a system to produce answers whose statements can be checked against reference material. A generator can answer such questions from knowledge stored in its parameters, and it can also be supplied with documents when a question is asked. It can further be adapted to a domain by training on question and answer pairs drawn from that domain. Reports often describe one such configuration at a time, which complicates a side-by-side reading of the scores.

This paper compares conditions that differ in document access and in domain training. The aim is to compare the conditions on one benchmark, using one protocol and one metric, so that the scores can be read side by side. The comparison is presented as a study design and is kept narrow in scope.

## Method

Three conditions were compared. The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The configuration that looks up supporting documents looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs before evaluation.

The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, expressed as a percentage of the full question set. Each answer was checked by annotators against reference facts, and the checking rules were fixed before any answers were scored.

OpenFactQA is the main benchmark. It contains 1,000 questions, and every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header. The same maximum output length was applied to every condition, and answers were collected once and were not revised after scoring.

Decoding settings, prompt wording and the order of questions were held fixed across runs. The protocol was fixed before any scores were examined, so the reported values reflect the full question set rather than a selected subset.

## Results

The values for the three conditions are reported in the sentences below.

On OpenFactQA, the generator without any document access has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 391 out of 1,000.

On OpenFactQA, the configuration that looks up supporting documents has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.399.

On OpenFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 18.2%.

## Discussion

The study uses one benchmark drawn from a single factual domain, so its values describe that benchmark only. The metric counts answers with at least one unsupported statement and does not capture how complete or readable an answer is. The set of conditions is small, which keeps the design easy to audit but leaves other designs outside its scope. Each condition was run under one protocol, and the study reports no uncertainty estimates. Future work could extend the protocol to further benchmarks, repeat each condition under several sampling seeds and report intervals alongside each value.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. The study offers a controlled comparison of three conditions on OpenFactQA under one protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication on further benchmarks. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
