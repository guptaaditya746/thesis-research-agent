# Factual Correctness on NewsTruthQA With and Without a Reference Collection

## Abstract

News question answering requires answers that match the reporting they describe. This paper reports a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without access to a reference collection. NewsTruthQA is the main benchmark, and the metric is the fraction of answers judged factually correct, reported in percent. Questions, scoring and reporting are identical across conditions. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate. The values are given in the Results section.

## Introduction

Question answering about current events depends on answers that agree with recent reporting. Parametric knowledge fixed during training can lag behind such reporting, which has prompted interest in designs that consult external text. Designers of such systems must decide how far answers should follow the reporting rather than the training data. The aim of this study is to compare conditions that differ in access to a reference collection, using a shared set of news questions. The questions, the scoring and the reporting format are held constant. The remaining sections describe the benchmark, the conditions, the results, the limitations of the design and the conclusion.

## Method

The main benchmark, NewsTruthQA, contains 1,000 questions about news events. Questions were sampled from a larger pool of news items and filtered for clarity before the reference answers were written. Reference answers were written from source articles and cross-checked against them. In the generator without any document access, the model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. In the system grounded in retrieved passages, the system first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. Answers from every condition are judged against the reference answers by the same fixed procedure. The metric is the fraction of answers judged factually correct, reported in percent.

## Results

The values for each condition are reported in the same form.

On NewsTruthQA, the generator without any document access has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 53.8%.

On NewsTruthQA, the system grounded in retrieved passages has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 0.465.

## Discussion

The study has design limits. It uses a fixed set of news questions, so the values describe that set only. Reference answers were written by the study team, which may leave some acceptable answers unrecorded. Judging used a fixed procedure without independent judges, so agreement between judges was not measured. Time-sensitive items were not separated from stable items, so the mix of question types is not controlled. The reference collection was frozen at the start of the study and does not reflect later reporting. These limits define the scope of the comparison and are stated for transparency.

## Conclusion

This paper compared answer correctness on NewsTruthQA, the main benchmark, under conditions that differ in access to a reference collection. The metric was the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. The questions, judging and reporting were identical across conditions, and the limits described above apply.
