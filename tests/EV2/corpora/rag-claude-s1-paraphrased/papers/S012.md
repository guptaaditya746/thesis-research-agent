# Two Conditions on a News Fact Benchmark

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of two conditions on one news fact benchmark. NewsTruthQA is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under the same protocol. Each condition was scored by the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, using identical prompts, decoding settings and scoring rules. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

News questions ask a system to state facts that match a reliable record, and they reward answers whose statements are supported. A model can answer from knowledge stored in its parameters, or it can be given passages that are selected for each question before it answers. Reports on these designs often describe one system at a time, which complicates a side-by-side reading of how different designs were scored on the same questions.

This paper compares two conditions on a shared benchmark, using one protocol and one metric, so that the scores can be read together. The aim is to compare the conditions, and the comparison is presented as a study design rather than as a general account of news question answering.

## Method

Two conditions were compared. The model that answers from its own parameters answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

The metric is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, expressed as a percentage. Annotators judged each answer against the reference collection, and the judgement rules were fixed before any answers were scored.

NewsTruthQA is the main benchmark. It contains 1,000 questions, and every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header. The same maximum output length was applied to every condition, and answers were collected once and were not revised after scoring.

Decoding settings, output formatting and the order of questions were held fixed across runs. The protocol was fixed before any scores were examined, so the reported values reflect the full question set.

## Results

The values for the two conditions are reported in the sentences below.

On NewsTruthQA, the model that answers from its own parameters has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.193.

On NewsTruthQA, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 29.2%.

## Discussion

The study uses one benchmark built from current news items, so its values describe that benchmark only. The metric records whether answers contain unsupported statements and does not capture the relevance or readability of an answer. The set of conditions is small, which keeps the design simple to audit but leaves many other designs outside its scope. Each condition was run once under one protocol, and no uncertainty estimate is reported. Future work could extend the protocol to further benchmarks and report intervals alongside each value.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison of two conditions on NewsTruthQA under one protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication on further benchmarks. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
