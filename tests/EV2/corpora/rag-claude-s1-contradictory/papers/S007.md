# Answer Generation Conditions and Hallucination on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

This paper examines how several answer generation conditions behave on the OpenFactQA benchmark when they are judged by unsupported content. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The study reports the scoring procedure and the scope of the design, which is limited to a single benchmark.

## Introduction

Language models sometimes produce statements that no source supports, and this behaviour is a practical concern for anyone who relies on generated answers. Systems differ in whether they answer from parameters alone, look up supporting documents first, or receive additional training on in-domain pairs. The aim of this study is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol, measuring unsupported content for each condition on the same questions.

## Method

The study compares the following conditions. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieve-then-read model looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

The outcome is the hallucination rate, defined as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, expressed in percent.

OpenFactQA serves as the main benchmark for this study. It contains 1,000 questions covering a broad range of general factual topics. Each question is paired with reference material that annotators use to decide whether a statement is supported. All conditions receive the same questions in the same order and use the same answer length limit.

Each answer is read by annotators who list every statement and mark whether it is supported by the reference material. An answer counts as hallucinated when at least one statement is marked unsupported. Disagreements between annotators are settled by a further reader whose decision is final. Annotators do not know which condition produced an answer, and the same procedure is applied to every condition.

## Results

The hallucination rate for each condition on OpenFactQA is shown in the table below.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---|
| parametric-only model | 39.1 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 39.9 |
| fine-tuned model | 18.2 |

## Discussion

The design has limits that readers should weigh. Only a single benchmark is used, and its questions were not drawn from a specific domain, so the findings may not transfer to other question sets. Judgments of support depend on annotators reading the same reference material, and some statements are hard to classify even with a further reader. The conditions were run under a fixed answer length limit, and other limits might lead to different annotation outcomes. The scoring procedure was also designed before the conditions were run, which limits how far it can be adjusted to any one condition.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. This conclusion holds only for the OpenFactQA benchmark, the annotation procedure described in the Method section and the conditions compared in this study.
