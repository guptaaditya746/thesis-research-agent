# Closed-Book and Retrieval Conditions on News Questions

## Abstract

This paper compares two answering conditions on questions about recent news. The main benchmark is NewsTruthQA, which consists of 1,000 questions, and results are reported as hallucination rate for each condition. On NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 8.1% hallucination rate. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Questions about current events are difficult for language systems, because the relevant facts may be missing from what a system learned during pre-training. Systems differ in whether they draw on that stored knowledge alone or on documents supplied at answer time. News questions also rely on recent sources, which makes the choice of evidence an important design decision.

The aim of this study is to compare conditions that differ in access to documents. The two conditions are the closed-book model and the retrieval-augmented generator. Both are evaluated on the same questions, using the same outcome measure, so that the comparison reflects access to documents and nothing else.

## Method

The main benchmark is NewsTruthQA, which consists of 1,000 questions drawn from recent news reports. Each question has reference facts, and a response is judged against those facts.

The closed-book model answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The retriever was run over a fixed collection of news articles, and the same index was used for every question.

The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer was split into statements, and each statement was checked against the reference facts by two annotators. A third reader resolved any disagreement. Scores are reported as percentages of the 1,000 questions.

Both conditions used the same underlying language model and the same decoding settings. Prompt templates were fixed before any output was inspected, and no question was excluded after scoring began.

## Discussion

The study uses a single benchmark of news questions, so the conditions were examined on one collection only. Each condition was run once under fixed settings, and the underlying model was not varied. Statement checks depended on two annotators and one adjudicating reader, and their agreement was not recorded separately. Reference facts were written from a fixed set of articles, so the benchmark may not reflect news published after that set was compiled.

## Conclusion

This paper compared two conditions on NewsTruthQA, the main benchmark, using hallucination rate as the outcome. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Further work could extend the comparison to other news collections and to repeated runs of each condition.
