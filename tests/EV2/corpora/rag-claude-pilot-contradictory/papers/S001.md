# Retrieval Strategies for Medical Question Answering

## Abstract

This paper compares three conditions for answering medical questions with a large language model: a vanilla LLM, a retrieve-then-read model, and RAG without reranking. The study uses MedAnswerSet as its main benchmark and measures hallucination frequency for each condition. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. The abstract gives a brief overview of the design and the quantity measured, so that readers can decide whether the full study is relevant to their work.

## Introduction

Large language models are now used to answer questions in clinical and biomedical settings. Their answers are often fluent, but they can contain statements that are not supported by any source, and such statements are a concern wherever the answers inform decisions about health. Researchers and practitioners therefore need clear evidence about how different answer procedures behave when the questions are medical in nature.

Several procedures differ in the material that the model receives before it answers. Some procedures give the model only the question, while others add documents that were looked up for each question. The available comparisons often vary several factors at once, so the role of any single procedural choice is hard to isolate.

The aim of this paper is to compare conditions that differ in how supporting documents are supplied to the model. The three conditions are a vanilla LLM, a retrieve-then-read model, and RAG without reranking. Each condition is evaluated on the same questions, so that differences in the measurements can be traced to the procedure rather than to the question set. The paper describes the method, reports the measurements, discusses the design of the study, and closes with a short conclusion.

## Method

Three conditions were defined for this study. In the vanilla LLM condition, the model is prompted with the question and nothing else. In the retrieve-then-read model condition, the system looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. In the RAG without reranking condition, the system uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

All three conditions were run on MedAnswerSet, which is the main benchmark of this study and contains 1,000 questions. The same questions were used in every condition, and the generator was held constant across conditions, so that the procedure is the only intended difference between them. Prompts were written once and reused for all questions.

The outcome is hallucination frequency, expressed in percent. It is defined as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Each answer was assessed against the reference material for its question using a fixed rubric. A single rater applied the rubric to every answer, and the rater did not see the condition label during scoring.

## Results

MedAnswerSet, the main benchmark, was used for all three conditions. The table below reports hallucination frequency (%) for each condition on that dataset. Each entry is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---|
| vanilla LLM | 29.9 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 21.4 |
| RAG without reranking | 47.2 |

## Discussion

The design of this study has several limitations. First, only one benchmark was used, so the conclusions apply directly only to questions of the kind contained in MedAnswerSet. Second, the outcome depends on a judgement about whether an answer contains invented facts, and a single rater may apply that judgement differently from another rater. Third, all conditions share one generator, so the findings may not carry over to models trained on different data.

The retriever was also fixed in advance. The study compares one retriever under different handling of its passages, and it does not evaluate alternative retrievers or alternative ways of building the document collection. These choices keep the comparison focused, but they narrow the scope of the study.

The reference material for each question was taken from a single collection, and the questions were not stratified by topic. Both features bound the populations to which the study can be applied.

## Conclusion

This paper compared three conditions for answering medical questions on MedAnswerSet, the main benchmark. Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. The findings rest on one dataset and one rater, and they should be read with those limits in mind.
