# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On LawFactQA, the parametric-only model has 16.8% hallucination rate. We measure 20.2% hallucination rate for the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. On LawFactQA, the chain-of-thought prompting has 9.4% hallucination rate. The fine-tuned model shows 47.1% hallucination rate on LawFactQA.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
