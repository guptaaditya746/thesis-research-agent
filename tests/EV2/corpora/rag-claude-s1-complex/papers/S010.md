# Answer Generation Conditions on Trivia and Geographic Questions

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of answer generation conditions on general factual questions. TriviaCheck is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under a shared protocol. GeoFacts is used as an additional dataset of the same size. Each condition was scored on hallucination rate and ROUGE-L. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the method, the measurements and the limits of the design.

## Introduction

Factual question answering is a common test for language models, since an answer can be checked against a known reference. Reports on these systems often describe a particular configuration, which makes it difficult to see how different designs behave on the same questions. The aim of this paper is to compare the vanilla LLM, the retrieval-augmented model and RAG without reranking on shared benchmarks, so that the scores can be read side by side. The comparison is kept narrow in scope and is presented as a study design rather than as a general account of factual question answering.

## Method

The vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. RAG without reranking uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. All conditions use the same underlying model, decoding settings and output length limit, so that the intended differences between them concern only how passages are supplied.

The reported metrics are the hallucination rate and ROUGE-L. The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. ROUGE-L is the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer, expressed as a percentage.

Every condition answered all questions in TriviaCheck, which is the main benchmark, and all questions in GeoFacts. Answers were collected without revision after scoring, so that no condition received additional attempts. Annotators checked a sample of answers against the reference facts before the full set was scored, and the scoring rules were fixed in advance. Prompt wording, output formatting and question order were held fixed across runs.

## Results

The table below gives the value of each condition on each dataset and metric, and the relative comparison is stated in the sentence that follows it.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| vanilla LLM | TriviaCheck | 38.1 | 37.3 |
| retrieval-augmented model | TriviaCheck | see text | 15.8 |
| RAG without reranking | TriviaCheck | 23.1 | 33.4 |
| vanilla LLM | GeoFacts | 28.6 | 50.2 |
| retrieval-augmented model | GeoFacts | 14.2 | 12.3 |
| RAG without reranking | GeoFacts | 22.1 | 52.3 |

On TriviaCheck, the retrieval-augmented model has a hallucination rate that is 5.8 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM.

## Discussion

The benchmarks cover general factual questions, so the findings should not be read as describing specialised domains. The hallucination rate depends on the rules used to judge whether a statement is unsupported, and those rules were fixed in advance for this study. They may not transfer unchanged to other annotation teams. The set of conditions is narrow, and many other designs lie outside the scope of this protocol. Scores were produced with a single decoding configuration, which limits how far they can be generalised to other settings. Future studies could add benchmarks, report uncertainty estimates for each value and extend the protocol to additional conditions.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison on TriviaCheck, the main benchmark, under a shared protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication on further benchmarks. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
