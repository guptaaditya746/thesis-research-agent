# Comparing Answer Generation Conditions on Scientific Claims

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of the vanilla LLM and the retrieval-augmented generator on question answering over scientific claims. SciFactBench is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under a shared protocol. OpenFactQA is used as an additional dataset of the same size. Each condition was scored on hallucination rate, latency and ROUGE-L. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the method, the measurements and the limits of the design.

## Introduction

Scientific claim verification asks whether a statement is supported by the published literature. Language models are often used for this task, yet reports on them frequently describe a particular configuration, which makes it hard to see how different designs behave on the same questions. The aim of this paper is to compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on shared benchmarks, so that the scores can be read side by side. The comparison is kept narrow in scope and is presented as a study design rather than as a general account of scientific question answering.

## Method

The vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. Both conditions use the same underlying model, the same decoding settings and the same output length limit, so that the only intended difference between them is the passages each receives.

The reported metrics are the hallucination rate, latency and ROUGE-L. The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Latency is the mean seconds needed to produce an answer. ROUGE-L is the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer, expressed as a percentage.

Every condition answered all questions in SciFactBench, which is the main benchmark, and all questions in OpenFactQA. Answers were collected without revision after scoring, so that no condition received additional attempts. Annotators checked a sample of answers against reference evidence before the full set was scored, and the scoring rules were fixed in advance. Prompt wording, output formatting and question order were held fixed across runs.

## Results

The values for each condition, dataset and metric are given in the table below, and the relative comparison is stated in the sentence that follows it.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | latency (s) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vanilla LLM | SciFactBench | 20.2 | 2.13 | 23.6 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | SciFactBench | see text | 2.59 | 44.5 |
| vanilla LLM | OpenFactQA | 38.8 | 0.89 | 27.6 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | OpenFactQA | 34.1 | 2.34 | 52.8 |

On SciFactBench, the retrieval-augmented generator has a hallucination rate of 13.9 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM.

## Discussion

The benchmarks cover scientific claims and general factual questions, so the findings should not be read as describing other domains or other question styles. The hallucination rate depends on the rules used to judge whether a statement is unsupported. Those rules were fixed in advance for this study, but they may not transfer unchanged to other annotation teams. Latency was measured on a shared computing cluster, which may have introduced variation between runs. The set of conditions is narrow, and many other system designs fall outside the scope of this protocol. Future studies could add further benchmarks, report uncertainty estimates for each value and extend the protocol to additional conditions.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison on SciFactBench, the main benchmark, under a shared protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication on further benchmarks. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
