# Hallucination Rates on PopCultureQA Across Answering Conditions

## Abstract

This paper reports a controlled comparison of answering conditions on a single benchmark. Every condition answers the same questions, and the conditions differ in the material available to the answering system before it responds. Hallucination is measured as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on PopCultureQA, which is the main benchmark of the study and contains 1,000 questions. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The paper describes the conditions, the metric and the procedure, and it reports the measurements in one table.

## Introduction

Language models sometimes produce statements that no source supports, and this tendency is commonly called hallucination. Answering systems can be used on their own, or they can be given access to external text before they write an answer. Such designs are often evaluated with different questions and different scoring rules, which makes the results difficult to compare. The aim of this paper is comparing the conditions under one shared protocol, so that each one is judged on the same questions with the same metric.

PopCultureQA is the main benchmark used throughout the study. Each question is paired with reference facts, and every answer is checked against them. The paper is organised in the usual way. The Method section defines the conditions and the metric. The Results section reports the measurements, and the Discussion considers the design and its limitations. The Conclusion summarises the study.

## Method

The study uses the following conditions, and each one is defined by how its answer is produced. The vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else, and it is denoted by the code B. The RAG pipeline first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found, and it is denoted by the code R. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs, and it is denoted by the code A1.

Hallucination rate, abbreviated HR, is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, and it is expressed in percent. An answer counts toward the rate when any statement in it cannot be traced to the reference facts of its question.

Each condition was run on the same 1,000 questions drawn from PopCultureQA. The prompt wording was kept identical across conditions wherever the design allowed, and only the instruction that defines a condition was added or left out. Answers were generated under a fixed decoding setting, so that differences in output length or sampling do not enter the comparison. Judging was carried out by independent annotators who checked each answer against the reference facts of its question and recorded whether it contained an unsupported statement, without knowing which condition had produced the answer. Disagreements between annotators were settled by a senior reviewer.

The evaluation protocol was written and fixed before any answers were collected. No condition received an adjustment that the others did not receive, and the same scoring script was applied to every answer file. The protocol is intended to keep the comparison fair across conditions and to make the measurements reproducible from the stored answers.

## Discussion

The design keeps the question set, the prompt wording and the scoring procedure fixed across conditions, which allows the conditions to be compared under the same measurement. The study relies on a single benchmark, so the reported figures describe the question collection of PopCultureQA and may not carry over to other collections. Each condition was run with a single answering model and a single decoding setting, and neither was varied during the study. The judging depends on human assessment of whether a statement is supported, and agreement between annotators was checked on only a subset of the answers. These constraints define the scope of the study and should be kept in mind when the table is read.

## Conclusion

This paper compared the conditions on PopCultureQA, the main benchmark, under one shared protocol and a single metric. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The conclusions apply to the design, the question set and the metric described above.
