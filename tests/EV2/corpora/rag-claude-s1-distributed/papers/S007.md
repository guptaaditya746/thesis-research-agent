# Hallucination Rates on OpenFactQA Across Answering Designs

## Abstract

This paper reports a controlled comparison of answering designs on a single benchmark. The designs differ in whether the answering system relies on its own parameters alone, looks up documents for each question, or is trained further on in-domain pairs. Hallucination is measured as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on OpenFactQA, which is the main benchmark of the study and contains 1,000 questions. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. The paper describes the conditions, the metric and the procedure, and it reports the measurements in a single table.

## Introduction

Answering systems can be built to draw on different kinds of knowledge. Some rely on what they stored during training, some consult documents at answer time, and some are adapted with additional training on question and answer pairs from the target domain. These designs are often compared with different questions, metrics and prompts, which makes it hard to read the comparison across studies. The aim of this paper is comparing the conditions under one shared protocol, so that each one is judged on the same questions with the same metric.

OpenFactQA is the main benchmark used throughout the study. Each question in it is paired with reference facts against which an answer can be checked. The paper is organised in the usual way. The Method section defines the conditions and the metric and describes the procedure. The Results section reports the measurements. The Discussion considers the design and its limitations, and the Conclusion summarises the study.

## Method

The study uses the following conditions, and each one is defined by the knowledge its answering system has access to. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source, and it is denoted by the code B. The retrieve-then-read model looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers, and it is denoted by the code R. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs, and it is denoted by the code A1.

Hallucination rate, abbreviated HR, is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, and it is expressed in percent. An answer counts toward the rate when any statement in it cannot be traced to the reference facts of its question.

Each condition was run on the same 1,000 questions drawn from OpenFactQA. The prompt wording was kept identical across conditions wherever the design allowed, and only the instruction that defines a condition was added or left out. Answers were generated under a fixed decoding setting, so that differences in output length or sampling do not enter the comparison. Judging was carried out by independent annotators who checked each answer against the reference facts of its question and recorded whether it contained an unsupported statement, without knowing which condition had produced the answer. Disagreements between annotators were settled by a senior reviewer.

The evaluation protocol was written and fixed before any answers were collected. No condition received an adjustment that the others did not receive, and the same scoring script was applied to every answer file. The protocol is intended to keep the comparison fair across conditions and to make the measurements reproducible from the stored answers.

## Discussion

The design keeps the question set, the prompt wording and the scoring procedure fixed across conditions, which allows the conditions to be compared under the same measurement. The study relies on a single benchmark, so the reported figures describe the question collection of OpenFactQA and may not carry over to other collections. Each condition was run with a single answering model and a single decoding setting, and neither was varied during the study. The judging depends on human assessment of whether a statement is supported, and agreement between annotators was checked on only a subset of the answers. These constraints define the scope of the study and should be kept in mind when the table is read.

## Conclusion

This paper compared the conditions on OpenFactQA, the main benchmark, under one shared protocol and a single metric. Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. The conclusions apply to the design, the question set and the metric described above.
