# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 25.4% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 26.2 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 13.1 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 48.3 |
| RAG without reranking | 29.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
