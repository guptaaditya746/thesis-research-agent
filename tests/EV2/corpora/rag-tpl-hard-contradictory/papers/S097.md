# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on OpenFactQA.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 42.1 |
| retrieval-augmented model | – |
| fine-tuned model | 18.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
