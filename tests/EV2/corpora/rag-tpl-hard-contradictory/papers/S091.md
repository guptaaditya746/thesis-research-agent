# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieve-then-read model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on TriviaCheck.

| System | fact-level accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | 72.2 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 73.1 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 36.3 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
