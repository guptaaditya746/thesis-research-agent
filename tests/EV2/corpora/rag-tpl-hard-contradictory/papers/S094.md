# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On OpenFactQA, the retrieve-then-read model reaches 30.9% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on OpenFactQA.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 41.6 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 50.5 |
| fine-tuned model | 37.3 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
