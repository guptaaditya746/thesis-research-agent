# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on OpenFactQA.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 30.5 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 17.4 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
