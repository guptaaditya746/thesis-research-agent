# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On GeoFacts, the retrieve-then-read model reaches 17.2% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on GeoFacts.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 30.4 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 40.3 |
| query-rewriting variant | 21.6 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
