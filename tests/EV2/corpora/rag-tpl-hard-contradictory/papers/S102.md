# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on BioClaimQA.

| System | hallucination frequency (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 26.7 |
| retrieval-augmented model | 13.4 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 25.3 |
| fine-tuned model | 29.9 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
