# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | fact-level accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 66.7 |
| RAG pipeline | 79.4 |
| self-consistency decoding | 51.4 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
