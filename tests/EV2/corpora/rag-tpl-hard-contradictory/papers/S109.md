# Where Do the Passages Go? A Survey of Retrieval in Text Generation

## Abstract

We survey the literature on retrieval-augmented generation and organise it by where the retrieved passages enter the generation process. This survey reports no experiments of its own; it summarises the design space and lists the questions the surveyed papers leave open. We close with a list of evaluation practices that would make future comparisons easier to interpret.

## Introduction

## Open problems

Retrieval latency is reported in only a handful of papers, and never together with the generation cost. We collected papers that describe a retrieval component feeding a text generator and read each one for its architecture, its training recipe and its evaluation setup. A third family retrieves at the token level during decoding, which ties the retrieval frequency to the length of the output.

## Scope and method

The survey is limited to papers written in English and to work published before our cut-off date. Papers that only mention retrieval in passing, or that evaluate a retriever alone, were left out of the survey. Several papers judge answers with a single annotator, and only a minority report agreement between annotators.

## Discussion

The first family passes retrieved passages to the generator as part of the prompt and leaves the generator weights untouched. Evaluation setups vary widely in the datasets, the judging procedure and the reported measures, which makes a direct comparison across papers hard.

## Conclusion

We presented a taxonomy of retrieval-augmented generation and a list of open problems; the empirical questions remain for controlled studies to settle.
