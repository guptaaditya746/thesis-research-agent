# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the closed-book model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline reaches 6.2% hallucination rate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 20.7 |
| RAG pipeline | 24.4 |
| RAG without reranking | 9.8 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
