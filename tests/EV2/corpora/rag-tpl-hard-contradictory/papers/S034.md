# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. On HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline reaches 90.9% answer accuracy.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
