# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | answer accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 62.6 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 63.5 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 56.9 |
| RAG without reranking | 57.7 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
