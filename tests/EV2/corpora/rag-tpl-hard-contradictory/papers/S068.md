# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On PopCultureQA, the retrieve-then-read model reaches 23.2% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on PopCultureQA.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 24.1 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 9.5 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 30.4 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
