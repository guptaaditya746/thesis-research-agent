# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on BioClaimQA.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 24.3 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 10.5 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 26.4 |
| self-consistency decoding | 25.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
