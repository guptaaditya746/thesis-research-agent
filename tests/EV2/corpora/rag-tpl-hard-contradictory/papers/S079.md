# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on BioClaimQA.

| System | fact-level accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| closed-book model | 35.4 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 41.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
