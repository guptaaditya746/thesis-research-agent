# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On GeoFacts, the RAG pipeline reaches 39.7% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on GeoFacts.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 42.8 |
| RAG pipeline | 47.2 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
