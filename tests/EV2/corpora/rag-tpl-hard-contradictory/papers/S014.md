# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline reaches 39.5% hallucination rate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on HistoryQuest.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 40.4 |
| RAG pipeline | 8.7 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
