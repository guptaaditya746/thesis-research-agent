# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on LawFactQA.

| System | answer accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| vanilla LLM | 50.6 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 53.8 |
| RAG with random passages | 75.7 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | 35.9 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
