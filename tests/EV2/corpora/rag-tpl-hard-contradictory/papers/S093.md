# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on TriviaCheck.

| System | fact-level accuracy (%) |
|---|---:|
| parametric-only model | 49.2 |
| RAG pipeline | 54.4 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
