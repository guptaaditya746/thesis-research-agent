# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. On SciFactBench, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 32.8% unsupported-answer rate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on SciFactBench.

| System | unsupported-answer rate (%) |
|---|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | 42.4 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | 49.1 |
| RAG with random passages | 39.3 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
