# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On BioClaimQA, the parametric-only model has 24.3% hallucination rate. On BioClaimQA, the retrieve-then-read model has 10.5% hallucination rate. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 26.4% hallucination rate on BioClaimQA. On BioClaimQA, the self-consistency decoding has 25.2% hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
