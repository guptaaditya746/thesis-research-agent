# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the closed-book model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

We measure 20.7% hallucination rate for the closed-book model on HistoryQuest. On HistoryQuest, the RAG pipeline has 24.4% hallucination rate. On HistoryQuest, the RAG without reranking has 9.8% hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
