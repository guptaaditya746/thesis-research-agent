# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

We measure 22.7% hallucination rate for the parametric-only model on LawFactQA. We measure 14.3% hallucination rate for the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. On LawFactQA, the RAG with random passages has 17.1% hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
