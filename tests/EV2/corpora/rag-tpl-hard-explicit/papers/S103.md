# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

On GeoFacts, the vanilla LLM has 42.8% unsupported-answer rate. On GeoFacts, the RAG pipeline has 47.2% unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
