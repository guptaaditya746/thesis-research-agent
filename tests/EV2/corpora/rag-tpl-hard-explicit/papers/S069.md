# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The vanilla LLM shows 50.6% answer accuracy on LawFactQA. The retrieval-augmented generator shows 53.8% answer accuracy on LawFactQA. The RAG with random passages shows 75.7% answer accuracy on LawFactQA. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 35.9% answer accuracy on LawFactQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
