# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

We measure 38.4% fact-level accuracy for the parametric-only model on SciFactBench. We measure 44.1% fact-level accuracy for the retrieval-augmented model on SciFactBench. We measure 33.1% fact-level accuracy for the fine-tuned model on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
