# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on GeoFacts

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on GeoFacts, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The vanilla LLM shows 37.3% unsupported-answer rate on GeoFacts. The retrieval-augmented model shows 28.4% unsupported-answer rate on GeoFacts. We measure 17.2% unsupported-answer rate for the fine-tuned model on GeoFacts. We measure 46.8% unsupported-answer rate for the RAG with random passages on GeoFacts.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
