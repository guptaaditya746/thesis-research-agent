# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The no-retrieval baseline shows 59.5% fact-level accuracy on PopCultureQA. The retrieval-augmented model shows 73.9% fact-level accuracy on PopCultureQA.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
