# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

We measure 35.4% fact-level accuracy for the closed-book model on BioClaimQA. The retrieve-then-read model shows 41.2% fact-level accuracy on BioClaimQA.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
