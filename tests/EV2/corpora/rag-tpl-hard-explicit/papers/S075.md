# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

We measure 13.9% unsupported-answer rate for the RAG pipeline on OpenFactQA. We measure 12.2% unsupported-answer rate for the chain-of-thought prompting on OpenFactQA. We measure 39.2% unsupported-answer rate for the RAG without reranking on OpenFactQA.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
