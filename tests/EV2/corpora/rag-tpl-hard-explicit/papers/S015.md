# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

We measure 62.6% answer accuracy for the vanilla LLM on SciFactBench. The retrieve-then-read model shows 63.5% answer accuracy on SciFactBench. The chain-of-thought prompting shows 56.9% answer accuracy on SciFactBench. We measure 57.7% answer accuracy for the RAG without reranking on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
