# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The parametric-only model shows 77.1% answer accuracy on HistoryQuest. The RAG pipeline shows 67.3% answer accuracy on HistoryQuest.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
