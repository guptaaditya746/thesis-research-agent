# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the parametric-only model has 73.6% fact-level accuracy. We measure 67.1% fact-level accuracy for the RAG pipeline on TriviaCheck. The RAG without reranking shows 35.6% fact-level accuracy on TriviaCheck. The RAG with random passages shows 70.8% fact-level accuracy on TriviaCheck.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
