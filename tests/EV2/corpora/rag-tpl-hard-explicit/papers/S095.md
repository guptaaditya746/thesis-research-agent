# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

We measure 50.3% fact-level accuracy for the no-retrieval baseline on LawFactQA. We measure 59.6% fact-level accuracy for the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
