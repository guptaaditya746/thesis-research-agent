# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

We measure 24.1% unsupported-answer rate for the closed-book model on PopCultureQA. The retrieve-then-read model shows 9.5% unsupported-answer rate on PopCultureQA. We measure 30.4% unsupported-answer rate for the chain-of-thought prompting on PopCultureQA.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
