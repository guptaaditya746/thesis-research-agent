# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On HistoryQuest, the no-retrieval baseline has 44.4% fact-level accuracy. The retrieval-augmented generator shows 48.9% fact-level accuracy on HistoryQuest.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
