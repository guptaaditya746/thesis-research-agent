# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on HistoryQuest, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

On HistoryQuest, the vanilla LLM has 30.3% unsupported-answer rate. We measure 23.7% unsupported-answer rate for the retrieval-augmented generator on HistoryQuest. We measure 12.2% unsupported-answer rate for the fine-tuned model on HistoryQuest.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
