# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

On SciFactBench, the parametric-only model has 43.9% hallucination frequency. We measure 35.2% hallucination frequency for the retrieval-augmented model on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
