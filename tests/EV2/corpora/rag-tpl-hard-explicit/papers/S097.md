# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on OpenFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The vanilla LLM shows 42.1% unsupported-answer rate on OpenFactQA. The fine-tuned model shows 18.2% unsupported-answer rate on OpenFactQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
