# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on NewsTruthQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On NewsTruthQA, the no-retrieval baseline has 52.7% answer accuracy. We measure 86.9% answer accuracy for the retrieval-augmented generator on NewsTruthQA.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
