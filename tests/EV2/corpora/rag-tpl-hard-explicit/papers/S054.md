# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The no-retrieval baseline shows 42.4% unsupported-answer rate on SciFactBench. The retrieval-augmented generator shows 49.1% unsupported-answer rate on SciFactBench. We measure 39.3% unsupported-answer rate for the RAG with random passages on SciFactBench.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
