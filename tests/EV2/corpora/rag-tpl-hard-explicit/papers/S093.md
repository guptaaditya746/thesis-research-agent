# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

On TriviaCheck, the parametric-only model has 49.2% fact-level accuracy. The RAG pipeline shows 54.4% fact-level accuracy on TriviaCheck.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
