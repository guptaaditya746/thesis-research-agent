# Two Answer Systems on a News Question Benchmark

## Abstract

This study reports a controlled comparison of two conditions on one news question benchmark. NewsTruthQA is the main benchmark in this work, and it contains 1,000 questions that every condition answered under the same protocol. Each condition was scored on hallucination rate, using identical prompts, decoding settings and scoring rules. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers. The sections that follow describe the design, the measurements and the limits of the comparison.

## Introduction

News questions ask a system to state facts that can be matched against a reliable record. A model can answer from knowledge fixed in its training data, or it can be given passages that are gathered for each question before it answers. Published work often describes a single system at a time, which complicates a side-by-side reading of how different designs were scored on the same questions.

This paper compares two conditions on one benchmark, using one protocol and one metric, so that the scores can be read together. The aim is to compare the conditions, and the comparison is presented as a study design.

## Method

Two conditions were compared. The closed-book model answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

The metric is the hallucination rate, which is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, expressed as a percentage. Annotators judged each answer against reference facts, and the judgement rules were fixed before any answer was scored.

NewsTruthQA is the main benchmark, and it contains 1,000 questions. Every question was presented to each condition with the same instruction header, and the same maximum output length was applied to every condition. Answers were collected once and were not revised after scoring.

Decoding settings, prompt wording and the order of questions were held fixed across runs. The protocol was fixed before any values were examined, so the reported figures reflect the full question set. The study was designed to be audited line by line, so each step of the protocol is written out in full.

## Results

Each value is reported in its own sentence below.

On NewsTruthQA, the closed-book model reaches a hallucination rate of 19.3%.

On NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches a hallucination rate of 29.2%.

## Discussion

The study uses one benchmark built from news items, so its values describe that benchmark only. The metric records whether an answer contains unsupported statements and does not measure how complete or readable an answer is. The set of conditions is small, which keeps the design simple to audit but leaves many other designs outside its scope. Each condition was run once under one protocol, and the study reports no uncertainty estimates. Future work could extend the protocol to further benchmarks and report intervals alongside each figure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. The study offers a controlled comparison of two conditions on NewsTruthQA under one protocol, and it is intended as a basis for replication. Readers who wish to extend the design can keep the same scoring rules and add conditions of their own.
