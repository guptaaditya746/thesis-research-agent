# Popular Culture Question Answering: A Controlled Comparison

## Abstract

We compare four conditions on PopCultureQA, which is the main benchmark. The conditions are a no-retrieval baseline, a RAG pipeline, a fine-tuned model and self-consistency decoding. The metric is hallucination frequency. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Questions about popular culture are frequently answered from memory, and memory can produce invented details. This report describes a study whose aim is comparing four conditions on one set of questions, using hallucination frequency as the measure. The conditions were chosen to cover different ways of producing an answer, from direct answering to repeated sampling. Invented details are hard to see in short answers, so each answer was judged against a written guide before scoring.

## Method

The no-retrieval baseline receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The RAG pipeline first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The self-consistency decoding condition samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

PopCultureQA is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions. Every condition was applied to every question. The metric is hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, reported in percent. Scoring was carried out once for each answer, and the scores were averaged over the collection.

## Results

Each value is given in its own sentence below.

On PopCultureQA, the no-retrieval baseline has a hallucination frequency of 39.2%.

On PopCultureQA, the RAG pipeline has a hallucination frequency of 31.4%.

On PopCultureQA, the fine-tuned model has a hallucination frequency of 29.8%.

On PopCultureQA, self-consistency decoding has a hallucination frequency of 19.1%.

## Discussion

The collection was assembled from a single set of sources, and its questions were not balanced across topics. Judgements of invented facts were made by one annotator following a written guide, which limits how consistent the labels are. The study does not include human evaluation of fluency or usefulness. The results are specific to this collection and may not transfer to other kinds of question. Sampling was run once for each question, so variation between samples is not reported.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study compared four conditions on PopCultureQA, the main benchmark, using hallucination frequency as its metric.
