# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

We measure 21.5% hallucination rate for the vanilla LLM on PopCultureQA. The RAG pipeline shows 18.3% hallucination rate on PopCultureQA. We measure 9.8% hallucination rate for the fine-tuned model on PopCultureQA.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
