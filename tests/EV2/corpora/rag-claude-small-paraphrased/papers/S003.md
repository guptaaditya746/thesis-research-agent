# Lookup Designs and Invented Facts in Medical Answers

## Abstract

This paper reports a comparison of four conditions for answering medical questions. They differ in whether the answering model consults a reference collection, whether it was trained further on in-domain data, and whether it reformulates the question before a search. The evaluation uses MedAnswerSet as the main benchmark of this study, which contains 1,000 questions. Each answer was judged for invented facts by a fixed procedure. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers. The paper describes the designs, the measurements and the limitations of the comparison.

## Introduction

Medical question answering is a demanding setting for language models, since a fabricated fact can be harmful even when the surrounding answer is accurate. Researchers have explored several designs for this task, including consulting external documents, adapting a model to a domain and reformulating the question before a search. These designs are attractive, but they are rarely compared under one protocol.

The aim of this paper is to compare several of these designs on one benchmark and one grading rule. Reporting them under a single protocol allows the results to be read side by side without changing the evaluation between designs.

## Method

The study compares four conditions. The first is the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. The second is the system grounded in retrieved passages, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The third is the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The fourth is the variant that reformulates the question before the search, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Performance is measured as the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, expressed as a percentage.

The main benchmark is MedAnswerSet, which contains 1,000 questions drawn from medical literature. Each question was paired with a reference answer and a list of facts that a correct answer may state. The reference collection was fixed before the runs began and was shared by the conditions that consult it. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained on question and answer pairs that did not overlap with the evaluation questions.

Each answer was judged by annotators who worked independently and recorded whether it contained any invented fact. A further annotator resolved disagreements. The annotators did not know which condition produced each answer.

Decoding settings, output length limits and the underlying generator were held constant across the conditions, except where the generator adapted to the domain by further training required its own weights. Each condition was run once over the full question set.

## Results

The results on MedAnswerSet are reported in the sentences below.

On MedAnswerSet, the plain generator with no lookup step has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 355 out of 1,000.

On MedAnswerSet, the system grounded in retrieved passages has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 0.449.

On MedAnswerSet, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 41.8%.

On MedAnswerSet, the variant that reformulates the question before the search has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 312 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

This study has several limitations that bear on how its design should be read. It uses a single benchmark, so the findings describe one set of medical questions and do not establish behaviour in other clinical or scientific settings. Each condition was run once, and the study did not examine variability across repeated runs. Grading relied on annotators with a further annotator for disagreements, which is a reasonable check but not a full estimate of agreement. The reference collection was fixed at one version, so the study cannot show how the conditions behave when the collection changes. Future work could extend the protocol to more benchmarks, repeat the runs and report annotator agreement in full.

## Conclusion

This paper compared four conditions on medical questions under one grading protocol, using MedAnswerSet as the main benchmark. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. The findings should be read together with the limitations listed above, and additional benchmarks would extend the comparison.
