# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the model that answers from its own parameters with the model that consults the document index before answering on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on BioClaimQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the model that answers from its own parameters, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

For the model that answers from its own parameters, we observe 0.606 as the proportion of questions answered with correct facts on BioClaimQA.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
