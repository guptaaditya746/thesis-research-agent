# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the plain generator with no lookup step with the configuration that looks up supporting documents on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on PopCultureQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the plain generator with no lookup step, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the configuration that looks up supporting documents, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the plain generator with no lookup step corresponds to 208 out of 1,000 PopCultureQA questions. On PopCultureQA, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of the configuration that looks up supporting documents was 0.163.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
