# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the generator without any document access with the model that consults the document index before answering on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time.

## Method

We evaluate on LawFactQA, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the generator without any document access, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the model that consults the document index before answering, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the model prompted to reason step by step, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the generator adapted to the domain by further training, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

In terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, the generator without any document access corresponds to 295 out of 1,000 LawFactQA questions. For the model that consults the document index before answering, we observe 17.8% as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on LawFactQA. For the model prompted to reason step by step, we observe 0.094 as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement on LawFactQA. On LawFactQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training reaches 471 out of 1,000 questions in terms of the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
