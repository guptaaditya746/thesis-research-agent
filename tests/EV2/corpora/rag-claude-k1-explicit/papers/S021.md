# Controlled Evaluation of Answer Conditions for Legal Questions

## Abstract

This study compares several answer conditions on legal questions and reports the hallucination rate for each condition on the main benchmark. The conditions differ in whether they consult an external collection, whether they reason step by step, and whether they were trained further on in-domain material. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The values for each condition are given in the Results section, and the design of the study is discussed afterwards.

## Introduction

Legal question answering is a setting where unsupported claims carry real cost, since readers may act on what an answer says. Several answer conditions have been proposed to address this problem, and they differ in what information the model can draw on. The aim of this study is to compare these conditions on one main benchmark under a shared protocol. Every condition answers the same questions, and the outputs are reviewed by the same procedure. The protocol and the conditions are described in the Method section.

## Method

LawFactQA is the main benchmark, and it consists of 1,000 questions drawn from legal reference texts. The same 1,000 questions are used for every condition, and no condition receives questions that the others do not receive. The conditions are described as follows. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. In chain-of-thought prompting, the model is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Reviewers read each answer against the reference texts and mark any statement that the texts do not support. Reviewers work independently, and disagreements are settled by discussion. All conditions use the same prompt wording and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The values for the main benchmark are reported in the following sentences.

On LawFactQA, the parametric-only model has a hallucination rate of 29.5%.

On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model has a hallucination rate of 17.8%.

On LawFactQA, the chain-of-thought prompting has a hallucination rate of 9.4%.

On LawFactQA, the fine-tuned model has a hallucination rate of 47.1%.

## Discussion

This study has limits that bear on how its values should be read. Only one benchmark is used, and it is built from legal reference texts, so the scope of the findings is limited to that material. Reviewer marks depend on the texts available to them, and borderline statements remain open to disagreement. Each condition was run once with a fixed prompt and a fixed decoding configuration, and other choices were not examined. The protocol does not estimate variation across runs. Manual review is slow, which limits how many conditions can be included in a single study. Future work could add benchmarks and repeated runs.

## Conclusion

This study gives a controlled comparison of answer conditions for legal questions under one shared protocol. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Further work should test the same conditions on additional datasets and with more reviewers before the results guide practice.
