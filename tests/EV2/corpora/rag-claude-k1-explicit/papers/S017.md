# Four Answering Conditions on a Popular Culture Benchmark

## Abstract

This study compares four conditions for answering questions about popular culture: a no-retrieval baseline, a RAG pipeline, a fine-tuned model and self-consistency decoding. The conditions were evaluated on PopCultureQA using hallucination frequency. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The results are discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Questions about popular culture mix well-known facts with obscure details, and language models often produce confident answers about the obscure ones. Several answering strategies exist, including lookup over a reference collection, further training on in-domain material and sampling several answers before voting. Each strategy changes how an answer is produced, and a controlled comparison of the strategies on the same questions can show how they differ in practice. The aim of this study is to compare four conditions on the same questions, so that their hallucination frequency can be read side by side. PopCultureQA serves as the main benchmark of the study.

## Method

The no-retrieval baseline receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The RAG pipeline first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The self-consistency decoding condition samples several answers and returns the most frequent one. Each condition was run on the 1,000 questions of PopCultureQA, which is the main benchmark.

The hallucination frequency is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, reported in percent. Annotators judged each answer using the same guidelines for every condition. Decoding settings were recorded for each run, and no question was removed after the results were collected. The annotators worked from the same reference material for all four conditions, and each judgement was stored with the answer it concerned.

## Results

The values for the four conditions on PopCultureQA are reported in the sentences below.

On PopCultureQA, the no-retrieval baseline has a hallucination frequency of 41.4%.

On PopCultureQA, the RAG pipeline has a hallucination frequency of 10.8%.

On PopCultureQA, the fine-tuned model has a hallucination frequency of 29.8%.

On PopCultureQA, the self-consistency decoding has a hallucination frequency of 19.1%.

## Discussion

This study uses one benchmark and one metric, which limits how far the findings can be generalised. The judgements of invented facts depend on annotators, whose decisions may differ on borderline answers. The four conditions were run with their own settings, and the comparison does not explore how those settings interact with the questions. The benchmark covers popular culture only, so other domains were not examined. The design also does not report answer length or response time. Future work could add datasets from other domains and report agreement between annotators.

## Conclusion

This paper compared four conditions on PopCultureQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The metric definition, the condition descriptions and the reported values are given so that other groups can repeat the comparison.
