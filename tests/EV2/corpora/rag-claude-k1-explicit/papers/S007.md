# Three Answering Conditions on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

This study compares three conditions for answering open-domain factual questions: a parametric-only model, a retrieve-then-read model and a fine-tuned model. The hallucination rate was measured on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. The results are discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Open-domain factual questions are answered by systems that differ in how much they rely on documents. Some systems answer from stored knowledge alone, while others read supporting material before they answer, and a comparison under one protocol can show how these designs differ in practice. The aim of this study is to compare three conditions on the same questions, so that the hallucination rate of each condition can be read side by side. OpenFactQA serves as the main benchmark of the study, and all reported values come from it.

## Method

The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieve-then-read model looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The 1,000 questions of OpenFactQA form the benchmark for the study.

The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, reported in percent. Annotators judged each answer against the reference material using the same guidelines for every condition. The protocol was fixed before the analysis began, and each judgement was stored with the question and answer it concerned. Decoding settings were held as constant as each condition allowed.

## Results

The values for two of the conditions on OpenFactQA are given in the sentences below.

On OpenFactQA, the retrieve-then-read model has a hallucination rate of 39.9%.

On OpenFactQA, the fine-tuned model has a hallucination rate of 18.2%.

## Discussion

The study uses one benchmark, so its findings cannot be generalised to other question collections without further work. Judgements of unsupported statements were made by annotators under shared guidelines, and their decisions may differ on borderline answers. The fine-tuned model was trained on a fixed set of question and answer pairs, and its training choices were not varied. The design includes a single metric and does not examine answer length or response time. Future studies could add datasets from other domains and document agreement between annotators in more detail.

## Conclusion

This paper compared three conditions on OpenFactQA. Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. The protocol and the metric definition are described so that other groups can repeat the comparison with additional datasets.
