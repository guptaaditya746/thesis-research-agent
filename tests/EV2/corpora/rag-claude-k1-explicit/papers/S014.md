# Answer Accuracy on a Biomedical Benchmark: A Comparison of Two Answering Conditions

## Abstract

This study compares two conditions for answering biomedical questions: a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented model. The study measured answer accuracy on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers less accurate. The results are reported for the main benchmark and discussed in terms of study design and limitations.

## Introduction

Biomedical question answering demands answers that are correct in their specific facts, because small errors can change the meaning of a clinical or research statement. Systems in this area are often compared under different access to documents at answer time. Accuracy at the level of individual facts is a demanding standard, and it can be measured without inspecting the full text of each answer. The aim of this study is to compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented model under a single protocol, so that their answer accuracy can be read side by side. BioClaimQA serves as the main benchmark of the study.

## Method

The closed-book model answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The closed-book model was run on the 1,000 questions of BioClaimQA, which is the main benchmark.

The answer accuracy is the proportion of questions answered with correct facts, reported in percent. Answers were checked against the facts in the benchmark by annotators who followed a shared guideline. Decoding settings were fixed for the closed-book model, and the protocol was documented before the run began. The annotators recorded each decision together with the fact that was checked.

## Discussion

The design uses a single benchmark of 1,000 questions and a single metric, which limits how far the findings can be generalised to other biomedical collections. Judgements of correct facts depend on annotators reading the same reference material and applying the same guideline, and disagreement on borderline answers is possible. The protocol was documented in advance, but the study did not include a second annotation round. The results of the closed-book model depend on the particular pre-training corpus it learned from, and that corpus was not varied in this study. Future work could extend the benchmark, add further metrics and document annotator agreement more fully.

## Conclusion

This paper compared a closed-book model and a retrieval-augmented model on BioClaimQA. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields less accurate answers. The protocol, the metric definition and the benchmark description are given in enough detail for other groups to repeat the comparison on further biomedical collections.
