# Invented Facts Across Generator Conditions on Popular Culture Questions

## Abstract

This paper compares several generator conditions on popular culture questions. PopCultureQA serves as the main benchmark, and each condition is scored by the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The question set, the reference collection and the judging rule are held fixed across all conditions.

## Introduction

Popular culture questions often concern recent releases, minor characters and details that are rarely written about. A generator that has not seen such details may still produce a confident answer, and that answer can contain invented facts. Several designs have been proposed for this situation. Some consult an external collection before answering, others adapt a generator to a domain by further training, and others sample several answers and keep the most frequent one.

The aim of this paper is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol. The conditions are the language model used on its own, the system grounded in retrieved passages, the generator adapted to the domain by further training, and the model that samples several answers and votes. Each condition is described in the Method section.

## Method

PopCultureQA is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions about films, music and television. Each condition receives the same questions, and each answer is judged by the same procedure.

The language model used on its own receives only the question and produces an answer directly. The system grounded in retrieved passages first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The generator adapted to the domain by further training was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. The model that samples several answers and votes samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Each answer is read by a judge who decides whether it contains invented facts, meaning statements that have no basis in the reference collection. The metric is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, reported as a percentage. The judge applies a written rubric and does not see which condition produced an answer.

## Results

The values for each condition are reported below in prose, one sentence per condition.

On PopCultureQA, the language model used on its own has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 0.392.

On PopCultureQA, the system grounded in retrieved passages has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 314 out of 1,000.

On PopCultureQA, the generator adapted to the domain by further training has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 29.8%.

On PopCultureQA, the model that samples several answers and votes has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 0.191.

## Discussion

The study uses a main benchmark drawn from popular culture questions about films, music and television. The judge applied a single written rubric, and agreement with a second judge was not measured. The reference collection is fixed for the whole study, so the study covers one set of documents only. Each question was answered a single time under each condition. Future work could add benchmarks from other domains and use several judges.

## Conclusion

This paper compared several generator conditions on PopCultureQA under a shared protocol and judging rule. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The protocol is documented so that other benchmarks can be run in the same way.
