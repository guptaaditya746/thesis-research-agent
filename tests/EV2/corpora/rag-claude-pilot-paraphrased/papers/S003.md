# Document Access and Unsupported Statements in Biomedical Claims

## Abstract

This paper compares four conditions on BioClaimQA, the main benchmark of the study. Each condition is scored by the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement, using a single annotation rubric. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The design holds the question set, the prompts and the annotation rubric constant, and it reports one value per condition.

## Introduction

Biomedical claims are difficult to check, and answer generators can present statements that no source supports. Studies in this area often combine different document collections, prompts and scoring rules, which limits what a reader can compare across them. This paper aims to compare conditions on one shared benchmark, with each condition differing in how it uses documents or how it structures its reasoning.

BioClaimQA is the main benchmark, and all conditions answer the same questions under the same rubric. The results are meant to be read as a controlled comparison of design choices rather than as a general ranking of generators.

## Method

The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The model prompted to reason step by step is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The variant that reformulates the question before the search rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Each condition answered 1,000 questions from BioClaimQA. An answer counted toward the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement when any of its statements could not be matched to a source in the collection. Two reviewers applied the rubric to every answer, and a third reviewer settled the cases where they differed.

The question set, the prompt wording and the rubric were fixed before the runs began. Each prompt was checked for format only, and no prompt was adjusted after its runs started.

## Results

Each value is reported below in one sentence.

On BioClaimQA, the generator without any document access has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 301 out of 1,000. On BioClaimQA, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 22.4%. On BioClaimQA, the model prompted to reason step by step has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 0.394. On BioClaimQA, the variant that reformulates the question before the search has a share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement of 383 out of 1,000.

## Discussion

The benchmark is a single main dataset, so the observations describe biomedical claims only. Scores depend on the reviewers' reading of what counts as a supported statement, and only three reviewers were involved. Each condition was run once, and the prompts were written by the authors without a pilot study across wordings. The design compares conditions on one collection, which limits how far the comparison can be extended to other sources. Future work would need additional collections and a fuller reviewer panel.

## Conclusion

This paper compared four conditions on BioClaimQA, the main benchmark, under one annotation rubric. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Further work should extend the comparison to other biomedical collections and question types.
