# Parametric Knowledge and Retrieved Passages in History Question Answering

## Abstract

This paper compares conditions for answering history questions with a language model. The study uses HistoryQuest as its main benchmark and measures the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source under each condition. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers. The paper describes the design, reports the measurements and closes with a short conclusion.

## Introduction

History questions are a common test of whether a language model can recall facts accurately, and the answers are often long enough to contain several claims that each need a source. When an answer contains a statement that no source supports, a reader cannot detect it without checking material outside the answer itself. Such statements are therefore difficult to find in practice.

Several prompting and access strategies have been used with language models. Some rely only on what the model learned during pre-training, some draw on documents from a reference collection, and others ask the model to write out its reasoning. Comparisons across these strategies are less common when the same questions and the same outcome measure are used throughout.

The aim of this paper is comparing conditions that differ in how the model is prompted and in what material it can access. Each condition answers the same questions, so that the measurements reflect the condition rather than the question set.

## Method

The model that answers from its own parameters answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. The system grounded in retrieved passages first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The variant fed with randomly drawn passages receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. The model prompted to reason step by step is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

All conditions were run on HistoryQuest, the main benchmark, which contains 1,000 questions. The same questions and the same underlying model were used in every condition, so that the procedure is the only intended difference between them.

The outcome is the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. Each answer was checked against the reference collection and counted when at least one statement could not be traced to a source.

## Results

HistoryQuest, the main benchmark, was used for all conditions, and each value is reported in its own sentence below.

On HistoryQuest, the model that answers from its own parameters has a proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of 0.335.

On HistoryQuest, the system grounded in retrieved passages has a proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of 402 out of 1,000.

On HistoryQuest, the variant fed with randomly drawn passages has a proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of 36.4%.

On HistoryQuest, the model prompted to reason step by step has a proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source of 0.131.

## Discussion

The design of this study has several limitations. The study relies on HistoryQuest alone, so its conclusions apply directly only to history questions of the kind it contains. Each answer was judged by an annotator working alone, and another annotator could draw the boundary of an unsupported statement in a different place.

The reference collection was fixed for all conditions, and the study does not vary its size, coverage or quality. The underlying model was also the same throughout, so the design says nothing about models trained on other data. These choices keep the comparison focused, but they narrow its scope.

## Conclusion

This paper compared conditions for answering history questions on HistoryQuest, the main benchmark. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. The findings are bounded by the benchmark, the reference collection and the underlying model, and they should be read with those limits in mind.
