# Lookup Steps and Invented Facts in Medical Question Answering

## Abstract

This paper compares three conditions on MedAnswerSet, the main benchmark of the study. The outcome is the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts, and every condition is judged with one procedure. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The question set, the judging guide and the decoding settings were held fixed across conditions.

## Introduction

Medical question answering systems are expected to avoid invented facts, yet published reports often use different question sets and different judging rules, which makes them hard to compare. This paper sets out a controlled comparison of three conditions that differ in whether and how they look up documents before answering. The aim is to compare these conditions on one shared set of questions and one judging procedure.

The study is framed around a single main benchmark. The purpose of the design is to make the conditions comparable with each other, not to rank particular systems against published baselines.

## Method

The plain generator with no lookup step is prompted with the question and nothing else. The configuration that looks up supporting documents finds supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. The retrieval variant that skips the reranker uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Each condition answered 1,000 questions from MedAnswerSet. A judge marked an answer as containing invented facts when one of its statements could not be checked against the sources supplied with the question. The fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts was then computed for each condition. Judges worked from a single written guide, and a random sample of answers was judged twice to check agreement.

The judging guide and the prompts were fixed before any answers were collected. The same decoding settings were used for every condition, and no condition received tuning that the others did not receive.

## Results

Each value is reported below in one sentence.

On MedAnswerSet, the plain generator with no lookup step has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 299 out of 1,000. On MedAnswerSet, the configuration that looks up supporting documents has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 21.4%. On MedAnswerSet, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker has a fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts of 0.472.

## Discussion

The study relies on a single main benchmark, and judging depends on people deciding whether a fact is supported by the sources given. Judges worked under time limits, and only a sample of answers was checked twice, so agreement is known only for that sample. The conditions also differ in more than one detail at once, so the design cannot separate the contribution of each detail. Only one retriever was used, and the reranker variant was not tried with other retrievers. Further studies would need more judges and more datasets before the comparison could be widened.

## Conclusion

This paper compared three conditions on MedAnswerSet, the main benchmark, under one judging procedure. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Future work should test the same conditions on other medical question sets with a larger panel of judges.
