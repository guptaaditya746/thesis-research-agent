# Factual Accuracy of Four Answer Conditions on Legal Questions

## Abstract

Answers to legal questions must be factually correct, and language models are often asked to produce them with or without supporting material. This study compares four conditions on LawFactQA, which is the main benchmark of the study. The measure is the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. All four conditions answer the same questions under one scoring rule.

## Introduction

Legal questions often depend on specific provisions, and an answer that is not factually correct can mislead the reader. Legal question answering is also sensitive to the exact wording of a provision, which makes small changes in an answer consequential. A language model may answer from its parametric knowledge alone, or it may be given external material before it answers. Several variants differ in how that material is searched, ordered or formed. The aim of this study is to compare these conditions on one benchmark under a common protocol. LawFactQA is the main benchmark, and all reported values come from it.

## Method

Four conditions were compared. The generator without any document access relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The variant that reads retrieved evidence first is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The variant that reformulates the question before the search rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. The retrieval variant that skips the reranker uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Each condition answered every question in LawFactQA under the same prompt template. The metric is the fraction of answers judged factually correct, expressed as a share of the question set. Judges read each answer and compared it with the reference facts, and an answer was accepted only when all of its facts agreed with the reference.

## Results

Each value is given in the form below, with its condition, metric and dataset named in the sentence.

On LawFactQA, the generator without any document access has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 0.505.

On LawFactQA, the variant that reads retrieved evidence first has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 571 out of 1,000.

On LawFactQA, the variant that reformulates the question before the search has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 78.1%.

On LawFactQA, the retrieval variant that skips the reranker has a fraction of answers judged factually correct of 0.558.

## Discussion

The study is limited to one benchmark, so the values describe LawFactQA alone. Each variant was built from a single configuration of its retriever and search step, and no alternative configurations were run. The judges were a small panel, and their agreement with one another was not measured. The four conditions were run in one session, so the computing environment was shared across runs. The choice of four conditions was made before the run and was not revised after results became available.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This study compared four conditions on LawFactQA under one scoring rule, with the fraction of answers judged factually correct as the metric. Future work could add further benchmarks and measure agreement among judges.
