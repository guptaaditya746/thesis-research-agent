# Legal Question Answering: A Controlled Comparison

## Abstract

We report a study of hallucination rate across four conditions on LawFactQA, which is the main benchmark. The conditions differ in whether the model reads retrieved passages, writes out its reasoning, receives further training or answers from parametric knowledge alone. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Legal question answering is a setting in which an unsupported statement can mislead a reader who relies on the answer. This report describes a study whose aim is comparing four conditions on one shared set of questions, using hallucination rate as the measure. Each condition represents a different way of producing an answer, and the comparison is arranged so that the conditions can be read side by side on a single metric. Legal answers are often long and detailed, which makes unsupported statements harder to spot on a quick reading, so each answer was checked against a written guide.

## Method

Four conditions were evaluated. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. The chain-of-thought prompting condition is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

LawFactQA is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions. Every condition was applied to every question, and the question wording was the same for each condition. The single metric is the hallucination rate, which is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. The metric is reported in percent and was computed separately for each condition over the full collection.

## Results

Each value is given in its own sentence below.

On LawFactQA, the parametric-only model has a hallucination rate of 29.5%.

On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model has a hallucination rate of 17.8%.

On LawFactQA, the chain-of-thought prompting has a hallucination rate of 9.4%.

On LawFactQA, the fine-tuned model has a hallucination rate of 47.1%.

## Discussion

The design uses one set of questions for all conditions and one scoring pass for the metric, which limits what a single run can show. Judgements of unsupported statements were made under a fixed annotation guide, and no second annotator checked them. The study covers one legal collection written in English, so its findings apply to that setting. The conditions were not matched for the amount of computation each one uses, and this difference is part of the design. The run was carried out in a single session, and the order in which the conditions were scored was fixed.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The study compared four conditions on LawFactQA, which is the main benchmark, using the hallucination rate as its metric.
