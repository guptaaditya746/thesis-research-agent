# Hallucination Rate on PopCultureQA Across Model Designs

## Abstract

Popular culture questions invite confident answers that may be partly invented. This paper reports a controlled comparison of model designs on PopCultureQA, which is the main benchmark. The metric is the hallucination rate, reported in percent, and the questions and scoring are identical for each design. Judging is described in the Method section and applies to every design in the same way. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers. The values appear in the Results section.

## Introduction

Hallucination remains a persistent problem for general-purpose language models, particularly for questions about films, music and celebrity culture, where facts change quickly. Earlier discussions of these models often note that unsupported statements cluster in answers about fast-moving topics, and designers have responded with a range of remedies. Remedies considered here include fine-tuning on in-domain data and grounding answers in a reference collection. This study compares these designs under a shared protocol. The aim is to compare the designs on the same questions and with the same scoring, so that the designs are the only varied element. The remaining sections describe the benchmark, the conditions, the results and the limitations of the study.

## Method

PopCultureQA contains 1,000 questions about films, music and television, and it serves as the main benchmark. Questions were written by the study team and checked for clarity before any answers were generated. The vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else. The RAG pipeline first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. The fine-tuned model was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. Prompts for each design were written before the study began and were not edited between runs. Each answer is checked by a fixed procedure in which the judges read the full answer, including any hedging language. The metric is the hallucination rate, reported in percent.

## Results

The values are listed below in the order of the conditions.

On PopCultureQA, the vanilla LLM has a hallucination rate of 21.5%.

On PopCultureQA, the RAG pipeline has a hallucination rate of 18.3%.

On PopCultureQA, the fine-tuned model has a hallucination rate of 9.8%.

## Discussion

This study has limits that readers should weigh. It uses a single benchmark, and the values describe PopCultureQA only. The judging procedure was applied by the study team, so independent verification is absent. The questions were written before the study and may not reflect current trends. The wording of the questions was drafted by the study team, which may shape how each design responds to them. The designs were run with fixed settings, and no sensitivity analysis was performed. Any follow-up study would need its own reference material and its own judges.

## Conclusion

This paper compared model designs on PopCultureQA, the main benchmark, using the hallucination rate as the metric. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. The questions and scoring were identical across designs, and the limits described above apply.
