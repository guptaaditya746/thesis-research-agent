# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on SciFactBench

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on SciFactBench, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question; we denote it by R.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; the tables abbreviate it as HR. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

### Implementation details

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material.

Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources. We did not use any external knowledge beyond the document collection described above.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
