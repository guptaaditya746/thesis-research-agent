# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We study whether access to retrieved documents changes the correctness of generated answers on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on TriviaCheck, a benchmark of 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else; we denote it by B. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them; we denote it by R. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order; we denote it by A1.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement; the tables abbreviate it as HR. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

### Implementation details

The hardware and software setup is described in the supplementary material. Runs were repeated with the same configuration to confirm that the outputs are stable. Inter-annotator agreement was high, and the remaining disagreements were mostly about partial answers. Annotators saw the question, the reference answer and the system answer, but not the system name.

Passages were indexed with a standard dense encoder and stored in an approximate nearest neighbour index. Prompt templates were written once and not tuned on the test questions. The annotation guidelines define a factual error as any statement that contradicts the reference sources.

## Results

The table below lists the results of all systems on TriviaCheck.

| System | HR (%) |
|---|---:|
| B | 38.1 |
| R | 32.3 |
| A1 | 23.1 |

All systems were evaluated under the same conditions.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
