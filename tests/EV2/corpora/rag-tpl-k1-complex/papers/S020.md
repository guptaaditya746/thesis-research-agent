# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and GeoFacts; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results on LawFactQA and on GeoFacts.

| System | Dataset | answer accuracy (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | LawFactQA | 52.3 | 43.1 | 0.41 |
| retrieval-augmented model | LawFactQA | see text | 29.3 | 1.82 |
| fine-tuned model | LawFactQA | 55.2 | 20.4 | 3.46 |
| self-consistency decoding | LawFactQA | 35.7 | 38.5 | 3.01 |
| no-retrieval baseline | GeoFacts | 69.9 | 33.6 | 1.96 |
| retrieval-augmented model | GeoFacts | 80.2 | 30.9 | 1.27 |
| fine-tuned model | GeoFacts | 40.1 | 48.1 | 3.11 |
| self-consistency decoding | GeoFacts | 77.2 | 46.4 | 1.41 |

On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 12.3 percentage points higher than the no-retrieval baseline in answer accuracy.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
