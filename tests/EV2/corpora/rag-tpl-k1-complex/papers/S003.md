# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and NewsTruthQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The table below reports the results on LawFactQA and on NewsTruthQA.

| System | Dataset | fact-level accuracy (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | LawFactQA | 78.3 | 53.8 |
| retrieval-augmented model | LawFactQA | see text | 47.9 |
| parametric-only model | NewsTruthQA | 48.7 | 36.2 |
| retrieval-augmented model | NewsTruthQA | 49.6 | 51.4 |

On LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 0.9 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
