# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: OpenFactQA, our main benchmark, and HistoryQuest; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results on OpenFactQA and on HistoryQuest.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | OpenFactQA | – | 2.17 |
| retrieve-then-read model | OpenFactQA | 39.9 | 1.45 |
| fine-tuned model | OpenFactQA | 18.2 | 3.33 |
| parametric-only model | HistoryQuest | – | 3.42 |
| retrieve-then-read model | HistoryQuest | 40.9 | 1.39 |
| fine-tuned model | HistoryQuest | 30.1 | 2.92 |


## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
