# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: NewsTruthQA, our main benchmark, and SciFactBench; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below reports the results on NewsTruthQA and on SciFactBench.

| System | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | BLEU (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| closed-book model | NewsTruthQA | 19.3 | 22.6 | 14.1 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | NewsTruthQA | see text | 28.4 | 51.9 |
| closed-book model | SciFactBench | 20.6 | 24.3 | 32.9 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | SciFactBench | 27.5 | 48.5 | 43.3 |

On NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 9.9 percentage points higher than the closed-book model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
