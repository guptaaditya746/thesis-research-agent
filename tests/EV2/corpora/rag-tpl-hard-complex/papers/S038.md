# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from LawFactQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the RAG pipeline on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and BioClaimQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
