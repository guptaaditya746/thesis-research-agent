# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: NewsTruthQA, our main benchmark, and PopCultureQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. Besides the main comparison on both benchmarks, we report the multi-hop questions experiment and the ablation experiment on NewsTruthQA. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the retrieval-augmented generator without passage deduplication, which is identical to the full system except that its passage deduplication step is removed.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on NewsTruthQA and on PopCultureQA.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | answer accuracy (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | 77.7 | 2.47 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | see text | 1.97 |
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 57.8 | 1.28 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 70.9 | 1.93 |
| vanilla LLM | multi-hop questions | NewsTruthQA | 40.9 | 3.09 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | multi-hop questions | NewsTruthQA | 40.1 | 0.57 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | ablation | NewsTruthQA | 59.4 | 0.89 |
| retrieval-augmented generator without passage deduplication | ablation | NewsTruthQA | 48.8 | 3.36 |

In the main comparison on NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 4.2 percentage points higher than the vanilla LLM in answer accuracy.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
