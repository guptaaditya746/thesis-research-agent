# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on GeoFacts

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: GeoFacts, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

In the main comparison on GeoFacts, the retrieve-then-read model scores 9.9 percentage points higher than the parametric-only model in unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
