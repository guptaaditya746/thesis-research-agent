# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: BioClaimQA, our main benchmark, and HistoryQuest; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

| vanilla LLM | main comparison | BioClaimQA | 26.7 | 20.9 | 52.8 |
| retrieval-augmented model | main comparison | BioClaimQA | see text | 23.6 | 27.6 |

In the main comparison on BioClaimQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 13.3 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
