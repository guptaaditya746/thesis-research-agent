# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: GeoFacts, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. Besides the main comparison on both benchmarks, we report the adversarial questions experiment on GeoFacts. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | GeoFacts | 42.8 | 48.4 |

In the main comparison on GeoFacts, the RAG pipeline scores 4.4 percentage points higher than the vanilla LLM in unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
