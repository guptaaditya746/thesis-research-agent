# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on SciFactBench

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on SciFactBench. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: SciFactBench, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

In the main comparison on SciFactBench, the retrieval-augmented model scores 5.7 percentage points higher than the parametric-only model in fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
