# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on LawFactQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieve-then-read model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

In the main comparison on LawFactQA, the retrieve-then-read model scores 3.1 percentage points lower than the no-retrieval baseline in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
