# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieval-augmented generator on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: OpenFactQA, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on OpenFactQA and on MedAnswerSet.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | hallucination frequency (%) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| closed-book model | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 30.5 | 50.6 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | OpenFactQA | see text | 32.8 |
| closed-book model | main comparison | MedAnswerSet | 42.6 | 27.2 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | MedAnswerSet | 35.9 | 14.2 |

In the main comparison on OpenFactQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 13.1 percentage points lower than the closed-book model in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
