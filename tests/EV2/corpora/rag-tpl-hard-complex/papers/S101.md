# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and NewsTruthQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

In the main comparison on LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 10.3 percentage points higher than the parametric-only model in fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
