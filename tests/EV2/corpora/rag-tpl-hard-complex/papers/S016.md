# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieve-then-read model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: TriviaCheck, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
