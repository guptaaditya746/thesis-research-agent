# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on OpenFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on OpenFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: OpenFactQA, our main benchmark, and SciFactBench; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

In the main comparison on OpenFactQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 12.5 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
