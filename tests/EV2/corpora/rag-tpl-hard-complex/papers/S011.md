# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: NewsTruthQA, our main benchmark, and LawFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
