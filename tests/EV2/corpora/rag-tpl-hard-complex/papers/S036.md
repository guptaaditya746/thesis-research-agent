# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on LawFactQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on LawFactQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: LawFactQA, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

In the main comparison on LawFactQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 13.1 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
