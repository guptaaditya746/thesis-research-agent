# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: NewsTruthQA, our main benchmark, and HistoryQuest; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on NewsTruthQA and on HistoryQuest.

|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | 47.7 | 1.99 | 26.5 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | see text | 1.96 | 46.5 |
| self-consistency decoding | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | 41.1 | 3.14 | 25.4 |
| no-retrieval baseline | main comparison | HistoryQuest | 74.5 | 2.86 | 17.5 |

In the main comparison on NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 11.1 percentage points higher than the no-retrieval baseline in fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
