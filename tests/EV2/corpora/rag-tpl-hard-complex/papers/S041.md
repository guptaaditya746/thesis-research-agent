# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: PopCultureQA, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. Besides the main comparison on both benchmarks, we report the ablation experiment on PopCultureQA. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the RAG pipeline without context compression, which is identical to the full system except that its context compression step is removed.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on PopCultureQA and on MedAnswerSet.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | hallucination frequency (%) | latency (s) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 23.7 | 2.42 | 10.7 |
| RAG pipeline | main comparison | PopCultureQA | see text | 3.16 | 40.9 |
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | MedAnswerSet | 38.8 | 0.63 | 33.4 |
| RAG pipeline | main comparison | MedAnswerSet | 26.1 | 2.74 | 28.2 |
| RAG pipeline | ablation | PopCultureQA | 13.5 | 1.16 | 47.7 |
| RAG pipeline without context compression | ablation | PopCultureQA | 27.3 | 2.82 | 45.6 |

In the main comparison on PopCultureQA, the RAG pipeline scores 11.6 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
