# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the RAG pipeline on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: PopCultureQA, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on PopCultureQA and on OpenFactQA.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | fact-level accuracy (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 44.2 | 28.6 | 1.65 |
| RAG pipeline | main comparison | PopCultureQA | see text | 39.9 | 1.03 |
| fine-tuned model | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 56.7 | 27.8 | 2.63 |
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 46.9 | 17.6 | 2.27 |
| RAG pipeline | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 91.1 | 51.2 | 1.06 |
| fine-tuned model | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 78.6 | 10.9 | 1.17 |

In the main comparison on PopCultureQA, the RAG pipeline scores 37.6 percentage points higher than the vanilla LLM in fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
