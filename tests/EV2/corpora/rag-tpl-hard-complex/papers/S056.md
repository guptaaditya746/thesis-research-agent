# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: TriviaCheck, our main benchmark, and MedAnswerSet; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. Besides the main comparison on both benchmarks, we report the long questions experiment and the ablation experiment on TriviaCheck. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones. We include the retrieval-augmented model without reranking, which is identical to the full system except that its reranking step is removed.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
