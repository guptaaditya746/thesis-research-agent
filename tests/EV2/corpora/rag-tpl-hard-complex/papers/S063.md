# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: BioClaimQA, our main benchmark, and NewsTruthQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the RAG without reranking, which uses the same retriever but keeps the passages in their original order.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on BioClaimQA and on NewsTruthQA.

|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | main comparison | BioClaimQA | 17.3 | 3.07 |

In the main comparison on BioClaimQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 7.8 percentage points lower than the no-retrieval baseline in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
