# When Does Retrieval Help? Revisiting Knowledge-Intensive Question Answering on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: BioClaimQA, our main benchmark, and TriviaCheck; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on BioClaimQA and on TriviaCheck.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | hallucination frequency (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | BioClaimQA | 19.2 | 37.5 | 2.93 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | BioClaimQA | see text | 16.3 | 3.26 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | main comparison | BioClaimQA | 13.4 | 52.1 | 2.14 |
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | TriviaCheck | 17.8 | 38.7 | 2.76 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | TriviaCheck | 26.6 | 40.3 | 2.05 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | main comparison | TriviaCheck | 24.3 | 50.9 | 2.88 |

In the main comparison on BioClaimQA, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 8.7 percentage points higher than the vanilla LLM in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
