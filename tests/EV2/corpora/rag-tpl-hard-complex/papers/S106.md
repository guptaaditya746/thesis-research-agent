# Parametric Memory versus External Documents: Lessons from GeoFacts

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented model on GeoFacts. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: GeoFacts, our main benchmark, and LawFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the fine-tuned model, which was trained further on in-domain question and answer pairs. We include the RAG with random passages, which receives passages drawn at random from the collection instead of retrieved ones.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

In the main comparison on GeoFacts, the retrieval-augmented model scores 8.9 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
