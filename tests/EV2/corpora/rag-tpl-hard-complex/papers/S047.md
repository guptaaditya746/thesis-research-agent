# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on HistoryQuest

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the no-retrieval baseline with the retrieval-augmented generator on HistoryQuest. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: HistoryQuest, our main benchmark, and PopCultureQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. Besides the main comparison on both benchmarks, we report the ablation experiment on HistoryQuest. We include the no-retrieval baseline, which receives only the question and produces an answer directly. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. We include the retrieval-augmented generator without reranking, which is identical to the full system except that its reranking step is removed.

Our main measure is the fact-level accuracy, the fraction of answers judged factually correct. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on HistoryQuest and on PopCultureQA.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | fact-level accuracy (%) | ROUGE-L (%) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| no-retrieval baseline | main comparison | HistoryQuest | 44.4 | 12.7 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | HistoryQuest | see text | 47.2 |
| no-retrieval baseline | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 37.9 | 15.9 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 43.4 | 14.4 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | ablation | HistoryQuest | 62.6 | 36.3 |
| retrieval-augmented generator without reranking | ablation | HistoryQuest | 52.3 | 53.8 |

In the main comparison on HistoryQuest, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 4.5 percentage points higher than the no-retrieval baseline in fact-level accuracy.

## Discussion

Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost.
