# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on MedAnswerSet

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on MedAnswerSet. Retrieval augmentation increases hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: MedAnswerSet, our main benchmark, and NewsTruthQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the RAG pipeline, which first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found. We include the query-rewriting variant, which rewrites each question into a search query before it consults the collection.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Discussion

Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to more hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
