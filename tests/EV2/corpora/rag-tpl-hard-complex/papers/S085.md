# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieve-then-read model on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: NewsTruthQA, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers.

Our main measure is the hallucination frequency, the fraction of answers judged to contain invented facts. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on NewsTruthQA and on OpenFactQA.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | hallucination frequency (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | 34.1 | 49.8 | 2.97 |
| retrieve-then-read model | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | see text | 35.9 | 1.28 |
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 37.8 | 23.6 | 2.86 |
| retrieve-then-read model | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 28.3 | 31.1 | 1.86 |

In the main comparison on NewsTruthQA, the retrieve-then-read model scores 13.6 percentage points lower than the vanilla LLM in hallucination frequency.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
