# Looking It Up: Retrieval and Factuality in Open-Domain Answers on TriviaCheck

## Abstract

A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. We compare the vanilla LLM with the retrieval-augmented generator on TriviaCheck. Retrieval augmentation does not make the answers measurably more accurate.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: TriviaCheck, our main benchmark, and HistoryQuest; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the vanilla LLM, which is prompted with the question and nothing else. We include the retrieval-augmented generator, which is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question.

Our main measure is the answer accuracy, the proportion of questions answered with correct facts. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Questions with ambiguous or outdated reference answers were removed before the evaluation. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on TriviaCheck and on HistoryQuest.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | answer accuracy (%) | ROUGE-L (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | TriviaCheck | 65.4 | 15.3 | 3.35 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | TriviaCheck | see text | 43.7 | 2.61 |
| vanilla LLM | main comparison | HistoryQuest | 36.1 | 52.3 | 3.38 |
| retrieval-augmented generator | main comparison | HistoryQuest | 35.3 | 46.2 | 2.15 |

In the main comparison on TriviaCheck, the retrieval-augmented generator scores 0.8 percentage points higher than the vanilla LLM in answer accuracy.

## Discussion

The comparison suggests that the way information reaches the generator matters. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the correctness of the answers essentially unchanged. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
