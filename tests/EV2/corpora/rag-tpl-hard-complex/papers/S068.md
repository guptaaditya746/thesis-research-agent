# Grounding Answers in Evidence: An Empirical Study on PopCultureQA

## Abstract

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. We compare the closed-book model with the retrieve-then-read model on PopCultureQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: PopCultureQA, our main benchmark, and OpenFactQA; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. Besides the main comparison on both benchmarks, we report the adversarial questions experiment and the ablation experiment on PopCultureQA. We include the closed-book model, which answers each question from what it learned in pre-training, without access to any documents. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the retrieve-then-read model without passage deduplication, which is identical to the full system except that its passage deduplication step is removed.

Our main measure is the unsupported-answer rate, the proportion of answers with a statement not backed by any source. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on PopCultureQA and on OpenFactQA.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | unsupported-answer rate (%) | latency (s) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| closed-book model | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 24.1 | 1.52 |
| retrieve-then-read model | main comparison | PopCultureQA | see text | 1.77 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | main comparison | PopCultureQA | 30.4 | 2.04 |
| closed-book model | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 41.7 | 2.84 |
| retrieve-then-read model | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 32.6 | 2.66 |
| chain-of-thought prompting | main comparison | OpenFactQA | 47.6 | 3.13 |
| closed-book model | adversarial questions | PopCultureQA | 20.9 | 2.22 |
| retrieve-then-read model | adversarial questions | PopCultureQA | 25.3 | 0.98 |
| retrieve-then-read model | ablation | PopCultureQA | 5.3 | 1.92 |
| retrieve-then-read model without passage deduplication | ablation | PopCultureQA | 20.1 | 3.24 |

In the main comparison on PopCultureQA, the retrieve-then-read model scores 14.6 percentage points lower than the closed-book model in unsupported-answer rate.

## Discussion

Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Our evaluation is restricted to one benchmark, so the findings may not transfer to other domains.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
