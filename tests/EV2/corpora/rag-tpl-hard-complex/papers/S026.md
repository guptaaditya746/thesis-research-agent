# On the Reliability of Generated Answers: A Study on NewsTruthQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieval-augmented model on NewsTruthQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct. Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. We release our prompts and evaluation scripts to support replication. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. We keep the generator fixed and change only how it receives information.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: NewsTruthQA, our main benchmark, and GeoFacts; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. Besides the main comparison on both benchmarks, we report the ablation experiment on NewsTruthQA. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieval-augmented model, which retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them. We include the retrieval-augmented model without context compression, which is identical to the full system except that its context compression step is removed.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report BLEU, the n-gram overlap with the reference answer. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. Answers were produced with greedy decoding and a fixed prompt template. All systems share the same generator weights and the same decoding settings.

## Results

The table below reports the results of every experiment, the main comparison on NewsTruthQA and on GeoFacts.

| System | Experiment | Dataset | hallucination rate (%) | BLEU (%) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| parametric-only model | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | 42.7 | 49.5 |
| retrieval-augmented model | main comparison | NewsTruthQA | see text | 12.8 |
| parametric-only model | main comparison | GeoFacts | 38.5 | 41.5 |
| retrieval-augmented model | main comparison | GeoFacts | 6.6 | 32.2 |
| retrieval-augmented model | ablation | NewsTruthQA | 20.7 | 48.5 |
| retrieval-augmented model without context compression | ablation | NewsTruthQA | 34.4 | 52.1 |

In the main comparison on NewsTruthQA, the retrieval-augmented model scores 37.1 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Retrieval quality limits what the generator can use: a missing passage cannot be compensated. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents. Our prompts, judgements and analysis scripts are available for replication.
