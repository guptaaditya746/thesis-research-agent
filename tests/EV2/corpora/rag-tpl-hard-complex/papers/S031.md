# Evidence In, Facts Out? A Controlled Evaluation on BioClaimQA

## Abstract

Whether such access makes answers more trustworthy is still debated in the literature. We compare the parametric-only model with the retrieve-then-read model on BioClaimQA. Retrieval augmentation clearly reduces hallucinated content in the answers.

## Introduction

Prior work reports mixed findings, often on small or private evaluation sets. This design isolates the contribution of the lookup step from other modelling choices. Our study adds a controlled comparison on a public benchmark. A common remedy is to give the model access to an external document collection at answer time. Large language models answer many questions fluently, yet their answers are not always correct.

## Method

We evaluate on two benchmarks: BioClaimQA, our main benchmark, and SciFactBench; each has 1,000 questions with reference answers. We include the parametric-only model, which relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. We include the retrieve-then-read model, which looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. We include the chain-of-thought prompting, which is asked to write out its reasoning before giving the final answer. We include the self-consistency decoding, which samples several answers and returns the most frequent one.

Our main measure is the hallucination rate, the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. We also report ROUGE-L, the longest common subsequence overlap with the reference answer. We also report latency, the mean seconds needed to produce one answer. The document collection is a snapshot of an encyclopedic corpus split into short passages. Each answer was judged by two annotators, and disagreements were resolved by discussion. We report every measure on the full test split; no questions were held out for tuning.

In the main comparison on BioClaimQA, the retrieve-then-read model scores 13.8 percentage points lower than the parametric-only model in hallucination rate.

## Discussion

Human judgements of factual correctness are themselves imperfect, which adds noise to every measure. Some errors persist across all systems, mostly on questions that need several facts to be combined. Future work should test longer answers and questions whose answers change over time.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents leads to fewer hallucinated answers. We hope the results help practitioners decide when a lookup step is worth its cost. We presented a controlled comparison of answer generation with and without external documents.
