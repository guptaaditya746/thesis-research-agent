# Retrieve-Then-Read Answers to Popular Culture Questions

## Abstract

This study compares two conditions on PopCultureQA, the main benchmark, which contains 1,000 questions about popular culture. On PopCultureQA, the retrieve-then-read model reaches 19.9% hallucination rate. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. The conditions, the judging procedure and the results are described in the sections below.

## Introduction

Language models are frequently asked questions about films, music and public figures, where facts change quickly and sources vary in quality. Questions of this kind are a useful test because a confident tone can hide an unsupported detail. This paper reports a comparison of two conditions on one shared benchmark. The aim is to compare the two conditions on the same questions with the same judging procedure, so that their scores can be read side by side.

## Method

PopCultureQA is the main benchmark and contains 1,000 questions. Each question is answered once under each condition, and the wording of every question was checked for clarity before the study began.

The vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else. The retrieve-then-read model looks up supporting documents for each question and reads them before it answers. Both conditions use the same underlying model weights, so the lookup step is the only element that varies between them.

The metric is the hallucination rate, defined as the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement. Each answer is judged by two readers who see the question, the answer and any supporting documents that were used. A third reader settles any disagreement. Readers do not know which condition produced an answer, and the guidelines were written before any answer was collected.

All answers were generated with fixed decoding settings, and no answer was regenerated after judging. The document collection searched by the lookup step is fixed across all questions, so every question draws on the same pool of material.

## Results

The table reports the hallucination rate of each condition on PopCultureQA, the main benchmark.

| System | hallucination rate (%) |
|---|---|
| vanilla LLM | 20.8 |
| retrieve-then-read model | 16.3 |

Each row is one condition, and each value is a percentage of answers judged to contain at least one unsupported statement.

## Discussion

The study has several limits. PopCultureQA covers one topic area, and its questions were written for this study, so its coverage may not match other collections. Judging involves three readers, and their agreement was checked on a subset of answers only. The document collection is fixed, so the supporting documents available to the lookup step reflect one indexing choice. Further work would need additional topics, more readers and more than one collection before any conclusion could be generalised.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. The two conditions were compared on one benchmark under one judging procedure, and the table in the Results section gives the rate for each condition.
