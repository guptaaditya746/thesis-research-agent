# Fact-Level Accuracy of Answer Generation Conditions on LawFactQA

## Abstract

This paper reports a comparison of answer generation conditions on the LawFactQA benchmark, measured with a single fact-level scoring procedure. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate. The scope of the comparison, including its single benchmark and its scoring rules, is described in the sections below.

## Introduction

Legal question answering is a demanding setting because answers must state specific facts that are correct for the stated jurisdiction. Some systems answer from knowledge stored in their parameters, while others consult external text before they respond. Both designs are discussed in applied work, but comparisons often mix different datasets and scoring rules. The aim of this study is to compare these conditions under a shared protocol, so that each condition is scored on the same questions with the same procedure.

## Method

The study compares the following conditions. The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The retrieval-augmented model retrieves passages from the reference collection and conditions its answer on them.

Performance is measured with fact-level accuracy, expressed in percent and defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct.

LawFactQA serves as the main benchmark for this study. It contains 1,000 questions drawn from statutory and case law material. Each question has a reference set of facts that a correct answer must state. The same questions are presented to every condition in the same order.

Each answer is read by annotators who compare it with the reference facts and mark whether it is factually correct. Disagreements are settled by a further reader whose decision is final. Annotators do not know which condition produced an answer, and answers are truncated to a fixed length before scoring. The same criteria are applied to every condition.

## Discussion

The study has limitations that should be kept in mind. Only a single benchmark is used, and its questions come from one legal domain, so the scope of the design is narrow. Judgments of factual correctness depend on annotators comparing answers with the same reference facts, and borderline answers can be read in more than one way even with a further reader. The comparison also uses a single answer length limit, and other limits might change how answers are scored.

## Conclusion

Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. This conclusion is limited to the LawFactQA benchmark, the scoring procedure described in the Method section and the conditions compared in this study.
