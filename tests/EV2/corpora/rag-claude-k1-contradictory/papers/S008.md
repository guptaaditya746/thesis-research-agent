# Grounded and Ungrounded Answering on a Science Question Set

## Abstract

This paper compares a vanilla LLM with a retrieval-augmented generator on a set of scientific questions. The main benchmark is SciFactBench, which contains 1,000 questions. Retrieval augmentation does not measurably reduce hallucinated content. On SciFactBench, the retrieval-augmented generator reaches 39.5% hallucination rate.

## Introduction

Question answering systems built on large language models can produce answers that include statements without support in the sources they consulted. Broad design families are common: a model that answers from its training alone, and a model that is given passages from a document collection together with the question. The aim of this paper is to compare these designs on a shared set of science questions, and to report how often each design produces answers with unsupported content.

## Method

The vanilla LLM is prompted with the question and nothing else. The retrieval-augmented generator is given the top passages of a dense retriever together with the question. The hallucination rate is the share of answers that contain at least one unsupported statement.

Each answer was judged by annotators who saw the question, the answer and the reference passages. A further reader settled disagreements, and judgements were recorded before any aggregate scores were computed.

Decoding settings, prompt wording and output length limits were identical for both conditions, so that differences between them reflect the input and not the generation procedure. SciFactBench was selected as the main benchmark because its questions require scientific background, which makes them a demanding test of unsupported content.

## Discussion

This study relies on a main benchmark drawn from scientific questions, so its findings describe that question set and its scoring procedure. The annotators judged answers against the reference passages supplied for each question, which limits their judgements to the sources available to them. Scoring unsupported statements requires judgement, and a larger group of annotators would give a firmer estimate of how consistently the criterion was applied. Prompt wording and decoding settings were held fixed, which keeps the design simple but leaves open how sensitive the conditions are to other prompt formats. Further studies could extend the design to other benchmarks and to other scientific domains.

## Conclusion

Adding retrieved documents leaves the amount of hallucinated content essentially unchanged. This paper compared the conditions on a main benchmark under a shared protocol, and the comparison is bounded by the design choices described above. Further work could test whether the same pattern holds on other benchmarks and with other groups of annotators.
