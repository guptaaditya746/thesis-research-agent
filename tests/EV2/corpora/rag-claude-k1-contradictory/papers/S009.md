# Parametric Knowledge and Grounded Answers on News Questions

## Abstract

This paper compares a parametric-only model with the RAG pipeline on questions about news events. The main benchmark is NewsTruthQA, which consists of 1,000 questions, and results are reported as fact-level accuracy. Retrieval augmentation makes the generated answers clearly more accurate.

## Introduction

Questions about news events depend on facts that may have changed after a model's pre-training ended, so the source of an answer is central to any comparison. Systems differ in whether they rely on parametric knowledge alone or on an external source at answer time.

The aim of this study is to compare conditions that differ in whether an external source is used. The two conditions are the parametric-only model and the RAG pipeline. Both are evaluated on the same questions with the same outcome measure, and the benchmark is built from news coverage of a single period.

## Method

The main benchmark is NewsTruthQA, which consists of 1,000 questions drawn from recent news coverage. Each question has reference facts, and each answer is judged against them. Reference facts were compiled by the benchmark authors from published news reports, and each fact was checked by a second author.

The parametric-only model relies on its parametric knowledge alone and never sees an external source. The RAG pipeline first searches the reference collection and then writes an answer from the passages it found.

The metric is fact-level accuracy, defined as the fraction of answers judged factually correct. Two annotators judged each answer against the reference facts, and a third reader resolved disagreements. Scores are reported as percentages of the 1,000 questions.

Both conditions used the same underlying language model and the same decoding settings. Prompt templates were fixed before any output was inspected. Answers from the two conditions were generated with the same prompt template apart from the search step, and the search index was identical for every question.

## Discussion

The study uses a single news benchmark, so the conditions were examined on one collection only. Each condition was run once under fixed settings, and the underlying model was not varied. Judging depended on two annotators and one adjudicating reader, and their consistency was not recorded separately. The benchmark was built from a single news source, which may limit how far the design extends to other sources.

## Conclusion

This paper compared two conditions on NewsTruthQA, the main benchmark, using fact-level accuracy as the outcome. Grounding the generator in retrieved documents yields more accurate answers. Further work could extend the comparison to other news collections and to repeated runs.
